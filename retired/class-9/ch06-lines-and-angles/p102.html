The angle $\angle DAE$ is the gap between two lines from $A$, and each of them can be placed from a triangle. First the whole of $\angle A$: the angle sum gives $\angle A = 180^\circ - 70^\circ - 40^\circ = 70^\circ$, so the bisector makes $\angle BAD = 35^\circ$. In triangle $ABE$ the angle at $E$ is $90^\circ$, so $\angle BAE = 90^\circ - 70^\circ = 20^\circ$. $E$ lies between $B$ and $D$, and so

$$\angle DAE = \angle BAD - \angle BAE = 35^\circ - 20^\circ = 15^\circ$$

The same two steps work with letters. The bisector gives $\angle BAD = \tfrac{1}{2}(180^\circ - \angle B - \angle C)$, and the right angle gives $\angle BAE = 90^\circ - \angle B$. Take one from the other:

$$\angle DAE = 90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle B - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle C - 90^\circ + \angle B = \tfrac{1}{2}(\angle B - \angle C)$$

With $70^\circ$ and $40^\circ$ that is half of $30^\circ$, the $15^\circ$ found above. Notice that $\angle A$ has dropped out of the general answer. The question gave two angles, and the angle sum settled the third whether anyone asked for it or not.

In Fig. 6.13, $AB \parallel CD$, and $P$ lies between the two lines with $\angle BAP = 55^\circ$ and $\angle PDC = 40^\circ$. Find $\angle APD$.

Every parallel result in the chapter is about a pair of angles with a name — corresponding, alternate, co-interior — and a pair only earns its name when both angles sit at a crossing of the same two lines. Here $P$ is on neither parallel, so $\angle APD$ is at no crossing at all, and the chapter appears to have nothing to say about it.

So make a crossing. Draw through $P$ the line parallel to $AB$; because $AB \parallel CD$, that line is parallel to $CD$ as well. It cuts $\angle APD$ into two parts. The upper part is alternate to $\angle BAP$, and the lower part is alternate to $\angle PDC$, so

$$\angle APD = 55^\circ + 40^\circ = 95^\circ$$
