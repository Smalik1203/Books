The line through $P$ was not given; it was drawn, and drawing it is the whole of the work. It is the same move the angle sum makes in Fig. 6.11, where a line through $A$ turns two angles that were nowhere near each other into two that lie along one straight line. When a figure offers no named pair, that is the question to ask: which line would create one?

Two lines cross at $O$, making four angles, and the bisectors of all four are drawn. Show that the four bisectors make up two straight lines, and that those two lines are at right angles.

Nothing is given in degrees, so the answer has to come from totals. Going round $O$, the four angles are $a$, $b$, $a$, $b$ in order, since vertically opposite angles are equal, and each neighbouring pair is a linear pair, so $a + b = 180^\circ$.

Take the bisector of one angle $a$ and the bisector of the angle $b$ next to it. Between them lie half of $a$ and half of $b$, so the angle between them is

$$\tfrac{1}{2}a + \tfrac{1}{2}b = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b) = 90^\circ$$

Now take the bisectors of the two angles $a$. Going round from one to the other passes half of one $a$, the whole of $b$, and half of the other $a$, which is $a + b = 180^\circ$. A turn of $180^\circ$ carries a ray onto its opposite ray, so those two bisectors lie in one straight line. So do the two bisectors of the angles $b$, and the first calculation settled the angle between the two lines: $90^\circ$. The size of $a$ never mattered. Only the total of a linear pair did.

Two mirrors $PQ$ and $RS$ are placed parallel to each other, as in Fig. 6.14. A ray of light travels along $AB$, strikes $RS$ at $B$, is reflected along $BC$, strikes $PQ$ at $C$ and leaves along $CD$. A mirror sends light away at the same angle to its surface as the light arrived at. Show that $AB \parallel CD$.

Nothing in the chapter mentions mirrors, but the one fact the question hands over is a fact about angles. At $B$ the ray arrives at some angle to $RS$ and leaves at the same angle, so $\angle ABR = \angle CBS$; call each of them $\theta$. The three angles at $B$ along the line $RS$ make a straight angle, and so

$$\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 2\theta$$
