7.7.1 Volume and capacity

Capacity is volume looked at from the point of view of what will fit inside, and it is measured in litres rather than cubes. The two are the same measurement in different units, and two conversions carry everything.

1 cm3 = 1 millilitre
1000 cm3 = 1 litre
1 m3 = 1000 litres

The last of those surprises people, and it is worth checking rather than believing: a cubic metre is $100 \times 100 \times 100$ cubic centimetres, which is a million of them, and a million millilitres is a thousand litres.

Example 6

A tank is $2$ m long, $1.5$ m broad and $1$ m deep. How many litres does it hold?

Solution.

Step 1 $2 \times 1.5 \times 1 = 3$ m3 the volume
Step 2 $3 \times 1000 = 3000$ litres 1 m3 is 1000 litres
Answer 3000 litres

Work in metres throughout and convert once at the end. Converting each measurement to centimetres first gives $200 \times 150 \times 100 = 3\,000\,000$ cm3, which is the same answer with a great deal more chance of dropping a zero.

Volume questions also run backwards, and again the tool is Chapter 6.

Example 7

A cylindrical drum of radius $7$ cm holds $3080$ cm3. How tall is it? Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 7^2 \times h = 3080$ the volume formula
Step 2 $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 49 = 154$ the base area
Step 3 $154h = 3080$ divide by 154
Answer $h = 20$ cm

Working out the base area as a single number first turns the problem into $154h = 3080$, which is an equation of the kind Chapter 6 opened with.
