Example 14 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) $6 - 5x = 21$(1) 2
(Q) $8x + 3 = 7$(2) $-3$
(R) $9x - 7 = 4x + 13$(3) $\frac{1}{2}$
(S) $6 - 3(x - 2) = 2(x + 1)$(4) 4

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  2. P–2, Q–3, R–4, S–1
  3. P–2, Q–4, R–3, S–1
  4. P–1, Q–3, R–4, S–2

Solution.

Step 1$-5x = 15$ gives $-3$; $8x = 4$ gives $\frac{1}{2}$; $5x = 20$ gives 4.
Step 2$12 - 3x = 2x + 2$ gives $5x = 10$ and $x = 2$.
Answer(b) P–2, Q–3, R–4, S–1
Example 15 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) Middle one of three consecutive numbers adding to 48(1) 14
(Q) Middle one of three consecutive odd numbers adding to 63(2) 11
(R) Middle one of three consecutive even numbers adding to 42(3) 16
(S) A number that, added to 5 more than itself, makes 27(4) 21

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–2, Q–4, R–1, S–3
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–1, S–2
  3. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  4. P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2

Solution.

Step 1Three evenly spaced numbers add to 3 times the middle one: $48 \div 3 = 16$, $63 \div 3 = 21$ and $42 \div 3 = 14$.
Step 2$n + (n + 5) = 27$ gives $2n = 22$ and $n = 11$.
Answer(d) P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
