## 6.2 What an Equation Says

### Exercise Set 6.1

1. (a) left side $5x$, right side $30$ (b) left side $2y - 1$, right side $7$
   (c) left side $9$, right side $4 + m$ (d) left side $3t + 2$, right side
   $t - 6$.

2. All three are solutions.
   - (a) $3 \times 4 - 5 = 7$: **yes**.
   - (b) $5 \times (-2) + 1 = -9$: **yes**.
   - (c) $2 \times 6 + 3 = 15$ and $4 \times 6 - 9 = 15$: **yes**.

3. (a) **linear** in one variable. (b) **not** — the letter is squared.
   (c) **not** — two variables. (d) **not** — the letter is in a denominator.

4. (a) $x + 7 = 12$ gives $x = 5$ (b) $4x = 28$ gives $x = 7$
   (c) $x - 3 = 0$ gives $x = 3$ (d) $2x + 1 = 11$ gives $x = 5$

5. At $x = 3$ the left side is $4 \times 3 - 1 = 11$, not 12, so $x = 3$ is not
   a solution. The left side grows as $x$ grows, and 11 is too small, so the
   solution is **bigger** than 3. (In fact $4x - 1 = 12$ gives $x = \dfrac{13}{4}$.)

6. With $n$ for the number: (a) $n + 5 = 19$ (b) $3n - 4 = 20$
   (c) $\dfrac{n}{7} = 6$.

7. $x = 3$ and $x = -3$, since $3 \times 3 = 9$ and $(-3) \times (-3) = 9$. A linear
   equation has the letter only to the first power, so as the letter grows one
   side changes steadily and can meet the other side only once: it has exactly
   one solution (or, in § 6.7, none or every number — never two).

8. At $x = 5$ both sides are $16$; at $x = -4$ both sides are $-2$. It is true
   for every value of $x$: an **identity**, as Chapter 4 called it.

## 6.3 The Same to Both Sides

### Think and Reflect (moving the 7 across)

Nothing moves: 7 is **subtracted from both sides**, giving
$x + 7 - 7 = 12 - 7$. The left side simplifies to $x$, and the right side is
still written $12 - 7$. Leave out the middle line and it looks as if the $+7$
had crossed over and become $-7$ — that is why the sign rule comes out true.

### Exercise Set 6.2

1. (a) $x + 9 = 15$ gives $x = 6$ (b) $y - 6 = 2$ gives $y = 8$
   (c) $m + 11 = 4$ gives $m = -7$ (d) $t - 3 = -8$ gives $t = -5$
   Checks: $6 + 9 = 15$, $8 - 6 = 2$, $-7 + 11 = 4$, $-5 - 3 = -8$.

2. (a) $6x = 42$ gives $x = 7$ (b) $-3y = 21$ gives $y = -7$

3. (a) $2x + 5 = 17$ gives $x = 6$ (b) $4y - 3 = 25$ gives $y = 7$
   (c) $9 - m = 2$ gives $m = 7$ (d) $7 - 3t = 19$ gives $t = -4$

4. (a) $2x = 7$ gives $x = \dfrac{7}{2}$ (b) $4y + 1 = 4$ gives $y = \dfrac{3}{4}$
   (c) $10m - 3 = 0$ gives $m = \dfrac{3}{10}$ (d) $5 - 2t = 6$ gives $t = -\dfrac{1}{2}$

5. $\dfrac{x}{3} + 2 = 9$ gives $x = 21$. Subtract 2 from both sides:
   $\dfrac{x}{3} = 7$. Multiply both sides by 3: $x = 21$. Check: $7 + 2 = 9$.

6. Check: $5 - 2 \times (-2) = 9$, not 1, so Ravi is wrong. Subtracting 5 from
   both sides gives $-2x = -4$; he lost the sign when dividing by $-2$.

7. Answers will vary. For example $3(x - 2) = 12$ gives $x = 6$ (it has a
   subtraction and a multiplication), and $4x + 2 = 0$ gives $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.

8. Both sides become $0$: $0 = 0$. It is true whatever $x$ is, so the
   information that $x$ is 3 has been thrown away.

9. $x = \dfrac{b}{a}$, and **$a$ must not be zero**, because the last step

## 6.4 Unknowns on Both Pans

### Exercise Set 6.3

1. (a) $5x = 2x + 12$ gives $x = 4$ (b) $7y - 3 = 4y$ gives $y = 1$
   (c) $8m = 3m + 20$ gives $m = 4$ (d) $6t + 1 = t + 21$ gives $t = 4$

2. (a) $4x + 3 = 2x + 11$ gives $x = 4$ (b) $9y - 5 = 3y + 7$ gives $y = 2$
   (c) $2m + 9 = 5m - 6$ gives $m = 5$ (d) $7 - t = 3t - 1$ gives $t = 2$

3. $3x - 4 = 8x + 11$ gives $x = -3$. On the left: take $8x$ and add 4, so
   $-5x = 15$ and $x = -3$. On the right: take $3x$ and 11, so $-15 = 5x$ and
   $x = -3$. Gathering on the right is easier, because it already has more
   $x$s and the coefficient stays positive.

4. (a) $5 - 2x = 3 - x$ gives $x = 2$ (b) $4y + 7 = 7y - 8$ gives $y = 5$
   (c) $10 - 3m = m - 6$ gives $m = 4$ (d) $2t - 9 = 5 - 5t$ gives $t = 2$

5. $4x + 1 = 2x + 9$ gives $x = 4$. The common value is $4 \times 4 + 1 = 17$.

6. $2(x + 7) = 2(2x + 3)$ gives $x = 4$. Check: $2 \times (4 + 7) = 22$ and
   $2 \times (8 + 3) = 22$.

7. The step from $4x - 4 = 8$ to $4x = 4$ is wrong: 4 must be **added** to
   both sides, giving $4x = 12$. $6x - 4 = 2x + 8$ gives $x = 3$. Check:
   $18 - 4 = 14$ and $6 + 8 = 14$.

8. $5x - 2 = 3(x + 2)$ gives $x = 4$. Check: $5 \times 4 - 2 = 18 = 3 \times 6$.

9. $ax - cx = d - b$, so $x = \dfrac{d - b}{a - c}$. **$a$ must not equal $c$.**
   If $a = c$, the pans carry the same number of bags, and taking them off
   leaves $b = d$: no solution if $b$ and $d$ differ, every number if they are
   equal. Dividing by $a - c$ would be dividing by zero.

   $4(x + 2) - 3(x - 1) = 12$, so $x + 11 = 12$.

## 6.6 From Words to an Equation

### Exercise Set 6.5

1. (a) $n$ is the number. $2n + 7 = 31$ gives $n = 12$
   (b) $n$ is the number. $n = 40 - 13$, so the number is **27**.
   (c) $n$ is the number. $\dfrac{n}{3} + 4 = 10$ gives $n = 18$

2. $n$ is the smaller. $n + (n + 1) = 75$ gives $n = 37$: **37 and 38**.

3. $n$ is the smallest. $n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 84$ gives $n = 26$:
   **26, 28 and 30**.

4. $d$ is the daughter's age in years. $3d + 8 = 2(d + 8)$ gives $d = 8$:
   daughter **8**, mother **24**. In eight years: 16 and 32.

5. $w$ is the width in cm. $2(w + 2w) = 54$ gives $w = 9$: 9 cm by 18 cm, area
   **162 cm²**.

6. $x$ is each equal angle in degrees. $x + x + (x + 30) = 180$ gives $x = 50$:
   **50°, 50° and 80°**.

7. $p$ is the number of pens. $8p + 3(2p) = 98$ gives $p = 7$: **7 pens and 14
   pencils**. Check: $56 + 42 = 98$.

8. $x + (x + 10) + (x + 20) + (x + 30) = 360$ gives $x = 75$ (the angles of a
   quadrilateral add to 360°): **75°, 85°, 95° and 105°**.

9. $n - 30 = 58 - n$ gives $n = 44$. Without an equation: the number is
   halfway between 30 and 58, and $(30 + 58) \div 2 = 44$.

10. $x$ is the smaller share in rupees. $x + (x + 60) = 720$ gives $x = 330$:
    **₹330 and ₹390**. With a difference of ₹800: $x + (x + 800) = 720$
    gives $x = -40$. A share of $-40$ rupees is not a share, so it cannot be
    done: the difference is bigger than the whole sum.

## 6.7 No Answer, and Every Answer

### Think and Reflect ($k$)

Answers will vary for the first part. For example $k = 0$: $3x + 7 = 3x + 0$ gives
$7 = 0$, so there is no solution. Any $k$ other than 7 does the same. The one
value that makes every number a solution is **$k = 7$**.

For $kx + 7 = 3x + 7$: $k = 3$ makes every number a solution. For every other
$k$, $(k - 3)x = 0$, so the one solution is **$x = 0$**.

### Exercise Set 6.6

1. (a) $4x + 1 = 4x + 9$: no solution ($1 = 9$ is false)
   (b) $3(x - 2) = 3x - 6$: every number ($-6 = -6$)
   (c) $5x + 2 = 3x + 12$ gives $x = 5$ — one solution

2. (a) $2(3x + 1) = 6x + 2$: every number ($2 = 2$)
   (b) $7 - x = 7 - x + 1$: no solution ($7 = 8$ is false)
   (c) $4(x + 1) = 4x + 1$: no solution ($4 = 1$ is false)

3. **$k = 9$**. For every other value of $k$ the letter cancels and a false
   statement is left, so there is no solution.

4. **$k = 5$**. Then every number is a solution. For any other $k$ the only
   solution is $x = 0$.

5. Answers will vary. No solution: $x + 1 = x + 2$ — the same letter part on
   both sides with different numbers. Every number: $2(x + 1) = 2x + 2$ — one
   expression written two ways.

6. $50 + 12d = 80 + 12d$ leaves $50 = 80$, which is false: **no distance**.
   Both firms add ₹12 a kilometre, so the second always costs ₹30 more.

7. $50 + 12d = 20 + 15d$ gives $d = 10$: **10 km**, when each costs
   $50 + 120 = 170$ rupees.

8. $3x + 5 = 3x + 5$ leaves $5 = 5$, which is true for **every** number. $x = 0$
   is a solution ($5 = 5$), but so is every other number; writing $x = 0$ claims
   it is the only one.

9. **No.** Solving always ends in one of three ways: the letter survives and
   there is exactly one solution; or the letter cancels and there is none; or it
   cancels and every number is a solution. None of the three gives exactly two.
