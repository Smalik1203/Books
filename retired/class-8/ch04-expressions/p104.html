the square $(n^2 + 3n + 1)^2$

Check it at $n = 3$: $9 + 9 + 1 = 19$, and $19^2 = 361$. The pairing was the whole idea — pair them the other way and the two brackets differ by $3$, and nothing tidy happens.

Two of those — the sixth and the seventh — are a different kind of question from the rest. They ask you to show that something holds for every value, and no amount of trying numbers will settle that. What settles it is finding a square somewhere in the expression, because a square is never negative, and that single fact carries more of this section than any other.

The rest reward one habit: before calculating anything, look at what you have been given and ask which identity has those pieces in it. A question that hands you $a+b$ and $ab$ is handing you the two halves of $(a+b)^2$, and is almost certainly not asking you to find $a$ and $b$ at all.

The last question looks like arithmetic and is not. Multiplying $3 \times 4 \times 5 \times 6$ tells you about one case; what makes it a proof is choosing which two of the four to multiply together first.

Example 1 · Single correct

What is the degree of $4x^3y - x^2y^2 + 7xy - 2$?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 7
  4. 2

Solution.

Step 1The degree of a term is the sum of the exponents of its letters: 4, 4, 2 and 0.
Step 2The degree of the expression is the largest of these, 4.
Answer(b) 4
Example 2 · Single correct

$(3a - 2b)(a + 4b)$ is equal to

Choose one correct option.

  1. $3a^2 + 14ab - 8b^2$
  2. $3a^2 - 10ab - 8b^2$
  3. $3a^2 + 10ab - 8b^2$
  4. $3a^2 + 10ab + 8b^2$

Solution.

Step 1The four products are $3a^2$, $12ab$, $-2ab$ and $-8b^2$.
Step 2$12ab - 2ab = 10ab$, so the result is $3a^2 + 10ab - 8b^2$.
Answer(c) $3a^2 + 10ab - 8b^2$
