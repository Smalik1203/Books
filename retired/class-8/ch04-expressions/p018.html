4.7Putting Them to Work

An identity is true for every value of its letters, and numbers are values. So the three results are quick arithmetic too, if you can see the sum or the difference in the numbers.

The trick, and the only one, is to write each number as a round number plus or minus a small one.

Example 9

Find $102^2$, $97^2$ and $103 \times 97$ without a written multiplication.

Solution.

Step 1 $102^2 = (100+2)^2 = 10000 + 400 + 4$ square of a sum
Step 2 $97^2 = (100-3)^2 = 10000 - 600 + 9$ square of a difference
Step 3 $103 \times 97 = (100+3)(100-3) = 10000 - 9$ difference of squares
Answer $102^2 = 10404$,   $97^2 = 9409$,   $103 \times 97 = 9991$

The third is the striking one. Two three-figure numbers multiplied, and the whole calculation is $10000 - 9$ — because they happen to sit the same distance either side of $100$.
