4.4.2 One term by a whole expression

Here is the rectangle from the first page, drawn properly. A rectangle $a$ tall and $b + c$ wide is cut by one vertical line into a rectangle $a$ by $b$ and a rectangle $a$ by $c$.

So to multiply an expression by a single term, multiply every term of it by that term. The picture only shows two pieces, but nothing stops you cutting the rectangle three times or ten, and the argument does not change.

$$a(b + c + d + \cdots) = ab + ac + ad + \cdots$$

Doing this is called expanding, and the brackets are said to have been removed. It is worth being clear that they have not been ignored: every term inside has been used, and nothing inside has been left out.

Example 4

Expand $4x(2x^2 - 3x + 5)$.

Three terms inside, so three multiplications.

Solution.

Step 1 $4x \times 2x^2 = 8x^3$ the powers add
Step 2 $4x \times (-3x) = -12x^2$ the sign comes too
Step 3 $4x \times 5 = 20x$
Answer $8x^3 - 12x^2 + 20x$

Every term of the answer has degree one more than the term it came from, because $4x$ has degree $1$.

When the term outside is negative, every sign inside is reversed — which is the rule about subtracting a bracket, arrived at a second time and from a different direction.
