Example 3

Subtract $4ab - 3b^2 + 5$ from $7ab + b^2 - 2$.

From tells you which way round: the second expression is the one being reduced.

Solution.

Step 1 $(7ab + b^2 - 2) - (4ab - 3b^2 + 5)$ write it out
Step 2 $7ab + b^2 - 2 - 4ab + 3b^2 - 5$ change all three signs
Answer $3ab + 4b^2 - 7$

The $-3b^2$ became $+3b^2$ and the $+5$ became $-5$. A subtraction in which only the first sign changes is the error to watch for. Checking at $a = b = 1$ catches it at once, since $7+1-2 = 6$ and $4-3+5 = 6$, so the answer must come to $0$ — and $3 + 4 - 7$ does.

That check is worth making a habit of, because it costs almost nothing and catches most slips. Put a convenient number into every letter — $1$ is usually the convenient one — and work out both the expression you started with and the expression you finished with. If the two numbers differ, something between them is wrong.

What the check cannot do is prove you right. Two different expressions may agree at $a = b = 1$ by accident, and $a$ and $a^2$ agree there. Agreement at one value is weak evidence; disagreement at one value is proof of an error, and that one-way test is what makes it worth its few seconds.
