Then try $x^5 - y^5$. Do you think $x - y$ divides $x^n - y^n$ for every $n$? What happens if you put $x = y$?

Three letters at once

The same experiment works with three letters. Multiply $x + y + z$ by $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xy - xz - yz$, taking the second bracket once with each term of the first:

$x\left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xy - xz - yz\right) = x^3 + xy^2 + xz^2 - x^2y - x^2z - xyz$

$y\left(\;\cdots\;\right) = x^2y + y^3 + yz^2 - xy^2 - xyz - y^2z$

$z\left(\;\cdots\;\right) = x^2z + y^2z + z^3 - xyz - xz^2 - yz^2$

Now add the three lines. Every term with two different letters appears twice, once with each sign: $xy^2$ against $-xy^2$, $xz^2$ against $-xz^2$, $x^2y$ against $-x^2y$, and so on. All of them vanish. What survives is the three cubes and three copies of $-xyz$:

$$(x + y + z)\left(x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - xy - xz - yz\right) = x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$$

That identity looks forbidding, but it earns its keep. It ties together four quantities — the sum of three numbers, the sum of their squares, the sum of their cubes, and their product — so that knowing any three of them gives you the fourth.

Example 15

Three numbers add to 10, multiply to 25, and their squares add to 38. What do their cubes add to?

Call them $x$, $y$ and $z$, so $x + y + z = 10$, $xyz = 25$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 38$. Substituting into the identity:

$10\left(38 - (xy + xz + yz)\right) = x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3(25)$

$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 455 - 10(xy + xz + yz)$
