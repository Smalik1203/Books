An identity is not a fact about algebra. It is a fact about numbers, which means it turns up wherever numbers do — in a distance, in a pattern of differences, in a surd, in a measured area. Each question below reaches into a chapter you have already met, or out of the book altogether. Naming the identity you need is most of the work.

B3 · Connect the Concepts
  1. Chapter 1 measured a point $P(a, b)$'s distance from the origin by $OP^2 = a^2 + b^2$. A point in the first quadrant has $a + b = 7$ and $ab = 10$.
    1. Find $OP^2$ without finding $a$ or $b$.
    2. Now find $a$ and $b$, and say which quadrilateral the four points $(\pm a, \pm b)$ form.
  2. A linear pattern (Chapter 2) has a constant difference between consecutive terms. Evaluate $x^2 + 7x + 12$ at $x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5$ and write down the differences; they are not constant. Take the differences again. Use the identity $(n+1)^2 = n^2 + 2n + 1$ to explain the number you get, and say what it would be for $x^2 + 7x + 100$.
  3. Chapter 3 met numbers that are not ratios. Let $x = 2 + \sqrt{3}$.
    1. Multiply $\left(2+\sqrt{3}\right)\left(2-\sqrt{3}\right)$ using one identity, and deduce $\dfrac{1}{x} = 2 - \sqrt{3}$.
    2. Hence find $x + \dfrac{1}{x}$ and then $x^2 + \dfrac{1}{x^2}$, without squaring any surd.
  4. In science you met $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ for motion with constant acceleration. Factorise the left-hand side, and write the equation as a statement about the sum and the difference of the two speeds. A car goes from 18 to 22 metres per second over a distance of 80 metres: find $a$ using the factorised form, and say why the numbers are kinder that way round.
  5. A square field of side $a$ metres has a square pond of side $b$ metres cut out of one corner. The owner never measures $a$ or $b$ at all; he measures their sum as $84$ m and their difference as $12$ m.
    1. Find the area of field that is left.
    2. Explain why no further measurement is needed — and what he would have to measure instead if the pond were rectangular.
  6. A shopkeeper sells 49 items at 51 rupees each and works the total out in his head as $2500 - 1$.
    1. Which identity is he using, and with which $a$ and $b$?
    2. Use the same trick on $58 \times 62$ and on $97 \times 103$.
