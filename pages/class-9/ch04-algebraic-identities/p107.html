Example 9

Factorise $6x^2 + 5x - 6$ by splitting the middle term.

Solution.

Step 1 $6 \times (-6) = -36$ the two end coefficients multiplied
Step 2 $9 \times (-4) = -36$ and $9 + (-4) = 5$ the pair whose sum is the middle coefficient
Step 3 $6x^2 + 5x - 6 = 6x^2 + 9x - 4x - 6$ split $5x$ as $9x - 4x$
Step 4 $= 3x(2x + 3) - 2(2x + 3)$ group in pairs
Answer $6x^2 + 5x - 6 = (2x + 3)(3x - 2)$
Check $(2x + 3)(3x - 2) = 6x^2 - 4x + 9x - 6 = 6x^2 + 5x - 6$

When the coefficient of $x^2$ is not $1$, the pair comes from the product of the two end coefficients, here $-36$, and not from the constant alone. That is the rule behind $(px + a)(qx + b)$ in Section 4.5.

Example 10

Find $998^3$ using an identity.

Solution.

Step 1 $998^3 = (1000 - 2)^3$
Step 2 $= 1000^3 - 3 \times 1000^2 \times 2 + 3 \times 1000 \times 2^2 - 2^3$ $(a - b)^3$ identity
Step 3 $= 1000000000 - 6000000 + 12000 - 8$
Answer $998^3 = 994011992$
Example 11

Show that $27x^3 - 54x^2y + 36xy^2 - 8y^3$ is a perfect cube, and find what it is the cube of.

Solution.

Step 1 $27x^3 = (3x)^3$ and $8y^3 = (2y)^3$ the cubes at the ends; the signs alternate
Step 2 $3(3x)^2(2y) = 54x^2y$ the second term agrees
Step 3 $3(3x)(2y)^2 = 36xy^2$ the third term agrees
Answer $27x^3 - 54x^2y + 36xy^2 - 8y^3 = (3x - 2y)^3$

Both middle terms have to be checked. With $30xy^2$ in place of $36xy^2$ the two ends would still match, and the expression would not be a cube.
