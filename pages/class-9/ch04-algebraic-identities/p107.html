The Challenge Zone
Think, then apply, then challenge.

Not one of these says which idea to use. Show every step of the reasoning.

  1. Think Show that
    $$a^2 + b^2 + c^2 - ab - bc - ca = \tfrac{1}{2}\left[(a-b)^2 + (b-c)^2 + (c-a)^2\right]$$
    and deduce that this quantity is never negative, and is zero exactly when $a = b = c$. Now read the chapter's last identity again: what does your result say about the sign of $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$ when $x$, $y$ and $z$ are all positive?
  2. Apply Three numbers satisfy $a + b + c = 6$, $a^2 + b^2 + c^2 = 14$ and $abc = 6$.
    1. Find $ab + bc + ca$, then $a^3 + b^3 + c^3$, without finding $a$, $b$ or $c$.
    2. Now find the three numbers themselves, and check both answers against them.
  3. Apply Factorise $x^4 + 4y^4$ into two quadratic factors.

    Nothing here is a difference of squares yet. Add $4x^2y^2$ to make the first three terms a perfect square, then subtract it again — and one identity finishes the job.

  4. Challenge A rectangle has perimeter $34$ cm and diagonal $13$ cm (Fig. 4.12). Find its area without finding either side. Then find the sides, and say which of the two routes would still work if the diagonal were $\sqrt{200}$ cm.
  5. Challenge Show that $n^4 + 4$ is never a prime number when $n$ is a whole number greater than $1$. Then say what happens to your argument at $n = 1$, and why that single case escapes.
