Why the other options are wrong

B6 If a product is zero, one factor must be zero. If a product is $5$, this tells us nothing about the factors. Put $x = 17$ back in. You get $5 \times 25 = 125$, not $5$. Multiplying out gives $x^2 - 4x - 101 = 0$. This equation has two solutions. So (d) is wrong too.
C2 First, $ab + bc + ca = \tfrac{1}{2}\left(6^2 - 14\right) = 11$. So $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 - 3abc = 6 \times (14 - 11) = 18$. Then $a^3 + b^3 + c^3 = 18 + 18 = 36$. Option (a) is the $18$ before $3abc$ is added back. The numbers are $1$, $2$, $3$.
C5 $n^5 - n = (n-2)(n-1)\,n\,(n+1)(n+2) + 5\,(n-1)\,n\,(n+1)$. Five numbers in a row always include a multiple of $2$, a multiple of $3$ and a multiple of $5$. The second term is $5$ times a multiple of $6$. So $30$ always divides $n^5 - n$. At $n = 2$ the value is exactly $30$. This rules out (d).
