A reasoning question has no working to show. It wants the sentence that would convince someone who disagreed — so answer in words, name the identity you lean on, and kill a false claim with one counter-example rather than an argument.

B2 · Think and Reason
  1. Section 4.2 checked $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$ at three pairs of numbers, and then proved it in four lines. Explain what the four lines gave you that three thousand checks could not — and say what a single failed check would have given you.
  2. The picture in Fig. 4.2 argues the identity only for positive $a$ and $b$: a side of length $-2$ cannot be drawn. Yet the identity holds for negatives too.
    1. Does that make the picture worthless? Say precisely what it establishes and what it leaves open.
    2. Which later argument in the chapter closed the gap, and what was it about that argument that made it general?
  3. Without expanding anything, decide whether $(a+b+c)^2 - (a-b-c)^2$ is a multiple of $4$ for every choice of $a$, $b$ and $c$. Which single identity settles it, and what are its two brackets here?
  4. In Example 7 a common factor of $2$ came out first and the surds vanished.
    1. Does the same rescue work on $75p^2 + 90pq + 27q^2$?
    2. Invent an expression $Ap^2 + Bpq + Cq^2$ for which taking out the common factor leaves something that is not a perfect square, and say what fails.
  5. The chapter factorises $x^3 + y^3$, $x^3 - y^3$ and $x^2 - y^2$, but never $x^2 + y^2$. Try to build a factorisation for it by the method of Section 4.7, and report where the attempt stops.
  6. Put $x = y$ into $x^3 - y^3 = (x-y)\left(x^2+xy+y^2\right)$. Both sides become $0$: the statement stays true and tells you nothing. Explain how a true statement can carry no information, and what that means for the claim $x - y$ divides $x^n - y^n$.
  7. For positive $a$ and $b$ the chapter found $(a+b)^2 > a^2 + b^2$.
    1. Give a pair for which the inequality runs the other way, and a pair for which the two sides are equal.
    2. State the one condition on $ab$ that decides all three cases at once, and prove it from the identity.
