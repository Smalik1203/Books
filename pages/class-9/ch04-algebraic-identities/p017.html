Factorising by sum and product

To factorise $x^2 + px + q$, look for two numbers whose sum is $p$ and whose product is $q$. Their signs are settled before you start hunting:

Example 12 is the middle row: the product 6 is positive, so the numbers share a sign, and the sum $-5$ tells you which. Reading the signs off first halves the search.

The bottom row is the one worth practising, because there the search is widest. In $x^2 - x - 12$ the product $-12$ is negative, so the two numbers carry opposite signs. The pairs are $1$ and $-12$, $2$ and $-6$, $3$ and $-4$, with sums $-11$, $-4$ and $-1$. Only the last is the $-1$ wanted, so $x^2 - x - 12 = (x + 3)(x - 4)$.
