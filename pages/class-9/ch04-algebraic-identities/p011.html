A difference of squares

In Grade 8 you met one more identity, and it is the odd one out — the only one so far that is not about squaring a sum:

$$a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$$

Read left to right it factorises. But rearranged, it becomes something else entirely — a way of computing a square:

$$a^2 = (a + b)(a - b) + b^2$$

That looks like a poor trade — one square swapped for a product and another square. It is not, and a picture shows why. Slice a strip of width $b$ off the bottom of a square of side $a$, then cut that strip again at $a - b$. What is left is a $b \times b$ square; the long piece, stood on its end, is exactly the width needed to widen the top block from $a$ to $a + b$.

Now the trade makes sense. Choose $b$ so that $a - b$ and $a + b$ are both round numbers, and the product on the right is one you can do in your head, with $b^2$ small. An awkward square becomes an easy multiplication plus a correction.
