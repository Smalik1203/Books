The Trap Room
Two students, one question, and a mistake.

Answer (a) A only, (b) B only, (c) both, (d) neither — then say in one line what went wrong.

  1. A. $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$ — the square goes into the bracket a term at a time.  B. $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + ab$.
  2. A. $\dfrac{x^2 + 9}{x + 3}$ simplifies to $x + 3$.  B. It does not simplify at all.
  3. $x^2 - 4x + 4$ factorises as A. $(x - 2)^2$.  B. $(x - 2)(x + 2)$ — the constant comes out right.
  4. A. $(a - b)^2 = (b - a)^2$.  B. $(a - b)^2 = -(b - a)^2$, because reversing a subtraction changes the sign.
  5. A. $4x^2 + 9y^2$ cannot be factorised.  B. Therefore $4x^2 + 12xy + 9y^2$ cannot be either.
  6. To solve $(x - 12)(x + 8) = 5$: A. Set $x - 12 = 5$ or $x + 8 = 5$.  B. Multiply out first, then bring everything to one side.
  7. A. $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} = x + 2$ for every $x$.  B. It equals $x + 2$ for every $x$ except $2$.
  8. A. $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 3xyz$ whenever $x + y + z = 0$.  B. It holds whenever $xyz \neq 0$.
  9. A. $a^2 + b^2 \geq 2ab$ for every $a$ and $b$.  B. Equality happens only when $a = b$.
  10. A. $x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0$ is an identity.  B. $(x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + 5x + 6$ is an identity.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Distributing what does not distribute (1, 2, 5). A power does not go into a sum a term at a time, and a sum on top of a fraction cannot be cancelled piece by piece.
  2. Using a rule outside its one case (3, 4, 6, 8). A product equals zero only if a factor does — the rule says nothing about a product equal to $5$ — and the cube identity needs $x + y + z = 0$, not $xyz \neq 0$.
  3. Forgetting where an expression is undefined (7, 10). Cancelling $x - 2$ is legal only away from $x = 2$; an identity holds for every value, an equation for some.
