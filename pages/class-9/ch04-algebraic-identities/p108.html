B8 · The Trap Room
  1. Ravi writes $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$, because the square "goes into the bracket, one term at a time". Destroy the claim with one pair of numbers, and name the term he has lost.
  2. Meera simplifies $\frac{x^2 + 9}{x + 3}$ to $x + 3$, because "$x^2$ over $x$ is $x$, and $9$ over $3$ is $3$". Test her answer at $x = 1$, and say what she is cancelling that she may not cancel.
  3. Asked to factorise $x^2 - 4x + 4$, a student writes $(x - 2)(x + 2)$: both brackets look plausible and the constant comes out right. Multiply them back, and say which term exposes it.
  4. Amit says $(a - b)^2 = -(b - a)^2$, because reversing a subtraction changes the sign; Nisha says the two are equal. Decide with one substitution, then say exactly where Amit's rule is true and where it is not.
  5. "$4x^2 + 9y^2$ cannot be factorised, so $4x^2 + 12xy + 9y^2$ cannot be either." Is the conclusion right? Is the reason?
  6. To solve $(x - 12)(x + 8) = 5$ a student writes $x - 12 = 5$ or $x + 8 = 5$, giving $x = 17$ or $x = -3$. Substitute both back. Which rule of Section 4.9 was used outside the one case where it holds, and why does that case work when no other does?
  7. A student simplifies $\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}$ to $x + 2$, evaluates at $x = 2$ and reports $4$. The algebra is right and the answer is not. Explain the gap in one sentence.
Four shapes of mistake
  1. Spreading an operation that does not spread (traps 1, 4). Multiplication distributes over addition; squaring does not, and a minus sign cannot be carried out through a square. The false rule is shaped like a true one.
  2. Cancelling a term instead of a factor (trap 2). A fraction cancels only what multiplies the whole of the top and the whole of the bottom; appearing on both lines earns nothing.
  3. Matching a shape on too little evidence (traps 3, 5). Two squares at the ends do not make a perfect square — the middle term decides — and what a two-term expression will not do says nothing about a three-term one.
  4. Using a rule outside its one condition (traps 6, 7). $AB = 0$ forces $A = 0$ or $B = 0$; $AB = 5$ forces nothing. Here the working is flawless — until the condition is checked.
