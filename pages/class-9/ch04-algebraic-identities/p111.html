Example 18

The number of whole numbers $n$ for which $n^4 + 4$ is a prime number is

  1. $0$
  2. $1$
  3. $2$
  4. infinitely many

Solution.

Step 1 $n^4 + 4 = \left(n^2 - 2n + 2\right)\left(n^2 + 2n + 2\right)$ $x^4 + 4y^4$ from Stage 1, with $y = 1$
Step 2 a product of two whole numbers is prime only if one of them is $1$
Step 3 $n^2 - 2n + 2 = (n - 1)^2 + 1$, which is $1$ only when $n = 1$ the other factor is at least $2$
Step 4 at $n = 1$, $n^4 + 4 = 5$, which is prime
Answer (b)

For every other whole number both factors are at least $2$, so $n^4 + 4$ is not prime. Option (a) misses $n = 1$. Option (c) also counts $n = 0$, but $0^4 + 4 = 4$ is not prime.
