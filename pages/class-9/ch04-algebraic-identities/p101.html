The Idea Underneath
An identity is an equation until you add one inequality.

Every identity in the chapter was an equation. Combine one with the single fact that no square is negative and out comes a statement of a different kind. From $(a - b)^2 \geq 0$:

$$a^2 + b^2 \geq 2ab$$

with equality only when $a = b$. Put $a = t$ and $b = 1$, divide by a positive $t$, and $t + \frac{1}{t} \geq 2$, always — which is why the chapter offered $\frac{10}{3}$ and never $\frac{1}{3}$.

Read it both ways
$a$ and $b$ move apart↑ $a^2 + b^2$ beats $2ab$ by more↑
$a$ and $b$ move together↓ the gap closes to nothing↓
$(a+b)^2$ is four pieces
$a^2 - b^2$ is a square minus a square
A product is a rectangle
Off the middle, the two squares win
