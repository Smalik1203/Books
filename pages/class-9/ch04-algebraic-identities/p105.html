Problem 2

If $a + b = 7$ and $a^3 + b^3 = 133$, the number of real pairs $(a, b)$ is

  1. $0$
  2. $1$
  3. $2$
  4. $4$
Solution

A sum and a sum of cubes are given, and the identity $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)^3 - 3ab(a + b)$ connects them through the product.

$133 = 343 - 21ab$

$ab = 10$

$(a - b)^2 = (a + b)^2 - 4ab = 49 - 40 = 9$

$a - b = 3$  or  $a - b = -3$

The pairs are $(5, 2)$ and $(2, 5)$: two of them, option (c). Option (b) comes of writing $a - b = 3$ and stopping there, but a square root has two signs. Option (a) would be right only if $(a - b)^2$ had come out negative — and it could have.

Problem 3

A rectangle has perimeter $34$ cm and diagonal $13$ cm (Fig. 4B.4). Its area, in cm$^2$, is

  1. $30$
  2. $60$
  3. $120$
  4. cannot be found
Solution

Call the sides $x$ and $y$; the area is $xy$. The square of the sum is what contains $2xy$.

$x + y = 17$

$x^2 + y^2 = 169$  (Pythagoras)

$2xy = (x + y)^2 - \left(x^2 + y^2\right) = 289 - 169 = 120$

$xy = 60$

The area is $60$ cm$^2$, option (b), and no side was ever found. It could have been: $(x - y)^2 = 169 - 120 = 49$ makes the sides $12$ and $5$. Option (c) is $2xy$ itself, not halved.
