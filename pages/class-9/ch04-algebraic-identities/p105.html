Type 4 · Values without finding the letters

Example 5

If $a - b = 4$ and $ab = 21$, find $a^2 + b^2$ and $a + b$.

Solution.

Step 1 $(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ an identity holding both facts
Step 2 $16 = a^2 + b^2 - 42$, so $a^2 + b^2 = 58$
Step 3 $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + 2ab = 58 + 42 = 100$ $(a + b)^2$ identity
Answer $a^2 + b^2 = 58$, and $a + b$ is $10$ or $-10$
Check $7$ and $3$ fit, and so do $-3$ and $-7$

A square has two square roots, so $a + b$ is not fixed by the two facts, although $a^2 + b^2$ is.

Example 6

A rectangle has perimeter $34$ cm and diagonal $13$ cm (Fig. 4B.2). Its area, in cm$^2$, is

  1. $30$
  2. $60$
  3. $120$
  4. cannot be found

Solution.

Step 1 $x + y = 17$ half the perimeter
Step 2 $x^2 + y^2 = 169$ Pythagoras
Step 3 $2xy = (x + y)^2 - \left(x^2 + y^2\right) = 289 - 169 = 120$ the square of the sum
Step 4 $xy = 60$
Answer (b)

No side was found, and none was needed. It could have been: $(x - y)^2 = 169 - 120 = 49$ makes the sides $12$ and $5$. Option (c) is $2xy$, not halved; option (a) halves it twice; option (d) assumes the sides must come first.
