$(a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + 2a(b + c) + (b + c)^2$

$= a^2 + 2ab + 2ac + b^2 + 2bc + c^2$

Those six terms are easier to remember grouped by kind: the three squares first, then the three cross terms.

$$(a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca$$

Notice its shape. Every term is a square of one letter or twice a product of two, and every pairing appears once. If one is missing, something has gone wrong.

The picture behind it is the one from Section 4.2, grown by a term. Build a square of side $a + b + c$ and cut it at $a$ and at $a + b$ along both edges: nine pieces, three squares and six rectangles.
