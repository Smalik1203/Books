In an assertion–reason question, answer from this list every time: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

B6 · Competitive-Style Thinking
  1. Assertion. $(a - b)^2 = (b - a)^2$ for every $a$ and $b$.  Reason. A number and its negative have the same square.
  2. Assertion. $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 3xyz$ for all $x$, $y$, $z$ with $xyz \neq 0$.  Reason. $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz = (x+y+z)\left(x^2+y^2+z^2-xy-yz-zx\right)$.
  3. Which of the following are identities?
    1. $(x + 2)(x + 3) = x^2 + 5x + 6$
    2. $x^2 + 5x + 6 = 0$
    3. $\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} = x + 2$
    Choose: (a) (i) only   (b) (i) and (iii)   (c) (ii) and (iii)   (d) all three. Justify the narrowest exclusion.
  4. Given $a + b = 7$ and $a^3 + b^3 = 133$, find $ab$, and then find $(a - b)^2$. How many pairs $(a, b)$ are there, and are they all real?
  5. Take $n = 2, 3, 4, 5$ and compute $n^3 - n$: every answer is a multiple of $6$. Which of these explains it, and why do the others not?
    1. $n^3 - n$ is even, and every even number is a multiple of 6;
    2. $n^3 - n = (n-1)n(n+1)$, a product of three consecutive whole numbers;
    3. it was checked for four values of $n$.
