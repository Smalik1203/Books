Problem 4

For every whole number $n$, the number $n^3 - n$ is a multiple of

  1. $4$
  2. $6$
  3. $12$
  4. $24$
Solution

The question says for every $n$, so trying values can rule options out but cannot rule one in.

at $n = 2$:   $n^3 - n = 6$

$6$ is not a multiple of $4$, $12$ or $24$

$n^3 - n = n\left(n^2 - 1\right) = (n - 1)\,n\,(n + 1)$

Three whole numbers in a row (Fig. 4B.5): of any two in a row one is even, and of any three one is a multiple of $3$, so the product always carries a factor $2$ and a factor $3$. Option (b). Trying $n = 3, 4, 5$ gives multiples of $12$, which is how (c) is chosen.
