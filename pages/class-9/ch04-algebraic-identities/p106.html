Competitive-Style Thinking
Four moves worth more than another identity.
Eliminate The middle term is the fastest check. Multiply a candidate factorisation back and look only at the term in $x$.
Check sufficiency $a + b$ and $ab$ together fix every symmetric expression in $a$ and $b$. Neither on its own fixes any of them.
Find the constraint $(a - b)^2 = (a + b)^2 - 4ab$. Any two of those three quantities hand you the third.
Estimate first No square is negative. If an answer would need $(a-b)^2$ to be negative, the two numbers are not real.

Name the move, then use it.

  1. Eliminate. Check only the middle term. $x^2 - 4x + 4$ factorises as
    1. $(x-2)(x+2)$
    2. $(x-2)^2$
    3. $(x+2)^2$
    4. $(x-4)(x-1)$
  2. Sufficiency. Given $a + b = 7$ and $a^3 + b^3 = 133$, find $ab$ and then $(a - b)^2$. How many real pairs $(a, b)$ are there?
  3. Constraint. A student squares numbers by $a^2 = (a+b)(a-b) + b^2$. One row is wrong: find it, say what was done, and give the right answer.

  4. Estimate. $n^3 - n$ comes out a multiple of $6$ at $n = 2, 3, 4, 5$. Which of these explains it?
    1. It is even, and every even number is a multiple of $6$
    2. $n^3 - n = (n-1)n(n+1)$, three consecutive whole numbers
    3. It was checked for four values of $n$
    4. $6$ divides $n^3$ for every $n$
