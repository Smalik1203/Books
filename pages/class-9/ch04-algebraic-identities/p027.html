That question handed you the pieces and asked what they made. The next one goes the other way: it hands you the finished rectangle — its area, and how its two sides are related — and asks what those sides measure.

Example 18

A rectangular pool is 4 metres narrower than it is long, and its surface covers 96 square metres. How long and how wide is it?

Solution.

Step 1 let the length be $x$ metres; the breadth is $x - 4$ metres
Step 2 $x(x - 4) = 96, \quad\text{so}\quad x^2 - 4x - 96 = 0$ the area
Step 3 $(-12) \times 8 = -96$ and $(-12) + 8 = -4$, so write $-4x$ as $-12x + 8x$ product $-96$, sum $-4$
Step 4 $x^2 - 12x + 8x - 96 = x(x - 12) + 8(x - 12) = (x - 12)(x + 8)$ splitting the middle term
Step 5 $(x - 12)(x + 8) = 0$, so $x - 12 = 0$ or $x + 8 = 0$
Step 6 $x = 12$ or $x = -8$
Step 7 $x$ cannot be negative it is the length of a pool
Answer the length is 12 metres and the breadth is $12 - 4 = 8$ metres
Check $12 \times 8 = 96$, as required

Something new happened there. Every earlier use of factorising rewrote an expression; this one solved an equation. The step that allowed it is easy to walk past:

$$\text{if } AB = 0, \text{ then } A = 0 \text{ or } B = 0$$

Nothing like this holds for any other number. That $(x - 12)(x + 8) = 5$ tells you nothing about either bracket, because 5 splits as a product in endlessly many ways — but zero cannot. So the equation had to be rearranged with 0 on one side before factorising.

The last step is worth a habit too: the algebra offered two answers, the situation kept one.

It is worth being exact about what was discarded. $x = -8$ is not a mistake and not a false answer: it satisfies $x^2 - 4x - 96 = 0$ perfectly, and if you substitute it the equation holds. What it fails is the question, which asked for the length of a pool. Solving an equation and answering a question are two different acts, and the second one is where a negative length gets shown the door.
