Case-based questions

  1. Ravi has a box of algebra tiles like those in Section 4.5. He takes out the tiles in the table and tries to fit all of them, with no gaps or overlaps, into one rectangle.

    TileNumber
    $x^2$-tile$2$
    $x$-tile$7$
    unit tile$6$
    1. Write the total area of the tiles as an expression in $x$.
    2. Factorise it, and give the two sides of the rectangle.
    3. If $x = 10$ cm, find the length and the breadth of the rectangle.
    4. Ravi adds one more unit tile. Can the tiles still make a rectangle? Give a reason.

  1. A wooden cube of edge $(a + b)$ cm is sawn three times, once in each direction, each cut $a$ cm from the same corner, with $a = 10$ and $b = 2$. The eight pieces are listed below.

    PieceNumberEdges (cm)
    large cube$1$$10$, $10$, $10$
    thick slab$3$$10$, $10$, $2$
    thin rod$3$$10$, $2$, $2$
    small cube$1$$2$, $2$, $2$
    1. Find the volume of the whole cube.
    2. Find the total volume of the three thick slabs.
    3. Find the total volume of the three thin rods.
    4. Show that the eight pieces together make up the volume in (a), and name the identity this checks.
