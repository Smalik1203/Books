Example 12

Factorise $x^2 - 5x + 6$.

Solution.

Step 1 $a + b = -5$ the sum; the coefficient of $x$ is negative
Step 2 $ab = 6$ the product, comparing with $x^2 + (a + b)x + ab$
Step 3 both numbers are negative a positive product with a negative sum
Answer $-2$ and $-3$, in either order

That last step deserves a second look, because it did real work before any candidates were tried. The product 6 is positive, which means the two numbers share a sign — a positive times a negative could never reach it. The sum $-5$ then says which sign they share. Between them the two conditions settled the signs before a single pair was tested.

The saving is larger than it looks. Without that reasoning the factor pairs of 6 are $1$ and $6$, $2$ and $3$, and each may carry either sign — eight candidates. With it there are two.

Counting the candidates before starting is worth the moment it takes. Eight against two is the difference between a search and a check, and the reasoning that produced it used nothing but the signs of the two coefficients.

It is the same economy the identities themselves buy. Recognising a form saves the multiplying; reading the signs saves the searching. Neither is a new technique, and both are the difference between a method that works and a method that finishes.
