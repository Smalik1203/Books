Look at the coefficients: $1, 3, 3, 1$, against the $1, 2, 1$ of the squared identity. Powers of $a$ fall as powers of $b$ rise, and the numbers in front are symmetric — a pattern you will meet again.

So take a cube of edge $a + b$ and cut it with three planes, one perpendicular to each direction, each at a distance $a$ from the same corner. Fig. 4.9 shows those cuts on the three visible faces.

  • Three planes, one per direction, each cutting at $a$.
  • They divide the cube into eight pieces.
  • Two are cubes; the other six are cuboids.
The whole cube has volume $(a + b)^3$.

Think and Reflect

Work out the volume of each of the eight pieces. You should find one $a^3$, one $b^3$, and six cuboids of two kinds — then add them up.

Here is what the three cuts leave. Two of the eight pieces are cubes — one of edge $a$, one of edge $b$. The other six are cuboids in two kinds: three $a \times a \times b$, and three $a \times b \times b$.

Add the volumes. The two cubes give $a^3$ and $b^3$. Each of the three $a \times a \times b$ cuboids has volume $a^2b$, and each of the three $a \times b \times b$ cuboids has volume $ab^2$, so the six contribute $3a^2b + 3ab^2$ — the identity again, counted rather than multiplied out.
