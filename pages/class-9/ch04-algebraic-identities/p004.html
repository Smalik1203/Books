Think and Reflect
  1. What can you say about $a$ and $b$ if $(a + b)^2 < a^2 + b^2$?
  2. What can you say about $a$ and $b$ if $(a + b)^2 > a^2 + b^2$?
  3. When is $(a + b)^2$ equal to $a^2 + b^2$?

Write out the expansion of $(a + b)^2$: only one term separates the two sides. What does its sign depend on?

Because the identity holds for every $a$ and $b$, it reads in both directions: left to right it expands a squared bracket, right to left it collects one back up.

Example 3

Expand $(5x + 2y)^2$.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 5x$, $b = 2y$ match the identity
Step 2 $(5x + 2y)^2 = (5x)^2 + 2(5x)(2y) + (2y)^2$ $(a + b)^2$ expanded
Answer $(5x + 2y)^2 = 25x^2 + 20xy + 4y^2$
Example 4

Calculate $43^2$ without a calculator.

Solution.

Step 1 $43^2 = (40 + 3)^2$ split 43 into a round number and a small one
Step 2 $= 40^2 + 2 \times 40 \times 3 + 3^2$ take $a = 40$ and $b = 3$
Step 3 $= 1600 + 240 + 9$
Answer $43^2 = 1849$

The split is a choice, and only one choice helps. Writing $43$ as $20 + 23$ is just as true, but it leaves you squaring $23$ — which was the difficulty you started with. What makes $40 + 3$ the useful split is that both $40^2$ and $2 \times 40 \times 3$ can be done in the head.
