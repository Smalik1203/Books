The identity also settles something you may have suspected: $(a + b)^2$ and $a^2 + b^2$ are not the same. Which is larger? With $a = 10$ and $b = 2$,

$(a + b)^2$ $= 12^2 =$ 144
$a^2 + b^2$ $= 100 + 4 =$ 104

So here $(a + b)^2 > a^2 + b^2$. But that is one pair out of infinitely many. Is it always so?

Write out the expansion of $(a + b)^2$: only one term separates the two sides. What does its sign depend on?

Because the identity holds for every $a$ and $b$, it reads in both directions: left to right it expands a squared bracket, right to left it collects one back up.

Example 3

Expand $(5x + 2y)^2$. Match the identity with $a = 5x$, $b = 2y$:

$(5x + 2y)^2 = (5x)^2 + 2(5x)(2y) + (2y)^2 = 25x^2 + 20xy + 4y^2$

Example 4

Calculate $43^2$ without a calculator. Split 43 into a round number and a small one, then take $a = 40$ and $b = 3$:

$43^2 = (40 + 3)^2 = 40^2 + 2 \times 40 \times 3 + 3^2$

$= 1600 + 240 + 9 = 1849$
