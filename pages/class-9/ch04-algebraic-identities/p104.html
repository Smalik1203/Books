Solution 1

There are two conditions and three unknowns. So $x$, $y$ and $z$ cannot be found. That is why option (d) is there. But both conditions are sides of identities. From $(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2(xy + yz + zx)$,

$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 81 - 52 = 29$

The identity for $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$ has two factors. They are $x + y + z$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - (xy + yz + zx)$. We now have both of these:

$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz = 9 \times (29 - 26) = 27$

The answer is (b). As a check, $2, 3, 4$ add to $9$, and $6 + 12 + 8 = 26$. Their cubes add to $99$, and $99 - 72 = 27$.

Problem 2 · Try it first

If $a + b = 7$ and $a^3 + b^3 = 133$, the number of real pairs $(a, b)$ is

  1. $0$
  2. $1$
  3. $2$
  4. $4$
Solution 2

We are given a sum and a sum of cubes. The identity $a^3 + b^3 = (a + b)^3 - 3ab(a + b)$ connects them through the product:

$133 = 343 - 21ab$,  so  $ab = 10$

Now the sum and the product are both known. The difference follows from $(a - b)^2 = (a + b)^2 - 4ab = 49 - 40 = 9$. So $a - b$ is $3$ or $-3$. The pairs are $(5, 2)$ and $(2, 5)$. There are two of them, option (c). Check: $125 + 8 = 133$.

Option (b) comes from writing $a - b = 3$ and stopping there. A square root has two signs. Option (a) would be right only if $(a - b)^2$ had come out negative. It is worth noticing that it could have.
