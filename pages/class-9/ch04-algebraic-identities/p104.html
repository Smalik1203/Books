Behind Each Answer
Five questions, with the reasoning and the working. Try each one before you read its solution.
Problem 1

If $x + y + z = 9$ and $xy + yz + zx = 26$, then $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$ equals

  1. $9$
  2. $27$
  3. $81$
  4. cannot be found
Solution

Two conditions and three unknowns, so $x$, $y$ and $z$ cannot be found — which is why option (d) is there. But both conditions are sides of identities.

$(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2(xy + yz + zx)$

$81 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 52$

$x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 29$

The identity for $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$ has the two factors $x + y + z$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 - (xy + yz + zx)$, and both are now known.

$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz = 9(29 - 26) = 27$

Option (b). Check with $2, 3, 4$: they add to $9$, their products to $26$, their cubes to $99$, and $99 - 72 = 27$.

Option (d) is not a careless answer, and it is worth seeing exactly where it goes wrong. Half of it is true: two conditions cannot pin down three letters, and there are infinitely many triples fitting both, of which $2, 3, 4$ is only the tidiest. What (d) assumes is that a quantity built out of $x$, $y$ and $z$ must therefore be beyond reach as well — and that does not follow.

The reason it does not follow is worth a name. Swap any two of the three letters in $x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3xyz$ and the expression is unchanged; the same is true of $x + y + z$ and of $xy + yz + zx$. An expression that survives every such swap cannot tell the letters apart, so it cannot depend on which of them is which — only on what they come to together. That is why the two sums given were enough, and why no amount of extra information about the individual values would have been of any use.
