Example 15

Three numbers add to 10, multiply to 25, and their squares add to 38. What do their cubes add to?

Call them $x$, $y$ and $z$, so $x + y + z = 10$, $xyz = 25$ and $x^2 + y^2 + z^2 = 38$. Substituting into the identity:

$10\left(38 - (xy + xz + yz)\right) = x^3 + y^3 + z^3 - 3(25)$

$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 455 - 10(xy + xz + yz)$

One quantity is still missing: $xy + xz + yz$. Nothing in the question gives it directly — but the squared identity from Section 4.4 does, because it is the only place that quantity ever appears:

$(x + y + z)^2 = x^2 + y^2 + z^2 + 2(xy + xz + yz)$

$100 = 38 + 2(xy + xz + yz), \quad\text{so}\quad xy + xz + yz = 31$

Putting that back:

$x^3 + y^3 + z^3 = 455 - 10(31) = 455 - 310 = 145$

Notice that the three numbers were never found. They did not need to be: the identities work with the sums and products directly.

4.8Simplifying Rational Expressions

Everything so far has been about turning expressions into products. Here is where that pays off. A rational expression is one algebraic expression divided by another, and simplifying it means the same thing as simplifying a fraction of numbers: cancel whatever the top and the bottom have in common.

You cannot cancel a term, only a factor. So before anything can be cancelled, both the numerator and the denominator have to be factorised — which is exactly what this chapter has been teaching you to do.

So simplifying is a three-step routine: factorise the top, factorise the bottom, then strike out the factors they share. Almost all of the work is in the first two steps. The cancelling itself is the easy part, and it is also where the temptation to cheat arrives.

$\frac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}$ simplifies to $x + 3$, because the top is the product $(x - 3)(x + 3)$ and $x - 3$ is one of its factors. But $\frac{x^2 + 9}{x + 3}$ simplifies to nothing at all: $x^2 + 9$ is a sum, and $x + 3$ is not a factor of it. Striking out matching symbols is not cancelling.
