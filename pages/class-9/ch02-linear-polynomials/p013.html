2.4Linear Growth and Linear Decay

A quantity that changes by the same amount over every equal interval is described by a linear expression — and there are only two ways it can go. It can climb, or it can fall. Both are worth a name.

The phrase over equal intervals is carrying weight, so it is worth pausing on. It is not enough that a quantity keeps increasing; the increases have to come in equal steps, and the steps have to be counted against something that itself advances evenly — a month, a kilometre, a stage of the pattern. A tree that grows faster in some years than in others is still growing, but not linearly, and no expression of the form $ax + b$ will describe it.

That is a real restriction, and it is why the examples in this section look alike: a fixed charge and a fixed rate, a starting amount and a fixed loss. Whenever you meet a situation described by the words per or each — so much per kilometre, so much each month — a linear expression is almost certainly waiting behind it.

Example 9
