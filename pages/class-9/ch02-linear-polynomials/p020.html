How do the graphs of $y = 3x + 1$ and $y = -3x + 1$ differ? Say it in terms of slope before you draw either.

There is a case the two figures between them leave out, and it is worth naming. If $a = 0$ the expression is not $ax$ at all but the constant $0$, and the graph is the $x$-axis itself — flat, going neither up nor down. A slope of zero is a pattern that never changes, which is why a constant polynomial sits at degree $0$ rather than degree $1$.

So much for $a$. Every line so far has passed through the origin, because every equation so far has been $y = ax$ with nothing added. That was not an accident of the examples chosen: $x = 0$ forces $y = 0$ whenever there is nothing on the end to stop it. It is time to put the $b$ back.
