Five questions, with the reasoning as well as the working. Try each one before reading its solution.
Problem 1

A linear polynomial has $p(2) = 7$ and $p(5) = 16$. Then $p(0)$ is

  1. $1$
  2. $3$
  3. $4$
  4. $10$
Solution

Write it as $p(x) = ax + b$. Three steps in $x$ carry $p$ from $7$ to $16$.

$3a = 16 - 7 = 9$

$a = 3$

From $x = 2$ back to $x = 0$ is two steps, so $p$ falls by $2a$.

$p(0) = 7 - 2(3) = 1$

Option (a). Check: $p(x) = 3x + 1$ gives $p(5) = 16$. Option (b) is the slope, not the value at $0$. Option (c) takes one step back instead of two. Option (d) goes forward three steps instead of back two.

There is a second road to the same place, and it is worth seeing because it does not depend on the steps being whole ones. Write down what the two given values say, exactly as they stand.

$2a + b = 7$

$5a + b = 16$

Subtract the first from the second. The $b$ disappears — it is the same $b$ in both lines, and subtracting removes it without any arrangement on our part — leaving $3a = 9$, so $a = 3$; and then either line gives $b = 1$.

That is the step-counting written out formally, and it is the reason the step-counting worked. Two conditions, two unknowns, and one subtraction that clears the constant away.
