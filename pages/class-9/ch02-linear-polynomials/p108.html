B8 · The Trap Room
  1. "$3x^2 + 2x$ is a linear polynomial — it has an $x$ in it, and the coefficients are numbers." Say what the definition actually asks for, and give the degree.
  2. "$1, 2, 4, 8, 16$ is a linear pattern: the same thing is done at every step." Something is the same at every step here. Say what, say why it is not what the definition wants, and write the differences out to prove it.
  3. "$y = 2x + 5$ and $y = 5x + 2$ are the same line, since they are built from the same two numbers." Refute it with one value of $x$ — then find the one value at which the two do agree, and say what that point is.
  4. Asked for the y-intercept of $y = 3x - 6$, a student answers $2$, "because that is where the line crosses". Which crossing has been found, and what is the y-intercept?
  5. To find $a$ and $b$ in $y = ax + b$, a student uses two readings taken at $x = 4$: one giving $y = 9$ and the other, after a repeat measurement, $y = 9$ again. Show that the two equations cannot be solved, and say what the second reading contributed.
  6. "The fare formula is $15n - 5$, so a $1$ km ride costs ₹$10$." Compare that with the fare table, and state the condition under which the formula is entitled to be used.
  7. "A linear decay has a negative slope, so its graph lies below the x-axis." Draw $y = 100 - 5x$ in your head for $x = 0$ to $10$ and decide. What has been confused with what?
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Matching a word to a feature instead of a definition (traps 1, 2). Linear is about the highest power and about constant differences; that an $x$ appears, or that a rule is repeated, is not the test.
  2. Losing track of which number does which job (traps 3, 4, 7). $a$ tilts and $b$ slides. Swapping them, or reading $b$ off the wrong axis, produces answers that look plausible because both numbers are in the equation somewhere.
  3. Using a rule outside the range it was built for (traps 5, 6). Two readings determine a line only if they differ in $x$; a formula fitted from the second kilometre onward says nothing about the first.
