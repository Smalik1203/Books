The Trap Room
Two students, one question, and a mistake.

Answer (a) A only, (b) B only, (c) both, (d) neither — then say in one line what went wrong.

  1. A. $3x^2 + 2x$ is linear — it has an $x$ in it and its coefficients are numbers.  B. It is not: the definition asks about the highest power, which here is $2$.
  2. A. $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$ is not a linear pattern; its differences are $1, 2, 4, 8$.  B. It is linear, since the same thing is done at every step.
  3. A. $y = 2x + 5$ and $y = 5x + 2$ meet at exactly one point.  B. They are the same line — the same two numbers built them.
  4. The $y$-intercept of $y = 3x - 6$ is A. $2$, where the line crosses.  B. $-6$, the value at $x = 0$.
  5. A. $y = 100 - 5x$ is positive for every $x$ below $20$.  B. A linear decay has a negative slope, so its graph lies below the $x$-axis.
  6. The fare rule is $15n - 5$, valid from the second kilometre on. What does a $1$ km ride cost? A. ₹$10$.  B. ₹$15$.
  7. Differences of a measured quantity come out $7, 7, 7, 7$. A. The rule is $7t + b$, and $b$ is not yet known.  B. The rule must be $7t$.
  8. A. $y = ax$ passes through the origin for every $a$.  B. So does $y = ax + b$ for every $b$.
  9. A. $y = 4x - 3$ and $y = -4x - 3$ share exactly one point.  B. That point is $(0, -3)$.
  10. A tree grows every year, by more in some years than others. A. Its height is linear, since it always rises.  B. It is not linear, because the yearly rise is not constant.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Matching a word to a feature, not a definition (1, 2, 10). Linear is about the highest power and about constant differences. That an $x$ appears, or that something rises, is not the test.
  2. Losing track of which number does which job (3, 4, 5, 8). $a$ tilts and $b$ slides. Swap them and the answer still looks plausible, because both numbers are in the equation somewhere.
  3. Using a rule outside what fixed it (6, 7). A formula fitted from the second kilometre on says nothing about the first, and constant differences fix $a$ but never $b$.
