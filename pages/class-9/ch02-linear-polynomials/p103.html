A line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0, 6)$ and the $x$-axis at $(3, 0)$. Find its equation. Then find where the line $y = 4x - 10$ crosses each axis.

The two crossing points are two points on the line, and two points are all we need. The crossing on the $y$-axis gives $b$ at once: $b = 6$. From $x = 0$ to $x = 3$, $y$ falls from $6$ to $0$, so $a = -2$. The line is $y = -2x + 6$.

For the second line, a point on the $y$-axis has $x = 0$. Put $x = 0$: $y = -10$, and the crossing is $(0, -10)$. A point on the $x$-axis has $y = 0$. Put $y = 0$:

$$4x - 10 = 0, \quad x = 2.5$$

and the crossing is $(2.5, 0)$. Notice that the two crossings come from the same idea, one coordinate is zero, but they are not found the same way. The $y$-crossing is read off the equation. The $x$-crossing needs a linear equation to be solved, just as the tank of Example 10 needed one to say when it was empty.

None of these questions used anything outside the chapter. What each needed was the question: what can I use from what I already know? The examples that follow ask it again.

Type 1 · Writing an expression and naming its parts

Example 1

A hall has $r$ rows of $25$ chairs, and $8$ more chairs stand along one wall. Write the number of chairs as an expression in $r$. Name its terms, the coefficient of $r$ and the constant term, and say what kind of polynomial it is.

Solution.

Step 1 the $r$ rows hold $25r$ chairs $25$ chairs a row
Step 2 the whole count is $25r + 8$ add the $8$ by the wall
Step 3 its terms are $25r$ and $8$; the coefficient of $r$ is $25$, and the constant term is $8$
Step 4 the highest power of $r$ in it is $1$ this is its degree
Answer $25r + 8$, a linear polynomial in $r$
Check $4$ rows give $25 \times 4 + 8 = 108$ chairs
