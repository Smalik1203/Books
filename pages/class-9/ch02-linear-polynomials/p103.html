A linear relationship is the commonest shape in the whole of school science, so a question about $ax + b$ is rarely only about algebra. Each of these reaches into a chapter you have met, or out of the book altogether.

B3 · Connect the Concepts
  1. Chapter 1 put points on axes; this chapter put lines through them. The line $y = 2x - 1$ passes through $A(1, 1)$ and $B(4, 7)$.
    1. Find $AB$ with the distance formula.
    2. The slope is $2$ and the distance is not. Say what each of the two numbers measures, and why one changes if the line is drawn on a different scale while the other does not.
  2. Chapter 4 expanded $(n+1)^2$. Use that identity to show that the first differences of the sequence $n^2$ are $2n + 1$ — which is itself linear — and hence that the second differences are all $2$.
  3. In science, a body moving with constant acceleration obeys $v = u + at$.
    1. Match this against $y = ax + b$: what plays the part of the slope, and what of the intercept?
    2. A body starts at 4 m/s and reaches 19 m/s after 5 s. Find its acceleration, and its speed after 12 s.
  4. Celsius and Fahrenheit are related linearly: $0^\circ$C is $32^\circ$F, and $100^\circ$C is $212^\circ$F. Find the rule, then find the one temperature at which the two scales read the very same number.
  5. A workshop's costs are ₹$4000$ a month plus ₹$120$ for each item made, and each item sells for ₹$200$.
    1. Write linear expressions for the total cost and the total income after $n$ items.
    2. How many items must be sold before the income overtakes the cost? Explain why the answer depends on the difference of the two slopes and not on either alone.
  6. Chapter 3 sorted the numbers into ratios and non-ratios. A line has slope $\sqrt{2}$ and passes through the origin. Does it pass through any point whose coordinates are both non-zero whole numbers? Give your reasoning.
