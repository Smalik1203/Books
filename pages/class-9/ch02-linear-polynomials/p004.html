1. $5y^{3} + y^{2} + 2y - 1$ has degree $3$ — a cubic polynomial.
  2. $x^{2} + 5x + 1$ has degree $2$ — a quadratic polynomial.
  3. $3z + 7$ has degree $1$ — a linear polynomial.
  4. The constant $8$ has degree $0$, since it can be written $8x^{0}$ — a constant polynomial.

The whole of the rest of this chapter lives on the third line.

2.2Linear Polynomials

A polynomial of degree $1$ is a linear polynomial: the variable appears, but no power of it does. That is the whole of the restriction, and it is worth seeing what such a plain object is good for before anything is proved about it.

Example 4

A square has side $x$. Its perimeter is $4x$, a linear polynomial in $x$.
