A chess club charges a joining fee of ₹$200$, and ₹$50$ for every match a member plays. What does a member pay?

So a member who has played $m$ matches has paid ₹$(200 + 50m)$, and $200 + 50m$ is a linear polynomial in $m$. Each new match adds ₹$50$ — the same ₹$50$, whether it is her second match or her fortieth.

Examples 4 and 5 have the same shape underneath. In the first, every extra half-centimetre of side added $2$ cm to the perimeter. In the second, every extra match added ₹$50$. Neither increase grows or fades: it is the same step each time, and that constant step is what a linear polynomial is for.

Linear pattern

Take a linear polynomial and evaluate it at $1$, $2$, $3$, and so on. The difference between one value and the next is the same throughout. A sequence of numbers built this way — with a constant difference between consecutive terms — is a linear pattern.
