Three things sat under all three sets, and they are easier to see now that the answers are beside you.

The first is that a linear polynomial has exactly one zero — never none, and never two. Setting $ax + b = 0$ gives $x = -b/a$, and that division is always allowed, because $a \ne 0$ is part of what being linear means. A question that seems to offer two answers, or none, has been misread somewhere: the polynomial in it was not linear, or the condition was not what it appeared to be.

The second is that two facts settle a linear polynomial completely, and a third is a check rather than more information. Two points, or a point and a step, or a zero and one value — any two of these pin down both $a$ and $b$, and anything further given must agree with what they have already fixed.

The third is that one object here wears three costumes. A rule such as $3x - 5$, a table whose entries go up by $3$ at a time, and a straight line climbing three units for each one across are the same thing described in three languages. Most of these questions arrive in one costume and are easiest in another, and changing costume costs nothing.
