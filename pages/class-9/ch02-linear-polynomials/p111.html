Type 9 · Proofs about linear polynomials and their lines

Example 16

Prove that for any linear polynomial $p(x) = ax + b$, the values $p(1), p(2), p(3), \ldots$ go up by the same amount each time, and that the amount is $a$.

Solution.

Step 1 take any whole number $n$
Step 2 $p(n) = an + b$ and $p(n + 1) = a(n + 1) + b$ the rule at $n$ and at $n + 1$
Step 3 $p(n + 1) - p(n) = an + a + b - an - b$ opening the bracket
Step 4 $p(n + 1) - p(n) = a$
Answer whatever $n$ is, each value is $a$ more than the one before, so the values make a linear pattern with difference $a$
Example 17

Prove that the lines $y = ax + b$ and $y = ax + c$, where $b \ne c$, never meet.

Solution.

Step 1 suppose a point $(x, y)$ lies on both lines assume they meet
Step 2 then $y = ax + b$ and $y = ax + c$ it satisfies both
Step 3 $ax + b = ax + c$ both equal $y$
Step 4 $b = c$ take $ax$ from both sides
Step 5 this contradicts $b \ne c$
Answer no point lies on both lines, so they never meet: they are parallel
