A pattern need not grow. The next one shrinks, and the arithmetic is identical apart from a sign.

Example 7

Bela starts the month with ₹$100$ of pocket money and spends ₹$5$ a day. After how many days is she left with ₹$40$?

3$100 - 3 \times 5 = 85$
4$100 - 4 \times 5 = 80$

On day $n$ she has ₹$(100 - 5n)$ left. Setting that equal to $40$ gives $5n = 60$, so $n = 12$: on the twelfth day she is down to ₹$40$.

How much has Bela left on day $15$? On which day does the money run out altogether?

Compare that with the chess club of Example 5. There the total was $200 + 50m$ and every match added ₹$50$; here it is $100 - 5n$ and every day takes away ₹$5$. The two expressions are the same shape, and the only thing distinguishing a total that climbs from one that falls is the sign in front of the coefficient. Nothing else in the algebra changes at all.

The constant term differs in what it means, though, and it is worth being clear about. In $200 + 50m$ the $200$ is the joining fee — what is owed before any chess is played. In $100 - 5n$ the $100$ is what Bela starts with — what she has before any spending. Both are the value of the expression when the variable is $0$, which is the one interpretation that covers every case.

One more, because it has a wrinkle the others do not: the pattern does not start at the beginning.
