  1. A learning platform charges a fixed monthly fee plus a cost for every module accessed. Ten modules came to ₹$400$; fourteen came to ₹$500$. Taking the bill to be $y = ax + b$ for $x$ modules, find $a$ and $b$.
  1. A gym charges a fixed monthly fee plus an hourly rate for the badminton court. Ten hours came to ₹$800$; fifteen hours came to ₹$1100$. Taking the bill to be $y = ax + b$ for $x$ hours, find $a$ and $b$.
  1. Temperature in degrees Celsius and in degrees Fahrenheit are related by $C = aF + b$. Ice melts at $0$ °C and $32$ °F; water boils at $100$ °C and $212$ °F. Find $a$ and $b$.

2.6Visualising Linear Relationships

Everything so far has been done with tables and algebra. There is a third way of holding a linear relationship in mind, and for most purposes it is the best: draw it. A relation $y = ax + b$ drawn on the coordinate plane is always a straight line, and — this is what makes it worth doing — the two numbers $a$ and $b$ turn out to be things you can see.

A straight line is settled by two of its points, so drawing $y = ax + b$ needs only two — pick any two values of $x$, work out the matching $y$, plot them, and lay a ruler along them.

Take $y = 2x + 1$. Putting $x = 0$ gives $y = 1$, so $A\,(0, 1)$ is on the line; putting $x = 3$ gives $y = 7$, so $B\,(3, 7)$ is too. In the pair $(0, 1)$, the first number is the x-coordinate and the second the y-coordinate. Plot $A$ and $B$, join them, and continue the line past both ends.
