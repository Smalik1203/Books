That last sentence is the point of the whole subject. The expression $4x + 5y + 3$ is not an answer waiting to be worked out; it is a rule that holds for all the purchases at once, and a particular purchase is got by putting numbers in.

Its pieces have names. The three parts added together — $4x$, $5y$ and $3$ — are the terms of the expression. The letters $x$ and $y$ stand for numbers that may vary, so they are called variables. The numbers $4$ and $5$ multiplying them are the coefficients of $x$ and of $y$, and the lone $3$, which multiplies no variable at all, is a constant.

Those four words will be used on every page of this chapter, so it is worth testing them once. In $4x + 5y + 3$ the term $4x$ has coefficient $4$; the term $3$ has no variable and so is the constant. Whether $3$ counts as a term is settled the same way — it is one of the parts being added, so it is.

Expressions of this kind are worth building for yourself before they are studied in the abstract. Here is one where three separate costs have to be collected into a single statement.

Example 2

A rectangular garden is $l$ metres long and $w$ metres wide. A wire fence is to run along its length at ₹$100$ a metre, a wooden fence along its width at ₹$80$ a metre, and the whole garden is to be sown with seed costing ₹$50$ a square metre. What is the total cost?

Take the three costs one at a time. There are two lengths to fence and two widths, and the seed covers the area.

wire fencing = ₹$(2l \times 100) =$ ₹$200l$

wooden fencing = ₹$(2w \times 80) =$ ₹$160w$

seed = ₹$(50 \times l \times w) =$ ₹$50lw$

So the whole job costs ₹$(200l + 160w + 50lw)$, and

$$200l + 160w + 50lw$$

is the algebraic expression for the total cost.
