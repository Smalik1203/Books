Example 8

An auto-rickshaw charges ₹$25$ for the first $2$ km and ₹$15$ for every kilometre after that. What is the fare for $10$ km?

Distance (km)Fare (₹)
125
225
3$25 + 1 \times 15 = 40$
4$25 + 2 \times 15 = 55$
5$25 + 3 \times 15 = 70$
6$25 + 4 \times 15 = 85$

The first two kilometres are already paid for, so a $10$ km ride is charged for $8$ of them: ₹$25 + 15 \times 8 =$ ₹$145$.

For any $n \geq 2$ the fare is

$$25 + 15(n - 2) = 15n - 5$$

a linear polynomial again — but only from the second kilometre on. The first two rows of the table do not obey it, and no amount of algebra will make them.
