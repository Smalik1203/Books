Find $y$ for each of $x = 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 12, 20$. Two are done for you: $x = 1$ gives $y = 3$, and $x = 7$ gives $y = 15$.

The point $(7, 15)$ is far off the edge of the figure. Check it lies on the line without drawing anything.

That was a line drawn from its equation. The next two examples run the other way: points first, and the equation read off the picture they make.

Example 12

Plot $(-1, -3)$, $(0, 0)$, $(1, 3)$, $(3, 9)$ and $(4, 12)$ on graph paper. Join $(-1, -3)$ to $(4, 12)$ with a ruler. What do you notice, and what is the equation of the line?

All five points lie on the line — the ruler passes through the three that were not used to draw it, which is a fact about the numbers and not about the drawing. Look down the pairs and the rule is plain: each $y$ is three times its $x$. So the line is $y = 3x$.
