y-intercept

The line $y = ax + b$ meets the $y$-axis at $(0, b)$. The number $b$ is the y-intercept of the line: its distance from the origin along the $y$-axis, positive upwards and negative downwards.

So $y = x + 3$ has $y$-intercept $3$, three units above the origin, and $y = 3x - 2$ has $y$-intercept $-2$, two units below it. Both numbers can be read straight off the equation without drawing anything — and, going the other way, read off the drawing without solving anything.

Putting the last several figures together, the two numbers in $y = ax + b$ have separate jobs and neither interferes with the other.

What the graphs showed
  1. $a$ is the slope of the line and $b$ is its $y$-intercept.
  2. Change $a$ and keep $b$ fixed: the line tilts, but goes on cutting the $y$-axis at the same point.
  3. Change $b$ and keep $a$ fixed: the line slides without tilting, so lines with equal slopes and different intercepts are parallel.
