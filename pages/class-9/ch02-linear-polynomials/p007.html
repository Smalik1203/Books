Example 6

Two numbers add to $64$, and one of them is $10$ more than the other. Find them.

Call the smaller one $x$. Then the larger is $x + 10$, and their sum is $64$.

$x + (x + 10) = 64$

$2x + 10 = 64$

$2x = 54$

$x = 27$

So the numbers are $27$ and $37$. Notice what was equated to what: $2x + 10$ is a linear polynomial, and setting it equal to $64$ turned it into a linear equation with a single answer.

There is another way to look at a polynomial, and for the rest of this chapter it is the more useful one. Rather than an expression to be simplified, treat it as a machine: feed it a number, and it hands one back.

Take $2x + 3$. Put in $x = 4$ and it returns $2 \times 4 + 3 = 11$. Put in $x = -6$ and it returns $2 \times (-6) + 3 = -9$. Every input has exactly one output, and a rule of that kind is called a function of $x$.
