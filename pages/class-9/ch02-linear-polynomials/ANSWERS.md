### Think and Reflect

### Exercise Set 2.2

1. - (i) $5 \times 0 - 3 = -3$
   - (ii) $5 \times (-1) - 3 = -8$
   - (iii) $5 \times 2 - 3 = 7$

2. - (i) $7 \times 0 - 4 \times 0 + 6 = 6$
   - (ii) $7 \times 9 + 12 + 6 = 81$
   - (iii) $7 \times 16 - 16 + 6 = 102$

3. Let Salil be $x$; his mother is $3x$. Then $(x + 5) + (3x + 5) = 70$,
   so $4x = 60$ and $x = 15$. Salil is $15$ and his mother is $45$.

4. Let the numbers be $2k$ and $5k$. Then $5k - 2k = 63$, so $k = 21$. The
   numbers are $42$ and $105$.

5. Let there be $x$ five-rupee coins and $3x$ two-rupee coins. Then
   $5x + 2 \times 3x = 88$, so $11x = 88$ and $x = 8$: $8$ five-rupee coins
   and $24$ two-rupee coins.

6. Let the shorter piece be $x$ feet. Then $x + 4x = 300$, so $x = 60$: the
   pieces are $60$ feet and $240$ feet.

7. Let the width be $w$ cm; the length is $2w + 3$. Then
   $2(w + 2w + 3) = 24$, so $6w + 6 = 24$ and $w = 3$: the width is $3$ cm
   and the length $9$ cm.

8. Let the smaller be $x$. Then $x + (x + 7) = 41$, so $2x + 7 = 41$ and
   $x = 17$: the numbers are $17$ and $24$. The linear polynomial
   $2x + 7$ was set equal to the number $41$.

9. The cost is $35n + 15$ rupees. Then $35n + 15 = 330$, so $35n = 315$ and
   $n = 9$: Ravi bought $9$ notebooks.

## 2.3 Exploring Linear Patterns

### Think and Reflect

1. (After Fig. 2.4.) Stages 5, 6 and 7 have $9$, $11$ and $13$ tiles. The
   counts run $1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13$.
2. (After $2n - 1$.) Stage 15 has $2 \times 15 - 1 = 29$ tiles and Stage 26
   has $2 \times 26 - 1 = 51$. $2n - 1 = 21$ gives $n = 11$, and
   $2n - 1 = 47$ gives $n = 24$. **No stage has $50$**: $2n - 1 = 50$ gives

## 2.4 Linear Growth and Linear Decay

### Think and Reflect

1. (After Example 9.) $C(15) = 100 + 60 \times 15 = 1000$ rupees.
   $100 + 60d = 700$ gives $d = 10$: $10$ km.
2. (After Example 10.) $h(5) = 3 - 0.5 \times 5 = 0.5$ m. $3 - 0.5t = 0$
   gives $t = 6$: the tank is empty after month $6$, and from then on the
   formula gives a negative depth ($h(7) = -0.5$), which is no longer about
   water.

### Exercise Set 2.4

   $a = 25$ and $b = 400 - 250 = 150$.

## 2.6 Visualising Linear Relationships

### Think and Reflect

1. (After Fig. 2.5.) At $x = 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 12, 20$ the line gives
   $y = 3, 5, 11, 15, 19, 25, 41$. And $2 \times 7 + 1 = 15$, so $(7, 15)$
   is on the line.
2. (After Fig. 2.8.) $y = 3x$ is steeper than $y = 2x$, between it and the
   $y$-axis; $y = \tfrac{1}{3}x$ is shallower than $y = \tfrac{1}{2}x$,
   between it and the $x$-axis. As $a$ grows without limit the line comes
   closer and closer to the $y$-axis; as $a$ shrinks towards $0$ it comes
   closer to the $x$-axis.

   slope $3$ and climbs to the right; $y = -3x + 1$ has slope $-3$ and
   falls, just as steeply. Each is the other reflected in the $y$-axis.
4. (After Fig. 2.10.) With $a$ fixed, changing $b$ slides the line up or
   down without tilting it: the lines are parallel, and they cut the
   $y$-axis at $(0, -1)$, $(0, 1)$ and $(0, 5)$.

What each drawing must show:

## End-of-Chapter Exercises

1. Answers will vary. For example, $x^{3} - 7x^{2} + 2x + 1$: any
   polynomial with a non-zero $x^{3}$ term, no higher power, and the term
   $-7x^{2}$.

2. - (i) $5 - 3 + 7 = 9$
   - (ii) $4a^{3} - a^{2} + 6$

3. $\tfrac{5}{2}x + \tfrac{2}{3} = -\tfrac{7}{12}$, so
   $\tfrac{5}{2}x = -\tfrac{7}{12} - \tfrac{8}{12} = -\tfrac{5}{4}$ and
   $x = -\tfrac{1}{2}$.

4. Let the numbers be $x$ and $5x$. Then $5x + 21 = 2(x + 21)$, so
   $3x = 21$ and $x = 7$: the numbers are $7$ and $35$. Check: $56 = 2 \times 28$.

5. - (i) $800 + 250 \times 6 = 2300$ rupees
   - (ii) $800 + 250 \times 24 = 6800$ rupees

   After $n$ months the amount is $800 + 250n$ rupees.

6. Let the digits be $p$ and $q$. The number and its reverse add to
   $11(p + q) = 143$, so $p + q = 13$; with $p - q = 3$, $p = 8$ and $q = 5$.
   The numbers are $85$ and $58$.

7. - (i) slope $-3$, $y$-intercept $4$, cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, 4)$
   - (ii) $y = 2x + 3.5$: slope $2$, $y$-intercept $3.5$, at $(0, 3.5)$
   - (iii) $y = 1.2x - 2$: slope $1.2$, $y$-intercept $-2$, at $(0, -2)$
   - (iv) $y = 2x - \tfrac{11}{3}$: slope $2$, $y$-intercept
     $-\tfrac{11}{3}$, at $(0, -\tfrac{11}{3})$

   **Yes**: (ii) and (iv) have the same slope and different intercepts, so
   they are parallel.

8. - (i) $y = \tfrac{9}{5} \times 40 + 32 = 104$ °F
   - (ii) $\tfrac{9}{5}(x - 273) = 158 - 32 = 126$, so $x - 273 = 70$ and
     $x = 343$ K

9. $w = Fd$, and with $F = 3$ the equation is $w = 3d$, a straight line
   through the origin with slope $3$. At $d = 2$, $w = 6$ units, and the
   point $(2, 6)$ is on the graph.

10. - (i) From $(1, 5)$ to $(3, 11)$, $x$ goes up $2$ and $y$ goes up $6$,
      so $a = 3$; then $5 = 3 + b$ gives $b = 2$. So $p(x) = 3x + 2$.
    - (ii) It cuts the $y$-axis at $(0, 2)$; $3x + 2 = 0$ gives
      $x = -\tfrac{2}{3}$, so it cuts the $x$-axis at $(-\tfrac{2}{3}, 0)$.
    - (iii) A line through $(1, 5)$ and $(3, 11)$ that passes through both
      crossing points.

11. - $p(0) = 5$, so $b = 5$.
    - $p(x) + q(x) = (a + c)x + (5 + d)$, and this is $6x + 4$ for every
      $x$, so $a + c = 6$ and $d = -1$.
    - $p(x) - q(x) = (a - c)x + 6$ is zero at $x = 3$, so $3(a - c) + 6 = 0$
      and $a - c = -2$.
    - So $a = 2$ and $c = 4$: $p(x) = 2x + 5$ and $q(x) = 4x - 1$.

    Check: $p(x) - q(x) = -2x + 6$, which is zero at $x = 3$.

12. - (i) Stages 4 and 5 need $21$ and $26$ matchsticks.
    - (ii) Stages $1$ to $5$: $6$, $11$, $16$, $21$, $26$; stage $n$:
      $5n + 1$.
    - (iii) $5n + 1$: the first hexagon takes $6$, and each new one takes
      $5$, since it shares a side.
    - (iv) $5 \times 15 + 1 = 76$.
    - (v) **No.** $5n + 1 = 200$ gives $5n = 199$, and $199$ is not a
      multiple of $5$.

13. $p$ has slope $(11 - 3) \div (6 - 2) = 2$, and $3 = 2 \times 2 + b$
    gives $b = -1$: $p(x) = 2x - 1$. Parallel means the same slope, so
    $q(x) = 2x + d$ with $-1 = 2 \times 4 + d$, $d = -9$: $q(x) = 2x - 9$.
    $2x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = \tfrac{1}{2}$, and $2x - 9 = 0$ gives
    $x = \tfrac{9}{2}$: $p$ meets the $x$-axis at $(\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)$ and
    $q$ at $(\tfrac{9}{2}, 0)$.

14. $f(x) = ax + a = a(x + 1)$, so $f(-1) = 0$ for every $a$: **all their
    graphs pass through $(-1, 0)$**. They all climb to the right (slope
    $a > 0$), and each cuts the $y$-axis at a height equal to its slope.
