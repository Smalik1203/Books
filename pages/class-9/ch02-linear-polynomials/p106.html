In an assertion–reason question, answer from this list every time: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

B6 · Competitive-Style Thinking
  1. Assertion. Evaluating a linear polynomial at $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ always gives a linear pattern.  Reason. $a(n+1) + b - (an + b) = a$, the same number for every $n$.
  2. Assertion. A sequence whose first differences are all equal comes from a polynomial of degree at most $1$.  Reason. A polynomial of degree $2$ has constant second differences.
  3. For the line $y = -3x + 4$, which of the following hold?
    1. Its slope is $-3$.
    2. It meets the y-axis at $(0, 4)$.
    3. It passes through $(2, 2)$.
    Choose: (a) (i) only   (b) (i) and (ii)   (c) (ii) and (iii)   (d) all three.
  4. The graph of $p(x) = ax + b$ passes through $(1, 5)$ and $(3, 11)$. Find $p(x)$, the point where it cuts each axis, and $p(10)$.
  5. A shop's bill is meant to follow one linear rule. One row of the table breaks it. Find the row, say what the bill there should be, and give the rule.
