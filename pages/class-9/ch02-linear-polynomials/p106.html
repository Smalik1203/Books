Competitive-Style Thinking
Four moves worth more than another formula.
Eliminate A rising rule has a positive $a$, a falling one a negative $a$. The sign alone usually kills two options.
Check sufficiency Two readings fix a line only if they differ in $x$. The same $x$ measured twice fixes nothing at all.
Find the constraint $a$ is the step per unit of $x$; $b$ is the value at $x = 0$. Any two points hand you both.
Estimate first Reading between your measurements is safe. Reading beyond them is a bet on the pattern continuing.

Name the move, then use it.

  1. Eliminate. $p(x) = ax + b$ passes through $(1,5)$ and $(3,11)$. Then $a$ is
    1. $2$
    2. $3$
    3. $6$
    4. $8$
  2. Eliminate. $p(0) = 7$ and $p(4) = -5$. Without solving, the sign of $a$ kills two options. Then $a$ is
    1. $-3$
    2. $-2$
    3. $3$
    4. $12$
  3. Sufficiency. To find $a$ and $b$, a student takes two readings both at $x = 4$, getting $y = 9$ each time. Show that the equations cannot be solved, and say what the second reading contributed.
  4. Sufficiency. Given the slope of a line, which one further fact fixes it completely?
    1. That it is steep
    2. One point that lies on it
    3. The slope of a parallel line
    4. The direction it rises in
