A shop's bill should follow one linear rule, but one row of the table was copied wrongly. Which row, and what should it say?

Units$0$$1$$2$$3$$4$
Bill (₹)$150$$170$$190$$215$$230$

The differences are $20$, $20$, $25$, $15$. They are not all equal, so as it stands the table is not linear. But look at where the differences go wrong. The first two are $20$. The last two are $25$ and $15$, and those two are the differences on either side of the bill for $3$ units. That is the only row that touches both. So the bill for $3$ units is the wrong one. The rule from the other rows is $150 + 20u$, and at $u = 3$ it gives $210$, not $215$. Check the last row: $150 + 80 = 230$. Notice that the differences told us more than yes or no. When one row is wrong, two differences are wrong, and they sit either side of it. Notice too that $25 + 15 = 40$, which is two steps of $20$. The wrong row pushed one difference up by $5$ and the next one down by $5$.

Do the points $(2, 1)$, $(4, 7)$ and $(7, 19)$ lie on one straight line?

Two points fix a line, so use the first two. From $x = 2$ to $x = 4$ is two steps, and $y$ rises by $6$, so $a = 3$. Then $1 = 6 + b$ gives $b = -5$, and the line through the first two points is $y = 3x - 5$. At $x = 7$ this line gives $16$, not $19$. So the third point is not on it, and no straight line passes through all three (Fig. 2.13). There is another way to see it. From $(4, 7)$ to $(7, 19)$ is three steps and a rise of $12$, which is $4$ a step. The first pair gave $3$ a step. A straight line has one slope, so the three points cannot share one.

Notice that nothing was drawn. A drawing shows that a point is far from a line. When it is only a little off, the arithmetic is what settles it.
