The difference is $7$, so the $n$th term is $7n + b$. The first term is $4$, so $7 + b = 4$ and $b = -3$. The $n$th term is $7n - 3$. For $179$, set $7n - 3 = 179$. Then $7n = 182$ and $n = 26$. So $179$ is the $26$th term. For $150$ the same step gives $7n = 153$. But $153$ is not a multiple of $7$, so $n$ is not a whole number, and $150$ is not a term. Notice that a term of the pattern is a value of a linear polynomial, so asking which term a number is means solving a linear equation. The equation always has an answer. Only a whole-number answer names a term.

A shop's bill should follow one linear rule, but one row of the table was copied wrongly. Which row, and what should it say?

Units$0$$1$$2$$3$$4$
Bill (₹)$150$$170$$190$$215$$230$

The differences are $20$, $20$, $25$, $15$. They are not all equal, so as it stands the table is not linear. But look at where the differences go wrong. The first two are $20$. The last two are $25$ and $15$, and those two are the differences on either side of the bill for $3$ units. That is the only row that touches both. So the bill for $3$ units is the wrong one. The rule from the other rows is $150 + 20u$, and at $u = 3$ it gives $210$, not $215$. Check the last row: $150 + 80 = 230$. Notice that the differences told us more than yes or no. When one row is wrong, two differences are wrong, and they sit either side of it. Notice too that $25 + 15 = 40$, which is two steps of $20$. The wrong row pushed one difference up by $5$ and the next one down by $5$.

Do the points $(1, 5)$, $(3, 11)$ and $(6, 23)$ lie on one straight line?

Two points fix a line, so use the first two. From $x = 1$ to $x = 3$ is two steps, and $y$ rises by $6$, so $a = 3$. Then $5 = 3 + b$ gives $b = 2$, and the line through the first two points is $y = 3x + 2$. At $x = 6$ this line gives $20$, not $23$. So the third point is not on it, and no straight line passes through all three (Fig. 2B.1). There is another way to see it. From $(3, 11)$ to $(6, 23)$ is three steps and a rise of $12$, which is $4$ a step. The first pair gave $3$ a step. A straight line has one slope, so the three points cannot share one.
