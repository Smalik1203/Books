A reasoning question has no working to show. It wants the sentence that would convince someone who disagreed — so answer in words, and kill a false claim with one counter-example rather than an argument.

B2 · Think and Reason
  1. The chapter says two observations are enough to recover $a$ and $b$, and that they must differ in $x$.
    1. Explain what goes wrong if both readings are taken at the same $x$.
    2. Two readings are to be taken between $x = 0$ and $x = 20$. Which two would you choose, and why?
  2. A tree grows every year, but by more in some years than in others. Its height is increasing, and yet no expression $at + b$ describes it. Say exactly which word in the definition of linear growth rules the tree out — and name one growing quantity that the definition does admit.
  3. The chess club charged $200 + 50m$ and Bela's money ran $100 - 5n$. Both constants are what you have at the start, and yet one is money owed and the other money held. Explain how a single piece of algebra can describe two opposite situations, and what in the expression records the difference.
  4. Predict, without drawing: how do the graphs of $y = 4x - 3$ and $y = -4x - 3$ differ, and what do they have in common? Name the single point both are certain to pass through, and say why.
  5. The auto-rickshaw fare obeys $15n - 5$ only from the second kilometre on. Put $n = 1$ into the formula, compare it with the real fare, and say what the algebra is describing at that point. Then invent one more charging rule with the same defect.
  6. $y = ax$ always passes through the origin. State the one-line reason, and then say what changes — and what does not — when a constant $b$ is added to it.
  7. A quantity is measured every hour and its differences come out $7, 7, 7, 7$. A student concludes the rule must be $7t$.
    1. Is the conclusion safe? Say what has been left undetermined.
    2. What single further measurement would settle it?
