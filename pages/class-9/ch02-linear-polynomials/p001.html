2.1Introduction

You have met algebraic expressions before. This chapter is about one particular family of them — the simplest family, and by some distance the most useful. Before naming it, it is worth seeing where such expressions come from, because they are rarely invented for their own sake. They turn up when a quantity has to be counted and the count is not yet known.

Example 1

Raju goes to a shop selling sealed boxes of two colours. Every red box holds $4$ pens; every blue box holds $5$ pencils. He buys $x$ red boxes and $y$ blue boxes, and the shopkeeper throws in $3$ pens free.

How many pens and pencils does he carry home?

The $x$ red boxes hold $4x$ pens and the $y$ blue boxes hold $5y$ pencils; add the three free pens, and the whole haul is

$$4x + 5y + 3$$

He does not have to know $x$ and $y$ to write that down. The expression counts for every purchase he could have made.

That last sentence is the point of the whole subject. The expression $4x + 5y + 3$ is not an answer waiting to be worked out; it is a rule that holds for all the purchases at once, and a particular purchase is got by putting numbers in.
