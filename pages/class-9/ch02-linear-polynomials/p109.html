Type 7 · Points on a line

Example 12

The points $(1, 2)$, $(4, 11)$ and $(6, k)$ lie on one straight line. Then $k$ is

  1. $14$
  2. $18$
  3. $17$
  4. $13$

Solution.

Step 1 $3a = 11 - 2 = 9$, so $a = 3$ three steps in $x$
Step 2 $2 = 3(1) + b$, so $b = -1$, and the line is $y = 3x - 1$
Step 3 $k = 3(6) - 1 = 17$ its value at $x = 6$
Answer (c)

The line need not be found in full: from $x = 4$ to $x = 6$ is two steps, so $k = 11 + 2 \times 3 = 17$ as well. Option (a) adds the slope only once. Option (b) is $3 \times 6$, which forgets $b$. Option (d) adds $2$, the step in $x$, as if the slope were $1$.

Example 13

Does the point $(-2, -11)$ lie on the line $y = 4x - 3$? For what value of $m$ does the point $(m, 13)$ lie on it?

Solution.

Step 1 at $x = -2$ the line gives $y = 4(-2) - 3 = -11$ put in the $x$-coordinate
Step 2 that is the point's $y$-coordinate, so $(-2, -11)$ is on the line
Step 3 $4m - 3 = 13$ the point satisfies the equation
Step 4 $4m = 16$
Answer yes, it lies on the line; and $m = 4$
Check $4 \times 4 - 3 = 13$
