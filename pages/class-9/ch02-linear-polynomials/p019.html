Example 13

Plot $(-3, 6)$, $(-2, 4)$, $(0, 0)$, $(1, -2)$, $(2, -4)$ and $(3, -6)$, and join $(-3, 6)$ to $(3, -6)$. What is the equation of the line?

Again the ruler catches every point. This time each $y$ is $-2$ times its $x$, so the line is $y = -2x$ — and it falls from left to right, where $y = 3x$ climbed. The sign of the multiplier is doing that.

Both lines went through the origin, and both had the form $y = ax$. It is worth seeing several of these together before saying what $a$ does, because one line on its own has nothing to be compared with.

Example 14

Draw $y = \dfrac{1}{2}x$, $y = x$ and $y = 2x$ on one pair of axes.

Two points are enough for each. For $y = \dfrac{1}{2}x$ take $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$; for $y = x$ take $(0, 0)$ and $(3, 3)$; for $y = 2x$ take $(0, 0)$ and $(2, 4)$.
