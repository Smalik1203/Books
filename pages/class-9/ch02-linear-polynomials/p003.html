Both expressions so far have used two letters. The next one needs only one, and that turns out to matter more than it looks.

Example 3

A wire $20$ cm long is bent into a rectangle. It could be bent $7$ cm by $3$ cm, or $5.5$ cm by $4.5$ cm, and there are many more. Write an expression for the area of such a rectangle.

The wire is the whole perimeter, so the length and the width together come to $10$ cm. Call the length $x$ cm; then the width is $(10 - x)$ cm, and the area is

$$x(10 - x) = 10x - x^{2}$$

One letter is enough, because fixing the length fixes everything else.

Notice the difference. Examples 1 and 2 needed two letters each; Example 3 needed one. From here on this chapter deals only with expressions in a single variable — and the reason is that they can be sorted into a clean order, which expressions in several variables cannot.

The sorting is done by powers. In $x^{2} + 5x + 1$ the highest power of $x$ is $2$; in $5y^{3} + y^{2} - 8$ the highest power of $y$ is $3$. That highest power is the one number that says most about how an expression behaves, so it is given a name.
