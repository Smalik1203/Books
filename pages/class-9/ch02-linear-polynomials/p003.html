Example 3

A wire $20$ cm long is bent into a rectangle — $7$ cm by $3$ cm, or $5.5$ cm by $4.5$ cm, or many another. Write an expression for its area.

Solution.

Step 1 the length and the width together come to $10$ cm the wire is the whole perimeter
Step 2 call the length $x$ cm; then the width is $(10 - x)$ cm
Answer the area is $x(10 - x) = 10x - x^{2}$

One letter is enough, because fixing the length fixes everything else.

Examples 1 and 2 needed two letters; Example 3 needed one. From here on this chapter deals only with expressions in a single variable, because those can be sorted into a clean order and expressions in several variables cannot.

The sorting is done by powers. In $x^{2} + 5x + 1$ the highest power of $x$ is $2$; in $5y^{3} + y^{2} - 8$ it is $3$. That highest power says most about how an expression behaves, so it is given a name.

Polynomial and degree

An algebraic expression built from one variable and its whole-number powers is a univariate polynomial — or, where the variable is not in doubt, simply a polynomial. The highest power of the variable appearing in it is its degree.

So $4x$, $x^{2} + 1$, $2y - 5$, $5y^{3} + y^{2} + 2y - 1$ and $3z + 7$ are all polynomials, each in one variable. Sorted by degree they fall into families, and the first four have names you will use constantly.

  1. $5y^{3} + y^{2} + 2y - 1$ has degree $3$ — a cubic polynomial.
  1. $x^{2} + 5x + 1$ has degree $2$ — a quadratic polynomial.
  1. $3z + 7$ has degree $1$ — a linear polynomial.
  1. The constant $8$ has degree $0$, since it can be written $8x^{0}$ — a constant polynomial.
