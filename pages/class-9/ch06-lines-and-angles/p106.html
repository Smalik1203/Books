Problem 4 · Try it first

In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A = 60^\circ$, and the bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ meet at $O$. Then $\angle BOC$ is

  1. $120^\circ$
  2. $60^\circ$
  3. $90^\circ$
  4. $150^\circ$
Solution 4

This is the first question of the section, with a number in it. The bisectors give $\angle BOC = 90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$, and with $\angle A = 60^\circ$ that is $120^\circ$ — option (a).

Option (b) is $\angle A$ itself. Option (c) is what the formula leaves if the $\tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$ is dropped, and it is the answer to a different question: $90^\circ$ is where the bisectors meet only when $\angle A$ is $0$, which no triangle allows. Option (d) is $90^\circ + \angle A$, the formula with the halving forgotten.

The halving is not decoration. $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ add to $120^\circ$ here, and it is their halves — $60^\circ$ between them — that sit in triangle $BOC$.

Problem 5 · Try it first

A transversal cuts two parallel lines, and one pair of co-interior angles turns out to be equal. Each of them is

  1. $45^\circ$
  2. $90^\circ$
  3. $60^\circ$
  4. impossible
Solution 5

Two facts are in play at once. Between parallels a co-interior pair is supplementary, and this particular pair is also equal. Two equal angles adding to $180^\circ$ are $90^\circ$ each, so the answer is option (b), and the transversal is perpendicular to both lines.

$a + b = 180^\circ$  and  $a = b$,  so  $a = b = 90^\circ$

Option (d) is the tempting one, because "equal co-interior angles" sounds like a contradiction of the result just quoted. It is not. Supplementary does not mean unequal; it leaves one case where the two are the same, and that case is a real drawing, not an impossibility.

Options (a) and (c) would need the pair to add to $90^\circ$ or $120^\circ$, and between parallels the sum is $180^\circ$ for every transversal there is. A theorem and one extra condition will often leave exactly one possibility standing; the question to ask is what the two of them together still allow.
