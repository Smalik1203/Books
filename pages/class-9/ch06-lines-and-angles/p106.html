Problem 4

In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A = 60^\circ$, and the bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ meet at $O$. Then $\angle BOC$ is

  1. $120^\circ$
  2. $60^\circ$
  3. $90^\circ$
  4. $150^\circ$
Solution

This is the first question of the section, with a number in it.

$\angle BOC = 90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$

$= 90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$

Option (a).

Option (b) is $\angle A$ itself. Option (c) drops the $\tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$, and $90^\circ$ would need $\angle A$ to be $0$, which no triangle allows. Option (d) forgets the halving. That halving is not decoration: $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ add to $120^\circ$ here, and it is their halves that sit in triangle $BOC$.

Problem 5

A transversal cuts two parallel lines, and one pair of co-interior angles turns out to be equal. Each of them is

  1. $45^\circ$
  2. $90^\circ$
  3. $60^\circ$
  4. impossible
Solution

Two facts are in play at once: between parallels a co-interior pair is supplementary, and this particular pair is also equal.

$a + b = 180^\circ$  and  $a = b$

$2a = 180^\circ$

$a = b = 90^\circ$

Option (b), and the transversal is perpendicular to both lines.

Option (d) tempts because "equal co-interior angles" sounds like a contradiction. It is not: supplementary does not mean unequal, and it leaves one case where the two are the same. Options (a) and (c) would need the pair to add to $90^\circ$ or $120^\circ$, and between parallels the sum is $180^\circ$ for every transversal there is.
