Competitive-Style Thinking
Four moves worth more than another theorem.
Eliminate No angle of a triangle reaches $180^\circ$, and no two of them can both be obtuse. Two options usually die on sight.
Check sufficiency A pair of equal angles proves nothing until you name its position — corresponding, alternate, or co-interior.
Find the constraint Every figure here carries one total: $180^\circ$ along a line, $360^\circ$ round a point, $180^\circ$ in a triangle. Decide which applies before writing anything.
Estimate first Read the drawing. An angle drawn clearly acute is not $130^\circ$, whatever the algebra seems to say.

Name the move, then use it.

  1. Eliminate. The angles of a triangle are $x$, $2x$ and $3x + 30$. Strike out two options on sight, then answer. Here $x$ is
    1. $25^\circ$
    2. $30^\circ$
    3. $50^\circ$
    4. $75^\circ$
  2. Eliminate. Two angles of a triangle are $95^\circ$ and $50^\circ$. The third is
    1. $45^\circ$
    2. $35^\circ$
    3. $85^\circ$
    4. $135^\circ$
  3. Sufficiency. A transversal cuts two lines and one pair of angles is found to be equal. Which pair proves the two lines parallel?
    1. A corresponding pair
    2. Any equal pair at all
    3. Two angles at the same crossing
    4. Two angles on the same side of the transversal
  4. Sufficiency. Two angles in a figure are equal. Say exactly what more must be known before that fact can prove two lines parallel.
  5. Constraint. Four triangles are recorded below, but one row is wrong. Find it, name the total that was mishandled, and give the right value.

    Third angle$80$$45$$55$$30$
