Example 2

Two adjacent angles on a line are in the ratio $4 : 5$. How big is each?

Being adjacent angles on a line, they are a linear pair, so they add to $180^\circ$. Write them as $4x$ and $5x$, which is what the ratio permits.

$4x + 5x = 180^\circ$
$9x = 180^\circ$, so $x = 20^\circ$

The angles are $80^\circ$ and $100^\circ$. As a check, they do add to $180^\circ$, and they are in the ratio $80 : 100 = 4 : 5$.

6.4Where Two Lines Cross

Now let two whole lines cross, rather than a ray meeting a line. Four angles appear at the crossing point. Two of them face each other across the vertex, sharing only that point and no arm; such a pair is called vertically opposite. In Fig. 6.4 the vertically opposite pairs are $\angle 1$ with $\angle 3$, and $\angle 2$ with $\angle 4$.

Draw such a crossing and measure, and you will find the vertically opposite angles equal. Draw another and measure again, and they are equal again. This is the point at which a textbook can either say "you will find that they are always equal" and move on, or stop and ask why. We stop.

Look at $\angle 1$ and $\angle 2$. They are adjacent, and their outer arms — $OB$ and $OA$ — make the straight line $AB$. So they are a linear pair, and add to $180^\circ$. Now look at $\angle 2$ and $\angle 3$: adjacent again, outer arms $OD$ and $OC$ making the line $CD$, so they too add to $180^\circ$. Both $\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are what is left when $\angle 2$ is taken away from $180^\circ$. They cannot help but be equal.
