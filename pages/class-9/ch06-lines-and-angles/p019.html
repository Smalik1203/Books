1. In triangle $ABC$, the bisectors of the exterior angles at $B$ and $C$ meet at $O$. Prove that $\angle BOC = 90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$. Compare this with the result of Example 10 and account for the difference.
  2. A line cuts two others so that the co-interior angles on one side add to $174^\circ$. Show that the two lines meet, and that they do so on that same side of the transversal.
  3. A triangle has all three angles equal to whole numbers of degrees, and the largest is exactly twice the smallest. How many such triangles are there?
What this chapter established
  1. An angle measures an amount of turning between two rays from a point. The lengths the arms are drawn to have nothing to do with its size.
  2. Two adjacent angles whose outer arms lie in a straight line form a linear pair, and are supplementary. The converse holds too: adjacent angles adding to $180^\circ$ have their outer arms in a line.
  3. Where two lines cross, the vertically opposite angles are equal. The proof uses nothing but the linear pair property, twice.
  4. The angles round a point add to $360^\circ$, because going round from a ray back to itself is one complete turn.
  5. A transversal cutting two lines makes eight angles, sorted by position into corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior and co-interior pairs. The names say where the angles are, not how big they are.
  6. When the two lines are parallel, corresponding angles are equal. This is assumed, not proved. From it follow the equality of alternate angles and the supplementarity of co-interior angles — and each of the three runs in reverse, which is the only way parallelism is ever established.
  7. Two lines parallel to the same line are parallel to each other.
  8. The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$. The proof draws a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side, so the result rests on the parallel axiom and would fail without it.
  9. An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles, and is therefore greater than either of them.
  10. Drawing a line that was not given — a construction — is often what turns an intractable figure into two ordinary ones. Example 7 and the angle sum proof both turn on a single added parallel.
