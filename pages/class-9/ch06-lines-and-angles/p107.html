The Challenge Zone
Think, then apply, then challenge.

Not one of these says which idea to use. Show every step of the reasoning.

  1. Think Prove that vertically opposite angles are equal, using the linear pair property and nothing else. Then say which step would fail if one of the two lines were bent.
  2. Think The angle sum proof rests on the parallel axiom. Name the step that uses it, and say what the proof would have left unproved without it.
  3. Apply In triangle $ABC$ the bisectors of the exterior angles at $B$ and $C$ meet at $O$. Prove that $\angle BOC = 90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$, and account for the difference from the interior case.

    Each exterior angle is $180^\circ$ less its interior one, so each bisected half falls by half of that.

  4. Apply Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. Prove that the bisectors of a pair of alternate interior angles are parallel to each other.
  5. Challenge In Fig. 6B.2, $AB \parallel CD$. Find $\angle APD$, and name the one line you had to draw to get it.

    $P$ lies on neither line, so no pair of angles in the figure is yet a named pair. Draw through $P$ the only line that makes two of them named.

  6. Challenge A transversal cuts two lines so that the co-interior angles on one side add to $174^\circ$. Show that the two lines meet, and that they meet on that side.

    Ask first what the sum would have to be for them never to meet, then say which way the shortfall tips them.
