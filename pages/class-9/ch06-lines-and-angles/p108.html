The Trap Room
Two students, one question, and a mistake.

Answer (a) A only, (b) B only, (c) both, (d) neither — then say in one line what went wrong.

  1. $\angle AOB$ and $\angle BOC$ are adjacent and add to $180^\circ$. A. They form a linear pair.  B. $OA$ and $OC$ lie in one straight line.
  2. A. Corresponding angles are always equal.  B. They are equal only when the two lines are parallel.
  3. A. Co-interior angles between parallel lines are supplementary.  B. They are equal, like every other pair.
  4. A. How long the arms are drawn has nothing to do with the size of an angle.  B. Drawing the arms longer makes the angle bigger.
  5. A. A triangle can have two right angles.  B. It cannot — the third angle would have to be $0^\circ$.
  6. An exterior angle of a triangle is $70^\circ$. A. The interior angle at that vertex is $70^\circ$.  B. The two interior opposite angles add to $110^\circ$.
  7. A. Two lines parallel to the same line are parallel to each other.  B. Two lines perpendicular to the same line must meet.
  8. A. Any two equal angles at a transversal prove the two lines parallel.  B. Only a correctly named pair does.
  9. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A = 40^\circ$. A. $\angle B + \angle C = 140^\circ$.  B. The exterior angle at $A$ is $140^\circ$.
  10. A. The angles round a point add to $180^\circ$.  B. They add to $360^\circ$.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. A parallel result quoted without its condition (2, 3, 7, 8). Corresponding angles are equal when the lines are parallel; co-interior angles are the one pair that comes out supplementary instead; and only a named pair proves anything.
  2. Reading the drawing instead of the definition (4, 6). An angle is an amount of turning, not a picture: arm length says nothing, and the exterior angle is not the interior one at that vertex.
  3. Losing which total applies (1, 5, 9, 10). $180^\circ$ along a line, $180^\circ$ in a triangle, $360^\circ$ round a point. Name the total before writing the equation.
