Type 4 · Proving that two lines are parallel

Example 7

A transversal $t$ cuts three lines $p$, $q$ and $r$. The corresponding angles it makes with $p$ and $q$ are both $72^\circ$, and a pair of co-interior angles between $q$ and $r$ are $72^\circ$ and $108^\circ$. Prove that $p \parallel r$.

Solution.

Step 1 $p \parallel q$ equal corresponding angles
Step 2 $72^\circ + 108^\circ = 180^\circ$
Step 3 $q \parallel r$ supplementary co-interior angles
Answer $p$ and $r$ are both parallel to $q$, so $p \parallel r$

Neither step compares $p$ with $r$ directly. The line $q$ carries the parallelism across, as in Section 6.7.

Example 8

In a quadrilateral $ABCD$, $\angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ$. Prove that $AD \parallel BC$.

Solution.

Step 1 $AB$ is a transversal of the lines $AD$ and $BC$
Step 2 $\angle DAB$ and $\angle ABC$ are co-interior same side of $AB$, between the lines
Step 3 $\angle DAB + \angle ABC = 180^\circ$ given
Answer $AD \parallel BC$, since supplementary co-interior angles make the lines parallel

Type 5 · The angle sum of a triangle

Example 9

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $3 : 5 : 7$. The largest of them is

  1. $36^\circ$
  2. $60^\circ$
  3. $84^\circ$
  4. $90^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 $3k + 5k + 7k = 180^\circ$ angle sum
Step 2 $15k = 180^\circ$, so $k = 12^\circ$
Step 3 the angles are $36^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $84^\circ$
Answer (c)

Options (a) and (b) are the other two angles. Option (d) was never available: a right angle would have to be half the total, so the last term of the ratio would be the sum of the first two, and $3 + 5$ is not $7$.
