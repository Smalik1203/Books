Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.

    Reason (R): A line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side carries the other two angles up beside the third, along a straight line.

  1. Assertion (A): Two lines that make equal corresponding angles with a transversal are parallel.

    Reason (R): When two lines are parallel, the corresponding angles a transversal makes with them are equal.

  1. Assertion (A): A triangle can have two right angles.

    Reason (R): The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): An exterior angle of a triangle is greater than each of its two interior opposite angles.

    Reason (R): An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the sum of all three interior angles.

Very short answer
  1. Find the supplement of the complement of $38^\circ$.
  1. The angles of a triangle are $x$, $2x$ and $3x$. Find the largest of them.
  1. In triangle $PQR$, $\angle P = 72^\circ$ and the exterior angle at $R$ is $130^\circ$. Find $\angle Q$.
Short answer
  1. The bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ of triangle $ABC$ meet at $I$, and $\angle BIC = 125^\circ$. Find $\angle A$.
  1. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A = 50^\circ$ and $\angle B = 60^\circ$, and $CD$ is perpendicular to $AB$, with $D$ on $AB$. Find $\angle ACD$ and $\angle BCD$, and check that they add to $\angle ACB$.
  1. One angle of a triangle is $20^\circ$ more than a second, and the exterior angle at the third vertex is $110^\circ$. Find all three angles.
  1. $AB \parallel CD$, and a transversal meets $AB$ at $P$ and $CD$ at $Q$, with $B$ and $D$ on the same side of it. If $\angle BPQ = (4x + 16)^\circ$ and $\angle PQD = (2x + 44)^\circ$, find $x$ and $\angle APQ$.
Long answer
  1. A pentagon $ABCDE$ has its diagonals $AC$ and $AD$ inside it. Joining them cuts the pentagon into three triangles. Prove that the five angles of the pentagon add to $540^\circ$.
  1. A transversal cuts two parallel lines, and one angle of a pair of co-interior angles is three times the other. Find all eight angles, and say how many of the eight are equal to each value.
