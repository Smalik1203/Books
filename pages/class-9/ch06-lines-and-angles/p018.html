Exercise Set 6.3
  1. Two angles of a triangle are $54^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. Find the third, and all three exterior angles.
  1. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$. Find them. Is the triangle acute, right-angled or obtuse?
  1. One angle of a triangle is twice the second and $30^\circ$ more than the third. Find all three.
  1. Can a triangle have exterior angles of $100^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $150^\circ$? Give a reason.
  1. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle B = 62^\circ$ and the exterior angle at $C$ is $118^\circ$. Find $\angle A$ and $\angle ACB$, and say what is special about this triangle.
  1. An exterior angle of a triangle measures $105^\circ$, and one of its interior opposite angles is said to be $105^\circ$ as well. Show that this is impossible, using the exterior angle theorem alone.
End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. Find the angle that is $24^\circ$ less than its own complement.
  1. Two adjacent angles at a point are $(5x - 8)^\circ$ and $(3x + 12)^\circ$, and together they make a straight angle. Find $x$ and both angles.
  1. Three lines pass through a single point, making six angles. If one of them is $38^\circ$, must any of the other five be $38^\circ$ as well? Explain.
  1. In a figure, $AB \parallel CD$ and $EF$ is a transversal cutting them at $G$ and $H$. If $\angle AGH$ and $\angle GHD$ are in the ratio $5 : 4$, find both.
