  1. A line cuts two others so that the co-interior angles on one side add to $174^\circ$. Show that the two lines meet, and that they do so on that same side of the transversal.
  1. A triangle has all three angles equal to whole numbers of degrees, and the largest is exactly twice the smallest. How many such triangles are there?

  1. The three angles of a triangle are whole numbers of degrees, and they increase by the same amount from one to the next. Show that one of them must be $60^\circ$, whatever the common step is.
  1. A transversal cuts two lines. On one side of it the co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$, and a pair of alternate interior angles are found to differ by $20^\circ$. Show that no such pair of lines can exist.
