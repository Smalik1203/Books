1. Can a triangle have exterior angles of $100^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $150^\circ$? Give a reason.
  2. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle B = 62^\circ$ and the exterior angle at $C$ is $118^\circ$. Find $\angle A$ and $\angle ACB$, and say what is special about this triangle.
  3. An exterior angle of a triangle measures $105^\circ$, and one of its interior opposite angles is said to be $105^\circ$ as well. Show that this is impossible, using the exterior angle theorem alone.
End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. Find the angle that is $24^\circ$ less than its own complement.
  2. Two adjacent angles at a point are $(5x - 8)^\circ$ and $(3x + 12)^\circ$, and together they make a straight angle. Find $x$ and both angles.
  3. Three lines pass through a single point, making six angles. If one of them is $38^\circ$, must any of the other five be $38^\circ$ as well? Explain.
  4. In a figure, $AB \parallel CD$ and $EF$ is a transversal cutting them at $G$ and $H$. If $\angle AGH$ and $\angle GHD$ are in the ratio $5 : 4$, find both.
  1. The arms of one angle are parallel to the arms of another, taken in the same sense. Prove that the two angles are equal.
  2. Prove that if a transversal cuts two parallel lines, the bisectors of a pair of corresponding angles are parallel to each other.
  3. In triangle $ABC$, $\angle A - \angle B = 15^\circ$ and $\angle B - \angle C = 25^\circ$. Find all three angles.
  1. A ladder leans against a wall, making $63^\circ$ with the level ground. The wall is vertical. What angle does the ladder make with the wall? Which fact of this chapter did you use?
  2. In triangle $ABC$, the bisector of $\angle A$ meets $BC$ at $D$. If $\angle B = 70^\circ$ and $\angle C = 50^\circ$, find $\angle ADB$ and $\angle ADC$, and check that they form a linear pair.
  3. Two lines $m$ and $n$ are cut by a transversal. A pair of co-interior angles are found to be $87^\circ$ and $91^\circ$. Are $m$ and $n$ parallel? If they meet, on which side of the transversal do they meet?
