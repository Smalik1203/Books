Example 5

In Fig. 6.6, $m \parallel n$ and the transversal makes an angle of $58^\circ$ as marked. Find $x$, $y$ and $z$.

Take them one at a time, and name the reason each time.

x $x = 180^\circ - 58^\circ = 122^\circ$ linear pair on $m$
y $y = 58^\circ$ alternate interior with $58^\circ$
z $z = 58^\circ$ corresponding to $58^\circ$

Check the answers against each other rather than against the drawing: $y$ and $z$ are vertically opposite at the lower crossing, so they had better be equal, and they are.

Example 6

A transversal cuts two lines so that a pair of co-interior angles measure $(3x + 10)^\circ$ and $(2x + 40)^\circ$. For what value of $x$ are the two lines parallel?

The lines are parallel exactly when co-interior angles are supplementary. So set the sum to $180^\circ$ and see what $x$ must be.

$(3x + 10) + (2x + 40) = 180$
$5x + 50 = 180$, so $x = 26$

Then the angles are $88^\circ$ and $92^\circ$, which do add to $180^\circ$. For any other value of $x$ the lines meet somewhere — and the algebra even tells you on which side, though we shall not chase that here.
