Example 1

In Fig. 6.3 (right), $\angle POR = 118^\circ$. Find $\angle ROQ$.

Solution.

Step 1 $OP$ and $OQ$ are opposite rays, so $\angle POR$ and $\angle ROQ$ are a linear pair given in the figure
Step 2 $\angle POR + \angle ROQ = 180^\circ$ linear pair property
Step 3 $118^\circ + \angle ROQ = 180^\circ$ putting in the given angle
Answer $\angle ROQ = 62^\circ$

Nothing was measured. The single fact that $P$, $O$ and $Q$ lie in a line fixed the answer.

One word in that converse is carrying the weight, and it is adjacent. Any two angles adding to $180^\circ$ are supplementary — one of them here, the other three pages away, or one in a triangle and one in a quadrilateral — and that says nothing whatever about arms lying in a line.

The converse needs the two to be adjacent, sharing a vertex and an arm, before there are any outer arms to speak of. Supplementary is a fact about two numbers; a linear pair is a fact about a drawing.

Example 2

Two adjacent angles on a line are in the ratio $4 : 5$. How big is each?

Solution.

Step 1 the two are a linear pair, so they add to $180^\circ$ adjacent angles on a line
Step 2 write them as $4x$ and $5x$ what the ratio permits
Step 3 $4x + 5x = 180^\circ$
Step 4 $9x = 180^\circ$, so $x = 20^\circ$
Answer The angles are $80^\circ$ and $100^\circ$
Check they add to $180^\circ$, and $80 : 100 = 4 : 5$
