Using What You Know
The chapter's results, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter ended with a short list: a linear pair adds to $180^\circ$, vertically opposite angles are equal, parallel lines hand you equal angles, an exterior angle is the two far ones added together, and the three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$. Every question below can be answered from that list alone. What is new is that the question no longer says which item to reach for. Try each one before you read on.

It is worth seeing first that the last item on the list is not an item of its own. Draw one line through $A$ parallel to $BC$, and the angles at $B$ and $C$ are carried up beside the angle at $A$ by alternate angles, where the three of them lie along a straight line. The angle sum is the parallel property and a line that was not given.

In triangle $ABC$ the bisectors of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ meet at $O$. Find $\angle BOC$ when $\angle A = 50^\circ$. Then find it for any $\angle A$.

In triangle $BOC$ the angles at $B$ and $C$ are halves of $\angle B$ and $\angle C$, so they add to $\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\angle B + \angle C\right)$. The angle sum in triangle $ABC$ says that $\angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ - \angle A$, and so

$$\angle BOC = 180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\left(180^\circ - \angle A\right) = 90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$$

With $\angle A = 50^\circ$ this gives $115^\circ$. Notice that $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ were never found, and could not have been: the question gives their total and nothing else. The bisectors did not need them separately, because halving each and adding is the same as halving the total.
