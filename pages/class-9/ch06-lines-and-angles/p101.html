The Idea Underneath
One axiom, and a line nobody drew.

The chapter's largest result is the angle sum:

$$\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ$$

It is not an independent fact. Draw one line through $A$ parallel to $BC$ and the three angles fall along a straight line, two of them carried there by alternate angles. The angle sum is the parallel axiom, plus a line that was not given.

Every parallel result runs both ways
the lines are parallel→ corresponding angles are equal
corresponding angles are equal→ the lines are parallel

The first direction computes an angle; the second proves a parallelism, and it is the only way parallelism is ever established. Collapsing the two is the commonest error in this chapter.

A linear pair adds to a straight angle
Across a crossing, opposites are equal
