Behind Each Answer
Five questions, with the reasoning as well as the working. Try each one before reading its solution.
Problem 1

A transversal cuts two parallel lines, making alternate interior angles $(2x + 15)^\circ$ and $(3x - 25)^\circ$. Each of them is

  1. $40^\circ$
  2. $95^\circ$
  3. $85^\circ$
  4. $91^\circ$
Solution

The pair is named, and it is named alternate interior. Between parallel lines that pair is equal, so the two expressions are equal:

$2x + 15 = 3x - 25$

$x = 40$

$2(40) + 15 = 95$,   $3(40) - 25 = 95$

Each angle is $95^\circ$ — option (b). Option (a) is where the working stops one line early: $40$ is the value of $x$, not the size of the angle.

Option (c) is $85^\circ$, the co-interior angle at the same crossing. It belongs to the right figure and the wrong name: co-interior angles are supplementary, not equal. Option (d) is what that mistake produces if it is carried through, since $2x + 15 + 3x - 25 = 180$ gives $x = 38$ and an angle of $91^\circ$. Every wrong answer here comes of naming the pair wrongly, or of stopping at $x$.

Two expressions and one equation is the shape of every question of this kind. The relation named in the question — corresponding, alternate, co-interior — decides whether the two expressions are set equal to each other or set to add to $180^\circ$, and that single choice is where the question is settled.

Everything after it is arithmetic, which is why a misreading cannot be caught further down: both readings run to a tidy value of $x$ and a plausible-looking angle, and neither leaves any trace of having gone wrong.
