Example 3

Four rays from a point $O$ make angles of $x$, $2x$, $3x$ and $120^\circ$ in order round the point. Find $x$, and say which of the four angles is obtuse.

The four angles fill one complete turn.

$x + 2x + 3x + 120^\circ = 360^\circ$
$6x = 240^\circ$, so $x = 40^\circ$

The four angles are $40^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $120^\circ$. Two of them are obtuse — the given $120^\circ$ and the $3x$, which turns out to be $120^\circ$ as well. It is worth noticing that the algebra did not know they would come out equal; we found it out only after solving.

Example 4

Two lines cross. One angle is $32^\circ$ more than three times another angle adjacent to it. Find all four angles.

Adjacent angles at a crossing form a linear pair, so they are supplementary. Call the smaller one $a$; the other is $3a + 32^\circ$.

$a + (3a + 32^\circ) = 180^\circ$
$4a = 148^\circ$, so $a = 37^\circ$

So the two adjacent angles are $37^\circ$ and $143^\circ$. Their vertically opposite partners repeat them, and the four angles round the crossing are $37^\circ$, $143^\circ$, $37^\circ$, $143^\circ$ — which do add to $360^\circ$, as they must.
