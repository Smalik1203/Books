1 $\angle 1 + \angle 2 + \angle 3 = 180^\circ$ angles on the line $PQ$ at $A$
2 $\angle 1 = \angle 4$ alternate interior, $PQ \parallel BC$, transversal $AB$
3 $\angle 3 = \angle 5$ alternate interior, $PQ \parallel BC$, transversal $AC$
4 $\angle 4 + \angle 2 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$ substituting into line 1

And $\angle 4$, $\angle 2$, $\angle 5$ are the three angles of the triangle. The proof is finished.

Angle Sum of a Triangle

The three interior angles of any triangle add to $180^\circ$.

It is worth being clear about what carried the weight. Line 1 of the proof is just the straight angle. Lines 2 and 3 are the parallel-line theorem — which came from the axiom. So the angle sum of a triangle rests on the axiom about parallels, and on nothing else. Change that axiom and the $180^\circ$ changes with it, which is not an idle remark: geometries in which it does change were built in the nineteenth century, and one of them describes the universe rather better than this one does.

Several familiar facts now come for free, and each takes one line.

Two right angles? impossible — the third angle would be $0^\circ$
Two obtuse angles? impossible — those two alone exceed $180^\circ$
Equilateral three equal angles, so each is $60^\circ$
Right-angled the other two angles are complementary

Now extend one side of the triangle past a vertex. The angle between the extended side and the next side of the triangle is an exterior angle. The two angles of the triangle not touching it are its interior opposite angles.
