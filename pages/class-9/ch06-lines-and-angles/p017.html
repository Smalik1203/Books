Example 9

In triangle $ABC$, side $BC$ is produced to $D$. If $\angle ACD = 128^\circ$ and $\angle B = 47^\circ$, find $\angle A$ and $\angle ACB$.

The exterior angle at $C$ is the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

$\angle A + \angle B = 128^\circ$
$\angle A = 128^\circ - 47^\circ = 81^\circ$

And $\angle ACB$ makes a linear pair with $\angle ACD$, so it is $180^\circ - 128^\circ = 52^\circ$. Check: $81 + 47 + 52 = 180$. The check is not a formality — it catches a wrong subtraction at once.

Example 10

In triangle $PQR$, the bisectors of $\angle Q$ and $\angle R$ meet at $O$. Show that $\angle QOR = 90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle P$.

Work in triangle $QOR$, whose angles are $\angle QOR$ and the two half-angles at $Q$ and $R$.

$\angle QOR = 180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle Q - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle R$

Now use the angle sum of the whole triangle, which gives $\angle Q + \angle R = 180^\circ - \angle P$. Halve it:

$\tfrac{1}{2}\angle Q + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle R = 90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle P$
$\angle QOR = 180^\circ - (90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle P) = 90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle P$

The result holds for every triangle, so $\angle QOR$ can never be a right angle or less — the bisectors always meet at an obtuse angle, however flat the triangle is made.
