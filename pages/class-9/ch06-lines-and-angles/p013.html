Example 7

In Fig. 6.7, $AB \parallel CD$. A point $E$ lies between the two lines, and $\angle BAE = 44^\circ$, $\angle ECD = 31^\circ$. Find $\angle AEC$.

As it stands there is nothing to work with: $\angle AEC$ is not part of any transversal figure. So make one. Through $E$ draw the line $k$ parallel to $AB$ — and therefore, by Section 6.7, parallel to $CD$ as well.

Now $AE$ is a transversal of $AB$ and $k$, and $CE$ is a transversal of $CD$ and $k$. The single line $k$ has turned one hard figure into two easy ones.

1 $\angle AEk = 44^\circ$ alternate interior, $AB \parallel k$
2 $\angle kEC = 31^\circ$ alternate interior, $CD \parallel k$
3 $\angle AEC = 44^\circ + 31^\circ = 75^\circ$ the ray $Ek$ lies inside

Adding a line that was not given is called a construction, and it is the single most useful move in geometry. Nothing forced us to draw $k$; we drew it because it made the transversal facts apply.

Think and Reflect
?
  1. In Example 7, suppose $E$ were moved so that $\angle AEC$ became a reflex angle. Would the construction still work? Draw it and see what the third line of the proof would have to become.
  2. A transversal cuts two parallel lines at right angles. What are all eight angles? Which of the four named pairs are still telling you anything new?
In the second, when every angle is $90^\circ$ the theorems are all still true — they have simply stopped being informative. That is worth noticing about theorems in general.
