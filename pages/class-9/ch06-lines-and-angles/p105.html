Problem 2

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$. The largest of them is

  1. $40^\circ$
  2. $60^\circ$
  3. $80^\circ$
  4. $90^\circ$
Solution

A ratio fixes the angles only once a total is supplied, and the total is the angle sum. Write the angles as $2k$, $3k$ and $4k$:

$2k + 3k + 4k = 180^\circ$

$9k = 180^\circ$, so $k = 20^\circ$

the angles are $40^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $80^\circ$

The largest is $80^\circ$ — option (c). Options (a) and (b) are the other two angles, there for a reader who does the work correctly and then answers a question that was not asked.

Option (d) is worth a second look. A right-angled triangle needs its largest angle to be half the total, so its ratio has to have the form $a : b : (a + b)$. In $2 : 3 : 4$ the last term is less than the first two added, so this triangle is acute-angled and $90^\circ$ was never available.

Problem 3

An exterior angle of a triangle is $3x$, and the two interior opposite angles are $x$ and $40^\circ$. The exterior angle is

  1. $20^\circ$
  2. $60^\circ$
  3. $120^\circ$
  4. $140^\circ$
Solution

An exterior angle equals the two interior opposite angles added together. That is one equation, and it is the only one the question offers:

$3x = x + 40$

$2x = 40$, so $x = 20$

$3x = 60$

The exterior angle is $60^\circ$ — option (b). Option (a) is $x$ again, the same slip as in Problem 1; option (c) is $3x$ with $x$ read as $40$. Option (d) is $180^\circ - 40^\circ$, from using the linear pair instead of the exterior angle result. The check is quick: the interior angles are $20^\circ$, $40^\circ$ and $120^\circ$, and $120^\circ$ is the supplement of $60^\circ$.
