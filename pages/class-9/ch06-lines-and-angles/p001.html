6

Lines and the Angles They Make

6.1Introduction

Stand at a crossroads and look down each of the four roads in turn. What you turn through, between one look and the next, is an angle. You could describe the crossroads by saying how long each road is, or where it goes, or how wide it is — but none of that is what makes it a crossroads. What makes it one is the way the roads are set against each other, and that is a statement about angles.

This chapter is about exactly that much and no more: straight lines, and the openings they make where they meet. It is a small vocabulary. Almost nothing in it will be new to you — you have known since primary school that a straight angle is $180^\circ$. What is new is that from here on, facts of that kind are not going to be things you were told. They are going to be things that follow, and we shall be careful about which follows from which.

That care pays off in a way you can see coming. By the end of the chapter we shall have proved that the three angles of any triangle add up to $180^\circ$ — any triangle at all, including ones nobody has ever drawn. No amount of measuring could establish that. A protractor can check a hundred triangles; it cannot check the hundred-and-first.

Measuring tells you about the figure in front of you. An argument tells you about every figure of that kind, drawn or not.

6.2The Things We Shall Argue About

Before anything can be proved, the words have to be pinned down. Four of them do most of the work in this chapter, and three of the four are pieces cut from a line.

A line runs on for ever in both directions. It has no ends, so it has no length; when we draw one we are drawing a piece of it and trusting you to imagine the rest. Cut it at a point and keep one half, ends and all, and you have a ray — one end, running on for ever the other way. Cut it twice and keep the piece between the cuts and you have a line segment, which does have a length, and is the only one of the three you can measure.

The fourth word is the one this chapter turns on.
