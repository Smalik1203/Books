For the second, there are only two ways a pair of the four can be related. Try each.

One more fact belongs here, and it comes from the same idea of turning. Draw any number of rays out from a single point. Going round from the first ray to the next, and the next, and so on all the way back to the first, you have turned through one complete revolution. So the angles round a point, taken in order, add to $360^\circ$.

The two facts about turning are really one. Draw a straight line through the point and it splits the full revolution into two straight angles, so $360^\circ$ is what a pair of $180^\circ$ turns comes to. The angles on one side of a line are half of the angles round a point, and neither fact is deeper than the other.

The words in order are doing real work, though. The rays must be taken as they come round, each angle beginning where the last one ended. Skip a ray and you have left a gap out of the count; double back and you have counted the same turn twice.

Example 3

Four rays from a point $O$ make angles of $x$, $2x$, $3x$ and $120^\circ$ in order round the point. Find $x$, and say which of the four angles is obtuse.

Solution.

Step 1 $x + 2x + 3x + 120^\circ = 360^\circ$ they fill one complete turn
Step 2 $6x = 240^\circ$, so $x = 40^\circ$
Step 3 the four angles are $40^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $120^\circ$
Answer $x = 40^\circ$. Two angles are obtuse: the given $120^\circ$, and the $3x$, which turns out to be $120^\circ$ as well

It is worth noticing that the algebra did not know the two would come out equal; we found it out only after solving.
