The bisectors meet at a right angle, and they do so whatever the transversal is. Nothing here depended on either angle by itself. The parallel property fixed the sum, the sum fixed the halves, and the halves were all the triangle needed. A result that holds for every position of the transversal is usually a result about a total.

A transversal cuts two lines so that the co-interior angles on one side add to $174^\circ$. Do the two lines meet? If they do, on which side of the transversal?

If the lines were parallel the two angles would add to $180^\circ$. They do not, so the lines are not parallel, and two lines in a plane that are not parallel meet. That settles the first half without a calculation.

For the second half, suppose they meet on the $174^\circ$ side. Then the two lines and the transversal make a triangle whose angles at the transversal are that co-interior pair. Those two account for $174^\circ$, leaving $6^\circ$ for the third angle, and a triangle with a $6^\circ$ angle is a perfectly ordinary triangle. On the other side the two angles would be $186^\circ$ between them, more than the whole angle sum, and no triangle could close. So the lines meet on the $174^\circ$ side. A shortfall from $180^\circ$ leans the lines towards the side it is short on; a surplus leans them the other way.

This time the bisectors of the exterior angles at $B$ and $C$ of triangle $ABC$ meet at $O$. Find $\angle BOC$, and compare it with the answer to the first question.

The exterior angle at $B$ is $180^\circ - \angle B$, so its half is $90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle B$, and the same at $C$. In triangle $BOC$ those two halves add to $180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\left(\angle B + \angle C\right)$, which by the angle sum is $180^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}(180^\circ - \angle A)$, or $90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$. The third angle is what is left:

$$\angle BOC = 180^\circ - \left(90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A\right) = 90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$$

The interior bisectors gave $90^\circ + \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$ and the exterior ones give $90^\circ - \tfrac{1}{2}\angle A$. The two answers differ by $\angle A$, which is exactly what the two pairs of halves differ by, and the whole difference between the two questions came from replacing each angle by its supplement.
