6.5A Line Across Two Others

Everything so far has come out of a single crossing. Now take two lines and cut them both with a third. A line that cuts two or more lines at distinct points is called a transversal, and it makes eight angles — four at each crossing.

Eight angles are more than anyone wants to keep track of, so they are sorted into groups, and the sorting is done by position. Look at Fig. 6.5. The transversal $t$ cuts lines $m$ and $n$ at $P$ and $Q$. The region between $m$ and $n$ is called interior; the two regions outside them are exterior.
