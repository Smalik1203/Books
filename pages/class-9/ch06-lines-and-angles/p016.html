The Exterior Angle Theorem

An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

The proof is two lines. Let the triangle be $ABC$ with $BC$ produced to $D$, so the exterior angle is $\angle ACD$.

1 $\angle ACB + \angle ACD = 180^\circ$ linear pair on $BD$
2 $\angle ACB + \angle A + \angle B = 180^\circ$ angle sum of the triangle
3 $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B$ take $\angle ACB$ from both

One consequence is used constantly and deserves its own sentence: an exterior angle is greater than either interior opposite angle, since it is their sum and neither is zero. That is often more useful than the equality, because it is a statement about which of two angles is bigger without either being known.

Example 8

In a triangle the angles are $(x + 20)^\circ$, $(2x - 10)^\circ$ and $(3x + 5)^\circ$. Find them, and say what kind of triangle it is.

$(x + 20) + (2x - 10) + (3x + 5) = 180$
$6x + 15 = 180$, so $x = 27.5$

The angles are $47.5^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $87.5^\circ$. All three are acute, so the triangle is acute-angled, and since no two are equal it is scalene.
