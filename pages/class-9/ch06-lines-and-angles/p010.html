Co-interior Angles

If a transversal cuts two parallel lines, each pair of co-interior angles is supplementary — the two add to $180^\circ$.

The pair travels under other names — same-side interior angles, or allied angles — and all three describe the same two. Whatever it is called, it is the one relation on the list where the angles are supplementary rather than equal. Reading a co-interior pair as equal produces an answer that looks perfectly reasonable and is wrong.

1 $\angle 4 = \angle 6$ alternate interior, just proved
2 $\angle 6 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$ linear pair on line $n$
3 $\angle 4 + \angle 5 = 180^\circ$ substituting from line 1

Each of these three results has a converse, and all three converses are true: equal alternate interior angles force the lines parallel, and so do supplementary co-interior angles. Each is proved the same way — get back to a pair of equal corresponding angles, then apply the converse half of the axiom.

Example 5

In Fig. 6.6, $m \parallel n$ and the transversal makes an angle of $58^\circ$ as marked. Find $x$, $y$ and $z$.

Solution.

Step 1 $x = 180^\circ - 58^\circ = 122^\circ$ linear pair on $m$
Step 2 $y = 58^\circ$ alternate interior with $58^\circ$
Step 3 $z = 58^\circ$ corresponding to $58^\circ$
Answer $x = 122^\circ$, $y = 58^\circ$, $z = 58^\circ$

Check the answers against each other rather than against the drawing: $y$ and $z$ are vertically opposite at the lower crossing, so they had better be equal, and they are.
