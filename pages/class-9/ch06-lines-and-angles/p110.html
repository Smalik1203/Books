Why the other options are wrong

B2 Both statements are true, so (c) and (d) go at once. But R is the direct result — parallel lines give equal corresponding angles — and A is its converse. A statement true in one direction is not a reason for the other, which leaves (b). Every parallel result in this chapter has a converse, and only the converse ever establishes a parallelism.
B4 A neighbour of the $130^\circ$ angle is $50^\circ$, by the linear pair, and the angle vertically opposite that neighbour is $50^\circ$ as well. Option (c) is the angle vertically opposite the $130^\circ$ one, which is the pair the question does not ask about.
B5 Two crossings, and only two sizes of angle between them, four of each. So four of the eight are $108^\circ$. Option (a) counts only the crossing where the angle was named; option (d) reaches six by treating the supplement, $72^\circ$, as equal too.
C6 A second obtuse angle would carry the total past $180^\circ$ between the two of them, and a right angle beside an obtuse one leaves less than nothing for the third. So both of the others are acute. Option (d) claims more than the question gives: an obtuse-angled triangle need not be isosceles.
