Selected solutions

B7.5 hint $P$ is on neither parallel, so no pair in the figure is yet a named pair.
solution Draw through $P$ the line parallel to $AB$. It is then parallel to $CD$ as well. The part of $\angle APD$ above it is alternate to $\angle BAP = 55^\circ$, the part below alternate to $\angle PDC = 40^\circ$, so $\angle APD = 95^\circ$.
key idea A line that was not given is what turns an unnamed angle into two named ones. It is the same move as the angle sum proof.
B5.9 Between parallels, co-interior angles are supplementary. If they are also equal, each must be $90^\circ$ — so the transversal is perpendicular to both.
key idea A theorem plus one extra condition often forces a single value. Ask what the two together leave possible.
B2.2 Both statements are true, so (c) and (d) are out. But R is the direct result — parallel lines give equal angles — and A is its converse. A true statement in one direction is not a reason for the other. Hence (b).
key idea In this chapter every parallel result has a converse, and only the converse ever proves a parallelism.
What to take away
  1. Name the total first: $180^\circ$ along a line, $360^\circ$ round a point, $180^\circ$ in a triangle.
  2. Equal angles prove nothing until the pair is named.
  3. Every parallel result runs both ways, and only the reverse direction establishes parallelism.
  4. When a figure gives no named pair, draw the line that creates one.
