Four subjects, one very ordinary object, and no way at all to answer the question with only one of them.

A mask is a small example of a large fact. It is an ordinary object that most of us handled every day for a couple of years, and explaining it properly took four subjects that a timetable keeps in different rooms and often on different days of the week.

The problems that matter most now are all of that kind, and much larger. Understanding a changing climate needs physics, chemistry, biology, earth science, statistics and economics at once. Designing a medicine needs chemistry and biology and a great deal of careful mathematics about who was tested and what the result actually shows. None of those questions respects the boundaries of a syllabus.

Science reaches outside itself as well. It borrows mathematics as its language, and technology both feeds it instruments and consumes its results. It needs the arts to picture and explain what it finds, and the social sciences to work out how people will really behave when they are told something true. A vaccine that works and that nobody will take has not solved the problem it was made for.

So read the chapter headings ahead as a convenience and nothing more. When a question in a physics chapter turns out to need something you learnt in a biology one, that is not a mistake in the book. It is the world declining to be filed.

What Did You Notice?

Pick a real object — a pressure cooker, a mobile phone, a bicycle — or a real problem, such as the traffic outside your school. Sketch it, and around the sketch write which ideas from physics, chemistry, biology, earth science and mathematics are involved. Then show how at least two of them have to meet.

1.9A Human Activity

One last thing, and it is the one most easily lost among the definitions. Science is not a body of facts sitting in a book waiting to be learnt. It is something people do — a long, argumentative, collaborative activity carried on by curious and fallible human beings, across many centuries and every part of the world.

It moves because somebody was puzzled enough to ask, patient enough to measure, and honest enough to publish a result that did not suit them. It is slowed by pride and hurried by fashion, as any human activity is, and it corrects itself only because other people are allowed to check.
