Imagine This

Varsha tells Meghna, “it will rain this afternoon, the clouds look dark”. What could Meghna ask to turn that into something testable?

“It will rain this afternoon” is already testable if the place, time interval and meaning of rain are clear. Dark clouds are a proposed reason, whose reliability needs separate checks. A yes-or-no prediction can fail.

How dark is dark — darker than last Tuesday, when it did not rain? What is the humidity now, and what was it on the last three afternoons that ended in rain? Which way is the wind coming from? Is the temperature dropping the way it did before the last shower?

Measurements and past records help assess whether dark clouds predict rain reliably. Define a location, an interval such as noon to 6 p.m., and a detectable amount of rain. Record both rainy and dry afternoons, including cases where the prediction failed.

How It Works

Why forecasts go wrong further ahead

Weather forecasting is not guesswork. Thousands of measurements of temperature, pressure, humidity and wind go into models that are tested every single day against what actually happens, which is more checking than most of science gets.

Small differences in initial atmospheric conditions can grow as a model runs forward. Forecasters use sets of model runs to explore uncertainty. Model limitations and incomplete observations also contribute to error.

Forecast uncertainty usually grows with lead time. A detailed forecast for one place on one day differs from a probabilistic outlook for average conditions over a month. Computing, measurements and models all affect skill; there is no single time limit for every forecast question.

When a prediction does miss, there is an order worth working through before anything dramatic is concluded. First suspect the measurement: instruments drift, scales are misread, and somebody writes down 27 when they meant 2.7. Then suspect the arithmetic. Then the assumptions the model was built on — the ones that were deliberately left out, which is why it was worth writing them down at the time.

Only when all of those survive is the theory itself in question, and that is rare. But it does happen, and when it does it is the most interesting day anybody in that field will have.
