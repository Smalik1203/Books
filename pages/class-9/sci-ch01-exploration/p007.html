What this rules out

Why is a kilogram the same everywhere?

Buy a kilogram of rice in Kochi and a kilogram in Kanpur and you expect the same amount of rice. That is not politeness on the seller's part; it is an international agreement, and it is what makes both trade and science possible.

For a long time the agreement rested on an object. Until 2019 the kilogram was defined by a single metal cylinder kept in a vault near Paris, and every kilogram on Earth was a copy of a copy of it. The arrangement worked, but it had an odd consequence: if that one cylinder picked up a smudge, then by definition everything else in the world became very slightly lighter.

Since 2019 the kilogram has been defined from a constant of nature instead. Nothing now depends on one object surviving, and a properly equipped laboratory anywhere can realise the kilogram for itself without ever going to Paris.

That is what every measurement is, underneath: a comparison against something the world has agreed on. A ruler compares a length with the metre, a thermometer measures temperature on a defined scale, a balance compares a mass with the kilogram. The instrument is never the authority. It only carries the agreement to where you are standing.
