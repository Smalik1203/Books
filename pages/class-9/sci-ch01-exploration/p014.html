Imagine This

Estimate how many litres of air you breathe in a day. Do not look anything up.

Two quantities are needed: how often you breathe, and how much goes in each time. Sitting still, a breath takes four or five seconds, so call it 15 a minute. There are $60 \times 24 = 1440$ minutes in a day, which gives about 20 000 breaths.

For a resting adult, use half a litre as an approximate quiet-breath volume. Do not infer it from blowing up a balloon: those breaths are usually deeper than normal breathing. The estimate varies with body size and activity.

$20\,000 \times 0.5\ \text{litres} \approx 10\,000$ litres a day

The same assumptions give about 7.5 litres per minute. Multiplying by 1440 minutes checks the arithmetic but is not independent evidence: it reuses the same breathing rate and volume. An independent check would need different measurements.

Estimating well is not the same as being vague. The estimate above names every quantity it used and every assumption it made, which is exactly why somebody who disagrees can say where — that you breathe more slowly than that, or that your breaths are larger. A vague answer cannot be argued with, and that is its weakness, not its strength.
