Revise, Reflect, Refine

Several of these have no single right answer. Where a question asks you to estimate or to design a test, write down your assumptions as you go — the assumptions are most of the answer.

  1. Each of these is a model. Say what question it was built to answer, and name one thing it deliberately leaves out.
    1. a globe in a classroom
    2. a seating plan for an examination hall
    3. a map of the metro
  1. Rewrite each of these so that an observation could show it to be wrong: this medicine works; the 7 o'clock bus is usually late; our school field floods badly.
  1. A student writes in a notebook: the mass of the trolley is 250. Give three different things that sentence could mean, and say what has to be added to settle it.
  1. Name the SI base unit and its symbol for length, mass, time, electric current and temperature. Then write down what a newton is, in base units only.
  1. Say whether each is a law, a theory or a principle, and give your reason.
    1. In the absence of air, all objects fall with the same acceleration.
    2. Many diseases are caused by micro-organisms.
    3. Energy is conserved.
  1. Estimate how many litres of water your household uses in a day. List every quantity you assumed, then say which one your answer is most sensitive to.
  1. Somebody claims that plants grow better if you talk to them. Design a test. What would you have to keep the same, and what result would show the claim to be false?
  1. When Uranus would not keep to its orbit, astronomers could have abandoned the law of gravitation or proposed an unseen planet. They chose the second. What made that a scientific choice rather than a convenient one — and what would have happened to it if no planet had been found?
