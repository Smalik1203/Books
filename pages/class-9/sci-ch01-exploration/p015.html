1.8One World, Many Branches

After Grade 10 science divides into physics, chemistry, biology and earth science, and even the chapters ahead of you lean towards one or another. It is worth being clear about what those divisions are. They are a way of organising a syllabus, a library and a teaching timetable. They are not a feature of the world.

Nature has no chapters. A leaf is simultaneously a chemical factory, a structure under load, a living thing with ancestors, and a small part of a planet's energy budget, and it does not become one of those things when a chemist looks at it. The divisions help us learn, and they get in the way as soon as a real question is asked.

That one object is a fair test of how artificial the boundaries are. Ask how a mask works — a question a child might ask, about a thing sold at every chemist — and no single subject can finish the answer.

How It Works

Four subjects in one mask

Physics decides whether a droplet carried along by the air will drift into a fibre and stick, and how an electric charge on the fibres pulls in particles that would otherwise sail past. Chemistry chose the polymer those fibres are made of — one that can be drawn fine, hold a charge, and not soak up water.

Biology sets the target: how large the virus is, what it travels on, and how much of it has to be stopped to matter. Mathematics puts the rest together, modelling the airflow to work out what fraction gets through — and what happens to that fraction when the mask is worn loosely at the sides.
