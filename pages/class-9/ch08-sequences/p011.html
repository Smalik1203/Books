Example 5

A taxi charges a fixed booking fee of ₹200, plus ₹40 for every kilometre travelled. Write the sequence of fares after $1$ km, $2$ km, $3$ km, and find the fare for a journey of $10$ km.

Work the first few out plainly before reaching for anything general.

Solution.

Step 1 $200 + 40 = 240$ after 1 km
Step 2 $200 + 80 = 280$ after 2 km
Step 3 $200 + 120 = 320$ after 3 km
Step 4 the fares are $240, 280, 320, \ldots$ — an AP with $a = 240$ and $d = 40$
Step 5 $t_n = 240 + (n-1) \times 40 = 200 + 40n$ $n$th term of an AP
Step 6 $t_{10} = 200 + 400$ a journey of $10$ km
Answer The fare for $10$ km is ₹600

The tidied form $200 + 40n$ is worth a second look: the ₹200 is the booking fee and the $40n$ is the running charge, so the formula has come out saying exactly what the fare card says.

8.5Adding Up the Naturals

What is $1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + 10$? You can add them one at a time and get $55$ in half a minute. But there is a way of doing it that takes no adding to speak of, and — this is the point — it goes on working when the ten becomes a hundred, or a million.

Call the answer $S$ and write it out. Then write it out again backwards.
