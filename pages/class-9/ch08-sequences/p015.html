A Geometric Progression

A GP with first term $a$ and common ratio $r$ runs

$a, \ ar, \ ar^2, \ ar^3, \ \ldots$

and its $n$th term is $t_n = ar^{\,n-1}$.

Set the two side by side and the family resemblance is exact. An AP adds $d$ at every step and its $n$th term is $a + (n-1)d$; a GP multiplies by $r$ at every step and its $n$th term is $ar^{\,n-1}$. The recursive rules match too: $t_n = t_{n-1} + d$ against $t_n = r\,t_{n-1}$.

The one difference that matters is what the constancy is measured on. To tell which kind a sequence is, do both tests: take differences, and take ratios. One of them will be constant, or neither.

Example 7

Which of these are geometric progressions, and what is the common ratio?

$1, 2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$   $1, 3, 9, 27, 81, \ldots$   $1, -1, 1, -1, \ldots$

Solution.

Step 1 in $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$ the ratios are all $2$ each term over the last
Step 2 in $1, 3, 9, 27, 81, \ldots$ the ratios are all $3$
Step 3 in $1, -1, 1, -1, \ldots$ the ratios are all $-1$
Answer All three are GPs, with $r = 2$, $r = 3$ and $r = -1$

The third is worth a moment. It neither grows nor shrinks — it alternates — and it is a perfectly good GP. A negative ratio flips the sign at every step, which is what a multiplier of $-1$ does.
