Example 9

A ball is dropped from a height of $24$ feet. Each bounce carries it back to $\tfrac34$ of the height it fell from. Write the heights of the first five bounces, and find how many bounces it takes before the ball stays below $\tfrac16$ of the height it started at.

Each height is $0.75$ times the one before, so the heights form a GP.

1st bounce $24 \times 0.75$ 18
2nd bounce $18 \times 0.75$ 13.5
3rd bounce $13.5 \times 0.75$ 10.125
4th bounce $10.125 \times 0.75$ 7.594
5th bounce $7.594 \times 0.75$ 5.695

A sixth of $24$ feet is $4$ feet. The fifth bounce is still above it, and so is the sixth at $4.271$ feet — but the seventh reaches only $3.203$ feet. So the ball first stays below a sixth of its starting height at the seventh bounce.

The chart makes the shape of a GP plain in a way the numbers do not. Each bar is three quarters of the one to its left, so the fall is steep at first and then gentles: the ball loses six feet on its first bounce and only a foot on its seventh. It never stops bouncing in the arithmetic, however — three quarters of a positive height is always positive — and it is friction, not the sequence, that brings the real ball to rest.
