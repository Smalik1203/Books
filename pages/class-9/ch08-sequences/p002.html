8.1.1 Two sequences worth a second look

The natural numbers and the odd numbers hold no surprises: each term of the first is one more than the term before, and each term of the second is two more. The other two lists are more interesting, and they are more interesting for the same reason — each is built out of the two before it.

Take the triangular numbers, $1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21$. The gaps between consecutive terms are

$2,\quad 3,\quad 4,\quad 5,\quad 6,\ \ldots$

which is itself the natural numbers, one step along. So each triangular number is the one before it plus the next natural number — which is another way of saying that each is a running total:

$1$ $= 1$
$3$ $= 1 + 2$
$6$ $= 1 + 2 + 3$
$10$ $= 1 + 2 + 3 + 4$

The $n$th triangular number is the sum of the natural numbers up to $n$. The fifth is $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 = 15$. The name comes from the picture: lay the terms out as dots and each one is a triangle, built by adding a new row along the bottom.

Now the square numbers, $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36$. Their gaps are

$3,\quad 5,\quad 7,\quad 9,\quad 11,\ \ldots$

the odd numbers, one step along. So each square number is a running total of the odd numbers: $1 = 1$, $4 = 1 + 3$, $9 = 1 + 3 + 5$, $16 = 1 + 3 + 5 + 7$.
