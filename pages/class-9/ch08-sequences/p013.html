The earliest written record of this result is in Āryabhaṭa's Āryabhaṭīya, chapter 2, verse 19, from the early sixth century. The verse gives two ways to reach it, and the second is the one drawn above in other words: take the first term and the last, halve their sum, and multiply by how many terms there are. For $1$ to $10$ that is $\tfrac{1 + 10}{2} \times 10 = 55$.

Stated that way it is not a fact about the naturals at all. It is a fact about any AP — first plus last, halved, times the count — and you will meet it again in that form.

That is worth holding on to. An AP is symmetrical about its middle in exactly the way the two written-out rows were: pair the first term with the last, the second with the second-last, and every pair has the same total. Which is why the average of the first and last terms is the average of all of them, and multiplying that average by the count gives the sum.

8.5.1 Using it on a stretch in the middle

The formula adds up from $1$. To add a stretch that starts somewhere else, subtract the part you do not want.

Example 6

Find $25 + 26 + 27 + \cdots + 58$.

Solution.

Step 1 this is everything up to $58$, less everything up to $24$
Step 2 $S_{58} = \dfrac{58 \times 59}{2} = 29 \times 59 = 1711$ sum of the naturals
Step 3 $S_{24} = \dfrac{24 \times 25}{2} = 12 \times 25 = 300$ sum of the naturals
Step 4 $1711 - 300$ the part not wanted
Answer The sum is 1411

The one thing to be careful of is the second subscript. It is $24$, not $25$: the terms you are removing are the ones before the stretch begins, and $25$ is wanted.
