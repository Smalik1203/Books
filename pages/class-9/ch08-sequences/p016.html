8.6Geometric Progressions

An AP grows by adding the same amount each time. There is one other rule of the same kind and the same simplicity, and it is what this section is about: growing by multiplying by the same amount each time.

Here is a second pattern of tiles.

The counts are $3, 6, 12, 24, \ldots$ Nothing is being added here; the pattern is doubling. So the terms can be written

$3, \quad 3 \times 2, \quad 3 \times 2^2, \quad 3 \times 2^3, \ \ldots$

and the $n$th term is $t_n = 3 \times 2^{\,n-1}$ — with the same $n-1$ as before, and for the same reason: the first term has been doubled no times at all.

The first six terms are $3, 6, 12, 24, 48, 96$. What is constant here is not the difference between consecutive terms but the ratio:

$\dfrac{6}{3} = \dfrac{12}{6} = \dfrac{24}{12} = \dfrac{48}{24} = 2$

A sequence in which the ratio of consecutive terms is the same throughout is a geometric progression, or GP, and that fixed multiplier is its common ratio.

A Geometric Progression

A GP with first term $a$ and common ratio $r$ runs

$a, \ ar, \ ar^2, \ ar^3, \ \ldots$

and its $n$th term is $t_n = ar^{\,n-1}$.

Set the two side by side and the family resemblance is exact. An AP adds $d$ at every step and its $n$th term is $a + (n-1)d$; a GP multiplies by $r$ at every step and its $n$th term is $ar^{\,n-1}$. The recursive rules match too: $t_n = t_{n-1} + d$ against $t_n = r\,t_{n-1}$.
