Using What You Know

The chapter left you with a short list. A sequence is an ordered list of terms, and $t_n$ is the one in position $n$. An explicit rule reaches any term from its position; a recursive rule walks from the terms before. An AP adds a fixed amount at every step, a GP multiplies by a fixed amount, and both carry the same $n - 1$ because the first term has taken no steps.

Every question below can be answered from that list. What is new is that none of them says which item to reach for, and two can be answered with no arithmetic at all if you see what is being asked. Try each before reading on.

A sequence begins $2, 6, 12, 20, 30, \ldots$ Is it an AP, a GP, or neither? Give a reason that would still hold if the list were a hundred terms long.

The sequence is $2, 6, 12, 20, 30$. Run both tests rather than one.

differences $4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10$ — not constant
ratios $3,\ 2,\ \tfrac53,\ \tfrac32$ — not constant

So it is neither. But the reason asked for is the one that survives a hundred terms, and for that look at what the numbers are: $1 \times 2$, $2 \times 3$, $3 \times 4$, $4 \times 5$, $5 \times 6$. The $n$th term is $n(n+1)$, which is twice the $n$th triangular number.

That settles it for good. The differences of $n(n+1)$ are $2n$, which grows, so no AP; and the ratios fall towards $1$, so no GP. Four differences would have been evidence — the formula is a proof.

The fourth term of an AP is $19$ and the ninth exceeds the sixth by $15$. Find the first term and the common difference.

Write both facts in terms of $a$ and $d$ and the question becomes a pair of equations. The ninth term exceeding the sixth by $15$ is worth reading carefully: it says $t_9 - t_6 = 15$, and $t_9 - t_6$ is three steps of $d$.

given $a + 3d = 19$
and $3d = 15$, so $d = 5$
then $a = 19 - 15$ 4
