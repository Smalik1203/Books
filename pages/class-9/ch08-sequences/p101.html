Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter left you with a short list. A sequence is an ordered list of terms, and $t_n$ is the one in position $n$. An explicit rule reaches any term from its position; a recursive rule walks from the terms before. An AP adds a fixed amount at every step, a GP multiplies by a fixed amount, and both carry the same $n - 1$ because the first term has taken no steps.

Every question below can be answered from that list. What is new is that none of them says which item to reach for, and two can be answered with no arithmetic at all if you see what is being asked. Try each before reading on.

A sequence begins $2, 6, 12, 20, 30, \ldots$ Is it an AP, a GP, or neither? Give a reason that would still hold if the list were a hundred terms long.

The third term of an AP is $16$ and the seventh exceeds the fifth by $12$. Find the first term and the common difference.

A GP has first term $5$ and common ratio $\tfrac12$. A classmate says the terms will eventually become negative. Is that right? Answer without computing any term.

How many three-digit numbers are divisible by $7$? You are not asked to list them.

A sequence is given by $t_1 = 1$ and $t_n = t_{n-1} + 2n - 1$ for $n \geq 2$. Find its first five terms, and then say what sequence it is.
