8.4.1 What an AP looks like

Take the tile pattern again and set the stage number against the count:

$(1, 1), \quad (2, 5), \quad (3, 9), \quad (4, 13), \quad (5, 17)$

Plot those five points and something worth noticing happens.

They lie on a straight line. That is not a coincidence, and it is not special to this AP: it happens for every one of them, because a constant step sideways always buys the same step upwards, which is exactly what a straight line is.

Example 5

A taxi charges a fixed booking fee of ₹200, plus ₹40 for every kilometre travelled. Write the sequence of fares after $1$ km, $2$ km, $3$ km, and find the fare for a journey of $10$ km.

Work the first few out plainly before reaching for anything general.

after 1 km $200 + 40 = 240$
after 2 km $200 + 80 = 280$
after 3 km $200 + 120 = 320$

The fares are $240, 280, 320, \ldots$ — an AP with $a = 240$ and $d = 40$. So

$t_n$ $= 240 + (n-1) \times 40$
$= 200 + 40n$
$t_{10}$ $= 200 + 400$ ₹600

The tidied form $200 + 40n$ is worth a second look: the ₹200 is the booking fee and the $40n$ is the running charge, so the formula has come out saying exactly what the fare card says.
