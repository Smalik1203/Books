Why the other options are wrong

B1 R is true and A is false, so (d). Doubling is exactly what makes the sequence geometric rather than arithmetic — the reason offered is correct and it establishes the opposite of the claim. A true reason attached to a false statement leaves the statement false.
B3 The differences are $3, 5, 7, \ldots$, which are not constant, so (i) fails and the squares are not an AP. That they are the odd numbers is (ii), and $n^2$ is (iii) — both true, which leaves (c). The trap is reading "the differences follow a pattern" as "it is an AP"; an AP needs the differences to be equal, not merely orderly.
B6 From $7$ to $205$ in steps of $6$ is $\tfrac{198}{6} = 33$ steps, and $33$ steps visit $34$ terms. Option (a) is the step count — the same fence-post that the $n-1$ in $t_n = a + (n-1)d$ exists to handle.
C3 Four steps take $2$ to $162$, so $r^4 = 81$ and $r = 3$. Option (a) comes from dividing $162$ by $2$ and looking for a factor; option (d) from solving $r^2 = 81$, which is the right equation for the third term and not the fifth.
C7 Write them out: $x = ar^3$, $y = ar^9$, $z = ar^{15}$. Then $y^2 = a^2r^{18}$ and $xz = a^2r^{18}$ as well, so (b). Option (a) is the corresponding fact for an AP, where the middle term is the average of the two either side — in a GP it is their geometric mean instead.
C8 The AP is $t_n = 4n$, reaching $1024$ at $n = 256$. The GP is $t_n = 4 \times 2^{\,n-1}$, reaching it at $n = 9$. Option (c) pairs the right number with the wrong sequence, which is the answer of somebody who found both positions and then stopped reading.
