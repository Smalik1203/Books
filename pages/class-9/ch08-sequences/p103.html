Neither, and how to be sure

The sequence is $2, 6, 12, 20, 30$. Run both tests rather than one.

differences $4,\ 6,\ 8,\ 10$ — not constant
ratios $3,\ 2,\ \tfrac53,\ \tfrac32$ — not constant

So it is neither. But the reason asked for is the one that survives a hundred terms, and for that look at what the numbers are: $1 \times 2$, $2 \times 3$, $3 \times 4$, $4 \times 5$, $5 \times 6$. The $n$th term is $n(n+1)$, which is twice the $n$th triangular number.

That settles it for good. The differences of $n(n+1)$ are $2n$, which grows, so no AP; and the ratios fall towards $1$, so no GP. Four differences would have been evidence — the formula is a proof.

An AP from two facts

Write both facts in terms of $a$ and $d$ and the question becomes a pair of equations. The seventh term exceeding the fifth by $12$ is worth reading carefully: it says $t_7 - t_5 = 12$, and $t_7 - t_5$ is two steps of $d$.

given $a + 2d = 16$
and $2d = 12$, so $d = 6$
then $a = 16 - 12$ 4

The AP is $4, 10, 16, 22, \ldots$ Notice that the second fact never needed the first: any two terms the same distance apart differ by that many $d$'s, whether they are the fifth and seventh or the fiftieth and fifty-second.

That is worth keeping. In an AP the gap between two terms depends only on how far apart they stand, never on where they stand — so a fact of the form "this term exceeds that one by so much" is a fact about $d$ alone, and it can be read off without touching $a$. Half the questions about APs are built on it.
