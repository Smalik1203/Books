The one difference that matters is what the constancy is measured on. To tell which kind a sequence is, do both tests: take differences, and take ratios. One of them will be constant, or neither.

Example 7

Which of these are geometric progressions, and what is the common ratio?

$1, 2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots$ ratios all $2$ GP, $r = 2$
$1, 3, 9, 27, 81, \ldots$ ratios all $3$ GP, $r = 3$
$1, -1, 1, -1, \ldots$ ratios all $-1$ GP, $r = -1$

The third is worth a moment. It neither grows nor shrinks — it alternates — and it is a perfectly good GP. A negative ratio flips the sign at every step, which is what a multiplier of $-1$ does.

Example 8

Is $5,\ \tfrac{15}{4},\ \tfrac{45}{16},\ \tfrac{135}{64}, \ldots$ a geometric progression? If so, find its $n$th term.

Take the ratio of each term to the one before it.

$\dfrac{t_2}{t_1}$ $= \dfrac{15}{4} \div 5 = \dfrac{15}{20} = \dfrac34$
$\dfrac{t_3}{t_2}$ $= \dfrac{45}{16} \div \dfrac{15}{4} = \dfrac{45}{16} \times \dfrac{4}{15} = \dfrac34$
$\dfrac{t_4}{t_3}$ $= \dfrac{135}{64} \times \dfrac{16}{45} = \dfrac34$

The ratio is $\tfrac34$ throughout, so this is a GP with $a = 5$ and $r = \tfrac34$. Its $n$th term is

$$t_n = 5 \left(\frac34\right)^{n-1}$$

Here the ratio is less than $1$, so the terms shrink. They never reach zero — multiplying by $\tfrac34$ can make a positive number small but not nothing — and that is a GP as much as a doubling one is.

Notice how the test was carried out. Three ratios were computed, not one. A single ratio proves nothing at all — any two numbers have a ratio — and it is only when the same one keeps coming back that the sequence has been shown to be geometric. The same warning applies to an AP and its differences.
