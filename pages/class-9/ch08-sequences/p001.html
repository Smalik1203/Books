What Comes Next

8.1Introduction

A pattern is a promise about what has not happened yet. That is what makes patterns worth studying, and it is why this chapter exists: given the beginning of a list, we want to say what comes next — and to say it with a reason, not a guess.

You have been reading patterns for years. Here are four lists you already know:

$1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 4,\ 5,\ 6,\ \ldots$

$1,\ 3,\ 5,\ 7,\ 9,\ 11,\ \ldots$

$1,\ 3,\ 6,\ 10,\ 15,\ 21,\ \ldots$

$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ \ldots$

The natural numbers, the odd numbers, the triangular numbers and the square numbers. The three dots at the end of each are doing real work: they say the list does not stop, and that you are expected to know how it goes on.

An ordered list of numbers like these is a sequence, and each number in it is a term. Order is part of what a sequence is: the list $1, 4, 9$ and the list $9, 4, 1$ are made of the same numbers and are not the same sequence. In the square numbers, $1$ is the first term, $4$ the second, $25$ the fifth.
