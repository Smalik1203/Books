8.2The Explicit Rule

Suppose you are asked for the $53$rd odd number. You could write the odd numbers out until you had fifty-three of them, and it would work. But there is a better way, and the difference between the two is the subject of this section.

The odd numbers are $1, 3, 5, 7, \ldots$ Look at each term beside its position:

position $1 \quad 2 \quad 3 \quad 4 \quad 5$
term $1 \quad 3 \quad 5 \quad 7 \quad 9$

Each term is one less than twice its position. In the notation of the last section, $t_n = 2n - 1$. That formula is an explicit rule: it takes the position of a term and hands back the term, with no need to know anything that came before.

So the $53$rd odd number is $2 \times 53 - 1 = 105$, and it took one line rather than fifty-three.

An Explicit Rule

An explicit rule gives $t_n$ in terms of $n$ alone. Put a position in, get that term out — whatever the position, and without finding any other term first.

Example 1

A sequence is given by $u_n = 2n - 1$. Write its first three terms.

Solution.

Step 1 $u_1 = 2 \times 1 - 1 = 1$ substitute $n = 1$
Step 2 $u_2 = 2 \times 2 - 1 = 3$ then $2$
Step 3 $u_3 = 2 \times 3 - 1 = 5$ then $3$
Answer The sequence begins $1, 3, 5$ — the odd numbers, as expected

8.2.1 Reading the rule backwards

An explicit rule is an equation, and an equation can be read in either direction. Forwards it turns a position into a term. Backwards it answers a different and more interesting question: is this number in the sequence at all, and if so, where?

Take $137$. Is it an odd number — and if it is, which one? Solve $t_n = 137$:

set $2n - 1 = 137$
so $2n = 138$
and $n = 69$
