The Challenge Zone
Think, then apply, then challenge.

Not one of these says which idea to use. Show every step of the reasoning.

  1. Think Let $M$ be the midpoint of $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$. Using the distance formula and nothing else, show that $AM = MB = \tfrac{1}{2}AB$. Then say which step would fail if $M$ were the point one third of the way from $A$ to $B$.
  2. Apply Find every point of the y-axis lying at distance $10$ from $(6, 3)$, and explain from the algebra why there are exactly two. For which points $(a, b)$ would there be exactly one?
  3. Apply $A(1, 1)$, $B(7, 1)$ and $C(7, 9)$ are three corners of a rectangle. Find the fourth, then prove by computing two midpoints that the diagonals of this rectangle bisect each other.
  4. Challenge Three villages stand at $A(0, 0)$, $B(8, 0)$ and $C(0, 6)$, in kilometres (Fig. 1.10). A water tank is to be built at a point equidistant from all three. Find it, give that common distance, and say what the point turns out to be with respect to $BC$.
  5. Challenge A point $P(x, y)$ moves so that its distance from $A(-3, 0)$ is always twice its distance from $B(3, 0)$. Show that $P$ satisfies $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 9 = 0$, and hence that $P$ travels on a circle. Give its centre and radius.

    Square both distances before doing anything else, then complete the square in $x$ — the move from Chapter 4.
