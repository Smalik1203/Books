Off the axes: the four quadrants

So far every point we have named has sat on an axis. Most points do not. What can we say about the coordinates of a point out in the open?

The flat surface the axes lie in is the Cartesian plane — also called the coordinate plane, or the xy-plane. The two axes cut it into four parts, called quadrants, numbered anticlockwise from the top right as in Fig. 1.4.

Keeping to the convention that right and up are positive:

  1. Quadrant I — both coordinates positive.
  2. Quadrant II — x-coordinate negative, y-coordinate positive.
  3. Quadrant III — both coordinates negative.
  4. Quadrant IV — x-coordinate positive, y-coordinate negative.

So the quadrant a point lands in is decided entirely by the pair of signs it carries, before you look at the sizes of the numbers at all.
