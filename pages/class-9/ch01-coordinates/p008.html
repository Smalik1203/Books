Now the general statement. The coordinates of a point $P$ in 2-D space are written $(x, y)$, where $x$ is the perpendicular distance of $P$ from the y-axis, measured along the x-axis, and $y$ is the perpendicular distance of $P$ from the x-axis, measured along the y-axis. We call $x$ the x-coordinate and $y$ the y-coordinate of the point $(x, y)$.

Try it on Fig. 1.4. The point $S (3, -5)$ lies in Quadrant IV, with x-coordinate 3 and y-coordinate $-5$; the point $Q (-5, 3)$ lies in Quadrant II, with x-coordinate $-5$ and y-coordinate 3. The same two numbers, swapped — and a different place entirely.
