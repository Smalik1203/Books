Two equations, two unknowns, and both contain $x^2$ and $y^2$. Subtract the second from the first. The $y^2$ goes at once, and $x^2 - (x - 6)^2 = 12x - 36$, so $12x - 36 = 0$ and $x = 3$. Then $y^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$, so $y = 4$ or $y = -4$. There are two such points, $(3, 4)$ and $(3, -4)$. The first is the point $P$ of the first question, and that is no accident: $P$ is $5$ from both $O$ and $(6, 0)$, so both of them are $5$ from $P$. Notice that the squares cancelled. When two distances from a point are given, subtracting the squared equations removes $x^2$ and $y^2$ together, and what is left is an equation of the kind you solved in Grade 8.

Problem 1 · Try it first

The points $A (2, 4)$, $B (5, 8)$, $C (9, 5)$ and $D (6, 1)$ are joined in that order (Fig. 1B.3). $ABCD$ is

  1. a square
  2. a rhombus that is not a square
  3. a rectangle that is not a square
  4. a parallelogram with no right angle
Solution 1

A square has four equal sides and a right angle. Both can be checked with distances, and the squares of the distances are enough:

$AB^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 25$,   $BC^2 = 4^2 + 3^2 = 25$,   $CD^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 25$,   $DA^2 = 4^2 + 3^2 = 25$

Four equal sides, so $ABCD$ is at least a rhombus. That is where option (b) stops. For the angle at $B$, take the diagonal: $AC^2 = 7^2 + 1^2 = 50$, and $AB^2 + BC^2 = 50$ too. So the angle at $B$ is a right angle, and a rhombus with a right angle is a square: option (a). Option (c) is what you get by checking the right angle and not the sides. Option (d) is a parallelogram checked by its opposite sides alone.
