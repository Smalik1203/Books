In an assertion–reason question, answer from this list every time: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

B6 · Competitive-Style Thinking
  1. Assertion. The distance between $(a, b)$ and $(b, a)$ is $\sqrt{2}\,|a - b|$.  Reason. The two points differ by $a - b$ in each coordinate, with opposite signs.
  2. Assertion. A point equidistant from the two axes must lie on the line through the origin at $45^\circ$ rising to the right.  Reason. The distance of $(x, y)$ from the x-axis is $|y|$ and from the y-axis is $|x|$.
  3. For the point $P(-4, 0)$, which of the following hold?
    1. $P$ lies in Quadrant II.
    2. $P$ lies on the x-axis.
    3. $P$ is $4$ units from the origin.
    Choose: (a) (i) only   (b) (ii) only   (c) (ii) and (iii)   (d) all three.
  4. $A$ is the origin and $B$ is $(6, 0)$. A third point $C$ lies on the y-axis with $BC = 10$. Find every possible $C$, and the area of triangle $ABC$ in each case.
  5. A student tabulates four points' distances from the origin. One is wrong: find it, name the mistake, and give the right value.
    Point$(3, 4)$$(-6, 8)$$(5, 12)$$(9, 12)$
    Distance$5$$14$$13$$15$
  6. A drone starts at $(2, 3)$, flies straight to $(14, 8)$, then straight to $(14, -4)$, all distances in kilometres.
    1. How far has it flown?
    2. How far is it now from where it began, and why is that not the same number?
  7. $ABCD$ has vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 1)$, $C(4, 5)$, $D(1, 5)$. Which one of these is correct?
    1. a square of area $12$
    2. a rectangle with diagonals $5$ long
    3. a rectangle of perimeter $12$
    4. a parallelogram, but no rectangle
