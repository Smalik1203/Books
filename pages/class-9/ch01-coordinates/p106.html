Type 4 · A distance with an unknown coordinate

Example 6

The distance between $(a, 2)$ and $(7, 6)$ is $5$. Then $a$ is

  1. $4$ only
  2. $10$ only
  3. $4$ or $10$
  4. $3$ or $11$

Solution.

Step 1 $(7 - a)^2 + (6 - 2)^2 = 5^2$ distance formula, squared
Step 2 $(7 - a)^2 = 25 - 16 = 9$
Step 3 $7 - a = 3$ or $7 - a = -3$ $9$ has two square roots
Step 4 $a = 4$ or $a = 10$
Answer (c)

The two points are $(4, 2)$ and $(10, 2)$, one on each side of $(7, 2)$ and $3$ units from it. Options (a) and (b) each keep one sign of the square root and drop the other. Option (d) takes the shift in $x$ to be $4$: $7 - a = 4$ or $7 - a = -4$ gives $a = 3$ or $a = 11$. It is the shift in $y$ that is $4$.

Type 5 · A point the same distance from two or three points

Example 7

The point of the y-axis that is equidistant from $A(-5, 2)$ and $B(3, 4)$ is

  1. $(0, -1)$
  2. $(0, 1)$
  3. $(0, 3)$
  4. $(-1, 0)$

Solution.

Step 1 the point is $(0, y)$, and equal distances have equal squares a point of the y-axis
Step 2 $(0 + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = (0 - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2$
Step 3 $29 - 4y = 25 - 8y$ $y^2$ cancels
Step 4 $4y = -4$, so $y = -1$
Answer (a)
Check $(0, -1)$ is $\sqrt{25 + 9} = \sqrt{34}$ from $A$ and $\sqrt{9 + 25} = \sqrt{34}$ from $B$

Option (d) is $1$ unit along the wrong axis. Option (c) is the height half way between $A$ and $B$, which would be the answer only if the two were the same distance from the y-axis. They are not: $A$ is $5$ away and $B$ is $3$. Option (b) is $\sqrt{25 + 1} = \sqrt{26}$ from $A$ but $\sqrt{9 + 9} = \sqrt{18}$ from $B$.
