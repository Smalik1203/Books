Solution 3

The distance of $(x, y)$ from the origin is $\sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$. To compare four of them there is no need for a single square root: the larger distance has the larger square.

$7^2 + 1^2 = 50$,   $5^2 + 5^2 = 50$,   $(-6)^2 + 4^2 = 52$,   $0^2 + (-7)^2 = 49$

The largest is $52$, so the farthest point is $(-6, 4)$: option (c). Option (d) has the largest single coordinate, but its other coordinate is $0$ and adds nothing. Options (a) and (b) are equally far from $O$, which is easy to miss without the squares. And the negative coordinate in (c) makes no difference to the distance, because it is squared.

Problem 4 · Try it first

The point of the y-axis that is equidistant from $A (-5, 2)$ and $B (3, 4)$ is

  1. $(0, -1)$
  2. $(0, 1)$
  3. $(0, 3)$
  4. $(-1, 0)$
Solution 4

A point of the y-axis is $(0, y)$. Equidistant means the two distances are equal, and then their squares are equal too:

$(0 + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = (0 - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2$

Expand both sides: $25 + y^2 - 4y + 4 = 9 + y^2 - 8y + 16$. The $y^2$ cancels, as it did in stage 1, and $29 - 4y = 25 - 8y$ gives $4y = -4$, so $y = -1$. The point is $(0, -1)$: option (a). Check: its distance from $A$ is $\sqrt{25 + 9}$ and from $B$ is $\sqrt{9 + 25}$, both $\sqrt{34}$. Option (d) is $1$ unit along the wrong axis. Option (c) is the height half way between $A$ and $B$, which is where the point would be if $A$ and $B$ were the same distance from the y-axis. They are not: $A$ is $5$ away and $B$ is $3$.
