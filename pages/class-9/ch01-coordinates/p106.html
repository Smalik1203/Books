Problem 4

The point of the y-axis that is equidistant from $A (-5, 2)$ and $B (3, 4)$ is

  1. $(0, -1)$
  2. $(0, 1)$
  3. $(0, 3)$
  4. $(-1, 0)$
Solution

A point of the y-axis is $(0, y)$. Equidistant means the two distances are equal, and then their squares are equal too:

$(0 + 5)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = (0 - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2$

$29 - 4y = 25 - 8y$

$4y = -4$, so $y = -1$

The $y^2$ cancels, as it did in stage 1. The point is $(0, -1)$: option (a), and its distance from each of $A$ and $B$ is $\sqrt{34}$. Option (d) is $1$ unit along the wrong axis. Option (c) is the height half way between $A$ and $B$, which would be the answer only if the two were the same distance from the y-axis. They are not: $A$ is $5$ away and $B$ is $3$.

Problem 5

The number of points with whole-number coordinates that are exactly $5$ units from the origin is

  1. $4$
  2. $8$
  3. $12$
  4. $16$
Solution

A point $(x, y)$ is $5$ from the origin when $x^2 + y^2 = 25$, so the question is in how many ways $25$ is a sum of two squares.

$25 = 25 + 0 \rightarrow (\pm 5, 0), (0, \pm 5)$ — $4$ points

$25 = 9 + 16 \rightarrow (\pm 3, \pm 4), (\pm 4, \pm 3)$ — $8$ points

Twelve in all, option (c), shown in Fig. 1B.4. No other pair of squares adds to $25$. Option (a) counts only the points on the axes. Option (b) forgets those four. Option (d) would allow $(\pm 5, \pm 5)$, which are $\sqrt{50}$ from the origin, not $5$.
