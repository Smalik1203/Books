A reasoning question has no working to show. It wants the sentence that would convince someone who disagreed — so answer in words, and kill a false claim with one counter-example rather than an argument.

B2 · Think and Reason
  1. The chapter says you never need to decide which of two points to call the first.
    1. Explain why, from the formula rather than from an example.
    2. Which step would break if the squares were replaced by the shifts themselves?
  2. Predict where $(3, -5)$ goes when its two coordinates are swapped, and which quadrant it lands in. Then describe, in one sentence, the move that carries every point $(x, y)$ to $(y, x)$, and name the set of points that do not move at all.
  3. Multiply both coordinates of a point by $-1$.
    1. What has happened to it, geometrically?
    2. Explain, from the formula, why its distance from the origin cannot change — and why its distance from $(2, 2)$ generally does.
  4. For a segment parallel to an axis the chapter offers two rules: a difference of coordinates, and the full distance formula. Show that they never disagree, and say exactly what the formula does with the coordinate that is not changing.
  5. A student writes: the distance of $(x, y)$ from the origin is $x + y$.
    1. Find four points for which the claim happens to be correct.
    2. State the condition on $x$ and $y$ that describes every such point, and show that no other point can satisfy it.
  6. Fig. 1.1 records Reiaan's floor and nothing else. Name two quantities about the room that the map fixes exactly, and two it cannot fix at all — then say what a second map would have to record to supply one of them.
  7. The quadrants are numbered anticlockwise from the top right.
    1. Say what would change if the convention ran clockwise instead, and what would not change at all.
    2. Is the numbering a fact about the plane, or an agreement between people? Name one other thing in this chapter that is the same kind of thing.
