8
The Trap Room
Two students, one question, and a mistake.

Answer (a) A only, (b) B only, (c) both, (d) neither — then say in one line what went wrong.

  1. How far is it from $(2,5)$ to $(7,9)$? A. $\sqrt{41}$, by the formula.  B. $9$ — five across and four up.
  2. A. $(-3,-3)$ is in Quadrant IV, since both coordinates are negative.  B. It is in Quadrant III.
  3. A. $(0,5)$ is in no quadrant at all.  B. It is in Quadrant I, since neither coordinate is negative.
  4. The midpoint of $(2,6)$ and $(8,10)$ is A. $(3,2)$, halving each difference.  B. $(10,16)$, adding each pair.
  5. A. The distance from $(5,0)$ to $(1,0)$ is $4$: a square root returns the size, not the sign.  B. It is $-4$, since $\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2} = x_2 - x_1$.
  6. A. $P(3,4)$ and $Q(4,3)$ are the same point — the same two numbers built them.  B. They coincide only when the two numbers are equal.
  7. For $A(1,2)$, $B(3,6)$, $C(4,8)$: A. The three lie in a straight line.  B. $AB \neq BC$.
  8. A. Doubling both coordinates of a point doubles its distance from the origin.  B. Doubling just the $x$-coordinate doubles it too.
  9. Two points are $10$ apart and one of them is $(0,0)$. A. The other is $(6,8)$.  B. The other cannot be named from what is given.
  10. A. The distance from $(x,y)$ to the $x$-axis is $y$.  B. It is $|y|$.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Measuring the wrong journey (1, 5, 10). Adding the shifts, or keeping a sign, answers a question nobody asked. A distance is one number and never negative.
  2. Trusting a half-remembered convention (2, 3). The quadrant order and the meaning of a zero coordinate are agreements. Check them; do not reconstruct them.
  3. Answering before the point is fixed (4, 6, 7, 8, 9). $(x,y)$ is an ordered pair, a midpoint is an average not a difference, three points in a row need not be evenly spaced, and one distance fixes a circle rather than a point.
