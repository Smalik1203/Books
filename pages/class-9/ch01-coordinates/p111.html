  1. Set A 1 (d)   2 (a)   3 (b)   4 (c)   5 (b)   6 (b)   7 (a)   8 (b)   9 (c)   10 (b)
  2. Set B 1 (a)   2 (d)   3 (c)   4 (c)   5 (a)   6 (b)   7 (c)   8 (b)   9 (c)
  3. Set C 1 (b)   2 (a)   3 (b)   4 (c)   5 (a)   6 (d)

Three things ran underneath all three sets, and they are worth naming now that the answers are in front of you.

The first is that the distance formula is not a new fact. It is Pythagoras, applied to a right triangle whose two short sides run along the grid lines. Every question here that looked like geometry — the rectangle, the parallelogram, the water tank — was answered by that one triangle, drawn in a different place each time.

The second is that signs settle the quadrant before sizes are looked at. Set C question 1 is decided entirely by which of $x$ and $y$ has been negated; their values never enter it.

The third is why $25$ and $625$ kept appearing. A distance is a whole number only when a square splits into two squares, and $625$ splits three ways — $25^2 + 0^2$, $7^2 + 24^2$ and $15^2 + 20^2$. Numbers that behave like this are rare, which is why the same few keep turning up.
