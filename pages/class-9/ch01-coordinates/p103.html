Connect the Concepts
Two results of the chapter, one after the other.

Name both results as you go.

  1. $M(2,-3)$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $A$ is $(-1,4)$. The distance $AB$ is
    1. $\sqrt{58}$
    2. $2\sqrt{58}$
    3. $\sqrt{116}$
    4. $29$
  2. A circle centred at the origin passes through $(9,12)$. The point $(-15,0)$ lies
    1. inside it
    2. on it
    3. outside it
    4. at the centre
  3. Points with whole-number coordinates at distance $13$ from the origin number
    1. $4$
    2. $8$
    3. $12$
    4. $16$
  4. $(1,5)$, $(2,8)$, $(3,11)$ and $(4,14)$ come from a pattern with a constant difference. Using distances alone, they are
    1. on one line, evenly spaced
    2. on one line, unevenly spaced
    3. not on one line
    4. on one line only if the first is dropped
  5. In the triangle $A(0,0)$, $B(6,0)$, $C(6,8)$, the median from $A$ has length
    1. $5$
    2. $\sqrt{52}$
    3. $8$
    4. $10$
  6. Two circular icons, of radii $80$ and $100$ pixels, sit at $(100,150)$ and $(250,230)$. They
    1. overlap
    2. touch exactly
    3. do not meet
    4. cannot be decided without a picture
  7. The distance between two grid points is a whole number exactly when the two shifts satisfy one condition. State it, then give three pairs of shifts that satisfy it, none a multiple of $(3, 4)$.
  8. On a globe, two places one degree of longitude apart lie about $111$ km apart at the equator and about $28$ km apart at $75^\circ$ north. Say what that shows about reading a coordinate difference as a distance.
