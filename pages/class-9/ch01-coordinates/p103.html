A point is $5$ units from the origin and also $5$ units from $(6, 0)$. Find it.

Call the point $(x, y)$. Both distances are given, so both can be squared:

$x^2 + y^2 = 25$

$(x - 6)^2 + y^2 = 25$

Two equations, two unknowns, and both contain $x^2$ and $y^2$. Subtract the second from the first. The $y^2$ goes at once, and $x^2 - (x - 6)^2 = 12x - 36$, so $12x - 36 = 0$ and $x = 3$. Then $y^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$, so $y = 4$ or $y = -4$. There are two such points, $(3, 4)$ and $(3, -4)$. The first is the point $P$ of the first question, and that is no accident: $P$ is $5$ from both $O$ and $(6, 0)$, so both of them are $5$ from $P$. Notice that the squares cancelled. When two distances from a point are given, subtracting the squared equations removes $x^2$ and $y^2$ together, and what is left is an equation of the kind you solved in Class 8.

None of these questions used anything outside the chapter. What each needed was the question: what can I use from what I already know? The examples that follow ask it again.

Example 1 · Single correct

For which values of $k$ does the point $(k - 3, 5)$ lie in Quadrant II?

Choose one correct option.

  1. $k \lt 3$
  2. $k \gt 3$
  3. $k = 3$
  4. $k \lt 5$

Solution.

Step 1Quadrant II needs $x \lt 0$ and $y \gt 0$; 5 is positive, so $k - 3 \lt 0$, that is $k \lt 3$.
Answer(a) $k \lt 3$

What is the distance between $P(-3, 7)$ and $Q(5, -8)$?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 13
  2. 17
  3. 23
  4. 15

Solution.

Step 1The shifts are 8 across and 15 down, so the distance is $\sqrt{64 + 225} = \sqrt{289} = 17$.
Answer(b) 17
