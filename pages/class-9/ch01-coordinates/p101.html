Using What You Know
The chapter's results, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter ended with one formula and a few facts about the plane. Two numbers fix a point. The signs of the two numbers fix its quadrant, and a zero puts it on an axis. And the distance between $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$ is $\sqrt{(x_2 - x_1)^2 + (y_2 - y_1)^2}$. Every question below can be answered with those alone. What is new is that the question no longer says which one to use. Try each before reading on.

Find every point of the x-axis that is $5$ units from $P (3, 4)$.

A point of the x-axis has the form $(x, 0)$. That is the chapter's first fact, and it turns the question into algebra. The distance from $(x, 0)$ to $(3, 4)$ is $5$, so

$$(x - 3)^2 + (0 - 4)^2 = 25$$

We squared both sides at once, and the root is gone. Now $(x - 3)^2 = 25 - 16 = 9$, so $x - 3 = 3$ or $x - 3 = -3$. That gives $x = 6$ or $x = 0$. The two points are $(6, 0)$ and the origin itself (Fig. 1B.1). Notice that we never drew the picture. We wrote the unknown point in the form the axis gives it, and the formula did the rest. Notice also that there were two answers. A square root has two signs, and a question that gives one distance usually leaves two points open.
