End-of-Chapter Exercises

Unless stated otherwise, use the approximation $\pi \approx \dfrac{22}{7}$.

  1. Identities in algebra can sometimes be shown as statements about area. The figure below (Fig. 6.41) shows the identity $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. Do you see how? Draw figures for the identities $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$ and $(a + b + c)^2 = a^2 + b^2 + c^2 + 2ab + 2bc + 2ca$.
  1. An isosceles triangle has perimeter $40$ cm, and each of its equal sides is $15$ cm. Find the area of the triangle.
  1. An isosceles triangle has base $10$ cm and area $60$ cm$^2$. How long is each of its equal sides?
  1. A right-angled triangle has area $54$ cm$^2$, and one of the sides that make the right angle is $12$ cm long. Find its perimeter.
  1. The sides of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 4$, and its perimeter is $45$ cm. Find its area.
  1. The sides of a triangle are $7$ cm, $24$ cm and $25$ cm. Find its area in two different ways.
  1. The wheel of a bicycle has diameter $60$ cm. How far has the cyclist travelled when the wheel has turned $100$ times?
  1. Find the area of a quadrant of a circle whose circumference is $66$ cm.
  1. The wheel of a car has an outer radius of $28$ cm. How far does the car travel while the wheel turns once? How many times does the wheel turn during a journey of $1$ km?
  1. Two rectangles have the same area and the same perimeter. Does this mean that they are congruent?
