Do you see why the two triangles fit together to make a parallelogram? Look at the angles in the figure, for example $\angle B'C'A'$ and $\angle BCA$, and you will see why. Keep in mind the test for two lines to be parallel.

We already know that the area of a parallelogram is base $\times$ height $= bh$. The triangle is half of the parallelogram, so its area is $\frac{1}{2}$(base $\times$ height) $= \frac{1}{2}bh$.

Area of a triangle

The area of a triangle is $\frac{1}{2} \times$ base $\times$ height, $\frac{1}{2}bh$, where the height $h$ is the perpendicular distance from the opposite vertex to the line of the base.

A property of the median of a triangle

From this formula we reach a simple but beautiful fact about the medians of a triangle. A median is the line segment joining a vertex of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side. In Fig. 6.22, $AD$ is a median of $\triangle ABC$.

Look at $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$. Their bases are equal, since $BD = DC = a$, and they have the same height $h$. So by the formula for the area of a triangle, both have area $\dfrac{ah}{2}$ sq. units! We state this as a theorem.

Theorem

A median of a triangle divides it into two triangles of equal area.

Does this surprise you? It should! In general, $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$ have different shapes — they are not congruent — and yet we have just proved that they have the same area.
