You may find this formula surprising, and strange to look at! Let us test it on some triangles whose areas we can already find.

Example 3

Test Heron’s formula on an equilateral triangle with side $a$ units.

Solution.

Step 1 $s = \frac{1}{2}(a + a + a) = \frac{3}{2}a$, so $s - a = \frac{1}{2}a$ for each side semi-perimeter
Step 2 area $= \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}a \times \frac{1}{2}a \times \frac{1}{2}a \times \frac{1}{2}a} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$ Heron’s formula
Step 3 height $h$: $a^2 - h^2 = \frac{a^2}{4}$, $\therefore h = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a$ Baudhāyana–Pythagoras
Step 4 $\frac{1}{2} \times a \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}a = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$ half base times height
Answer Both ways give the same area, $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4}a^2$ sq. units.

Look at the symbol $\therefore$ in Step 3. Mathematicians use it all the time. It is read “therefore”.

Example 4

Test Heron’s formula on an isosceles triangle whose equal sides are $a$ units and whose base is $2b$ units.

Solution.

Step 1 $s = \frac{1}{2}(a + a + 2b) = a + b$ semi-perimeter
Step 2 area $= \sqrt{(a + b) \times b \times b \times (a - b)} = b\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ Heron’s formula
Step 3 height $h$: $a^2 = h^2 + b^2$, $\therefore h = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ Baudhāyana–Pythagoras
Step 4 $\frac{1}{2} \times \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} \times 2b = b\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ half base times height
Answer Both ways give $b\sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ sq. units.
