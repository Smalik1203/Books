Now take two runners, one in the innermost lane and the other in the second lane. On the straights they run the same distance. But on the curved ends the runner in the second lane runs further, because her semicircles have a larger radius. The stagger is there to make up for this.

6.5Problems, Puzzles and Paradoxes on Perimeter

We close this part of the chapter with a few interesting problems about perimeter.

Example 1

Two circles of equal radius are placed so that each circle passes through the centre of the other (Fig. 6.12). The radius of each circle is $r$ units. Find the perimeter of the shape formed by the two circles, in terms of $r$. (Leave out the dotted arcs that lie inside the circles.)

The circles are centred at $A$ and $B$ and cross at $C$ and $D$. We need the total length of the two solid arcs.

Solution.

Step 1 $AB = AC = BC = r$, so triangle $ABC$ is equilateral and $\angle CAB = 60^\circ = \angle CBA$ all sides are radii
Step 2 in the same way $\angle BAD = 60^\circ = \angle ABD$, so $\angle CAD = 120^\circ = \angle CBD$
Step 3 so each dotted arc is $\dfrac{120^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{1}{3}$ of its circle, and each solid arc is $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of its circle
Step 4 total length $= 2 \times \dfrac{2}{3} \times 2\pi r$
Answer The perimeter is $\dfrac{8}{3}\pi r$ units.
