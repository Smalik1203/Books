This gives a way to argue that the area of the circle equals the area of a parallelogram:

area of the circle $=$ area of the parallelogram $=$ base $\times$ height $= \pi r \times r = \pi r^2$.

Area of a circle

A circle of radius $r$ encloses an area $A = \pi r^2$.

6.10.1 Area of a sector of a circle

A sector of a circle is the region bounded by an arc and the two radii to the ends of the arc (Fig. 6.38).

We can find the area of a sector in the same way as we found the length of an arc. Look at Fig. 6.39 and Fig. 6.40.

By symmetry, the area of a semicircular disc is $\dfrac{180^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ of the area of the circle, that is, $\frac{1}{2}\pi r^2$. We can see this from the reflection symmetry of the circle in the diameter $AB$.

By symmetry again, the area of a quarter circular disc is $\dfrac{90^\circ}{360^\circ} = \dfrac{1}{4}$ of the area of the circle, that is, $\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2$. We can see this from the quarter-turn symmetry of the circle.

From these we get at once the formula for any sector.
