Example 5

Test Heron’s formula on a triangle with sides $3$ units, $4$ units and $5$ units.

Solution.

Step 1 $s = \frac{1}{2}(3 + 4 + 5) = 6$ semi-perimeter
Step 2 area $= \sqrt{6 \times (6 - 3) \times (6 - 4) \times (6 - 5)} = \sqrt{6 \times 3 \times 2} = 6$ Heron’s formula
Step 3 $3^2 + 4^2 = 5^2$, so $\angle B = 90^\circ$ converse of Baudhāyana–Pythagoras
Step 4 base $= 3$, height $= 4$, area $= \frac{1}{2}(3 \times 4) = 6$ half base times height
Answer Both ways give $6$ sq. units.

The formula gives the right answer every time. You may wonder how Heron’s formula is proved. Many proofs are known. One uses the Baudhāyana–Pythagoras theorem and the difference of two squares, $a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)$, several times over. You will study some of these proofs in Class 10.

There are two more formulas like this for the area of a triangle, and both are connected with circles (Fig. 6.26). Through the three vertices of any triangle $ABC$ there passes exactly one circle, called the circumcircle of $\triangle ABC$. There is also exactly one circle that fits tightly inside the triangle, touching all three sides. It is called the incircle of $\triangle ABC$.
