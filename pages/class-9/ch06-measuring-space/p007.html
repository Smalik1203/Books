What is the length of a semicircle of the same radius $r$ (Fig. 6.8)?

Reflect the circle in the diameter $AB$. The upper semicircle and the lower semicircle change places, so they have equal length. Each is half the circumference: $2\pi r \div 2 = \pi r$.

Instead of reflecting the figure in $AB$, we can turn the whole figure about the centre $O$ through $180^\circ$, a half-turn. The effect is the same, and we get the same length for the semicircular arc.

What is the length of a quarter circle of the same radius (Fig. 6.9)?

Turn the whole figure through $90^\circ$, clockwise or anticlockwise. Each quarter circle moves onto the next one. So all four quarter circles have the same length, and each is $2\pi r \div 4 = \dfrac{\pi r}{2}$.

We can write both results in another way:

the length of a semicircle is $2\pi r \times \dfrac{180^\circ}{360^\circ}$;

the length of a quarter circle is $2\pi r \times \dfrac{90^\circ}{360^\circ}$.

From these we can guess the length of any arc of a circle from the angle it makes at the centre — the angle it subtends at the centre.
