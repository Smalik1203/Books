6.9Squaring a Rectangle

In ancient times, to square a shape meant to construct a square with the same area as the shape. The shape could be a rectangle, a triangle or a circle. Here we show how the ancient Indian mathematician Baudhāyana squared a rectangle. His construction, from his Śulbasūtra ($800$ BCE), squares a rectangle with sides $a$ units and $b$ units, where $a \gt b$. So we must construct a square of area $ab$ sq. units. Here is a slightly simplified form of the construction (Fig. 6.30).

Given: the rectangle $ABCD$ with $AD = a$ and $AB = b$, where $a \gt b$.

Mark $E$ on $AD$ so that $AE = AB$.

Mark the midpoint $F$ of $ED$.

Draw the square $AFGH$ with side $AF$ and with its vertex $H$ on $AB$ produced.

Draw the arc $AG$ with centre $H$. Let the arc cut the side $BC$ at $K$.

Draw a line through $K$ parallel to $AH$. Let it cut $GH$ at $P$.

Draw the square $HPQS$ with side $HP$.

Then the square $HPQS$ has the same area as the rectangle $ABCD$.

Try to work out why this method works. You will find that it is the geometry of the formula

$$\left(\frac{a + b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\frac{a - b}{2}\right)^2 = ab.$$
