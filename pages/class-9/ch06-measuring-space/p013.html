Area of a parallelogram

The area of a parallelogram is base $\times$ height, $bh$, where the height $h$ is the perpendicular distance between the base and the side opposite it.

What happens if the parallelogram is “thin”, as in Fig. 6.18, and the foot of the perpendicular from $C$ to $AD$ does not lie on the side $AD$? The construction then does not seem to work. How do we close this gap?

Here is a hint for the thin parallelogram. Choose a point $D'$ on $DA$, close to $D$, and a point $A'$ on $DA$ extended beyond $A$, with $A'A = D'D$ (Fig. 6.19). Then $A'BCD'$ is a parallelogram. Since $\triangle CDD' \cong \triangle BAA'$, its area is the same as the area of $ABCD$. Now repeat this step as many times as you need.
