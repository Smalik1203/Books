Chapter summary
  1. $\pi$ is the ratio of the circumference to the diameter, the same for every circle. It is approximately $\frac{22}{7}$ or $3.14$.
  2. The circumference of a circle of radius $r$ is $C = 2\pi r$.
  3. An arc that subtends an angle $\theta^\circ$ at the centre has length $l = 2\pi r \times \dfrac{\theta^\circ}{360^\circ}$.
  4. The area of a triangle is $A = \frac{1}{2} \times$ base $\times$ height.
  5. Heron’s formula gives the area of a triangle from its sides $a$, $b$, $c$: area $= \sqrt{s(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)}$, where $s = \frac{1}{2}(a + b + c)$.
  6. The area of a circle is $A = \pi r^2$.
  7. Estimates for $\pi$: Archimedes, $3\frac{10}{71} \lt \pi \lt 3\frac{1}{7}$; Zu Chongzhi, $\pi \approx \frac{355}{113}$; Āryabhaṭa, $\pi \approx 3.1416$. Mādhava’s exact formula: $\pi = 4\left(1 - \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{5} - \frac{1}{7} + \cdots\right)$.
  8. $\pi$ is an irrational number.
  9. The area of a sector with angle $\theta^\circ$ at the centre is $\pi r^2 \times \dfrac{\theta^\circ}{360^\circ}$.
  10. Brahmagupta’s formula gives the area of a cyclic quadrilateral from its sides $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$: area $= \sqrt{(s - a)(s - b)(s - c)(s - d)}$, where $s = \frac{1}{2}(a + b + c + d)$.
