6.3$\pi$ Is Irrational

The digits of $\pi$ go on for ever, with no pattern that anyone can see. You already know that fractions give decimal expansions with a repeating pattern, for example

$\frac{1}{3} = 0.33333\ldots, \quad \frac{1}{11} = 0.090909\ldots, \quad \frac{1}{7} = 0.142857\,142857\ldots$

But $\pi$ has no such pattern. In fact, $\pi$ cannot be written as a ratio of two integers. Numbers like this are called irrational. Remember that a rational number is a number that can be written as a fraction $\frac{a}{b}$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers and $b$ is not $0$ — for example $\frac{1}{3}$, $-\frac{7}{11}$ and $1.4$.

In Class 8 you learnt that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. From their writings, it seems that Āryabhaṭa and Zu Chongzhi thought of $\pi$ as irrational. Centuries later, in $1761$, the mathematician Lambert showed that it is. The proof needs more advanced mathematics, which you will learn much later.

The number $\pi$

For every circle, the ratio of the circumference to the diameter is the same number, called $\pi$. It is an irrational number: $\pi = 3.14159\ldots$, and $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$ or $3.14$.

Since $\pi$ is irrational, there is no “best fraction” for $\pi$: however close a fraction is to $\pi$, there is another fraction that is closer. The fraction $\frac{22}{7}$ is good enough for most practical uses. But $\pi$ is not equal to $\frac{22}{7}$; the two are close but not equal. So we write $\pi \ne \frac{22}{7}$, and $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$. In the same way, $\sqrt{2} \ne 1.414$, and $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$. As you saw earlier, a much better approximation for $\pi$ is $\frac{355}{113}$.

A way to remember the first few digits of $\pi$: How I wish I could recollect pi. Count the letters in each word — $3, 1, 4, 1, 5, 9, 2$ — and you have the digits of $\pi = 3.141592\ldots$ Because $\pi \approx 3.14$, 14 March (written 3-14 in North America) is celebrated each year as Pi Day; and because $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$, 22 July (written 22-7 in India) is celebrated as Pi Approximation Day.

6.4Length of an Arc of a Circle

The circumference of a circle of diameter $d$ is $\pi d$. The diameter is twice the radius, so we can also write the circumference of a circle as $2\pi r$, where $r$ is the radius.

Circumference of a circle

A circle of radius $r$ and diameter $d$ has circumference $C = \pi d = 2\pi r$.
