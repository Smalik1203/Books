Using What You Know

The chapter gave you formulas for the length of a circle and of an arc, for the areas of triangles, parallelograms, circles and sectors, and the formulas of Heron and Brahmagupta. Every question below can be answered with those. What is new is that the question does not say which formula to use, and sometimes the formula it seems to need is the wrong one. Try each question before reading on.

A piece of wire is bent into a square of side $22$ cm. The same wire is then bent into a circle. Which shape encloses more area, and by how much? (Use $\pi \approx \frac{22}{7}$.)

Bending the wire does not change its length, so the perimeter is the same both times: $4 \times 22 = 88$ cm. As a circle, $2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r = 88$, so $r = 14$ cm. The square encloses $22^2 = 484$ cm$^2$ and the circle encloses $\frac{22}{7} \times 14^2 = 616$ cm$^2$. The circle encloses $616 - 484 = 132$ cm$^2$ more. Section 6.10 explains why. For a square the ratio $P^2 : A$ is $16 : 1$, and for a circle $C^2 : A$ is $4\pi : 1$, about $12.6 : 1$. With the same perimeter, the shape with the smaller ratio encloses the larger area.

A sector of a circle of radius $10$ cm has perimeter $35$ cm. Find its area, without finding its angle.

The perimeter of a sector is its arc and two radii, so the arc is $35 - 2 \times 10 = 15$ cm. The arc is some fraction of the circumference, and the sector is the same fraction of the area of the circle, because both fractions are $\frac{\theta}{360}$. So

$$\text{area} = \frac{15}{2\pi \times 10} \times \pi \times 10^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 10 = 75 \text{ cm}^2.$$

The $\pi$ cancels, and the angle is never needed: the area of a sector is half its arc times its radius. That is Archimedes’ rule for the whole circle, half the circumference times the radius, used on a slice.
