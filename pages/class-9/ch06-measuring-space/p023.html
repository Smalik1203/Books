Why does this method work?

Look again at Fig. 6.30.

Step 1 $AE = b$, so $AF = \dfrac{AE + AD}{2} = \dfrac{a + b}{2}$ $F$ is the midpoint of $ED$
Step 2 $HG = \dfrac{a + b}{2} = HK$ $HK$ is a radius of the arc
Step 3 $BH = AH - AB = AF - AB = \dfrac{a + b}{2} - b = \dfrac{a - b}{2}$
Step 4 in the right-angled $\triangle HKP$, $KP = BH$, so $HP^2 = HK^2 - BH^2 =\left(\dfrac{a + b}{2}\right)^2 - \left(\dfrac{a - b}{2}\right)^2$ Baudhāyana–Pythagoras theorem
Step 5 $HP^2 = \dfrac{a^2 + 2ab + b^2}{4} - \dfrac{a^2 - 2ab + b^2}{4} = \dfrac{ab}{2} + \dfrac{ab}{2} = ab$
Answer The square $HPQS$ has area $HP^2 = ab$, the same as the rectangle $ABCD$.
