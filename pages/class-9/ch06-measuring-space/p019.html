$\text{area}(\triangle ABC) = \dfrac{abc}{4R}$

$\text{area}(\triangle ABC) = \dfrac{r(a + b + c)}{2}$

Both formulas have a beautiful symmetry! The proof of the second one needs a result that you will study in Class 10.

Brahmagupta’s formula for the area of a cyclic 4-gon

We have seen how to find the area of a triangle from the lengths of its three sides. The natural next question is: how can we find the area of a 4-gon from the lengths of its four sides?

Earlier we asked whether we can find the area of a parallelogram when we know only the lengths of its sides. The answer is no. In the same way, can we find the area of a 4-gon when we know only the lengths of its sides? Fig. 6.27 gives the answer. It shows a 4-gon whose sides are $3$, $3$, $3$, $3$ — a rhombus — drawn three ways, and the three areas are different. (The figures were drawn in GeoGebra and their areas found with its Area tool. Try this yourself, or with four rods joined at their ends.)

So we cannot find the area of a 4-gon from the lengths of its four sides alone; we need more information. It could be one angle of the 4-gon, or the length of one diagonal, or the angle at which the diagonals cross, and so on. Or it could be a key geometric property of the figure.

One such property is being a cyclic 4-gon: a 4-gon whose four vertices lie on one circle. In $628$ CE, Brahmagupta ($598$–$668$ CE) discovered a wonderful formula for the area of a cyclic 4-gon.
