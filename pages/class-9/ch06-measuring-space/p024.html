1. $ABCD$ is a parallelogram, and $P$ and $Q$ are any two points on the side $AB$. What can you say about the ratio area$(\triangle PCD)$ : area$(\triangle QCD)$?
  1. $O$ is any point on the diagonal $PR$ of a parallelogram $PQRS$. Prove that the triangles $PSO$ and $PQO$ have equal areas.
  1. The midpoints of the sides of a 4-gon are joined in order. (A 4-gon is also called a quadrilateral, but we prefer to call it a 4-gon.) Prove that the area of the parallelogram formed is half the area of the 4-gon.

    You may wonder whether the 4-gon formed is always a parallelogram, and if so, why. These questions are answered in the chapter on quadrilaterals.

  1. In $\triangle ABC$, the midpoint of $BC$ is $D$ (Fig. 6.32). The median $AD$ is drawn, and $P$ is any point on $AD$. Show that area$(\triangle ABP)$ = area$(\triangle ACP)$.
  1. $ABCD$ is a square and $P$ is a point inside it. Join $PA$, $PB$, $PC$ and $PD$ (Fig. 6.33). What is the ratio of the area of the shaded region, made of $\triangle PAB$ and $\triangle PCD$, to the area of the unshaded region, made of $\triangle PBC$ and $\triangle PDA$?
  1. In $\triangle ABC$, $D$ is the midpoint of $AB$. $P$ is any point on $BC$, and $Q$ is the point on $AB$ for which $CQ \parallel PD$. $PQ$ is joined (Fig. 6.34). Prove that area$(\triangle BPQ) = \frac{1}{2}$ area$(\triangle ABC)$.
