6.6Area of a Rectangle

From perimeter, we move to area: the amount of space taken up by a flat region of the plane.

Every measurement is made against something we call a unit. For area, the unit is a square of side $1$; its area is taken to be $1$ unit$^2$, also written $1$ sq. unit. You know from Class 8 that a rectangle with sides $a$ units and $b$ units has area $ab$ sq. units (Fig. 6.16).

Area of a rectangle

A rectangle with sides $a$ units and $b$ units has area $ab$ sq. units. A square of side $a$ units has area $a^2$ sq. units.

6.7Area of a Parallelogram

You also know from Class 8 the formula for the area of a parallelogram.

Fig. 6.17 shows how to turn a parallelogram $ABCD$ into a rectangle $EB'C'F$ with the same base $b$ and the same height $h$. (On the right, $A'B'C'D'$ is a copy of $ABCD$.) Cut off the triangle $EB'A'$ at one end and move it to the other end, where it fits exactly as $FC'D'$. The two shapes are different, but their areas are the same. So the area of the parallelogram is base $\times$ height $= bh$.
