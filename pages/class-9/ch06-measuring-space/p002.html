A rectangle with length $a$ units and width $b$ units has perimeter $2(a + b)$ units. The formula for the perimeter of a square is a special case of this one: put $b = a$, and $2(a + b)$ becomes $4a$.

To answer the question about the perimeter of a circle, we go one step at a time, starting with shapes we already know.

What happens to the perimeter of a square if we double its side? It doubles too. A square of side $2$ units has perimeter $8$ units, and a square of side $4$ units has perimeter $16$ units (Fig. 6.4). The ratio of perimeter to side is $4 : 1$, and it is $4 : 1$ for every square. Make the square larger or smaller, and the ratio of perimeter to side stays fixed at $4 : 1$.
