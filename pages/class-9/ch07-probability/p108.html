Example 6

Cards numbered 1 to 25 are shuffled, and one is drawn at random. The probability that its number is prime is

  1. $\tfrac{8}{25}$
  2. $\tfrac{9}{25}$
  3. $\tfrac25$
  4. $\tfrac{9}{24}$

Solution.

Step 1 the primes up to 25 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19 and 23 list them
Step 2 9 favourable cards out of 25
Step 3 $P(\text{prime}) = \dfrac{9}{25}$ favourable over possible
Answer (b)

Option (a) leaves out 2, the one even prime. Option (c) is $\tfrac{10}{25}$, which counts 1 as a prime; it is not. Option (d) divides by 24, as if the card numbered 1 were not in the pack.

Example 7

A jar holds 24 sweets, some red and the rest green. The probability of picking a red one is $\tfrac58$. How many green sweets must be added to make the probability of a red one $\tfrac12$?

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac58 \times 24 = 15$ red sweets the formula read backwards
Step 2 $24 - 15 = 9$ green sweets
Step 3 a probability of $\tfrac12$ needs as many green as red: 15 green an even chance
Step 4 $15 - 9 = 6$
Answer 6 green sweets must be added
Check 15 red of $24 + 6 = 30$ sweets, and $\dfrac{15}{30} = \dfrac12$
