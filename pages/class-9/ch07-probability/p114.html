Assertion and reason

In Questions 19 to 22, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): When a fair die is rolled, the probability of a prime number is $\tfrac12$.

    Reason (R): The primes on a die are 2, 3 and 5, three of the six faces.

  1. Assertion (A): The experimental probability of an event can change from one set of trials to the next.

    Reason (R): The theoretical probability of an event depends on how many trials are made.

  1. Assertion (A): The probability of picking a vowel from the letters of the word MATHS is $\tfrac15$.

    Reason (R): A probability always lies between 0 and 1.

  1. Assertion (A): An event with probability $0.5$ must happen exactly once in every two trials.

    Reason (R): A probability of $0.5$ means an even chance.
