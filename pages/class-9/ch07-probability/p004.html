A probability outside that range is not unlikely — it is meaningless, in the way a length of $-3$ metres is meaningless. If a calculation ever hands you one, the calculation is wrong, and it is worth stopping to find out where.

Reading the scale

It is worth walking along the line once with real events on it, because the five names are easy to say and easy to misapply.

impossible a number greater than 6 on a die — the faces stop at 6
less likely rolling a 3 — possible, but only one face in six
even chance a coin landing heads — the two faces have nothing to choose between them
more likely drawing a card numbered 2 to 10 — 36 of the 52 qualify
certain taking a red sweet from a bag of red sweets

Notice the second and fourth. Neither is settled, and both are often described loosely as "unlikely" and "likely" — but the die gives $\tfrac16$ and the card gives $\tfrac{36}{52}$, which is close to $\tfrac{7}{10}$. Those are very different positions on the line, and the words hide the difference.

Here is a way to watch a probability move. Take a deck of cards in which some are purple and the rest are green, and ask for the probability of drawing a purple one. With no purple cards at all the answer is 0. Add purple cards one at a time and the answer slides steadily to the right, through less likely, through the even chance where the two colours are matched, and on towards certain — which it reaches only when the last green card has gone.

Notice that the scale has no gaps in it. Any number between 0 and 1 can be the probability of something, and by adding purple counters one at a time we could land as close to any point on that bar as we liked. Probability is not a set of five verdicts with nothing in between; those are names for stretches of the scale, useful in conversation and too coarse for anything else.

What the numbers buy us is comparison. Told one event is unlikely and another very unlikely, we know which is rarer but not by how much; told $0.1$ and $0.001$, we know the first happens a hundred times as often.
