7.2Two Ways to Find It

So a probability is a number on a line. That leaves the harder question: given an event, where on the line does it go? There are two ways to answer, and they arrive from opposite directions.

The first is to go and look. Toss the coin two hundred times, keep count, and see what fraction came up heads. This uses evidence — it asks the world what happens, and takes the answer. It is called experimental probability.

The second is to think it through. A coin has two faces and no reason to prefer either, so heads must come up half the time. This uses reasoning rather than evidence, and it is called theoretical probability.
