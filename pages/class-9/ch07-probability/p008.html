Example 3

A letter is picked at random from the word PROBABILITY. What is the probability that it is a vowel? That it is a B?

Write the word out and count, because it is easy to miscount letters that repeat.

P R O B A B I L I T Y

There are 11 letters, so there are 11 equally likely outcomes — each position is as likely to be picked as any other.

vowels O, A, I, I   — four of them
$P(\text{vowel})$ $\dfrac{4}{11}$ 0.36
letter B two of them
$P(\text{B})$ $\dfrac{2}{11}$ 0.18

The repeated letters are the trap. There is one letter B in the alphabet but two Bs in this word, and it is positions we are drawing from, not letters. Counting B once would have given $\tfrac{1}{11}$, and would have been the answer to a question nobody asked.

Count outcomes, not names. Two Bs are two outcomes, however alike they look on the page.

The two answers are worth setting side by side. Four letters out of eleven are vowels and two out of eleven are Bs, so a vowel is twice as likely as a B — and the reason is nothing more than that there are twice as many of them. Every one of the eleven positions is as likely as every other; an event is likelier only because more of those positions belong to it.
