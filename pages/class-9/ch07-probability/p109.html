Type 5 · From a sample to a population

Example 8

A factory tests a random sample of 400 bulbs from a batch of 12000 and finds 14 of them faulty. Estimate how many bulbs in the batch are faulty, and the probability that a bulb picked from the batch works.

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{14}{400} = 0.035$ the sample's rate
Step 2 $0.035 \times 12000 = 420$ rate times batch
Step 3 $1 - 0.035 = 0.965$ the opposite event
Answer about 420 faulty bulbs; the probability that a bulb works is about $0.965$

The sample was random, which is what lets 400 bulbs speak for 12000. Had all 400 come from one machine, the estimate would describe that machine.

Example 9

A school has 1200 students, and you want to estimate how many of them play cricket. The best sample to ask is

  1. the 40 members of the school cricket club
  2. the first 40 students through the gate on sports day
  3. 40 students picked by drawing names from the school list
  4. 400 students, all of them from Class 12

Solution.

Step 1 the club members were chosen by the very thing being measured (a) is sorted already
Step 2 sports day and Class 12 each favour one kind of student (b) and (d) are biased
Step 3 drawing names gives every student the same chance a random sample
Answer (c)

Option (d) is ten times larger, and size does not repair a sample that leaves out every other class.
