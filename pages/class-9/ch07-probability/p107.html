Example 4

A die is rolled 120 times. The faces 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 come up 16, 22, 19, 21, 17 and 25 times. Find the experimental probability of rolling a 1 or a 2, and compare it with the theoretical probability.

Solution.

Step 1 $16 + 22 + 19 + 21 + 17 + 25 = 120$ every roll counted
Step 2 $16 + 22 = 38$ rolls showing 1 or 2
Step 3 $\dfrac{38}{120} = \dfrac{19}{60} \approx 0.317$ experimental
Step 4 $\dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac13 \approx 0.333$ two faces of six
Answer experimental $\tfrac{19}{60} \approx 0.317$, theoretical $\tfrac13 \approx 0.333$, about $0.02$ apart

A gap of two hundredths in 120 rolls is no evidence against the die. The law of large numbers says the gap should shrink as the rolls go on, not that it should already be zero.

Example 5

A letter is picked at random from the word ASSESSMENT. Find the probability that it is an S, and the probability that it is a vowel.

Solution.

Step 1 A S S E S S M E N T: 10 letters, so 10 equally likely positions count positions
Step 2 the letter S is in 4 positions, so $P(\text{S}) = \dfrac{4}{10} = \dfrac25$
Step 3 the vowels A, E, E are in 3 positions, so $P(\text{vowel}) = \dfrac{3}{10}$
Answer $P(\text{S}) = \tfrac25$ and $P(\text{vowel}) = \tfrac{3}{10}$

Counting the different letters instead — A, S, E, M, N and T, six of them — would give $\tfrac16$ for an S. That is the chance of an S only if each letter appeared once.
