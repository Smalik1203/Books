Type 6 · What many trials do

Example 10

Asha rolls a fair die 30 times and gets 9 sixes. Ravi rolls the same die 600 times and gets 104 sixes. Find each experimental probability of a six, and say which you would expect to be nearer $\tfrac16$.

Solution.

Step 1 Asha: $\dfrac{9}{30} = 0.3$
Step 2 Ravi: $\dfrac{104}{600} \approx 0.173$
Step 3 $\tfrac16 \approx 0.167$, so Asha is about $0.13$ away and Ravi about $0.007$ away the theoretical value
Answer $0.3$ and about $0.173$; Ravi's, from twenty times as many rolls, is nearer $\tfrac16$

That is what the law of large numbers leads you to expect. Asha's result is not a sign that the die is unfair: thirty rolls are too few for the settling to have happened.

Type 7 · Events as sets

Example 11

A spinner has ten equal sectors numbered 1 to 10. Let $A$ be the event "a multiple of 3" and $B$ the event "a number greater than 6". Write each event as a set, and find the probabilities of $A$, of $B$, of "$A$ and $B$", and of "$A$ or $B$".

Solution.

Step 1 $A = \{3, 6, 9\}$, so $P(A) = \tfrac{3}{10}$
Step 2 $B = \{7, 8, 9, 10\}$, so $P(B) = \tfrac{4}{10} = \tfrac25$
Step 3 in both lists: $\{9\}$, so $P(A \text{ and } B) = \tfrac{1}{10}$ and: in both
Step 4 in either list: $\{3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10\}$, so $P(A \text{ or } B) = \tfrac{6}{10} = \tfrac35$ or: in at least one
Answer $\tfrac{3}{10}$, $\tfrac25$, $\tfrac{1}{10}$ and $\tfrac35$

Adding the two counts gives $3 + 4 = 7$, and $\tfrac{7}{10}$ is wrong: 9 is in both lists and has been counted twice. Writing the sets out is what shows it.
