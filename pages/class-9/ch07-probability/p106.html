Example 2

Four events have probabilities $\tfrac25$, $0.55$, $48\%$ and $\tfrac37$. The event most likely to happen is the one with probability

  1. $\tfrac25$
  2. $0.55$
  3. $48\%$
  4. $\tfrac37$

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac25 = 0.4$ one form for all four
Step 2 $48\% = 0.48$
Step 3 $\tfrac37 \approx 0.43$
Step 4 $0.55$ is the largest of $0.4$, $0.55$, $0.48$ and $0.43$ larger is likelier
Answer (b)

Option (c) looks largest because 48 is the biggest number printed, but as a probability it is $0.48$. Option (d) has the largest denominator, which says nothing about its size: $\tfrac37$ is about $0.43$.

Type 2 · Experimental probability

Example 3

A basketball player takes 80 shots in practice and scores with 52 of them. Find the experimental probability that a shot scores. In a match she takes 20 shots. About how many would you expect her to score?

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{52}{80} = 0.65$ scores over shots
Step 2 $0.65 \times 20 = 13$ rate times shots
Answer $P(\text{score}) = 0.65$; about 13 of the 20 shots

About 13, not exactly 13: the $0.65$ was measured on 80 shots, and a match is not a practice session.
