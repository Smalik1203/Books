Example 7

A fair coin is tossed twice. List the sample space, and find the probability of two heads.

The first toss gives H or T. From each of those the second toss gives H or T again, which is two branches from each of two — four paths in all.

Solution.

Step 1 $S = \{\,\text{HH},\ \text{HT},\ \text{TH},\ \text{TT}\,\}$ the tips of the tree
Step 2 $n(S)$ is 4 count the tips
Step 3 two heads is the event $\{\,\text{HH}\,\}$, one outcome, so $\tfrac14$ favourable over possible
Answer $P(\text{HH}) = \tfrac14$

Notice that HT and TH are two different outcomes, not one: heads-then-tails is a different history from tails-then-heads, even though both leave you with one of each.

The same picture answers questions the list alone would not. Reading the four tips, exactly two of them — HT and TH — have one head and one tail, so that event has probability $\tfrac24 = \tfrac12$. Three of the four tips show at least one head, so $\tfrac34$ is the probability of that.

A tree also grows in an orderly way. Toss the coin a third time and every tip sprouts two more, giving eight outcomes; a fourth toss gives sixteen. Two stages of two choices make $2 \times 2$, and three make $2 \times 2 \times 2$. The tree is showing you a multiplication.
