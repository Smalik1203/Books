Think and Reason
Judge a claim — and the reason offered for it.

Questions 1–4 take this key. (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

  1. A. The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre.  R. The centre is equidistant from the chord's two ends, and every such point lies on that bisector.
  2. A. A quadrilateral whose opposite angles add to $180^\circ$ is cyclic.  R. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.
  3. A. A circle can be drawn through any three points.  R. Three points lying in a straight line have no circle through them.
  4. A. A chord subtending $90^\circ$ at the centre has length $r\sqrt{2}$.  R. The two radii to its ends are equal, so the triangle they close is isosceles.
  5. A circle has radius $10$ cm. Which must be true?
    1. Some chord of it is $20$ cm long.
    2. A chord $6$ cm from the centre is $16$ cm long.
    3. No chord of it is longer than $20$ cm.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
  6. $ABCD$ is cyclic with $\angle A = 70^\circ$. Which must be true?
    1. $\angle C = 110^\circ$.
    2. $\angle B = 110^\circ$.
    3. $\angle B = \angle D$.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
  7. Points $P$ and $Q$ lie on the same side of a segment $AB$, and $\angle APB = \angle AQB$. Which must be true?
    1. $A$, $B$, $P$, $Q$ are concyclic.
    2. $PQ$ is parallel to $AB$.
    3. $\angle APB$ is half the angle $AB$ subtends at that circle's centre.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
