Example 3

The angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 7$. Its circumcentre lies

  1. inside the triangle
  2. at the midpoint of a side
  3. outside the triangle
  4. at a vertex

Solution.

Step 1 $2k + 3k + 7k = 180^\circ$, so $k = 15^\circ$ angle sum
Step 2 the angles are $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $105^\circ$
Step 3 the largest angle is more than $90^\circ$, so the triangle is obtuse-angled
Step 4 the circumcentre of an obtuse-angled triangle lies outside it Section 5.4
Answer (c)

Option (b) is the right-angled case, which needs an angle of exactly $90^\circ$. Option (a) is what the two small angles suggest on their own. Option (d) is never right: a vertex is at distance $0$ from itself, and not from the other two vertices.

Type 2 · Equal chords and the angles at the centre

Example 4

$AB$ and $CD$ are equal chords of a circle with centre $O$, and $\angle AOB = 70^\circ$. Then $\angle OCD$ is

  1. $35^\circ$
  2. $55^\circ$
  3. $70^\circ$
  4. $110^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle COD = \angle AOB = 70^\circ$ Theorem 2
Step 2 $OC = OD$, so $\angle OCD = \angle ODC$ radii, so isosceles
Step 3 $2\,\angle OCD = 180^\circ - 70^\circ = 110^\circ$ angle sum of $\triangle OCD$
Step 4 $\angle OCD = 55^\circ$
Answer (b)

Option (a) halves $70^\circ$, as if the angle at $O$ were an angle at a point of the circle. Option (c) is the angle at the centre itself, and (d) is the two base angles together.
