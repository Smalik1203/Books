Questions 1 and 2 take this key. (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

  1. A. The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre.  R. The centre is equidistant from the two ends of the chord, and every point equidistant from them lies on the perpendicular bisector.
  2. A. A chord subtending $90^\circ$ at the centre of a circle of radius $r$ has length $r\sqrt{2}$.  R. The two radii to its ends are equal, so the triangle they make with the chord is isosceles.
  3. A circle has radius $10$ cm. Which of these must be true?
    1. Some chord of it is $20$ cm long.
    2. A chord $6$ cm from the centre is $16$ cm long.
    3. No chord of it is longer than $20$ cm.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
