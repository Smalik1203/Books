Problem 5 · Try it first

Two circles of radii $10$ cm and $17$ cm have centres $A$ and $B$ that are $21$ cm apart. They cross at $P$ and $Q$ (Fig. 5B.4). The length of $PQ$ is

  1. $8$ cm
  2. $15$ cm
  3. $16$ cm
  4. $20$ cm
Solution 5

$PQ$ is a chord of both circles. $A$ is the centre of one, so $A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$ (Exercise Set 5.4). So does $B$. Two points fix a line, so $AB$ is that perpendicular bisector, and it meets $PQ$ at its midpoint $M$. Let $AM = x$, so that $MB = 21 - x$. The half-chord $PM$ is a leg of two right-angled triangles:

$PM^2 = 10^2 - x^2 = 17^2 - (21 - x)^2$

So $100 - x^2 = 289 - 441 + 42x - x^2$, which gives $42x = 252$ and $x = 6$. Then $PM^2 = 100 - 36 = 64$, $PM = 8$ and $PQ = 16$ cm, option (c). Check from the other side: $289 - 225 = 64$.

Option (a) is the half-chord. Option (b) is $MB$. Option (d) is the diameter of the smaller circle, its longest chord, and $PQ$ does not pass through $A$, so it must be shorter. Notice that the two circles were never treated as two. One chord, two centres, and the relation $r^2 = d^2 + (\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{chord})^2$ written twice.
