Example 11

$AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is a point on the circle, with $\angle ABC = 35^\circ$. Find $\angle BAC$.

Nothing is said about the angle at $C$ — and nothing needs to be, because $AB$ is a diameter, and the corollary fixes that angle wherever on the circle $C$ happens to sit.

angle at C the angle in a semicircle $90^\circ$
angle at B given $35^\circ$

The three angles of $\triangle ABC$ add to $180^\circ$, so

$\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$

The corollary did all the work: without it, one angle and a diameter would not determine the triangle at all.
