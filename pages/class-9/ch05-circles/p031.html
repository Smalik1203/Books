What this chapter established
  1. A circle is the locus of points of a plane lying at a fixed distance, the radius, from a fixed point, the centre.
  2. A circle has complete rotational symmetry about its centre, and reflection symmetry in every diameter — and in no other line.
  3. Infinitely many circles pass through two given points, with their centres strung along the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining them. Through three points that are not collinear there passes exactly one.
  4. The segment from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it, and the perpendicular from the centre bisects it. Of the three facts — from the centre, through the midpoint, at a right angle — any two force the third.
  5. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre and lie at equal distances from it, and each of those statements holds in reverse. A chord at distance $d$ has length $2\sqrt{r^{2}-d^{2}}$, which settles all of it at once, including that the longer of two chords is the nearer.
  6. An arc subtends at the centre twice the angle it subtends at any point of the circle outside it. So every point of the far arc sees a chord under the same angle, and a diameter is seen at a right angle from everywhere on the circle.
  7. Four points are concyclic exactly when a segment joining two of them subtends equal angles at the other two on the same side — equivalently, when opposite angles of the quadrilateral they form add to $180^\circ$.
  8. Measuring confirms; only an argument explains. More than once in this chapter the measurements were right and still proved nothing at all.
The results, in one place
  1. There is exactly one circle through three non-collinear points.
  2. Equal chords subtend equal angles at the centre.
  3. Chords subtending equal angles at the centre are equal.
  4. The centre joined to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.
  5. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects it.
  6. Chords of equal length are equidistant from the centre.
  7. Chords equidistant from the centre are of equal length.
  8. Of two unequal chords, the longer lies nearer the centre.
  9. An arc subtends at the centre twice the angle it subtends at a point of the circle outside the arc.
  10. If a segment subtends equal angles at two points on the same side of it, the four points are concyclic.
  11. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.
  12. If opposite angles of a quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$, it is cyclic.
