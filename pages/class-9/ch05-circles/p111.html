Example 13

Which of these quadrilaterals must have its four vertices on one circle?

  1. a kite
  2. a quadrilateral with angles $100^\circ$, $80^\circ$, $100^\circ$, $80^\circ$ in order round it
  3. a trapezium whose two non-parallel sides are equal
  4. a quadrilateral with two right angles next to each other

Solution.

Step 1 in (c), let $AB \parallel DC$ and $AD = BC$, and drop the perpendiculars $DP$ and $CQ$ onto $AB$; then $DP = CQ$ the gap between parallels
Step 2 $\triangle APD \cong \triangle BQC$, so $\angle A = \angle B$ RHS
Step 3 $\angle A + \angle D = 180^\circ$ co-interior, $AB \parallel DC$
Step 4 $\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ$, so $ABCD$ is cyclic Step 2, then Theorem 12
Answer (c)

Option (b) is chosen by adding neighbours, $100^\circ + 80^\circ$; its opposite angles make $200^\circ$. A kite is cyclic only when the two equal angles between its unequal sides are right angles, so (a) need not be. In (d) the right angles are neighbours and say nothing about an opposite pair; such a quadrilateral is cyclic only when it is a rectangle.
