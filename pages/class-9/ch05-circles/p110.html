Type 6 · Concyclic points and cyclic quadrilaterals

Example 11

$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral in which $AB$ is a diameter and $\angle ADC = 130^\circ$. Then $\angle CAB$ equals

  1. $40^\circ$
  2. $50^\circ$
  3. $65^\circ$
  4. $80^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$ Theorem 11
Step 2 $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$ angle in a semicircle
Step 3 $\angle CAB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 50^\circ = 40^\circ$ angle sum of $\triangle ABC$
Answer (a)

Option (b) stops at $\angle ABC$. Option (c) halves $130^\circ$ as if it were an angle at the centre, but $D$ is a point of the circle. Option (d) is $130^\circ - 50^\circ$, a difference of two angles that share no triangle. A given that names a diameter is nearly always a given that names a right angle somewhere.

Example 12

In a quadrilateral $ABCD$, the vertices $A$ and $D$ lie on the same side of $BC$, $\angle BAC = \angle BDC = 35^\circ$ and $\angle ABD = 50^\circ$. Find $\angle ACD$.

Solution.

Step 1 the segment $BC$ subtends equal angles, $35^\circ$ each, at $A$ and $D$, which lie on the same side of it given
Step 2 so $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ are concyclic Theorem 10
Step 3 $\angle ACD = \angle ABD$ same segment, chord $AD$
Answer $\angle ACD = 50^\circ$

Nothing in the question mentions a circle. Theorem 10 put one there, and after that the angles in the same segment did the rest.
