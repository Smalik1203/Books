Selected solutions

B5.1 hint Find each chord's distance from the centre first — the question has not said which side.
solution $d_1 = \sqrt{100 - 64} = 6$ and $d_2 = \sqrt{100 - 36} = 8$. Opposite sides give $14$ cm; the same side gives $2$ cm.
key idea A figure the question has not fixed has two answers. Give both.
B7.4 hint $PQ$ is a chord of both circles, and the line of centres crosses it at right angles.
solution $d_1 + d_2 = 21$ and $10^2 - d_1^2 = 17^2 - d_2^2$ give $d_2 - d_1 = 9$, so $d_1 = 6$, the half-chord is $8$, and $PQ = 16$ cm.
key idea One length, read as a chord of two circles at once, gives two equations.
Foundation takeaways
  1. $c = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$ is the whole of Sections 5.6 and 5.7.
  2. Two equal angles on the same side of a segment mean a circle is present. Draw it.
  3. Read what a question has not said: which side, which arc.
  4. A right angle is one case of the angle-in-a-segment locus, not a separate theorem.
  5. Measuring confirms; only an argument explains.
