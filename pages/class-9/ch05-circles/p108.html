Solution

$PQ$ is a chord of both circles. $A$ is the centre of one, so $A$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $PQ$ (Exercise Set 5.4). So does $B$. Two points fix a line, so $AB$ is that perpendicular bisector, and it meets $PQ$ at its midpoint $M$. Let $AM = x$, so that $MB = 21 - x$. The half-chord $PM$ is a leg of two right-angled triangles:

$PM^2 = 10^2 - x^2 = 17^2 - (21 - x)^2$

$100 - x^2 = 289 - 441 + 42x - x^2$

$42x = 252$, so $x = 6$

$PM^2 = 100 - 36 = 64$, so $PM = 8$

$PQ = 2\,PM = 16$

$PQ = 16$ cm, option (c); from the other side $289 - 225 = 64$. Option (a) is the half-chord, option (b) is $MB$, and option (d) is the diameter of the smaller circle, which $PQ$ cannot reach since it does not pass through $A$. The two circles were never treated as two: one chord, two centres, and $r^2 = d^2 + (\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{chord})^2$ written twice.
