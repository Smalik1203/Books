B8 · The Trap Room
  1. "Every chord is a diameter — both join two points of the circle." Correct the statement, give the one extra condition a chord must meet to be a diameter, and say which of the two words is the more general.
  2. "A chord of $21$ cm can certainly be drawn in a circle of radius $10$ cm: the circumference is over $60$ cm, so there is room." Say what the reasoning confuses with what, and give the largest chord there is.
  3. In a cyclic quadrilateral $ABCD$ with $\angle A = 95^\circ$, a student writes $\angle B = 85^\circ$. Show why this need not be true, and say which angle $85^\circ$ actually belongs to.
  4. Asked how far apart two parallel chords of lengths $16$ cm and $12$ cm are in a circle of radius $10$ cm, a student answers $2$ cm. The arithmetic is right. What has been left out, and what is the other answer?
  5. "Angles in the same segment are equal, so a chord subtends the same angle at a point of the minor arc as at a point of the major arc." Test it on an arc subtending $110^\circ$ at the centre, and say which three words of the theorem were dropped.
  6. "A point $10$ cm from the centre of a circle of radius $10$ cm lies inside it, since $10$ is not more than $10$." Decide, quote the sentence of Section 5.2 that settles it, and say why the definition leaves no room for a near miss.
  7. "Since $c = 2\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$, doubling $d$ halves $c$." Disprove it with $r = 10$, $d = 3$ and $d = 6$, and say what kind of relationship the formula actually describes.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Weakening a definition until it fits (traps 1, 6). A chord through the centre is a diameter and no other chord is; a point at distance exactly $r$ is on the circle and neither in nor out. The definition allows no degrees.
  2. Quoting a theorem without its condition (traps 3, 5). Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral, and points on the same arc. Drop either qualifier and the statement is false in half of all cases.
  3. Answering before the figure is fixed (traps 2, 4, 7). Two chords may lie either side of the centre or both on one side; a formula with a square root in it is not a proportion. Ask what the question has not said before writing one answer down.
