Why the other options are wrong

B2 Both statements are true, so (c) and (d) are out. But equal radii give only an isosceles triangle. The length $r\sqrt{2}$ needs the right angle at the centre and the Baudhāyana–Pythagoras theorem, and R mentions neither. So (b).
C2 Call the angles $x$, $2x$, $3x$ and $kx$. The first and third are opposite, so $4x = 180^\circ$ and $x = 45^\circ$. Then $2x + kx = 180^\circ$ gives $90 + 45k = 180$, so $k = 2$. Option (b) comes from pairing the second angle with the third, which are neighbours and not opposites.
C3 $A$ is on the major arc $BC$, so $\angle BOC = 2 \times 40^\circ = 80^\circ$. Equal chords $AB$ and $AC$ subtend equal angles at the centre, and the three angles at $O$ add to $360^\circ$, so $\angle AOB = \tfrac{1}{2}(360^\circ - 80^\circ) = 140^\circ$. Option (a) is $\angle BOC$, and (d) is twice it.
C5 A $30$ cm chord lies $\sqrt{17^2 - 15^2} = 8$ cm from the centre. A line through $P$ is at most $OP = 8$ cm from $O$, and exactly $8$ cm only when it is at right angles to $OP$. So there is one such chord, option (b). Option (c) expects a second one on the other side, but every other chord through $P$ is nearer the centre and so longer.
