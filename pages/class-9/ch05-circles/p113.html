Example 15

$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $AB \parallel DC$. Prove that $AD = BC$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle A + \angle D = 180^\circ$ co-interior, $AB \parallel DC$
Step 2 $\angle B + \angle D = 180^\circ$ Theorem 11
Step 3 $\angle A = \angle B$ Steps 1 and 2
Step 4 drop the perpendiculars $DP$ and $CQ$ onto the line $AB$; then $DP = CQ$ the gap between parallels
Step 5 $\triangle APD \cong \triangle BQC$ AAS, from Steps 3 and 4
Answer $AD = BC$, as corresponding sides of congruent triangles

Example 13 went the other way, from equal sides to a circle. Together they say that a trapezium is cyclic exactly when its two non-parallel sides are equal.

Example 16

Two circles cross at $A$ and $B$, as in Fig. 5B.6. $AC$ is a diameter of one circle and $AD$ is a diameter of the other. Prove that $C$, $B$ and $D$ lie on one straight line.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$ angle in a semicircle
Step 2 $\angle ABD = 90^\circ$ angle in a semicircle
Step 3 $\angle ABC + \angle ABD = 180^\circ$ adding
Step 4 $BC$ and $BD$ lie on opposite sides of $AB$, and the two angles make a straight angle a linear pair, read backwards
Answer $C$, $B$ and $D$ lie on one straight line

Neither centre was used. Each diameter was there only to give a right angle at $B$.
