Solution 1

Let the $24$ cm chord lie $d_1$ from the centre and the $10$ cm chord $d_2$. Theorem 8 says the longer chord is the nearer, so $d_2 - d_1 = 7$. The perpendicular from the centre bisects each chord, and the same radius serves both:

$r^2 = d_1^2 + 12^2 = d_2^2 + 5^2$,  so  $d_2^2 - d_1^2 = 119$

Now $d_2^2 - d_1^2 = (d_2 - d_1)(d_2 + d_1) = 7\,(d_2 + d_1)$, so $d_2 + d_1 = 17$. Together with $d_2 - d_1 = 7$ this gives $d_2 = 12$ and $d_1 = 5$. Then $r^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$ and $r = 13$ cm, option (b). Check the other chord: $144 + 25 = 169$ as well.

Option (d) is chosen by a reader who sees two unknown distances and stops. Two unknowns need two equations, and the second one is the chapter's own: one circle, one radius, for both chords. Option (a) is $d_2$, the distance of the shorter chord. Option (c) is $d_1 + d_2$, which is not a length in the figure at all.

Problem 2 · Try it first

$ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral in which $AB$ is a diameter and $\angle ADC = 130^\circ$. Then $\angle CAB$ equals

  1. $40^\circ$
  2. $50^\circ$
  3. $65^\circ$
  4. $80^\circ$
Solution 2

Two facts are given, and each belongs to a different result. $\angle ADC$ is opposite $\angle ABC$, so by Theorem 11

$\angle ABC = 180^\circ - 130^\circ = 50^\circ$

$AB$ is a diameter and $C$ is a point of the circle, so the angle in a semicircle gives $\angle ACB = 90^\circ$. Triangle $ABC$ now has two known angles, and $\angle CAB = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 50^\circ = 40^\circ$, option (a).

Option (b) stops at $\angle ABC$. Option (c) halves $130^\circ$, as if it were an angle at the centre, but $D$ is a point of the circle and not the centre. Option (d) is $130^\circ - 50^\circ$, a difference of two angles that share no triangle.
