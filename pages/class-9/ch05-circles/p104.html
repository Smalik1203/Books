Type 1 · Symmetry, and the circles through given points

Example 1

A circle is drawn together with one of its diameters, $AB$. The number of lines of symmetry of the whole figure is

  1. $1$
  2. $2$
  3. $4$
  4. infinitely many

Solution.

Step 1 a line of symmetry of the figure must carry the circle onto itself, so it passes through the centre Section 5.3
Step 2 it must also carry $AB$ onto itself, so it is either the line $AB$ or the line through the centre at right angles to $AB$ the diameter stays put
Step 3 both lines work: a fold along either one lands the circle and $AB$ on themselves
Answer (b)

Option (d) is the circle's own count, before the diameter was drawn. Option (a) counts the line $AB$ and misses the fold that swaps $A$ and $B$. Option (c) is the count for a square.

Example 2

Two points $A$ and $B$ are $16$ cm apart. How many circles of radius $17$ cm pass through both, and how far is each centre from the segment $AB$?

Solution.

Step 1 a centre $O$ has $OA = OB$, so it lies on the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ Section 5.4
Step 2 the bisector meets $AB$ at its midpoint $M$, so $AM = 8$ cm and $\angle OMA = 90^\circ$
Step 3 $OM^2 = 17^2 - 8^2 = 289 - 64 = 225$, so $OM = 15$ cm Pythagoras in $\triangle OMA$
Step 4 there is one such point on each side of $AB$ the bisector crosses $AB$
Answer Two circles, each with its centre $15$ cm from $AB$

For a radius larger than the smallest possible one, the circles through two points always come in such a pair, one centre on each side of the segment.
