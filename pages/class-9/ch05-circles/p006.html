5.4How Many Circles?

In Section 5.2 the word locus was introduced and then left alone: a circle is the locus of points at a given distance from a given point. Change the condition and you get a different locus. Here is the change worth making.

Instead of one fixed point, take two. Let $A$ and $B$ be points of the plane, and ask a question not about points but about circles: how many circles pass through both of them?

At least one does. If a circle passes through $A$ and $B$, its centre $O$ must satisfy $OA = OB$ — and one point obviously does, the midpoint of $AB$. Take that as the centre, with radius half of $AB$, and the circle drawn passes through both. For that circle, $AB$ is a

diameter.

Are there others? Notice that the question has quietly turned back into a question about points: which points of the plane are equidistant from $A$ and $B$? You met the answer in an earlier grade. They are the points of the perpendicular bisector of $AB$ — and there are infinitely many of them.
