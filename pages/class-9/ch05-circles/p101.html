Using What You Know
The chapter's results, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter ended with twelve results, and every question below can be answered with those alone. What is new is that the question no longer says which result to use. Often it does not even mention the thing the result is about. Try each one before reading on.

A chord $AB$ of a circle of radius $8$ cm subtends an angle of $30^\circ$ at a point $P$ of the major arc. Find the length of $AB$.

The angle given is at $P$, and no result in the chapter finds a chord from an angle at a point of the circle. But Theorem 9 connects the angle at $P$ with the angle at the centre $O$:

$$\angle AOB = 2\,\angle APB = 60^\circ$$

Now the picture is the one in Example 5. $OA$ and $OB$ are radii, so triangle $OAB$ is isosceles with its apex angle $60^\circ$, and the two base angles are $60^\circ$ each. All three angles are equal, so all three sides are equal, and $AB = 8$ cm. Notice that the question never mentioned the centre. We put it in ourselves, because the results of the chapter are all about the centre. An angle on the circle became an angle at the centre, and there the chapter could work.
