Example 13

A quadrilateral has angles $85^\circ$, $100^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $80^\circ$, taken in order round the shape. Can a circle be drawn through all four vertices?

Theorem 12 is the test, and it is a test on opposite pairs — so the angles have to be paired first and second with third and fourth, not as they are listed.

Solution.

Step 1 first and third: $85^\circ + 95^\circ$, which is $180^\circ$ an opposite pair
Step 2 second and fourth: $100^\circ + 80^\circ$, which is $180^\circ$ the other pair
Answer Yes. Both pairs come to $180^\circ$, so the four vertices are concyclic and the circle can be drawn. Theorem 12

In fact only one pair ever needs checking: all four angles total $360^\circ$, so if one opposite pair makes $180^\circ$ the other pair has no choice but to do the same.

It is worth being clear about which theorem did the work. Theorem 12 is not the one that says a cyclic quadrilateral has supplementary opposite angles; it is the converse, running the other way, from the angles to the circle. Both are true here, but only one of them answers a question about four points that are not yet known to lie on a circle.
