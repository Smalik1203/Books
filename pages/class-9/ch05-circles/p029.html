End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. A chord of a circle lies $5$ cm from the centre, and the radius is $13$ cm. How long is the chord?
  1. A circle has diameter $26$ cm, and a chord of length $24$ cm is drawn in it. How far is that chord from the centre?
  1. A chord of length $16$ cm lies $6$ cm from the centre. Find the radius.
  1. Prove that the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of the circle.
  1. $AB$ is a diameter of a circle and $C$ is another point on it. What is $\angle ACB$? Give the reasoning, not just the number.
  1. A cyclic quadrilateral has $\angle A = 75^\circ$ and $\angle B = 110^\circ$. Find $\angle C$ and $\angle D$.
  1. A cyclic quadrilateral has sides $5$, $5$, $12$, $12$ units, in that order round the shape. Find its area.
  1. Two parallel chords, of lengths $10$ cm and $24$ cm, lie on the same side of the centre and $7$ cm apart. Find the radius of the circle.
  1. Draw a circle in which a chord of length $6$ cm stands $3$ cm from the centre. What must the radius be?
  1. Justify the claim that no chord of a circle is longer than a diameter. Give two arguments, one of them from Section 5.7.
  1. Take all the chords of a circle that have one fixed length. What shape do their midpoints trace out?
  1. Two chords $AB$ and $AC$ of a circle have the same length. Explain why the centre must lie on the bisector of $\angle BAC$.
  1. Let $A$ be a point inside a circle with centre $O$. Show that the shortest chord through $A$ is the one perpendicular to $OA$.
