What to take away
  1. A question about a chord or an angle on the circle is usually answered at the centre. Join the ends to the centre, and the radii give an isosceles triangle before any theorem is needed.
  2. One circle means one radius. Two chords in the same circle share $r^2 = d^2 + (\tfrac{1}{2}\,\text{chord})^2$, and that is a second equation whenever a question seems one fact short.
  3. Opposite angles, not neighbours, decide whether four points lie on one circle. And a right angle at two points on the same side of a segment puts both on the circle that has the segment as a diameter.
