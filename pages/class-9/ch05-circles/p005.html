So $O$ lies on $\ell$. Every crease of this kind is a line through the centre — a diameter. Two different creases are two different lines through $O$, and two different lines meet at no more than one point. They meet at $O$. That is why Jamuna’s construction cannot fail, and why her third fold was never going to tell her anything.

Lines of symmetry of a circle

A line is a line of reflection symmetry of a circle exactly when it passes through the centre. The lines of symmetry of a circle are its diameters — and there are infinitely many of them.

Infinitely many is a startling number of lines of symmetry, and it is worth comparing with what other shapes manage. An isosceles triangle has one. A rectangle that is not a square has two, a square four, a regular pentagon five, and a regular polygon of $n$ sides exactly $n$. Every one of those totals is a whole number you could count on your fingers if you had enough of them.

The circle is not the end of that sequence so much as a different kind of answer. No bounded figure other than a circle has more than finitely many lines of symmetry. That is one way of saying what makes the circle the shape it is, and why it turns up wherever a direction is not supposed to matter.
