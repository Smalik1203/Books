For an acute-angled triangle the circumcentre lies inside — Fig. 5.4 is one such. For an obtuse-angled triangle it lies outside the triangle altogether. And for a right-angled triangle it lands exactly on the midpoint of the hypotenuse, which is another way of saying that the hypotenuse is a diameter of the circumcircle.

That last case is not a coincidence, and it is not proved here. Section 5.8 will hand it to you in a line.

  1. What is the least possible radius of a circle passing through two points $8$ cm apart?
