Example 5

A chord of a circle of radius $9$ cm subtends $60^\circ$ at the centre. How long is the chord? And if a second chord of the same circle is also $9$ cm long, what angle does it subtend?

Join the ends of the chord to the centre. The two radii and the chord make a triangle, and here it is better than isosceles.

Solution.

Step 1 two sides: both are radii, so $9$ cm each isosceles
Step 2 apex angle: $60^\circ$, at the centre given
Step 3 the apex takes $60^\circ$, so the remaining $120^\circ$ splits into $60^\circ$ each equal base angles
Step 4 all three angles equal makes all three sides equal equilateral
Step 5 the other chord is the same length, so it subtends the same angle Theorem 2
Answer The chord is $9$ cm — exactly the radius. The second chord also subtends $60^\circ$.

The second part needed no calculation.

5.6Chords, Midpoints and Perpendiculars

Section 5.5 took the triangle made by a chord and the two radii to its ends, and got two theorems out of it. That triangle has more to give. It is isosceles — which is what Exercise Set 5.3 asked you to show — and an isosceles triangle is symmetric about the line through its apex and the midpoint of its base.

Here the apex is the centre. So draw a circle with centre $C$, take any chord $AB$, and let $M$ be the midpoint of $AB$. What can be said about the segment $CM$?
