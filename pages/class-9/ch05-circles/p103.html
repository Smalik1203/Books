A circle is a statement about distance, so it reaches into every chapter that measures one. Each of these questions borrows from a chapter you have met, or from outside the book altogether.

B3 · Connect the Concepts
  1. Chapter 1 measured distance from coordinates. A circle has centre $O(2, 3)$ and passes through $A(7, 15)$.
    1. Find its radius.
    2. Decide, with reasons, whether $B(-3, -6)$ and $C(15, 10)$ lie inside it, on it, or outside it.
  2. Chapter 4 gives $r^2 - d^2 = (r - d)(r + d)$.
    1. Use it to find the chord lying $99$ cm from the centre of a circle of radius $101$ cm, without squaring either number.
    2. Say when this route is worth taking, and when squaring is the shorter road after all.
  3. Chapter 3 sorted numbers into ratios and non-ratios. A circle has radius $10$ cm.
    1. Give a chord of it whose length is a whole number of centimetres, and one whose length is not.
    2. Is there a chord whose length and whose distance from the centre are both whole numbers? Find one, or say why not.
  4. Chapter 2 called a relationship linear when equal steps in one quantity make equal steps in the other. Tabulate the chord length $c = 2\sqrt{100 - d^2}$ for $d = 0, 2, 4, 6, 8$. Is the relationship linear? Say what the differences do, and what that means for a chord sliding outwards at a steady speed.
  5. A carpenter has a broken circular plate and only a fragment of the rim. Across the fragment he measures a chord of $24$ cm, and the rim rises $8$ cm above the middle of that chord. Find the radius of the whole plate.

    Draw the full circle, the chord and the diameter through its midpoint. One right-angled triangle does it.

  6. A cycle wheel is chalked at one point of its rim. After the wheel has rolled a little way the chalk mark is somewhere else, and the wheel looks identical. Say which symmetry of Section 5.3 that is, what the chalk mark is for, and which shape in your geometry box would not pass the same test.
