Competitive-Style Thinking
Four moves worth more than another theorem.
Eliminate No chord exceeds $2r$, and none is negative. Two options are usually dead before any arithmetic starts.
Check sufficiency Ask what has been fixed. "Two parallel chords" has not fixed which side of the centre — so the answer is a pair.
Find the constraint $r^2 = d^2 + \left(\tfrac{c}{2}\right)^2$. Any two of $r$, $d$, $c$ give the third; a question naming three is testing one.
Estimate first $\sqrt{r^2 - d^2}$ lies between $r - d$ and $r$. Bound the answer, then see which option survives.

Name the move, then use it.

  1. Eliminate. A chord of a circle of radius $9$ cm lies $6$ cm from the centre. Strike out two options on sight, then answer. Its length is
    1. $3\sqrt{5}$ cm
    2. $6\sqrt{5}$ cm
    3. $12\sqrt{5}$ cm
    4. $20$ cm
  2. Eliminate. In cyclic $ABCD$, $\angle B$ is twice $\angle D$. Then $\angle B$ is
    1. $60^\circ$
    2. $100^\circ$
    3. $120^\circ$
    4. $200^\circ$
  3. Sufficiency. Parallel chords $10$ cm and $24$ cm long lie $7$ cm apart. Say why the radius is not yet fixed, add the word that fixes it, and find it.
  4. Sufficiency. To find a chord's length, which one fact is enough on its own beside the radius?
    1. That it is longer than the radius
    2. Its distance from the centre
    3. That it is parallel to a diameter
    4. The area of the circle
