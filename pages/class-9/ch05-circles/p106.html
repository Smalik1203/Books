Type 3 · The perpendicular from the centre

Example 5

An arch in a wall is an arc of a circle. The opening is $80$ cm wide at its base, and the top of the arch is $32$ cm above the middle of the base. Find the radius of the circle.

Solution.

Step 1 the base is a chord $AB$ with midpoint $M$; the line through $M$ at right angles to it passes through the centre $O$ and through the top $T$ of the arch Exercise Set 5.4, Q1
Step 2 let the radius be $r$ cm; then $OM = r - 32$ and $AM = 40$ $OT = r$, $MT = 32$
Step 3 $r^2 = (r - 32)^2 + 40^2$ Pythagoras in $\triangle OMA$
Step 4 $r^2 = r^2 - 64r + 1024 + 1600$, so $64r = 2624$
Answer $r = 41$, so the radius is $41$ cm
Check $OM = 41 - 32 = 9$, and $9^2 + 40^2 = 81 + 1600 = 1681 = 41^2$
Example 6

A circular window has radius $37$ cm. A straight bar $70$ cm long is fixed across it, with both ends on the frame. How far is the bar from the centre? And how high is the smaller part of the window, measured from the middle of the bar to the frame?

Solution.

Step 1 the perpendicular $OM$ from the centre bisects the bar $AB$, so $AM = 35$ cm Theorem 5
Step 2 $OM^2 = 37^2 - 35^2 = 1369 - 1225 = 144$, so $OM = 12$ cm Pythagoras in $\triangle OMA$
Step 3 carried on past $M$, the line $OM$ meets the frame at $T$, with $OT = 37$ cm a radius
Step 4 $MT = 37 - 12 = 25$
Answer The bar is $12$ cm from the centre, and the smaller part is $25$ cm high.

The height is measured along the radius through $M$, which is why it is the radius less the distance of the bar.
