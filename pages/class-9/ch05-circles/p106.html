In an assertion–reason question, answer from this list every time: (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

B6 · Competitive-Style Thinking
  1. Assertion. The perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre.  Reason. The centre is equidistant from the two ends of the chord, and every such point lies on that bisector.
  2. Assertion. A quadrilateral whose opposite angles add to $180^\circ$ is cyclic.  Reason. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to $180^\circ$.
  3. A circle has radius $10$ cm. Which of these hold?
    1. It has a chord $20$ cm long.
    2. It has a chord $21$ cm long.
    3. A chord of it lying $6$ cm from the centre is $16$ cm long.
    Choose: (a) (i) only   (b) (i) and (iii)   (c) (ii) and (iii)   (d) all three.
  4. Two parallel chords of a circle, $10$ cm and $24$ cm long, lie on the same side of the centre and $7$ cm apart. Find the radius, then say what the distance between them would have been had they lain on opposite sides of the centre instead.
  5. All four chords below belong to one circle, but one row is wrong. Find the radius from the rows that agree, then correct the row that does not.
    Chord (cm)$48$$14$$40$$30$
    Distance (cm)$7$$24$$20$$20$
  6. $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral in which $AB = AD$. Prove that $CA$ bisects $\angle BCD$, naming every theorem you use.
  7. Three villages are to share one well, equidistant from all three.
    1. Which point of the chapter is the well, and how would a surveyor with only a rope find it?
    2. The villages turn out to lie almost in a straight line. Say what happens to the well, and what the surveyor should report.
