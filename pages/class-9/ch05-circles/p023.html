Angles in the same segment

All the points of a circle lying outside a given arc see that arc's chord under the same angle.

Example 10

An arc $AXB$ subtends $110^\circ$ at the centre of a circle. What angle does it subtend at a point of the major arc? And what angle does the other arc subtend at a point of the minor arc?

The first is Theorem 9 read straight off. The second asks about a different arc, so the angle to halve is the one that arc sweeps at the centre — the reflex one.

arc AXB $110^\circ$ at the centre, halved $55^\circ$
the other arc $360^\circ - 110^\circ = 250^\circ$, halved $125^\circ$

A point on the major arc sees the chord at $55^\circ$; a point on the minor arc sees it at $125^\circ$. Notice that the two add to $180^\circ$. That is not a coincidence of these numbers — the two central angles always make a full turn, so their halves always make a straight angle. Section 5.9 turns the observation into a theorem.

One case of Theorem 9 is used so often that it has a name of its own. A corollary is a fact that drops out of a result already proved, with no fresh argument needed.
