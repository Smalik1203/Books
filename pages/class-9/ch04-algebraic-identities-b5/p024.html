Example 16

Simplify $\dfrac{x^2 - 7x + 12}{5x^2 + 5x - 100}$, given that $5x^2 + 5x - 100 \neq 0$.

Factorise each part. For the numerator, find two numbers with sum $-7$ and product $12$. Both are negative, since the product is positive and the sum is not; the pair is $-3$ and $-4$:

$x^2 - 7x + 12 = (x - 3)(x - 4)$

The denominator needs a step first — all three terms are multiples of 5, so take that out before hunting for a pair. Then the sum is $1$ and the product $-20$, which gives $5$ and $-4$:

$5x^2 + 5x - 100 = 5\left(x^2 + x - 20\right) = 5(x - 4)(x + 5)$

Now the fraction can be written as a product of factors:

$\dfrac{x^2 - 7x + 12}{5x^2 + 5x - 100} = \dfrac{(x - 3)(x - 4)}{5(x - 4)(x + 5)}$

The factor $x - 4$ appears top and bottom, so it cancels — and it is allowed to, because $x - 4$ cannot be zero here: if it were, the whole denominator would be zero, which we were told it is not.

$\dfrac{x^2 - 7x + 12}{5x^2 + 5x - 100} = \dfrac{x - 3}{5(x + 5)}$

One caution. The two agree wherever the original makes sense, but not everywhere: at $x = 4$ the simplified form gives $\frac{1}{45}$, while the original reads $\frac{0}{0}$.
