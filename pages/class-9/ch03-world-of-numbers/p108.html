B8 · The Trap Room
  1. "$\dfrac{22}{7}$ is irrational, because it is $\pi$." Two things are wrong here. Separate them, and say what $\dfrac{22}{7}$ actually is.
  2. "$0.\overline{9}$ must be a little less than $1$: however far you go, there is always a bit missing." Name the number that is missing, and then find where the argument goes wrong.
  3. "$\sqrt{16}$ is irrational — it has a root sign." Give the rule that actually decides, and two more square roots that catch the same student out.
  4. "$\dfrac{6}{15}$ cannot terminate, because $15$ has a factor of $3$." Carry out the division and see. Which four words of the terminating test were skipped?
  5. "Since $|x| \geq 0$ for every $x$, we may always write $|x| = x$." Find the values of $x$ for which the conclusion holds, and the ones for which it fails.
  6. "The sum of two irrational numbers is irrational, since neither of them is a fraction." Destroy the claim with one pair, and then say what is true about a rational plus an irrational.
  7. "Between $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$ there are only finitely many rationals: over the denominator $6$ they are $\dfrac{2}{6}$ and $\dfrac{3}{6}$, and nothing lies between them." Show the student what to do next, and say what the argument really proved.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Judging a number by how it is written (traps 1, 3). A root sign, a fraction bar and a decimal point are notation. Whether a number is rational is a fact about the number, and $\sqrt{16}$, $\frac{22}{7}$ and $0.\overline{9}$ all catch somebody out on this.
  2. Dropping a condition from a rule (traps 4, 5). The terminating test needs in lowest terms; $|x| = x$ needs $x \geq 0$. A rule quoted without its condition will be right often enough to feel safe.
  3. Mistaking a failed search for a proof (traps 6, 7). Not finding a counter-example is not the same as there being none, and running out of numerators over one denominator says something about that denominator, not about the rationals.
