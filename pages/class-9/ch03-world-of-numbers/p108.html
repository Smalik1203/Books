Type 6 · Rational or irrational

Example 9

Prove that $\sqrt{6}$ is irrational.

Solution.

Step 1 suppose $\sqrt{6} = \dfrac{p}{q}$, with $p$, $q$ integers, $q \neq 0$, and $p$, $q$ co-prime the opposite, assumed
Step 2 $6q^2 = p^2$ square, multiply by $q^2$
Step 3 $p^2 = 2 \times 3q^2$ is even, so $p$ is even; write $p = 2k$ an even square
Step 4 $6q^2 = 4k^2$, so $3q^2 = 2k^2$ substitute, divide by 2
Step 5 $3q^2$ is even and $3$ is odd, so $q^2$ is even, and $q$ is even odd times odd is odd
Step 6 $p$ and $q$ share the factor $2$, against Step 1 a contradiction
Answer The supposition fails, so $\sqrt{6}$ is irrational

Step 5 is where $6$ differs from $2$: the $3$ left over had to be shown odd before $q$ could be called even.

Example 10

Given that $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $\pi$ are irrational, which of these is rational?

  1. $5\sqrt{3}$
  2. $2\pi$
  3. $\left(\sqrt{7}\right)^2$
  4. $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$

Solution.

Step 1 $\sqrt{7}$ is the number whose square is $7$, so $\left(\sqrt{7}\right)^2 = 7$ what a square root is
Step 2 if $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$ were a rational $s$, then $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{1}{s}$ would be rational closed under division
Answer (c)

Options (a) and (b) are a non-zero rational times an irrational, and so irrational: if $5\sqrt{3}$ were a rational $r$, $\sqrt{3} = \tfrac{r}{5}$ would be rational too. Option (d) is irrational by Step 2.
