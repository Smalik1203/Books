8
The Trap Room
Two students, one question, and a mistake.

Answer (a) A only, (b) B only, (c) both, (d) neither — then say in one line what went wrong.

  1. A. $\dfrac{22}{7}$ is irrational, because it is $\pi$.  B. It is rational, and it is not $\pi$.
  2. A. $0.\overline{9}$ is a little less than $1$: however far you go, a bit is missing.  B. $0.\overline{9}$ and $1$ are the same number.
  3. A. $\sqrt{16}$ is $4$, and rational.  B. It is irrational — it has a root sign.
  4. A. $\dfrac{6}{15}$ cannot terminate, because $15$ has a factor of $3$.  B. It terminates: in lowest terms it is $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
  5. A. $|x| = x$ exactly when $x$ is not negative.  B. $|x| = x$ for every $x$, since $|x|$ is never negative.
  6. A. A rational plus an irrational is always irrational.  B. Two irrationals may add to a rational.
  7. Between $\dfrac{1}{3}$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$: A. There are infinitely many rationals.  B. There are finitely many — over $6$ they are $\dfrac{2}{6}$ and $\dfrac{3}{6}$, with nothing between.
  8. The diagonal of a unit square is measured. A. A reading of $1.414$ m proves it is $\dfrac{1414}{1000}$.  B. A reading of $1.41421$ m would prove it.
  9. A. $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.  B. $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8}$ is irrational, because both factors are.
  10. A. The cycle of $\dfrac{1}{q}$ is always exactly $q - 1$ digits.  B. It is never longer than $q - 1$ digits.
Three shapes of mistake
  1. Judging a number by how it is written (1, 3, 9). A root sign, a fraction bar and a decimal point are notation. Whether a number is rational is a fact about the number.
  2. Dropping a condition from a rule (4, 5, 10). The terminating test needs in lowest terms; $|x| = x$ needs $x$ not negative; $q - 1$ is a ceiling, not a promise.
  3. Trusting a finite check on an infinite claim (2, 7, 8). One denominator does not exhaust the rationals, and no measurement ever settles a proof.
