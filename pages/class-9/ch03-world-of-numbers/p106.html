Problem 4

Which of these is rational?

  1. $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$
  2. $\left(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{3}\right)\left(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3}\right)$
  3. $\pi - 3$
  4. $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{3}$
Solution

Option (b) is a difference of squares, the identity from Chapter 4, and the roots disappear when it is multiplied out:

$\left(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{3}\right)\left(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3}\right) = 5 - 3 = 2$

Option (b). Option (a) was settled in the second question of this section: it is irrational. Option (c) is $\pi$ with a rational taken away, which leaves an irrational. Option (d) is $\sqrt{6}$, and it has to be checked rather than assumed: two irrationals multiplied can give a rational, since $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8} = 4$. What settles it is what is under the root, and $6$ is not a perfect square.
