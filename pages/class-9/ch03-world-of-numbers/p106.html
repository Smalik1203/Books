Type 4 · Rational numbers between two numbers

Example 6

How many numbers of the form $\dfrac{k}{24}$, with $k$ a whole number, lie strictly between $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$?

  1. $0$
  2. $1$
  3. $2$
  4. infinitely many

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{6}{24}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{8}{24}$ both ends over 24
Step 2 $6 \lt k \lt 8$, so $k = 7$ strictly between
Answer (b)

Option (c) counts $6$ and $8$ themselves, which the word strictly rules out. Infinitely many rationals do lie between $\tfrac{1}{4}$ and $\tfrac{1}{3}$ — that is what density says. But this question fixes the denominator at $24$, and a fixed denominator leaves only whole numbers to choose, so option (d) is wrong.

Example 7

Find two rational numbers between $0.\overline{2}$ and $0.25$.

Solution.

Step 1 $0.\overline{2} = \dfrac{2}{9}$ and $0.25 = \dfrac{1}{4}$ both as fractions
Step 2 $\dfrac{2}{9} = \dfrac{8}{36}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{9}{36}$: no numerator between common denominator
Step 3 $\dfrac{8}{36} = \dfrac{24}{108}$ and $\dfrac{9}{36} = \dfrac{27}{108}$: $25$ and $26$ between top and bottom times 3
Answer $\dfrac{25}{108}$ and $\dfrac{26}{108} = \dfrac{13}{54}$
Check $0.2222\ldots \lt 0.2314\ldots \lt 0.2407\ldots \lt 0.25$

A repeating decimal has to become a fraction before the numerators can be compared; the two ends were then only one thirty-sixth apart.
