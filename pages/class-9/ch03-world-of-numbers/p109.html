Type 7 · Terminating or repeating, from the denominator

Example 11

Without long division, decide whether $\dfrac{17}{64}$ and $\dfrac{5}{24}$ terminate, and write the one that does as a decimal.

Solution.

Step 1 $17$ is prime and does not divide $64$; $5$ does not divide $24$ both in lowest terms
Step 2 $64 = 2^6$, with no prime but $2$ it terminates
Step 3 $24 = 2^3 \times 3$, which has the prime $3$ it repeats
Step 4 $\dfrac{17}{64} = \dfrac{17 \times 5^6}{2^6 \times 5^6} = \dfrac{265625}{1000000} = 0.265625$ six 5s to match six 2s
Answer $\dfrac{17}{64} = 0.265625$, and $\dfrac{5}{24}$ repeats
Example 12

The decimal expansion of $\dfrac{11}{80}$

  1. terminates after $2$ places
  2. repeats
  3. terminates after $4$ places
  4. terminates after $5$ places

Solution.

Step 1 $80 = 2^4 \times 5$, and $11$ does not divide $80$ lowest terms, only 2s and 5s
Step 2 three more 5s make $2^4 \times 5^4 = 10^4$ match the four 2s
Step 3 $\dfrac{11}{80} = \dfrac{11 \times 125}{10^4} = \dfrac{1375}{10000} = 0.1375$
Answer (c)

Option (a) reads $80$ as $8 \times 10$ and counts only the $10$. Option (b) would need a prime other than $2$ or $5$ in $80$. Option (d) adds the two powers, $4 + 1$.
