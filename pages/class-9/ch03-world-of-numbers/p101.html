The Idea Underneath
Two kinds of number, interleaved everywhere.

Could a rational $r$ plus an irrational $i$ come out rational? One line settles it:

$$r + i = s \;\Longrightarrow\; i = s - r$$

A difference of two rationals is rational, and $i$ is not. So rational plus irrational is always irrational — but nothing here settles irrational plus irrational, and that is where examinations live.

What arithmetic does to the two kinds
rational $+$ irrational→ always irrational
irrational $+$ irrational→ may be either

$\sqrt{2} + \left(-\sqrt{2}\right) = 0$, and yet $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational. Both kinds sit everywhere on the line, however far you magnify it.
