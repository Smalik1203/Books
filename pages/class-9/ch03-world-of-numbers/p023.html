Example 12

Write $0.1\overline{6}$ in the form $\dfrac{p}{q}$.

This one does not repeat immediately: the 1 occurs once and only the 6 recurs. One shift will not do, because the two numbers must agree in their tails and the tails only match once the non-repeating part is out of the way. So shift twice, for two different reasons.

Solution.

Step 1 Let $x = 0.16666\ldots$
Step 2 one digit does not repeat; multiply by $10^1$: $\;10x = 1.6666\ldots$ push it past the point
Step 3 one digit repeats; multiply by $10^1$ again: $\;100x = 16.6666\ldots$ move a whole cycle
Step 4 both lines end in $.6666\ldots$, so $\;90x = 15$ Step 3 minus Step 2
Answer $x = \dfrac{15}{90} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
Check $1 \div 6 = 0.16666\ldots$

The two multipliers had different jobs — the first cleared the non-repeating digit, the second moved one full cycle — and it is worth naming which is which as you go.

There is a second way to it that needs no new machinery, and it is worth having because it explains the shape of the answer. Split the decimal where the repetition begins.

$0.1\overline{6} = 0.1 + 0.0\overline{6}$

The first piece terminates, so it is $\tfrac{1}{10}$ and needs no thought. The second is $0.\overline{6}$ pushed one place to the right, which is to say a tenth of it — and Example 10 has already told us that $0.\overline{6}$ is $\tfrac{2}{3}$, so this piece is $\tfrac{2}{30}$, or $\tfrac{1}{15}$.

$\dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{1}{15} = \dfrac{3}{30} + \dfrac{2}{30} = \dfrac{5}{30} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

The same answer, reached by treating the stray digit and the cycle separately instead of clearing both at once. That is what the two multipliers were doing all along.

The two multipliers in that example were doing quite different jobs. The general case does both at once, with a whole-number part along for the ride, and needs no new idea.
