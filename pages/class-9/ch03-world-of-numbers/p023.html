  1. The repeating block of $\dfrac{1}{7}$ is cyclic. Find other numbers $n$ whose reciprocal $\dfrac{1}{n}$ has a cyclic block. What do the values of $n$ that work have in common?

One last oddity, which question 4 walks into. Terminating decimals are not unique. Just as $1 = \tfrac{10}{10} = \tfrac{100}{100}$, every terminating decimal has a second form ending in repeating nines: $1.000\ldots = 0.999\ldots$ and $2.47000\ldots = 2.46999\ldots$

Most people meet $0.\overline{9} = 1$ with resistance, feeling it must fall a little short. It does not. There is no room between them — any number you propose as lying in the gap can be shown to be larger than $0.\overline{9}$, and the algebra of question 4 settles it outright.

3.7The Never-Ending Journey

We have come a long way. From notches cut into the Ishango bone to keep track of days, to the philosophical void of śūnya and Brahmagupta's decision to make a number of it. Across zero into debts and fortunes. Into the space between the integers, which turned out to be infinitely crowded with fractions — and then turned out, all the same, to have gaps in it that no fraction reaches.

Each stage was forced by a question the previous stage could not answer. That is worth noticing, because it is how the subject grows: not by decree, but because someone wrote down something the existing numbers could not name.
