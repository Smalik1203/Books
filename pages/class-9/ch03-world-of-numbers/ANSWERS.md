## 3.4 Filling the Spaces

### Think and Reflect (why $q \neq 0$)

A quotient $\tfrac{p}{q}$ is the number that gives $p$ when multiplied by $q$.
With $q = 0$ and $p \neq 0$, no number works, since anything times $0$ is $0$.
With $p = 0$ as well, every number works, so there is no single answer. The
first case has no value and the second has too many.

## 3.5 Numbers That Are Not Ratios

### Think and Reflect (the argument for $\sqrt{3}$, $\sqrt{5}$, $\sqrt{7}$, $\sqrt{9}$)

For $\sqrt{3}$:
- suppose $\sqrt{3} = \tfrac{p}{q}$, co-prime
- $3q^2 = p^2$ — squaring
- $3$ divides $p^2$, so $3$ divides $p$, since $3$ is prime; put $p = 3k$
- $3q^2 = 9k^2$, so $q^2 = 3k^2$
- so $3$ divides $q$ as well — against co-prime

$\sqrt{5}$ and $\sqrt{7}$ go the same way with $5$ and $7$. For $\sqrt{9}$ the
step "if $9$ divides $p^2$ then $9$ divides $p$" is false ($p = 3$), and that is
where the argument breaks: $\sqrt{9} = \tfrac{3}{1}$. What the argument used
about $2$, $3$, $5$ and $7$ is that each is prime: a prime that divides a
square divides the number.

### Think and Reflect (other roots on the line)

$\sqrt{3}$: legs $\sqrt{2}$ and $1$, since $2 + 1 = 3$. $\sqrt{5}$: legs $2$ and
$1$, since $4 + 1 = 5$. In general, legs $\sqrt{n}$ and $1$ give a hypotenuse
of $\sqrt{n + 1}$; repeating it for ever draws the square-root spiral of
Fig. 3.7.
