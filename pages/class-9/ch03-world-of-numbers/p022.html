$142857 \times 1 = 142857$

$142857 \times 2 = 285714$

$142857 \times 3 = 428571$

$142857 \times 4 = 571428$

$142857 \times 5 = 714285$

$142857 \times 6 = 857142$

Every answer uses the same six digits in the same cyclic order — they have only been rotated. A number that behaves this way is called a cyclic number, and $142857$ is the smallest of them. Nothing was arranged here; it falls out of the long division by 7, whose six possible remainders come round in a fixed order.

What an irrational decimal looks like

Irrational numbers have decimal expansions that never end and never settle into a cycle. There is no repeating block, however far you look:

$\sqrt{2} = 1.4142135623730950488\ldots$

$\pi = 3.1415926535897932384\ldots$

This gives a clean test in both directions. Terminating or repeating means rational; non-terminating and non-repeating means irrational. And it explains the earlier proof from a second angle: if $\sqrt{2}$ were $\tfrac{p}{q}$, its expansion would have had to fall into a cycle of at most $q - 1$ digits, and it never does.

Beware of $1.01001000100001\ldots$ — the pattern is obvious, but no fixed block repeats. A pattern you can describe is not the same as a cycle.
