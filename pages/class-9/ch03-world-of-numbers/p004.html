Brahmagupta gives it rules

That is Brahmagupta's contribution, in the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta of 628 CE. He defines zero not as a gap but as a result: what you get when a number is subtracted from itself, $a - a = 0$. Then he does the thing nobody had done before, and states how it behaves.

Brahmagupta's rules for śūnya

Adding zero changes nothing: $a + 0 = a$.

Subtracting zero changes nothing: $a - 0 = a$.

Multiplying by zero gives zero: $a \times 0 = 0$.

Read those three lines again and notice how ordinary they look. That is the achievement. Zero has stopped being a hole in the notation and become a number that arithmetic can be done to — and once it is, the question of what lies on the other side of it becomes impossible to avoid.

3.3Integers: Going Left of Zero

Brahmagupta did not stop at zero. He had defined it as $5 - 5 = 0$, and that definition invites an obvious next question: what is $3 - 5$? The notation permits it. Does anything answer it?

His answer came from commerce rather than from arithmetic. A merchant deals in two kinds of quantity, and always has: what he owns and what he owes. Brahmagupta named them dhana, fortunes, and ṛṇa, debts — and treated the second as a number in its own right rather than as a fortune with a note attached.

Going left of zero on the line gives the negative numbers. Together with the positive naturals and zero they make the integers, written $\mathbb{Z}$ — from the German Zahlen, numbers.
