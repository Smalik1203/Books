1. A rational number in lowest terms has denominator $2^3 \times 5$. How many decimal places will its expansion have? Explain what fixes the number.
  1. A terminating decimal has its last non-zero digit in the fourth place. Show it can be written as $\dfrac{p}{10^4}$ with $p$ not divisible by 10. Must the denominator in lowest terms be divisible by $2^4$ or by $5^4$? Give reasons.
  1. Let $a = \dfrac{7}{12}$ and $b = \dfrac{5}{6}$. Write both over one denominator $m$, as $\dfrac{k_1}{m}$ and $\dfrac{k_2}{m}$ with $k_2 - k_1 > 6$, then write five distinct rationals between them with whole-number numerators. Explain why $k_2 - k_1 > n + 1$ is needed to fit $n$ numbers in this way.
  1. Rationals $x$, $y$, $z$ satisfy $x + y + z = 0$ and $xy + yz + zx = 0$. Show that $x$, $y$ and $z$ must all be zero. (Consider $(x + y + z)^2$.)
Multiple-Choice Exercises

One option is correct in each. Decide which fact of the chapter the question is about before you start calculating; several of these need no calculation at all.

  1. Which of these is irrational?
    1. $\sqrt{36}$
    2. $0.25$
    3. $\sqrt{7}$
    4. $\dfrac{22}{7}$
  1. The decimal expansion of $\dfrac{17}{8}$ is
    1. terminating
    2. non-terminating, repeating
    3. non-terminating, non-repeating
    4. none of these
  1. $0.\overline{3} + 0.\overline{4}$ equals
    1. $0.\overline{7}$
    2. $\dfrac{7}{9}$
    3. both (a) and (b)
    4. neither
  1. Between any two distinct rational numbers there are
    1. exactly one rational number
    2. finitely many rational numbers
    3. infinitely many rational numbers
    4. no rational number at all
  1. In lowest terms, which of these cannot be the denominator of a terminating decimal?
    1. $16$
    2. $25$
    3. $40$
    4. $30$
