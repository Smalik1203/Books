Type 5 · Square roots on the number line

Example 8

Locate $\sqrt{13}$ on the number line, and say between which two whole numbers it lies.

Solution.

Step 1 $13 = 9 + 4 = 3^2 + 2^2$ two squares that make 13
Step 2 mark $OA = 3$ units on the line, and $AB = 2$ units at right angles to it at $A$
Step 3 $OB^2 = 3^2 + 2^2 = 13$, so $OB = \sqrt{13}$ Baudhāyana–Pythagoras
Step 4 an arc with centre $O$ and radius $OB$ cuts the line at $P$ $OP = OB$
Answer $OP = \sqrt{13}$, and since $9 \lt 13 \lt 16$, $P$ lies between $3$ and $4$

The legs need not be $1$: any two squares that add to $13$ will do, and here that saves building $\sqrt{12}$ first.
