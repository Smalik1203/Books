B7 · The Challenge Zone
  1. Think Prove that a rational number plus an irrational number is always irrational.
    1. Show, with two numbers of your own, that an irrational plus an irrational need not be.
    2. Say exactly which line of your first proof fails when both numbers are irrational.
  2. Apply Given that $\sqrt{6}$ is irrational, prove that $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational too.

    Suppose it equalled a rational $r$ and square both sides. One identity from Chapter 4 does the rest.

  3. Apply Fig. 3.9 places $\sqrt{10}$ on the number line with a straight edge and a compass.
    1. Say why $OB$ is $\sqrt{10}$, and why the arc puts the length down exactly rather than nearly.
    2. How would you get $\sqrt{11}$ from the same drawing? And $\sqrt{2}$, which is smaller than every length in it?
  4. Challenge Show that a decimal whose $n$-digit block repeats from the very first place is always that block divided by the $n$-digit number $99\ldots9$. Use it on $0.\overline{285714}$, put the answer in lowest terms, and say why the answer is one you have met already.
  5. Challenge Let $\dfrac{1}{n}$ have a terminating decimal, so that $n = 2^a 5^b$. Show that the expansion has exactly $\max(a, b)$ decimal places, test the rule on $n = 16$, $n = 40$ and $n = 125$, and say why the smaller of $a$ and $b$ has no say in the matter.
