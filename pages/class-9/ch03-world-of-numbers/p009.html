Example 3

Find $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{5}{6}$.

Thirds of a thing and quarters of it are not the same size, so the numerators cannot simply be added. Both have to be rewritten in parts of one common size first, and the smallest size that works is the LCM of the denominators.

Solution.

Step 1 LCM of 4 and 6 is 12 common denominator
Step 2 $\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \dfrac{9}{12}$ rewrite the first
Step 3 $\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 2} = \dfrac{10}{12}$ rewrite the second
Step 4 $\dfrac{9}{12} + \dfrac{10}{12} = \dfrac{19}{12}$ the parts now match
Step 5 the only factor of 19 is 19 itself, and 19 does not divide 12 lowest terms
Answer $\dfrac{19}{12}$
Check $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and $\tfrac{5}{6}$ are each a little under 1, so the sum should be a little over $1\tfrac{1}{2}$ — and $\tfrac{19}{12} = 1\tfrac{7}{12}$

The rationals are closed under addition, subtraction and multiplication: combine any two that way and the result is another rational. They are closed under division too, with the one exception you would expect — you may not divide by zero.
