Example 2

A trader takes a loan of ₹640. The next day he makes a profit of ₹900. The week after, he takes a loss of ₹380. Where does he stand?

Write each event as an integer before combining anything. A loan is a debt, so it is negative. A profit is a fortune, so it is positive. A loss reduces what he holds, so it is subtracted.

Solution.

Step 1 a debt of ₹640, written $-640$ the loan
Step 2 a fortune of ₹900, written $+900$ the profit
Step 3 ₹380 taken away, written $-380$ the loss
Step 4 $-640 + 900 = 260$ adding in order
Step 5 $260 - 380 = -120$
Answer He ends with $-120$: a debt of ₹120

Note that the answer is negative even though the single largest figure was a profit — which is the point of carrying the signs rather than the words.

The signs did the work there, not the words. Once each event is written as an integer the running total can be trusted, without asking at every stage whether the trader is up or down.

Why should a debt times a debt be a fortune? Think of it as removal rather than as multiplication.

If someone takes away four of your debts, each worth ₹3, you are ₹12 better off than you were. Taking away is the first minus; the debt is the second; and you end up richer.
