Why should a debt times a debt be a fortune? Think of it as removal rather than as multiplication.

If someone takes away four of your debts, each worth ₹3, you are ₹12 better off than you were. Taking away is the first minus; the debt is the second; and you end up richer.
Exercise Set 3.2
  1. The temperature in the high desert of Ladakh is $4^\circ$C at noon. By midnight it has fallen by $15^\circ$C. What is the midnight temperature?
  1. A spice trader takes a loan of ₹850. The next day he makes a profit of ₹1,200. The following week he takes a loss of ₹450. Write the sequence as one statement in integers, and find where he stands.
  1. Calculate, naming the law of Brahmagupta's you have used in each:
    1. $(-12) \times 5$
    2. $(-8) \times (-7)$
    3. $0 - (-14)$
    4. $(-20) \div 4$
  1. Using an example about debt, explain why subtracting a negative number is the same as adding a positive one — why, that is, $10 - (-5) = 15$.
  1. A shopkeeper's ledger shows four entries: $-300$, $+180$, $-45$, $+700$. He claims the order in which he adds them cannot change the total. Is he right? Say which law he is depending on, and whether the same freedom would hold if the entries were to be subtracted in turn instead.
