The arithmetic of integers

Brahmagupta wrote the rules out, and we still use them exactly as he stated them thirteen centuries ago. What is striking is that every one of them is justified by what it would mean for a merchant, not by any appeal to the symbols.

Brahmagupta's laws of signs

A fortune plus a fortune is a fortune: $5 + 4 = 9$.

A debt plus a debt is a debt: $(-5) + (-4) = -9$.

A fortune less zero is a fortune; a debt less zero is a debt.

A debt times a fortune is a debt: $(-3) \times 4 = -12$.

A debt times a debt is a fortune: $(-3) \times (-4) = 12$.

Example 2

A trader takes a loan of ₹640. The next day he makes a profit of ₹900. The week after, he takes a loss of ₹380. Where does he stand?

Write each event as an integer before combining anything. A loan is a debt, so it is negative; a profit is a fortune, so it is positive; a loss reduces what he holds, so it is subtracted.

the loan a debt of ₹640 $-640$
the profit a fortune of ₹900 $+900$
the loss ₹380 taken away $-380$

Now add them in order, keeping the running total:

$-640 + 900 = 260$

$260 - 380 = -120$

He ends with $-120$: a debt of ₹120. Note that the answer is negative even though the single largest figure was a profit — which is the point of carrying the signs rather than the words.

The signs did the work there, not the words. Once each event is written as an integer the running total can be trusted, without asking at every stage whether the trader is up or down.
