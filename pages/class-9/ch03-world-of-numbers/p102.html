Think and Reason
Judge a claim — and the reason offered for it.

Questions 1–4 take this key. (a) both true, R explains A; (b) both true, R does not explain A; (c) A true, R false; (d) A false, R true.

  1. A. Every terminating decimal is rational.  R. A decimal with $n$ places can be written over $10^n$.
  2. A. $\sqrt{9}$ is irrational.  R. The square root of a natural number is irrational unless that number is a perfect square.
  3. A. Between any two rationals lies an irrational.  R. The rationals are dense in the line.
  4. A. A non-zero rational times an irrational is irrational.  R. If $ri$ were a rational $s$, then $i = s / r$ would be rational.
  5. For $\dfrac{13}{125}$, which must be true?
    1. Its decimal terminates.
    2. It terminates after exactly two places.
    3. $125 = 5^3$, and that is what decides the matter.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (iii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
  6. For $\dfrac{1}{7} = 0.\overline{142857}$, which must be true?
    1. Its cycle is six digits long.
    2. Six is the longest a cycle over $7$ could be.
    3. The block $142857$ is cyclic.
    1. (i) only
    2. (i) and (ii)
    3. (ii) and (iii)
    4. all three
  7. $q - 1$ is a ceiling on the cycle length, not a promise: $7$, $17$ and $19$ reach it, while $11$ and $13$ do not. Say what a division reaching the ceiling means about its remainders.
