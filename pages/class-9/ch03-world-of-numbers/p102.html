A reasoning question has no working to show. It wants the sentence that would convince someone who disagreed — so answer in words, and kill a false claim with one counter-example rather than an argument.

B2 · Think and Reason
  1. The proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational never computes a decimal place.
    1. Explain why no amount of computing could have settled the question either way.
    2. What would a computation have settled, if the digits had turned out to repeat?
  2. Run the $\sqrt{2}$ argument on $\sqrt{9}$, where it must fail because $\sqrt{9} = 3$. Name the exact step that breaks, and say what the argument was really using about $2$ that $9$ does not have.
  3. The rationals are dense and yet do not fill the line. Say why dense and complete are different properties, and name the one drawing in the chapter that shows the difference in a single picture.
  4. Dividing by $11$ there are ten possible remainders, so the cycle of $\frac{1}{11}$ could be as long as ten digits. It is two. Say what the remainder argument does and does not promise, and find another denominator whose cycle falls well short of its ceiling.
  5. Brahmagupta justified a debt times a debt is a fortune by what it would mean for a merchant, not by a manipulation of symbols.
    1. Give his argument in one sentence.
    2. Say what kind of thing would have counted as a proof instead, and why his rule has nonetheless survived unchanged for fourteen centuries.
  6. $0.\overline{9} = 1$, and most people resist it.
    1. Propose a number strictly between them, then show it cannot exist.
    2. Say why the resistance is reasonable, and which word in the digits go on for ever is doing the damage.
  7. $1.01001000100001\ldots$ has an obvious pattern and is irrational. Explain how both can be true at once, state precisely what a decimal must do to be rational, and write down one more decimal with a describable pattern that is not rational.
