C6 $0.\overline{36} = \dfrac{36}{99}$ and $0.\overline{63} = \dfrac{63}{99}$, so the two add to $\dfrac{99}{99}$, which is $1$ exactly. Option (a) is the common feeling that $0.\overline{9}$ falls a little short of $1$. It does not: the two are names for one number, and this addition is one way of seeing it.
What to take away
  1. A decimal is a signature. Terminating or repeating means rational and nothing else does, and the prime factors of the denominator — in lowest terms — decide which of the two you get and how far it runs.
  2. Notation is not the number. A root sign, a fraction bar and a decimal point are ways of writing; whether a number is rational is a fact about the number, and $\sqrt{16}$ is as rational as $4$.
  3. Assume the opposite, and square. It is the shortest way through most questions about surds — and no measurement, however many places it runs to, settles one of them. Only an argument can.
