Why the other options are wrong

A5 The test is on the denominator in lowest terms, and the question has already put it there. $8 = 2^3$, $20 = 2^2 \times 5$ and $32 = 2^5$ carry no prime but $2$ and $5$, so all three terminate. Only $24 = 2^3 \times 3$ has a third prime, and that $3$ is what makes it repeat.
B2 $\sqrt{9} = 3$, so A is false, and that settles the question before R is even weighed. R is true and is the general rule; $9$ is a perfect square, which is the case the rule sets aside. A rule and its exception are easy to quote in the wrong order.
B3 Both statements are true, so (c) and (d) go. But R says the rationals are dense — that between any two of them lies another rational — and A is about an irrational. Density never produces a number from outside the set, so R is not the reason for A. Hence (b).
C6 $0.\overline{36} = \dfrac{36}{99}$ and $0.\overline{63} = \dfrac{63}{99}$, so the two add to $\dfrac{99}{99}$, which is $1$ exactly. Option (a) is the common feeling that $0.\overline{9}$ falls a little short of $1$. It does not: the two are names for one number, and this addition is one way of seeing it.
