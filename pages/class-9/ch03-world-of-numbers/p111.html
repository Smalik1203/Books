Type 9 · Rational numbers close to an irrational one

Example 15

Baudhāyana gave $\dfrac{577}{408}$ for $\sqrt{2}$. Find the square of $\dfrac{577}{408}$ exactly, and use it to say whether the fraction is above or below $\sqrt{2}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $577^2 = 332929$ and $408^2 = 166464$
Step 2 $2 \times 166464 = 332928$ what $2$ would need
Step 3 $\left(\dfrac{577}{408}\right)^2 = \dfrac{332929}{166464} = 2 + \dfrac{1}{166464}$ one more than that
Answer Its square is a little more than $2$, so $\dfrac{577}{408}$ is a little above $\sqrt{2}$

The square misses $2$ by $\tfrac{1}{166464}$, less than a hundred-thousandth, which is why the fraction is so good. It cannot hit $2$ exactly, since $\sqrt{2}$ is not a ratio.

Example 16

Using $\pi = 3.14159265\ldots$, decide which is closer to $\pi$: $\dfrac{22}{7}$ or Āryabhaṭa's $\dfrac{3927}{1250}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{22}{7} = 3.142857\ldots$ and $\dfrac{3927}{1250} = 3.1416$ as decimals
Step 2 $\left|\dfrac{22}{7} - \pi\right| = 0.00126\ldots$ distance on the line
Step 3 $\left|3.1416 - \pi\right| = 0.0000073\ldots$
Answer $\dfrac{3927}{1250}$ is closer, by more than a hundred times

Both are rational, so neither is $\pi$. The question is only which is nearer, and $|a - b|$ is the measure of that.
