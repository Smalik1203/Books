Example 5

Find three rational numbers lying strictly between $-\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$.

Averaging would give them one at a time. It is quicker to put both over one denominator and read off the numerators in between — but the first attempt shows why the denominator has to be chosen with care.

Solution.

Step 1 $-\dfrac{1}{2} = -\dfrac{2}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{4}$: only $0$ between common denominator
Step 2 between the numerators $-2$ and $1$ there is just one whole number to spare, and we need three
Step 3 $-\dfrac{8}{16}$ and $\dfrac{4}{16}$: $11$ numerators between top and bottom times 4
Step 4 take any three of them, say $-7$, $-4$ and $1$
Answer $-\dfrac{7}{16}, \qquad -\dfrac{4}{16} = -\dfrac{1}{4}, \qquad \dfrac{1}{16}$
Check $-0.5 < -0.4375 < -0.25 < 0.0625 < 0.25$, so all three are strictly inside

Widening changes neither number. Check the ends rather than trusting the method. The lesson generalises — to fit $n$ numbers between two rationals, widen the denominator until at least $n$ whole numbers separate the numerators.
