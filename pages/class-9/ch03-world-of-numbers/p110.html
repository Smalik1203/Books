Type 8 · A repeating decimal as a fraction

Example 13

$0.\overline{123}$ in lowest terms is

  1. $\dfrac{41}{333}$
  2. $\dfrac{61}{495}$
  3. $\dfrac{122}{990}$
  4. $\dfrac{123}{1000}$

Solution.

Step 1 $x = 0.123123\ldots$ the cycle starts at the point
Step 2 $1000x = 123.123123\ldots$ a three-digit cycle
Step 3 $999x = 123$ subtract: the tails cancel
Step 4 $x = \dfrac{123}{999} = \dfrac{41}{333}$ divide both by 3
Answer (a)
Check $41 \div 333 = 0.123123\ldots = 0.\overline{123}$

Each wrong option is the right answer to a question that was not asked. Option (c) is $\tfrac{122}{990}$, the answer to $0.1\overline{23}$, whose $0$ belongs to the one digit outside the cycle; option (b) is that fraction reduced. Option (d) is $0.123$ exactly. And $333 = 3^2 \times 37$ carries no $2$ and no $5$, so the reduced form still says the decimal repeats.

Example 14

$2.3\overline{5}$ in lowest terms is

  1. $\dfrac{47}{20}$
  2. $\dfrac{106}{45}$
  3. $\dfrac{212}{99}$
  4. $\dfrac{233}{99}$

Solution.

Step 1 $x = 2.3555\ldots$ one digit outside the cycle
Step 2 $10x = 23.555\ldots$ push the $3$ past the point
Step 3 $100x = 235.555\ldots$ move one cycle on
Step 4 $90x = 212$ Step 3 minus Step 2
Step 5 $x = \dfrac{212}{90} = \dfrac{106}{45}$ divide both by 2
Answer (b)

The $90$ is one $9$ for the digit that repeats and one $0$ for the digit that does not. Option (a) is $\tfrac{47}{20} = 2.35$, this number with the $5$ stopped instead of repeating. Options (c) and (d) carry a $99$, which belongs to a decimal repeating from the point: (d) is $2.\overline{35}$, and (c) puts $99$ where the $90$ belongs.
