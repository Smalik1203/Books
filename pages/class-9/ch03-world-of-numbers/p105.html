Multiple Choice · Level 2
Two steps, and a figure to settle first.

Choose the correct option.

  1. $\dfrac{1}{7}$ has a repeating block six digits long. The block of $\dfrac{3}{7}$ is
    1. $2$ digits
    2. $3$ digits
    3. $6$ digits
    4. $7$ digits
  2. $x$ is rational and not zero, $y$ is irrational. Then $xy$ is
    1. always rational
    2. always irrational
    3. rational only when $x = 1$
    4. sometimes one, sometimes the other
  3. $0.\overline{123}$ in lowest terms is
    1. $\dfrac{41}{333}$
    2. $\dfrac{41}{330}$
    3. $\dfrac{123}{990}$
    4. $\dfrac{123}{1000}$
  4. $2.3\overline{5}$ in lowest terms is
    1. $\dfrac{47}{20}$
    2. $\dfrac{106}{45}$
    3. $\dfrac{212}{99}$
    4. $\dfrac{235}{99}$
  5. $\dfrac{p}{q}$ is in lowest terms and terminates after exactly four decimal places. Then
    1. $q$ divides $10^4$ but not $10^3$
    2. $q = 10^4$
    3. $q$ is a multiple of $10^4$
    4. $q$ has $3$ as a prime factor
  6. Which of these is rational?
    1. $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$
    2. $\left(\sqrt{5} - \sqrt{3}\right)\left(\sqrt{5} + \sqrt{3}\right)$
    3. $\pi - 3$
    4. $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{3}$
  7. How many numbers of the form $\dfrac{k}{24}$, with $k$ a whole number, lie strictly between $\dfrac{1}{4}$ and $\dfrac{1}{3}$?
    1. $0$
    2. $1$
    3. $2$
    4. infinitely many
  8. If $a$ and $b$ are both irrational, then $a + b$ is
    1. always irrational
    2. always rational
    3. rational only when $a = b$
    4. sometimes one, sometimes the other
