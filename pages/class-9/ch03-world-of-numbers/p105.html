Solution 2

One digit after the point does not repeat and one does, so two multipliers are needed. Put $x = 2.3555\ldots$ The first pushes the $3$ past the point; the second moves the cycle on by one place, so the tails line up and cancel:

$10x = 23.5555\ldots$,   $100x = 235.5555\ldots$

$90x = 212$,  so  $x = \dfrac{212}{90} = \dfrac{106}{45}$

The answer is (b). Option (a) is $\dfrac{47}{20} = 2.35$, the number this would be if the $5$ stopped instead of repeating. Options (c) and (d) both carry a $99$, and $99$ belongs to a decimal that repeats from the point onwards: (d) is $2.\overline{35}$, and (c) is what comes of using $99$ where the $90$ belongs.

The $90$ is the whole point. One $9$ for the digit that repeats, one $0$ for the digit that does not — and the denominator says so before any cancelling is done.

Problem 3 · Try it first

$\dfrac{p}{q}$ is in lowest terms and terminates after exactly four decimal places. Then

  1. $q$ divides $10^4$ but not $10^3$
  2. $q = 10^4$
  3. $q$ is a multiple of $10^4$
  4. $q$ has $3$ as a prime factor
Solution 3

A decimal of four places is a whole number over $10^4$. So $\dfrac{p}{q} = \dfrac{N}{10^4}$ for some whole $N$, which needs $q$ to divide $10^4$. And exactly four places means it is not also a decimal of three, so $q$ must not divide $10^3$. That is option (a), and it is the answer.

Option (b) is one case of (a) rather than the whole of it: $\dfrac{1}{16} = 0.0625$ stops after exactly four places with $q = 16$, and $16$ divides $10^4 = 2^4 5^4$ without being it. Option (c) has the divisibility the wrong way round; a $q$ larger than $10^4$ runs longer, not the same.

Option (d) would stop the decimal terminating at all, since a prime factor other than $2$ or $5$ is exactly what makes a decimal repeat. It is the one option that can be struck out without reading the rest of the question.
