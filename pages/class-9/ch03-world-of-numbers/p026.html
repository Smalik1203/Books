End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. By long division, write each as a terminating or a repeating decimal, whichever it turns out to be: $\dfrac{3}{50}$ and $\dfrac{2}{9}$.
  1. Prove that $\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.
  1. Write each in the form $\dfrac{p}{q}$, in lowest terms:
    1. $12.\overline{6}$
    2. $0.\overline{012}$
    3. $2.\overline{05}$
    4. $1.2\overline{35}$
  1. Locate $0.532$ and $1.15$ on the number line, magnifying the interval at each step to place the next digit.
  1. Find six rational numbers between 3 and 4.
  1. Find five rational numbers between $\dfrac{2}{5}$ and $\dfrac{3}{5}$.
  1. Find five rational numbers between $\dfrac{1}{6}$ and $\dfrac{2}{5}$.
  1. Find the rational number $x$ for which $\dfrac{x}{3} + \dfrac{x}{5} = \dfrac{16}{15}$.
  2. Let $a$ and $b$ be non-zero rationals with $a + b = 0$. Without choosing any numbers, decide whether $ab$ is positive or negative, and justify it.
  3. Without dividing, decide whether $\dfrac{18}{125}$ terminates. If it does, say in how many decimal places, and why that many.
  4. A rational number in lowest terms has denominator $2^3 \times 5$. How many decimal places will its expansion have? Explain what fixes the number.
  5. A terminating decimal has its last non-zero digit in the fourth place. Show it can be written as $\dfrac{p}{10^4}$ with $p$ not divisible by 10. Must the denominator in lowest terms be divisible by $2^4$ or by $5^4$? Give reasons.
  6. Let $a = \dfrac{7}{12}$ and $b = \dfrac{5}{6}$. Write both over one denominator $m$, as $\dfrac{k_1}{m}$ and $\dfrac{k_2}{m}$ with $k_2 - k_1 > 6$, then write five distinct rationals between them with whole-number numerators. Explain why $k_2 - k_1 > n + 1$ is needed to fit $n$ numbers in this way.
  7. Rationals $x$, $y$, $z$ satisfy $x + y + z = 0$ and $xy + yz + zx = 0$. Show that $x$, $y$ and $z$ must all be zero. (Consider $(x + y + z)^2$.)
