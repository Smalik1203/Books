Problem 2

In Fig. 5.34, lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel. Find the angle marked with a question mark.

  1. $115^\circ$
  2. $65^\circ$
  3. $25^\circ$
  4. $130^\circ$
Solution

At $m$, the angle at the top right corresponds to the $65^\circ$ angle at $l$, so it is $65^\circ$. The marked angle, at the bottom left, is vertically opposite it. Neither step needed a protractor, only the parallel lines.

top right angle at $m$ $=$ top right angle at $l$ $= 65^\circ$

marked angle $=$ top right angle at $m$ $= 65^\circ$

The answer is (b). Option (a) is the angle that forms a linear pair with it. Option (c) takes $65^\circ$ from $90^\circ$, but nothing in the figure is a right angle. Option (d) doubles the angle, as if the angles at the two crossings had to be added together. It is an easy slip when two crossings look alike.

Problem 3

A transversal crosses lines $l$ and $m$. The two interior angles on one side of it are $105^\circ$ at $l$ and $75^\circ$ at $m$. Which is true?

  1. $l$ and $m$ are parallel.
  2. $l$ and $m$ meet on that side of the transversal.
  3. $l$ and $m$ meet on the other side of the transversal.
  4. We cannot tell without measuring more angles.
