Example 10

A line $l$ is drawn, and a point X is marked on it. Using a ruler and a set square, draw a line $m$ parallel to $l$, so that the line $t$ through X at $90^\circ$ to $l$ meets $m$ 3 cm from X (Fig. 5.35). Why is $m$ parallel to $l$?

Solution.

Step 1 Set square along $l$, corner at X: draw $t$ right angle at X
Step 2 Mark Y on $t$, 3 cm from X measure with the ruler
Step 3 Set square along $t$, corner at Y: draw $m$ right angle at Y
Step 4 With $t$ as transversal, the corresponding angles are both $90^\circ$ equal corresponding angles
Answer $m$ is parallel to $l$, because the corresponding angles are equal.
Example 11

A transversal crosses lines $l$ and $m$, with the angles numbered as in Fig. 5.14. $\angle 4 = 108^\circ$ and $\angle 6 = 108^\circ$. Are $l$ and $m$ parallel?

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle 2 = \angle 4 = 108^\circ$ vertically opposite angles
Step 2 $\angle 2 = \angle 6 = 108^\circ$ equal corresponding angles
Answer Yes, $l$ and $m$ are parallel.
