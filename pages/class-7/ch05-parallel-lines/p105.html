Example 9 · Multiple correct

Two lines cross, and one of the angles is 62°. Which statements are correct?

Select all correct options.

  1. The angle opposite it is 62°.
  2. An angle next to it is 118°.
  3. The four angles add to 360°.
  4. The lines are perpendicular.

Solution.

Step 1Vertically opposite angles are equal, and angles next to each other form a linear pair: 180° − 62° = 118°.
Step 262° + 118° + 62° + 118° = 360°. No angle is 90°, so the lines are not perpendicular.
Answer(a), (b), (c)
Example 10 · Multiple correct

A transversal crosses lines l and m. Which facts are enough to show that l and m are parallel?

Select all correct options.

  1. A pair of corresponding angles are 75° and 75°.
  2. A pair of interior angles on the same side are 100° and 80°.
  3. A pair of vertically opposite angles at l are equal.
  4. A pair of alternate angles are 64° and 64°.

Solution.

Step 1Equal corresponding angles, equal alternate angles, or interior angles on the same side adding to 180° each show that the lines are parallel.
Step 2Vertically opposite angles are equal at every crossing, parallel or not, so (c) tells us nothing about m.
Answer(a), (b), (d)
Example 11 · Numerical answer

Two lines intersect. One of the angles is 30° more than the angle next to it. Find the larger angle, in degrees.

Enter the numerical value.

Solution.

Step 1The two angles form a linear pair: small + (small + 30°) = 180°.
Step 2Twice the small angle is 150°, so it is 75° and the larger is 105°.
