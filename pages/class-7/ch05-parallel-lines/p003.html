Measuring and reasoning

When you measure a linear pair, the angles may not add up to exactly $180^\circ$. Vertically opposite angles may not measure exactly the same. Why? A protractor may not be placed exactly. And a pencil line has some thickness, while a line in geometry has none.

In geometry we reason about perfect lines and shapes. We know a straight angle is $180^\circ$, so if a line splits it into two parts, the parts must add up to $180^\circ$. We get this by reasoning, not by measuring. Our measurements still come out very close to what reasoning predicts, and that is why geometry is so useful in science, art, engineering and building.

5.2Perpendicular Lines

Can you draw two intersecting lines so that all four angles are equal? What would each angle measure? The four angles add up to $360^\circ$, so each one would be $90^\circ$, a right angle.

Two lines that intersect at right angles are perpendicular. In Fig. 5.4, lines $l$ and $m$ are perpendicular to each other. A small square at the point where they meet shows the right angle.
