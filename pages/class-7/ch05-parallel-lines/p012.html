5.8Alternate Angles

In Fig. 5.21, the parallel lines $l$ and $m$ are crossed by the transversal $t$. The angles $\angle d$ and $\angle f$ lie between the two lines, on opposite sides of $t$. They are called alternate angles. $\angle c$ and $\angle e$ are also alternate angles.

To find the alternate angle of $\angle f$, first find its corresponding angle, $\angle b$. The angle vertically opposite $\angle b$ is $\angle d$, the alternate angle of $\angle f$.

If $\angle f$ is $120^\circ$, what is $\angle d$? $\angle b$ corresponds to $\angle f$ and the lines are parallel, so $\angle b = 120^\circ$. And $\angle d$ is vertically opposite $\angle b$, so $\angle d = 120^\circ$ too. In fact, we did not need the $120^\circ$ at all:

$\angle f = \angle b$, because they are corresponding angles and the lines are parallel.

$\angle b = \angle d$, because they are vertically opposite angles.

So $\angle f = \angle d$, whatever the size of $\angle f$.

Alternate angles

When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, each pair of alternate angles are equal.
