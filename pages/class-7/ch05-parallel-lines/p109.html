Long answer
  1. Prove that when a transversal crosses two parallel lines, the two interior angles on the same side of it add up to $180^\circ$. Use the angles in Fig. 5.24, and name the fact you use at each step.
  1. In a quadrilateral ABCD, AB is parallel to DC, AD is parallel to BC, and AC is joined. Take $\angle BAC = 42^\circ$ and $\angle ACB = 58^\circ$. Find $\angle DCA$, $\angle CAD$, $\angle DAB$ and $\angle ADC$.
  1. A transversal crosses parallel lines $l$ and $m$, with the angles numbered as in Fig. 5.14. $\angle 1$ is 3 times $\angle 2$. Find all eight angles.
  1. A transversal crosses two parallel lines, but not at right angles. Explain why the eight angles have exactly two different sizes, and why those two sizes add up to $180^\circ$.

    1. Find $\angle 6$, at the Station Road crossing.
    2. Find $\angle 5$.
    3. Find $\angle 4$, at the MG Road crossing.

    5. What do $\angle 3$ and $\angle 6$ add up to? Why?
