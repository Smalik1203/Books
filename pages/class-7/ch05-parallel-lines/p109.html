Why the other options are wrong

B2 The three angles make a straight angle, so $x = 180^\circ - 35^\circ - 70^\circ = 75^\circ$. The answer is (b). Option (a) adds the two known angles instead of taking them away.
B3 Vertically opposite angles are equal, so they add up to $180^\circ$ only when each is $90^\circ$. A is false in general, and R is true. The answer is (d). It is a linear pair that adds up to $180^\circ$.
B6 The angle forming a linear pair with the $110^\circ$ angle is $70^\circ$, and it corresponds to the $70^\circ$ angle, so A is true. R is true too, but A was shown with a linear pair and corresponding angles, not with opposite angles. The answer is (b).
C1 At every crossing the four angles are two of one size and two of the other, and corresponding angles match from line to line. So there are still only 2 sizes. The answer is (a).
C2 Since $l$ is parallel to $m$, and $m$ to $n$, the top right angle at $m$ is $40^\circ$, and so is the top right angle at $n$. The bottom right angle at $n$ forms a linear pair with it: $180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ$. The answer is (c). Option (a) is the top right angle.
C3 $\angle DAB = 180^\circ - 75^\circ = 105^\circ$, because AB is parallel to DC. Then $\angle CAB = 105^\circ - 40^\circ = 65^\circ$. The answer is (a). Option (b) is the whole of $\angle DAB$.
C4 Each of the six angles has exactly one angle vertically opposite it, and each pair is counted from both ends. So there are $6 \div 2 = 3$ pairs. The answer is (b).
C6 At each crossing the four angles are two linear pairs, which make $180^\circ + 180^\circ = 360^\circ$. Two crossings make $720^\circ$, whatever the $64^\circ$ was. The answer is (d). Option (a) counts one crossing only.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Name the fact used at every step. A line drawn through a bend turns one unknown angle into two known ones. And a question can often be settled without drawing: angles that must form a linear pair, but do not add up to $180^\circ$, cannot be drawn.
