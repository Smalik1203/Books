5.9Parallel Illusions

Look at Fig. 5.32. The long lines do not seem to be parallel; they seem to lean towards and away from each other. Check them with a ruler or a set square. What makes them look as if they are not parallel?

What this chapter showed
  1. Two intersecting lines make four angles. Vertically opposite angles are equal, and the two angles of a linear pair add up to $180^\circ$.
  2. Lines that intersect at right angles are perpendicular.
  3. Lines on the same plane that never meet are parallel.
  4. A transversal crossing two lines makes eight angles: four at each line, in matching positions called corresponding angles.
  5. When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, corresponding angles are equal. And if corresponding angles are equal, the lines are parallel.
  6. When a transversal crosses two parallel lines, alternate angles are equal, and interior angles on the same side add up to $180^\circ$.
  7. A fact shown by reasoning for every case, rather than by measuring a few, is proved.

To find an angle between parallel lines, look for the transversal first. Then ask which angle corresponds to it, which is opposite it, and which forms a linear pair with it.
