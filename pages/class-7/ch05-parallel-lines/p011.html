Are the two new lines parallel? How can we be sure? The set square makes each of them meet $l$ at $90^\circ$. The lines are in different places, but they make the same angle with $l$. So if we think of $l$ as a transversal, the two corresponding angles are both $90^\circ$. Equal corresponding angles tell us the lines are parallel.

Parallel lines by folding

We can do the same with paper folding. Suppose a crease $l$ and a point A are marked on a sheet. We already know how to fold a crease perpendicular to $l$. Fold one that passes through A, and call it $t$. Now fold a crease through A that is perpendicular to $t$, and call it $m$. Fig. 5.20 shows the steps. Lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel.

Why are lines $l$ and $m$ parallel? Think of $t$ as a transversal of $l$ and $m$.
