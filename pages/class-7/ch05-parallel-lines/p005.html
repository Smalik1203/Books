5.4Parallel and Perpendicular Lines by Folding

Take a square sheet of paper; an old newspaper cut square works well.

1. How are the opposite edges of the sheet related? They are to each other.

2. How are two edges next to each other related? They meet at a corner and make a right angle. They are to each other.

3. Fold the sheet in half across the middle, as in Fig. 5.7, and open it. How many parallel lines can you see now? How is the fold related to the upright edges?

4. Fold it once more across, so that the crease lies halfway between the fold and an edge. How many parallel lines are there now? What happens if you fold again? Is there a pattern?

5. Now make an upright fold. This new crease is to the earlier creases.

6. Fold the sheet along a diagonal. Can you make another fold that is parallel to that diagonal?

Here is another fold to try. Fold a square sheet in half from top to bottom and open it. Then fold each side edge in to meet that middle crease, and open it again. Fold one corner down so that its edge lies along a crease, and fold the opposite corner the same way. Which of the new creases are parallel to each other? Explain why.
