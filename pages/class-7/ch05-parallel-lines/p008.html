5.5Transversals

So far, one line has crossed one other line. What happens when one line crosses two lines? In Fig. 5.14, line $t$ crosses lines $l$ and $m$. A line that crosses two or more lines is called a transversal. When a transversal crosses two lines, eight angles are formed.

Can all eight angles have different measures? Why or why not? Could the five angles $\angle 2$, $\angle 3$, $\angle 4$, $\angle 5$ and $\angle 6$ all be different?

In Fig. 5.14, $\angle 1$ and $\angle 3$ are vertically opposite, so they are equal. There are four pairs of vertically opposite angles, and the two angles in each pair are equal. So the eight angles have at most four different measures.

And the five angles $\angle 2$, $\angle 3$, $\angle 4$, $\angle 5$ and $\angle 6$ cannot all be different either, because $\angle 2$ and $\angle 4$ are vertically opposite. In fact, once $\angle 1$ is known, all four angles at $l$ are known: $\angle 3$ is equal to it, and $\angle 2$ and $\angle 4$ each form a linear pair with it.

5.6Corresponding Angles

The transversal $t$ makes four angles at each line. Each angle at $l$ has a partner at $m$ in the same position: $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$ are both at the top left. Such a pair are corresponding angles. The other pairs of corresponding angles are $\angle 2$ and $\angle 6$, $\angle 3$ and $\angle 7$, and $\angle 4$ and $\angle 8$.
