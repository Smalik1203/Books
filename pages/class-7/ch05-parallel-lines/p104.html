Solution

The chapter tells us that equal corresponding angles make lines parallel, so look for a corresponding pair. The angle just above $l$, on the same side, forms a linear pair with the $105^\circ$ angle.

angle above $l$ $= 180^\circ - 105^\circ = 75^\circ$

That angle corresponds to the $75^\circ$ interior angle at $m$, and the two are equal. So $l$ and $m$ are parallel, and the answer is (a). Options (b) and (c) would need the corresponding angles to differ, and they do not. Option (d) is wrong: the two angles given already decide the question.

Problem 4

In Fig. 5.35, lines $l$ and $m$ are parallel. Find the angle at the bend.

  1. $115^\circ$
  2. $15^\circ$
  3. $65^\circ$
  4. $90^\circ$
Solution

Draw a line through the bend, parallel to $l$ and $m$, as in Fig. 5.33. The part of the bend above it and the $40^\circ$ angle are alternate angles, and so are the part below it and the $25^\circ$ angle.

angle at the bend $= 40^\circ + 25^\circ = 65^\circ$

The answer is (c). Option (a) is the angle that forms a linear pair with the bend. Option (b) takes one angle from the other, $40^\circ - 25^\circ$. Option (d) is a guess from how the bend looks, which a drawing that is not to scale can get wrong.
