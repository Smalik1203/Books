5.7Drawing Parallel Lines

Can you draw two parallel lines with a ruler and a set square? Fig. 5.18 (i) shows one way. Draw a line $l$. Place one short side of the set square along $l$, and draw a line along the other short side. Then slide the set square along $l$ and draw another line in the same way.

Are the two new lines parallel? How can we be sure? The set square makes each of them meet $l$ at $90^\circ$. The lines are in different places, but they make the same angle with $l$. So if we think of $l$ as a transversal, the two corresponding angles are both $90^\circ$. Equal corresponding angles tell us the lines are parallel.

Now draw two parallel lines along the long side of the set square, as in Fig. 5.18 (ii), sliding it along a ruler. How do you know these lines are parallel? Check that the corresponding angles are equal, by tracing and by measuring.
