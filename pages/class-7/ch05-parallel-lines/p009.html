5.6Corresponding Angles

The transversal $t$ makes four angles at each line. Each angle at $l$ has a partner at $m$ in the same position: $\angle 1$ and $\angle 5$ are both at the top left. Such a pair are corresponding angles. The other pairs of corresponding angles are $\angle 2$ and $\angle 6$, $\angle 3$ and $\angle 7$, and $\angle 4$ and $\angle 8$.

Drawing two lines with equal corresponding angles

Let us draw two lines and a transversal so that the eight angles have only two different measures. Follow the steps in Fig. 5.15.

1. Draw a line $l$ and a transversal $t$ that crosses it at a point X.

2. Measure $\angle a$ between $l$ and $t$. Say it is $60^\circ$. The other angle of its linear pair is then $120^\circ$, so we already have two measures.

3. Mark a point Y on $t$.

4. Through Y, draw a line $m$ that makes a $60^\circ$ angle with $t$ in the same position as $\angle a$. You can copy $\angle a$ with tracing paper, or use a protractor.

What do you notice about lines $l$ and $m$? They look parallel. $\angle a$ and $\angle b$ are corresponding angles, and they are equal.

Equal corresponding angles

If a transversal crosses two lines and a pair of corresponding angles are equal, the two lines are parallel.

Now turn the question round. If a transversal crosses two parallel lines, what can we say about the corresponding angles? In Fig. 5.16, $l$ and $m$ are parallel; the arrowheads say so. Trace $\angle a$ on tracing paper and place it over $\angle b$. Do the angles match? Check the other pairs of corresponding angles with a protractor.
