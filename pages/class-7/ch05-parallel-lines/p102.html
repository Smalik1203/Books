Two lines cross. Can three of the four angles be $60^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $100^\circ$?

Any three of the four angles include two that sit next to each other, and two neighbouring angles form a linear pair. The pair here is either $60^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, which make $120^\circ$, or $60^\circ$ and $100^\circ$, which make $160^\circ$. Neither is $180^\circ$. So no such crossing exists.

Solved Examples
Example 1 · Single correct

Two lines intersect, and one of the four angles is 4 times the angle next to it. What is the smallest of the four angles?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 36°
  2. 45°
  3. 144°
  4. 72°

Solution.

Step 1Angles next to each other form a linear pair, so together they make 180°: 4 parts + 1 part = 5 parts.
Step 2One part = 180° ÷ 5 = 36°. The angles are 36°, 144°, 36° and 144°.
Answer(a) 36°
Example 2 · Single correct

Two lines cross, and one of the four angles is 47°. Going round the point from that angle, what are the other three angles?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 43°, 47°, 43°
  2. 47°, 133°, 47°
  3. 133°, 47°, 133°
  4. 133°, 133°, 47°

Solution.

Step 1The next angle forms a linear pair with 47°: 180° − 47° = 133°.
Step 2Vertically opposite angles are equal, so the angles round the point go 47°, 133°, 47°, 133°.
Answer(c) 133°, 47°, 133°
