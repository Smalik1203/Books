Two lines cross. Can three of the four angles be $60^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $100^\circ$?

Any three of the four angles include two that sit next to each other, and two neighbouring angles form a linear pair. The pair here is either $60^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, which make $120^\circ$, or $60^\circ$ and $100^\circ$, which make $160^\circ$. Neither is $180^\circ$. So no such crossing exists.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · Linear pairs and vertically opposite angles

Example 1

Two lines intersect as in Fig. 5.2, and $\angle a = 72^\circ$. Find $\angle b$, $\angle c$ and $\angle d$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle b = 180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ$ linear pair
Step 2 $\angle c = \angle a = 72^\circ$ vertically opposite angles
Step 3 $\angle d = \angle b = 108^\circ$ vertically opposite angles
Answer $\angle b = 108^\circ$, $\angle c = 72^\circ$, $\angle d = 108^\circ$
Example 2

The blades of a pair of scissors are opened to $35^\circ$. Find the angle between the handles, and between a blade and its handle.

Solution.

Step 1 angle between the handles $= 35^\circ$ vertically opposite angles
Step 2 blade and its handle $= 180^\circ - 35^\circ = 145^\circ$ linear pair
Answer $35^\circ$ and $145^\circ$
Example 3

Two lines intersect. One of the angles is 4 times the angle next to it. Find all four angles.

Solution.

Step 1 $180^\circ \div (4 + 1) = 36^\circ$ linear pair, 5 parts
Step 2 $4 \times 36^\circ = 144^\circ$; the opposite angles are $36^\circ$ and $144^\circ$ vertically opposite angles
Answer $36^\circ$, $144^\circ$, $36^\circ$, $144^\circ$
