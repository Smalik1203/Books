Using What You Know
The chapter’s ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter gave you linear pairs, vertically opposite angles, and the three facts about a transversal of parallel lines. Every question below needs only those. What is new is that the question does not tell you which one to use. So the first step each time is to find the lines, the transversal, and the angles that sit in matching places.

Two angles form a linear pair, and one of them is $30^\circ$ more than the other. How big is each angle?

Together they make $180^\circ$. Take away the extra $30^\circ$ from the larger angle, and the two parts left are equal and add up to $150^\circ$. So the smaller angle is $75^\circ$, and the larger is $75^\circ + 30^\circ = 105^\circ$. Check: $75^\circ + 105^\circ = 180^\circ$. We did not need to guess; we split what was known into equal parts.

A transversal crosses lines $l$ and $m$. The two interior angles on one side are $72^\circ$ and $118^\circ$. Are $l$ and $m$ parallel?

If the lines were parallel, these interior angles would add up to $180^\circ$. But $72^\circ + 118^\circ = 190^\circ$. So the lines are not parallel. The interior angles on that side are too big by $10^\circ$ between them, so the lines must lean towards each other on the other side of the transversal, and meet there.
