$3 + 2 \times (y - 1)$

We could also notice that the 3 in Step 1 is itself $1 + 2$. That way of counting gives the expression $2y + 1$. Do both expressions give the right number at every step? Simplifying the first one shows that they are equal:

$3 + 2 \times (y - 1) = 3 + 2y - 2 = 2y + 1$

There is another way to count. Some matchsticks lie flat, along the top and bottom, and the rest slope across the middle. Step 2 has 2 flat matchsticks and 3 sloping ones. How many of each are there in Step 3 and in Step 4? Write an expression for each kind at Step $y$. Do the two add up to $2y + 1$?
