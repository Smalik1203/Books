Imagine the calendar carrying on past 30, with rows that never end. Will the diagonal sums be equal in every square of four dates? We cannot check every square, because there are endless squares. Instead, let the top-left number be $a$, and describe the others in words. The number to its right is 1 more than $a$. The number below it is 7 more, and the number on the diagonal is 8 more. So the square is the one in Fig. 4.8 (ii). Now add the diagonals.

$a + (a + 8) = a + a + 8 = 2a + 8$

$(a + 1) + (a + 7) = a + a + 1 + 7 = 2a + 8$

Both diagonal sums are $2a + 8$, whatever the value of $a$. So the sums are equal in every square of four dates. An algebraic expression has shown that a pattern will always hold, without checking every case. Check the expression $2a + 8$ on a square of your own.

Now take five dates in a plus shape, as in Fig. 4.8 (iii). Their sum is $8 + 14 + 15 + 16 + 22 = 75$, which is 5 times the centre number, 15. Try another plus shape. What do you notice?
