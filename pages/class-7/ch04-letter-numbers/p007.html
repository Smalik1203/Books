Example 5

Table 4.4 shows how many pencils and erasers a shop sold on three days. A pencil costs ₹$c$ and an eraser costs ₹$d$. Find the money the pencils earned.

On Day 1 the pencils earned $5c$ rupees, on Day 2 $3c$, and on Day 3 $10c$, so together they earned $5c + 3c + 10c$. This means $c$ is added $5 + 3 + 10$ times, which is the distributive property at work:

$5 \times c + 3 \times c + 10 \times c = (5 + 3 + 10) \times c = 18c$

Can that total be made any shorter? Its two terms have different letter-numbers, so they cannot be joined into one term. The total is already in its simplest form. Check that $5c + 3c + 10c$ and $18c$ take the same value when $c$ is replaced by a few different numbers.

Example 6

The rectangle in Fig. 4.3 is split into two smaller rectangles. Write an expression for the area of the whole rectangle.

The parts have areas $4v$ and $3v$ square units. The whole has sides $v$ and $4 + 3$, so its area is $7v$; it is also $4v + 3v$. So $4v + 3v = 7v$.

In Fig. 4.4, rectangle ABCD is split into AEFD and EBCF. The area of AEFD can again be found in two ways. Its sides are $n$ and $12 - 4$, which gives $8n$. Or we take the area of EBCF away from the area of ABCD, which gives $12n - 4n$. So $12n - 4n = 8n$.
