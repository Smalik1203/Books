Why the other options are wrong

B1 $2(n + 4) = 2n + 8$, because both $n$ and 4 are multiplied by 2. So A is false. R is true, and R is what shows A to be false. The answer is (d).
B6 A is true: it is the distributive property. R is also true, but it is only a sum about the numbers 7, 2 and 1, and says nothing about every $y$. The answer is (b).
C2 Row $r$ ends with $4r$, so row 19 ends with 76, and row 20 begins at 77. Column 3 of row 20 is $4 \times 19 + 3 = 79$. The answer is (d). Option (b) is the end of row 20.
C3 $3 \times 33 + 1 = 100$, so 33 squares use exactly 100 matchsticks. The answer is (b). Option (c) would need 103.
C5 Call the number $n$: $3n + 12 - 3n = 12$, whatever $n$ is. The answer is (c).
C6 $5n - 2$ gives 3, 8, 13, and so on. Position 3 is yellow, and every 5 positions the colours start again. The answer is (c).
C7 Charu’s total is $15p - 6q$, and $16p - 4q - (15p - 6q) = 16p - 4q - 15p + 6q = p + 2q$. The answer is (b). Option (a) forgets to change the sign of $-6q$, and (d) adds the two totals.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. To show two expressions are not equal, one value where they differ is enough, but to show they are equal, simplify them. A minus sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of every term inside. And a letter-number can prove that a pattern holds for all numbers, which no amount of trying examples can do.
