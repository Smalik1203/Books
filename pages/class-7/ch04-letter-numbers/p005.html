4.2Arithmetic Expressions Again

Chapter 2 showed how to read an expression as a sum of terms. Terms can be swapped and grouped without changing the value. We also removed brackets, including brackets with a minus sign in front of them, and used the distributive property: a multiple of a sum is the sum of the multiples. Let us use these ideas again.

Take $23 - 10 \times 2$. One term is a number. The other is a product, which we work out before adding the terms.

$23 - 10 \times 2 = $ $23$ $+$ $(-10 \times 2)$ $= 23 + (-20) = 3$

In $83 + 28 - 13 + 32$, all four terms are numbers. It is easier to swap and group them first.

$83 + 28 + (-13) + 32 = \big(83 + (-13)\big) + (28 + 32) = 70 + 60 = 130$

The expression $68 - (18 + 13)$ has a bracket with a minus sign in front. We can work out the bracket first, or remove the bracket and change the sign of each term inside. Both ways give the same value.

$68 - (18 + 13) = 68 - 31 = 37$

$68 - (18 + 13) = 68 - 18 - 13 = 50 - 13 = 37$

4.3Leaving Out the Multiplication Sign

Look at the sequence 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, and so on. These are the multiples of 4, in order, and each term is 4 more than the one before it. The third term is $4 \times 3$, and the 29th term is $4 \times 29$. So the term in position $n$ is

$4 \times n$

Here $n$ is a letter-number that stands for a position in the sequence.
