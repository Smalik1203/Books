Problem 4

$3(2a - b) - 2(a - 2b)$ is equal to

  1. $4a - 7b$
  2. $4a - b$
  3. $4a + b$
  4. $8a - 7b$
Solution

Multiply out each bracket, taking care with the minus sign in front of the second.

$3(2a - b) - 2(a - 2b) = 6a - 3b - 2a + 4b$

$= (6a - 2a) + (-3b + 4b) = 4a + b$

The answer is (c). Option (a) writes $-2 \times (-2b)$ as $-4b$. Option (b) forgets to multiply $b$ by 2. Option (d) adds $2a$ to $6a$ instead of taking it away.

Problem 5

A square of 9 dates, 3 rows by 3 columns, is chosen in a calendar. The sum of the 9 dates is always

  1. 9 times the centre date
  2. 3 times the centre date
  3. the centre date plus 72
  4. 9 times the top-left date
Solution

Let the centre date be $a$. The dates to its left and right are $a - 1$ and $a + 1$; the row above is $a - 8$, $a - 7$, $a - 6$, and the row below is $a + 6$, $a + 7$, $a + 8$. Every number taken away is matched by the same number added.

$(a - 8) + (a - 7) + (a - 6) + (a - 1) + a + (a + 1) + (a + 6) + (a + 7) + (a + 8) = 9a$

The answer is (a). Option (b) counts only one row. Option (c) adds the 8, 7 and 6 without taking them away. Option (d) would be true only if every date equalled the top-left one. Try it on a real calendar: the dates 3 to 5, 10 to 12 and 17 to 19 add up to 99, which is 9 times 11.

Across the five problems, every wrong option came from one of a few slips: a sign not changed after a minus, a term multiplied wrongly, unlike terms joined, or a formula tested at only one value. Checking an answer against a number you can work out by hand catches most of them.
