Letter-Numbers

4.1Letters for Numbers

In this chapter we learn a short way of writing relationships and patterns in mathematics. Writing them this way helps us think about them, and even explain why a pattern must always hold. The idea is simple: a letter stands for a number that we do not know yet, or that can change.

Example 1

Shabnam is 3 years older than Aftab. When Aftab was 10 years old, Shabnam was 13. Now Aftab is 18. How old is Shabnam?

Shabnam is always 3 years older, so we add 3 to Aftab’s age: $18 + 3 = 21$. Shabnam is 21 years old.

Whatever Aftab’s age is, we find Shabnam’s age in the same way: we add 3. In words, Shabnam’s age $=$ Aftab’s age $+ 3$. Table 4.1 shows this for a few ages.

Table 4.1 Shabnam’s age from Aftab’s age
Aftab’s ageShabnam’s age
4$4 + 3$
10$10 + 3$
23$23 + 3$
any age, $a$$a + 3$

Writing “Aftab’s age” again and again takes a long time, so we use a letter instead. Let $a$ stand for Aftab’s age in years, and $s$ for Shabnam’s age. Then

$s = a + 3$

If $a$ is 23, we replace $a$ by 23, and $s = 23 + 3 = 26$. Shabnam is then 26 years old.
