Problem 2

A pencil costs ₹$p$ and a notebook costs ₹$n$. Asha buys 4 pencils and 3 notebooks and pays with a ₹100 note. How much change does she get, in rupees?

  1. $100 - 4p + 3n$
  2. $100 - (4p + 3n)$
  3. $100 - 7pn$
  4. $4p + 3n - 100$
Solution

She spends $4p + 3n$ rupees in all, and the change is what is left of ₹100 after that. The whole amount spent is taken away, so it goes in a bracket.

$\text{change} = 100 - (4p + 3n) = 100 - 4p - 3n$

The answer is (b). Option (a) takes away only the pencils and adds the notebooks. Option (c) joins unlike terms into one. Option (d) is the spending minus 100, which is the change with its sign turned round.

Problem 3

Hexagons are made with matchsticks, in a row, each one sharing a side with the next. Which expression gives the number of matchsticks for $h$ hexagons?

  1. $6h$
  2. $6h - 1$
  3. $5h + 6$
  4. $5h + 1$
Solution

One hexagon needs 6 matchsticks. Each new hexagon shares one side with the one before, so it adds only 5. For $h$ hexagons that is 6 and then $h - 1$ lots of 5.

$6 + 5(h - 1) = 6 + 5h - 5 = 5h + 1$

The answer is (d). Option (a) counts every shared side twice. Option (b) gives 11 for 2 hexagons, which is right, but 17 for 3 hexagons instead of 16. Option (c) gives 11 for a single hexagon. One value is not enough to test a formula; (b) passed the test at $h = 2$ and still failed.

In Problems 2 and 3 the expression came from describing the situation in words first: what is spent and what is left over, or one hexagon and then 5 more matchsticks for each one after it. The words gave the expression; the letters only made it short.
