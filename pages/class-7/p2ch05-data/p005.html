Know your onions!

Table 5.3 gives the price of onions, in rupees per kilogram, in two towns in each month of a year. Where are onions costlier, in your view?

Five students give their views.

  • Khushboo: “I think Wahapur is costlier, because it has the highest price, ₹60.”
  • Nafisa: “I added the twelve prices for each town. Yahapur’s total is 458, and Wahapur’s is 450.”
  • Vishal: “Wahapur is costlier, because 3 of its prices are ₹50 or more.”
  • Sampat: “I compared the towns month by month. Yahapur was costlier in 6 months and Wahapur in 5, and in 1 month the prices were the same. So I feel Yahapur is costlier.”
  • Jithin: “The difference between the highest and lowest prices is $59 - 24 = 35$ in Yahapur, and $60 - 17 = 43$ in Wahapur.”

So we can compare data by its smallest and largest values, its total, its average, and the difference between the largest and smallest. Can you think of other ways?

A picture of the data helps too. Fig. 5.4 shows one kind of picture, called a dot plot. Each price is a dot placed above its value on a number line. When a value occurs more than once, the dots are stacked one above another. A dot plot shows at a glance how the data varies, and where it bunches together.

Does the dot plot show all the data in Table 5.3? Can you read from it the price of onions in Yahapur in January?

A dot plot puts the values in order, but it loses the months they belonged to. In return, it lets us group the values in any way we like, as Vishal did. Wahapur has 2 prices between 11 and 20, and Yahapur has none. We can see where the values bunch together and where they spread out: Wahapur’s prices are more spread out than Yahapur’s. The highest and lowest values are also easy to spot.
