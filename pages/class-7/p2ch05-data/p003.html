Vaishnavi says, “This time Shubman did better. His total is 110 runs, and Yashasvi’s is only 96.” What do you think of her reason? Is it fair to compare the two totals, when the two players did not play the same number of matches? Look back at Table 5.2, and count the matches that each player actually played, before you decide.

Shreyas points out, “But Yashasvi made his 96 runs in 4 matches, and Shubman took 5 matches to make his 110.”

So how do we decide who did better? It is often hard to compare two groups of numbers and say that one is clearly better. Can a single number stand for a whole group of numbers? Could one number describe Shubman’s batting in this series, and another describe Yashasvi’s? Discuss this in class.

We have already used one such number: the total. But when the groups are of different sizes, comparing their totals is not fair. A player who plays more matches has more chances to score runs, so a bigger total does not always mean better batting.

A player scores more in some matches and less in others. We can find one number for the group by evening out these highs and lows. We add the runs from all the matches, and divide by the number of matches played. This number is called the average, or the arithmetic mean, of the data.

Shubman’s average $= 110 \div 5 = 22$ runs a match

Yashasvi’s average $= 96 \div 4 = 24$ runs a match

In this series, Yashasvi’s average is higher than Shubman’s.

The arithmetic mean

The average, or arithmetic mean, or simply the mean, of some data is

$\text{mean} = \dfrac{\text{sum of all the values in the data}}{\text{number of values in the data}}$

The average as a fair share

An average can also be read as a fair share: what each person gets when a total is shared equally. It is the number each would have if the total were shared out so that nobody had more than anyone else.

Shreyas and 4 of his friends collected 3, 8, 10, 5 and 4 guavas. Parag and 5 of his friends collected 5, 4, 6, 3, 4 and 8 guavas. Each group shares its guavas equally among its members. In which group does each member get more guavas? Make a guess before you work it out, and then check whether your guess was right.

First we find how many guavas each group collected. Then we share that total among the members of the group.

Shreyas’s group: $3 + 8 + 10 + 5 + 4 = 30$ guavas, and $30 \div 5 = 6$ guavas each

Parag’s group: $5 + 4 + 6 + 3 + 4 + 8 = 30$ guavas, and $30 \div 6 = 5$ guavas each

Both groups collected 30 guavas. But each member of Shreyas’s group gets 1 guava more than each member of Parag’s group, because Parag’s group has one more person to share with.
