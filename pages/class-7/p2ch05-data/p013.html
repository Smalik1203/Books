A graph often holds a lot of information, and it can be hard to take in. Two steps help us make sense of it.

Step 1: See what is given. Look at how the graph is arranged, what scale it uses, and what patterns the data shows.

  • For each organisation there are three bars side by side, for the years 2021, 2022 and 2023. The scale is 1 unit length for 20 rockets. The numbers are along the bottom.
  • “Others” puts together many organisations around the world, to keep the graph short.
  • In the graph of onion prices, the months are in order from January to December, so that we can see how the prices change over time. Here the organisations could be put in another order without changing what the graph means.

Guidelines at 5, 10 and 15 along the bottom would help us to estimate the smaller numbers. Why do you think the graph leaves them out?

Step 2: Draw conclusions. Think about what each of your observations tells you.

  • The USA, China and Russia launched the most rockets in these years.
  • SpaceX launched about twice as many rockets in 2022 as in 2021, and about 35 more in 2023 than in 2022.
  • Arianespace launched fewer rockets each year.
  • United Launch Alliance launched more rockets in 2022 than in 2021. In 2023 it launched fewer than in either of the other two years.
  • The other organisations together launched about 25 rockets in 2023.

Which of these statements can be justified using the graph?
