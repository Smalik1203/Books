Example 7

The median of 2, 3, 3, 5, 7, 10 is

  1. 3
  2. 5
  3. 4
  4. 8

Solution.

Step 1 6 values in order; the middle two are the 3rd and 4th, 3 and 5 even number of values
Step 2 Median $= (3 + 5) \div 2 = 4$
Answer (c)
Example 8

Eight students scored 34, 41, 28, 45, 37, 30, 49 and 36 marks out of 50 in a test. Find the median and the range of the marks.

Solution.

Step 1 In order: 28, 30, 34, 36, 37, 41, 45, 49
Step 2 Median $= (36 + 37) \div 2 = 36.5$ 4th and 5th values
Step 3 Range $= 49 - 28 = 21$ largest minus smallest
Answer median 36.5 marks, range 21 marks

Example 9

The scores of six players are 12, 15, 14, 13, 16 and 60. Which statement is true?

  1. The mean is less than the median.
  2. The mean and the median are equal.
  3. Leaving out 60 changes the median more than the mean.
  4. The mean is greater than the median.

Solution.

Step 1 Mean $= 130 \div 6 \approx 21.7$
Step 2 In order: 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 60; median $= (14 + 15) \div 2 = 14.5$
Step 3 $21.7 \gt 14.5$ 60 pulls the mean up
Step 4 Without 60: mean $= 70 \div 5 = 14$, median $= 14$ the mean moves more
Answer (d)
