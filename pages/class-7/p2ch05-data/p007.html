Can you think of a number that would represent this data better? One way is to put the values in order and pick the one in the middle. This number is called the median.

To find the median height of Poovizhi’s family, we first put the heights in order: 118, 165, 170, 173, 175. The middle value is 170, so the median height is 170 cm.

Now for Yaangba’s family. In order, the heights are 155, 160, 164, 165, 169, 173. The median has as many values below it as above it. This data has 6 values, an even number, so there is no single middle value. In such a case we take the average of the two middle values. So the median height of Yaangba’s family is $(164 + 165) \div 2 = 164.5$ cm.

In this case, does the median represent the heights of the families better than the average does?

In Poovizhi’s family, the youngest child’s height is very different from the heights of everyone else. A value like this is called an outlier. Outliers are values that lie far away from the rest of the values in the data.

In Yaangba’s family there is no outlier, and the mean and the median are close to each other. In Poovizhi’s family, the outlier pulls the mean well below the median.

Are you a bookworm?

After the summer holidays, a teacher asked each student to write on a slip of paper how many short stories they had read. The slips said 6, 8, 5, 15, 3, 0, 2, 7, 12, 40, 10, 5, 0, 1 and 8. Before you calculate, guess: will the mean be less than the median, or greater? Then find the mean and the median.

Draw a dot plot of this data on a number line from 0 to 40, and mark the mean and the median on it.

The median is 6. This means that at least half of the class read 6 stories or more. Which of the values would you call an outlier? Find the mean and the median without it. What change do you notice? Remember that without the outlier there are 14 values, an even number, so the median is the average of the two middle values.

The average is not always a good representative of data that has outliers. One very large or very small value changes the total a lot, and so it changes the average too. The height of 118 cm is an outlier at the lower end of its data, and 40 stories is an outlier at the higher end of its data. In both cases the median hardly changed when the outlier was left out. The median depends only on the values in the middle, so a single value far out at one end cannot move it much.
