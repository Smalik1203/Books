Averages around us

The arithmetic mean is used to stand for a set of data in mathematics, science, economics, the study of society, sport, biology and many other fields. One reason is that it is simple to define and easy to understand. It also uses every value in the data, so each value, large or small, plays its part in the result, and no value is left out. Here are some statements that use averages.

  • In July, Jharkhand gets an average of 37.2 mm of rain a day.
  • This year my scooter has run about 45 km on a litre of petrol, on average.
  • Wheat yields average 4.7 tonnes a hectare in Punjab and 2.9 tonnes a hectare in Bihar.
  • Smartphone users check their phones 58 times a day on average.
  • An average Indian produces 0.45 kg of waste a day.
  • From 2017 to 2024, an average of 3126 Indian feature films were released each year.

Outliers and medians

Does an average always sum up a collection of values well? If it does not, what else can we use? Let us find out.

Here are the heights of the members of Yaangba’s family and of Poovizhi’s family.

Yaangba’s family: 169 cm, 173 cm, 155 cm, 165 cm, 160 cm, 164 cm

Poovizhi’s family: 170 cm, 173 cm, 165 cm, 118 cm, 175 cm

Find the average height of each family. Can we say that Yaangba’s family is taller than Poovizhi’s family?

The average height of Poovizhi’s family is 160.2 cm, which is less than the average of Yaangba’s family, about 164.3 cm. Yet most members of Poovizhi’s family are taller. Their average is lower because one child is much younger, and much shorter than everyone else. In fact, their average of 160.2 cm is less than the heights of 4 of the 5 members. Here the average does not represent the data very well.
