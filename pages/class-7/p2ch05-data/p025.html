1. Estimate the lengths of a pen, an eraser, your palm, your geometry box and your maths notebook, and then measure them. Make a table of your estimates, your measurements and the differences between them, and draw a double bar graph of the estimates and the measurements. How close were your estimates? Find the average difference between the estimated and measured lengths.
  1. Aditi likes solving puzzles, and has started on the easy Sudoku puzzles. The times she took, in seconds, were 410, 400, 370, 340, 360, 400, 320, 330, 310, 320, 290, 380, 280, 270, 230, 220 and 240. The first nine times are from Week 1 and the rest from Week 2.
    1. Draw a dot plot of the data on a number line from 200 to 420, with the two weeks in different colours.
    2. Describe the mean and the median of each week, and anything else you notice about the data.
  1. Project on your own. Choose at least one of these.
    1. How long is a sentence? Take two textbooks of different subjects, and choose a page full of text from each. (i) Use a dot plot to show how many words the sentences on each page have. (ii) Compare the two pages using the mean and the median.
    2. What is in a name? Write down the names of all your classmates. (i) Find the mean and the median length of a name, counting letters. (ii) Show the data in a picture, and describe how it varies and where its centre lies. (iii) Which first letters are more common, and which are less common? (iv) What is the median first letter? What does it tell you about the number of names starting with A to M and with N to Z? (v) Draw a double bar graph of the number of boys’ names and girls’ names that start and end with a vowel; start with a vowel and end with a consonant; start with a consonant and end with a vowel; start and end with a consonant.
  1. A long project on your own. In and out: every day for a month, count how many times you step out of your house. (i) Make a dot plot, and describe how the data varies and where its centre lies. (ii) Do you find anything interesting in the data? Share your observations. (iii) You can ask a family member or a friend to do this as well.
