Problem 3

The scores of six players are 12, 15, 14, 13, 16 and 60. Which statement is true?

  1. The mean is less than the median.
  2. The mean and the median are equal.
  3. Leaving out 60 changes the median more than the mean.
  4. The mean is greater than the median.
Solution

mean $= 130 \div 6$, which is more than 21

in order: 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 60, so median $= (14 + 15) \div 2 = 14.5$

The outlier 60 is at the high end and pulls the mean up, so the answer is (d), and (a) and (b) are false. Without 60 the mean becomes $70 \div 5 = 14$, a drop of more than 7, while the median becomes 14, a drop of only 0.5. So (c) has it the wrong way round. An outlier moves the mean more than the median, because every value counts towards the total, but only the middle values decide the median. Here one very large score has lifted the mean above five of the six scores, so the mean describes almost nobody in the group.

Problem 4

A cricketer’s scores in a series are 45, 0, 30, — and 25, where the dash means that he did not play. His average score per match played is

  1. 25
  2. 20
  3. 27.5
  4. 33 and a third
Solution

He played 4 matches, including the one where he scored 0, and made $45 + 0 + 30 + 25 = 100$ runs. So his average is $100 \div 4 = 25$: (a). Option (b) divides by 5, counting the match he missed. Option (d) divides by 3, leaving out the 0, which is a real score. Option (c) is the median of the four scores, not the mean. A score of 0 belongs in the count because the match was played; a match not played has no score at all, so it does not. Dividing by the right number matters as much as adding the scores correctly, so always count the values first.
