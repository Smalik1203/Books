Clubbing the columns

Earlier we looked at the monthly prices of onions in Yahapur and Wahapur, in Table 5.3. We could draw a column graph for each town. Instead, Fig. 5.10 joins the two graphs into one: the columns for the two towns stand side by side in each month. Check that the graph matches the table.

The two colours keep the data of the two towns apart. A graph like this is called a clustered column graph. Since each cluster has two columns, it is also called a double column graph. What scale does this graph use?

The heights of the two columns in a month tell us in which town onions were costlier that month. We can also estimate the difference in price, using the guidelines across the graph. Is it easier now to compare the prices in the two towns month by month?
