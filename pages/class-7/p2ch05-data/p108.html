Why the other options are wrong

A7 The 5 bags weigh $4 \times 5 + 5 = 25$ kg, and $25 \div 5 = 5$ kg: (a). A value equal to the mean leaves the mean unchanged. Option (d) is the total, not the mean.
B2 110 cm is an outlier that pulls the mean down to $725 \div 5 = 145$ cm, below four of the five heights. In order the heights are 110, 150, 152, 155, 158, so the median is 152 cm: (d). Option (c) is the median of the list as it was given, not in order.
B3 She played 4 matches and made 120 runs, so the mean is 30. In order the scores are 0, 30, 40, 50, and the median is $(30 + 40) \div 2 = 35$: (a). Option (b) divides by 5, counting the match she missed.
B4 The mean goes from 12 to $136 \div 4 = 34$, up by 22. The median goes from 12 to $(12 + 14) \div 2 = 13$, up by 1. The answer is (b).
B6 The five numbers add up to 100. Without 40 the other four add up to 60, and $60 \div 4 = 15$: (c). Option (d) divides 80 by 5.
C1 Only (b) adds up to 50 with 8 in the middle. The data in (a) and (d) has a mean of 8, and the data in (c) has a median of 10.
C2 The sections are the same size, so the total is $25 \times 150 + 25 \times 154 = 7600$ cm, and $7600 \div 50 = 152$ cm: (a). Only when the sizes differ, as in Problem 5, does the average of the two means fail.
C7 The median is $(5 + x) \div 2$. For $x$ from 5 to 9 this runs from 5 to 7. A median of 8 needs $x = 11$, which is more than 9. The answer is (d).

Three things from this section are worth keeping. A mean multiplied by the number of values gives the total, and the total is often the quickest way into a question. Before trusting a mean, look for an outlier; if there is one, the median may say more. And count only the values that belong: a score of 0 is a value, and a match not played is not.
