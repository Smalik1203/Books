The data gives the hours of daylight in each month in the two cities, counting the time when the Sun is at least partly above the horizon. Dividing a month’s hours of daylight by the number of days in the month gives the average hours of daylight a day. Fig. 5.13 shows these averages as a clustered column graph. For example, City 1 has 564 hours of daylight in the 30 days of June, and $564 \div 30 = 18.8$, so it has about 19 hours a day.
