Example 8

Madhubanti is planning a party. She will buy snacks from the chaat shop in town, where a plate of snacks costs ₹25. The shop also charges a fixed ₹50 to deliver the snacks to her house.

There are 5 people in her family, including her. Her parents say she can spend ₹500 on the party. Every person at the party, family or friend, will get one plate. How many friends can she invite?

Fatima solved this with a rough diagram, shown in Fig. 7.10.

If we take the fixed delivery charge out of ₹500, Madhubanti has ₹450 left. So the question becomes: how many plates at ₹25 each can be bought for ₹450? The answer is $450 \div 25 = 18$. Madhubanti’s family are 5 of these 18 people, so she can invite $18 - 5 = 13$ friends.

Mahesh used a letter-number $p$ for the number of people at the party, including Madhubanti and her family. The cost is then $25p + 50$, and it must be 500.

$25p + 50 = 500$

$25p = 500 - 50$   (subtracting 50 from both sides)

$25p = 450$

$p = 450 \div 25 = 18$   (dividing both sides by 25)

So 18 people can be at the party, including the 5 in her family. That means 13 friends can be invited.
