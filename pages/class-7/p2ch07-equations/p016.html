7.4A Pinch of History

In ancient India, writing expressions with symbols and solving equations made from them was an important part of mathematics. This area was called bijaganita, and today we call it algebra. Ganita means calculation, so bijaganita is calculation with seeds. The word bija means seed. A whole tree is hidden inside a seed, and the answer to a problem is hidden inside an unknown number. Solving the problem is like helping the tree grow: step by step, we uncover what is hidden.

We have already met Brahmagupta’s work on integers and fractions. In Chapter 18 of his book Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE), he also explained how to add, subtract and multiply unknown numbers written as letters, much as we use $x$ or $y$ today. This chapter is one of the earliest known works on algebra anywhere in the world. The mathematician David Mumford, a winner of the Fields Medal, has described Brahmagupta as the key person in creating algebra as we know it.
