Brahmagupta’s rule

Problems like this were well understood in ancient India. A systematic way to solve problems with one unknown was first given by Aryabhata (499 CE), and Brahmagupta described it in his Brahmasphutasiddhanta (628 CE). Let us understand his method. Look at equations of this form:

$5x + 4 = 3x + 8$   or   $3x - 6 = 2x + 4$

Can we find a formula to solve such equations? For the first equation, can you do some operations on 5, 4, 3 and 8 that give the solution straight away? In a similar way, can you solve the second equation using the numbers 3, $-6$, 2 and 4?

To find a formula, we write the four numbers as A, B, C and D, so that every equation of this form looks like

$Ax + B = Cx + D$

Brahmagupta gave the solution of such an equation by this formula:

$x = \dfrac{D - B}{A - C}$

With this rule, we can solve the equation $650m + 4000 = 500m + 5050$ from Example 9 with just one calculation:

$m = \dfrac{5050 - 4000}{650 - 500}$

Use the formula to solve the equation $2x + 3 = 4x + 5$.

Ancient Indian mathematicians were very good at turning hard mathematical ideas into simple methods, so that anyone could use them to solve problems.

Bijaganita, or algebra, is the branch of mathematics that uses letters to solve mathematical problems. We have seen some examples in this chapter. By studying algebra, we learn to find general patterns in numbers, in shapes and in everyday situations. We write these patterns in the language of algebra, which is both exact and powerful.

Algebra also lets us show why a claim is true, such as the claim that the sum of two odd numbers is always even. And it helps us solve problems of many kinds. Ancient Indian mathematicians knew how powerful algebra is. We hope that you see this too, and enjoy using it!
