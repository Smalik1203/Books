This is the RHS, so $\frac{11}{5}$ is indeed the solution of the equation.

Example 5

Solve the equation $11y + (-5) = 61$.

Solution.

To leave only the term $11y$ on the LHS, we remove the term $-5$.

Step 1 $11y + (-5) - (-5) = 61 - (-5)$ subtract $-5$ from both
Step 2 $11y = 66$
Step 3 $y = 6$ since $11 \times 6 = 66$
Step 4 $11y \div 11 = 66 \div 11$, so $y = 6$ or divide by 11
Answer $y = 6$

Can you check that $y = 6$ is the correct solution?

Example 6

Solve $6y + 7 = 4y + 21$.

Solution.

Both sides of this equation have a term with the unknown. How can we bring the unknown terms to one side?

Step 1 $6y + 7 - 4y = 4y + 21 - 4y$, so $2y + 7 = 21$ subtract $4y$ from both
Step 2 $2y + 7 - 7 = 21 - 7$, so $2y = 14$ subtract 7 from both
Step 3 $2y \div 2 = 14 \div 2$ divide both by 2
Answer $y = 7$

It is always a good idea to check your solution.
