Brahmagupta’s rule gives the solution of $Ax + B = Cx + D$ as $x = \dfrac{D - B}{A - C}$. What happens if we use it on $4x + 1 = 4x + 9$?

Here $A = C = 4$, so $A - C = 0$, and the rule asks us to divide by 0, which we cannot do. Look at the equation itself: subtracting $4x$ from both sides leaves $1 = 9$, so it has no solution. The rule works only when $A$ and $C$ are different, and the equation tells us why.

Type 1 · Writing an equation and checking a solution

Example 1

Write an equation for “4 more than three times a number is 19”. Name its LHS and its RHS.

Solution.

Step 1 Three times the number $= 3n$ call the number $n$
Step 2 $3n + 4 = 19$ 4 more is 19
Answer $3n + 4 = 19$; the LHS is $3n + 4$ and the RHS is 19
Example 2

Is $y = 5$ a solution of $3y - 4 = 2y + 1$? Is $y = 4$ a solution?

Solution.

Step 1 $y = 5$: LHS $= 3 \times 5 - 4 = 11$, RHS $= 2 \times 5 + 1 = 11$ the sides are equal
Step 2 $y = 4$: LHS $= 3 \times 4 - 4 = 8$, RHS $= 2 \times 4 + 1 = 9$ the sides differ
Answer $y = 5$ is a solution; $y = 4$ is not
Example 3

Two equal packets of dal and 1 kg balance 5 kg. Write an equation, and find a packet’s weight.

Solution.

Step 1 $2p + 1 = 5$, so $2p = 4$ and $p = 2$ 1 kg off both pans
Step 2 $2 \times 2 + 1 = 5$ check
Answer $2p + 1 = 5$; one packet weighs 2 kg
