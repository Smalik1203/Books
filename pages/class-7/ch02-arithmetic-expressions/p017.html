What this chapter showed
  1. An arithmetic expression has a value, and different expressions can have the same value.
  2. Two expressions can often be compared by reasoning, without working out either one.
  3. Brackets and terms remove any doubt about how to work out an expression. The terms are the parts joined by + signs, once every subtraction is written as adding the inverse.
  4. The terms of an expression can be added in any order and grouped in any way (the commutative and associative properties of addition).
  5. When brackets with a minus sign in front of them are removed, each term inside changes its sign.
  6. A multiple of a sum is the sum of the multiples: $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$ for any numbers. This is the distributive property.

Before you work anything out, find the terms. Then each term can be worked out on its own.
