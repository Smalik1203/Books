Example 8

A class plays “Fire in the mountain, run, run, run!”. The teacher calls out a number, the children form groups of that size, and anyone left over is out. Ruby is resting, so 33 children are playing. The teacher calls out “5”. Ruby writes $6 \times 5 + 3$. Why?

Solution.

Step 1 33 children make 6 groups of 5, with 3 children left over the teacher’s number
Step 2 $33 = 6 \times 5 + 3$, which means 3 more than $6 \times 5$
Answer As a sum of terms it is $6 \times 5$ $+$ $3$

What would Ruby write if the teacher called out 4, or 7? Try it for your own class.

Example 9

Raghu buys 100 kg of rice and packs it into 2 kg packets. He already had four 2 kg packets. Write an expression for the number of packets he has now.

Solution.

Step 1 The new packets number $100 \div 2$ rice into packets
Answer So he has $4$ $+$ $100 \div 2$ $= 4 + 50 = 54$ packets
Example 10

Kannan has to pay a shopkeeper ₹432. He has ₹1 and ₹5 coins, and ₹10, ₹20, ₹50 and ₹100 notes. How can he pay?

There are many ways. Here are two.

Solution.

Step 1 $432 = 4 \times 100 + 1 \times 20 + 1 \times 10 + 2 \times 1$ one way
Step 2 $432 = 8 \times 50 + 1 \times 10 + 4 \times 5 + 2 \times 1$ another way
Answer The first way is 4 notes of ₹100, one of ₹20, one of ₹10 and two ₹1 coins

Name the terms of each expression, and find another way to pay ₹432.
