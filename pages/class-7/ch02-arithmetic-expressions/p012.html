Removing brackets — II

Example 15

Lhamo and Norbu go to a hotel. Each of them orders a vegetable cutlet for ₹43 and a rasgulla for ₹24. Write an expression for what they pay together.

Each person’s share is $43 + 24$. Can the total be written as $2 \times 43 + 24$? As a sum of terms, that is $2 \times 43$ $+$ $24$, which means 24 more than $2 \times 43$. We want double of $43 + 24$, so we need brackets: $2 \times (43 + 24)$.

That is the same as two cutlets and two rasgullas:

$2 \times (43 + 24) = 2 \times 43 + 2 \times 24$

If their friend Sangmu joins them and orders the same, what is the expression for the total?

Example 16

In a Republic Day parade, the scouts march in 4 rows with 5 in each row, and the guides march in 3 rows with 5 in each row (Fig. 2.4). How many are marching?

There are $4 \times 5$ scouts and $3 \times 5$ guides, so $4 \times 5 + 3 \times 5$ children in all. We can also count the rows first, $4 + 3$, and multiply by the 5 children in each row: $(4 + 3) \times 5$. So

$4 \times 5 + 3 \times 5 = 20 + 15 = 35$

$(4 + 3) \times 5 = 7 \times 5 = 35$
