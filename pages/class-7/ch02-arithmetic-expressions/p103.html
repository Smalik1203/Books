Problem 2

The value of $40 - 4 \times 6 + 12$ is

  1. 228
  2. 28
  3. 4
  4. 52
Solution

Find the terms first, then add them.

$40$ $+$ $(-4 \times 6)$ $+$ $12$ $= 40 + (-24) + 12 = 28$

The answer is (b). Option (a) works from left to right, $(40 - 4) \times 6 + 12$. Option (c) subtracts both $4 \times 6$ and 12, as if they were inside one bracket. Option (d) adds 24 instead of taking it away.

Working from left to right feels natural, because that is how we read a sentence. An expression is not read that way. It is split into terms first, each term is worked out on its own, and only then are the terms added. So $40 - 4 \times 6$ never means $36 \times 6$.

Problem 3

Tinku says that $27 \times 99 = 27 \times 100 - 99$. The correct value of $27 \times 99$ is

  1. 2601
  2. 2673
  3. 2727
  4. 2700
Solution

99 is $100 - 1$, so $27 \times 99$ is 27 times 100 minus 27 times 1.

$27 \times 99 = 27 \times 100 - 27 \times 1 = 2700 - 27 = 2673$

The answer is (b). Option (a) is Tinku’s expression: he took away 99 instead of 27. Option (c) adds 27 instead of taking it away. Option (d) treats 99 as if it were 100.

Tinku’s slip is easy to make. The distributive property takes away $27 \times 1$, the thing being multiplied, and not the 99. Checking with small numbers catches it: $2 \times 9$ is 18, and $2 \times 10 - 9$ is 11, but $2 \times 10 - 2$ is 18. A check with small numbers takes a few seconds, and it catches this kind of slip every time. It works because $2 \times 9$ is nine 2s, which is one 2 fewer than ten 2s.
