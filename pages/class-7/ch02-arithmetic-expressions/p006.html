Adding terms in any order

In an expression that has only additions, the terms can be added in any order and grouped in any way. The value stays the same. Swapping is called the commutative property of addition, and grouping is called the associative property of addition.

When an expression also has multiplications or divisions, and no brackets, we first work out each term. Then we add the terms.

$30$ $+$ $5 \times 4$ $= 30 + 20 = 50$

$5 \times (3 + 2)$ $+$ $7 \times 8$ $+$ $3$ $= 25 + 56 + 3 = 84$

In the second expression, we work out $3 + 2$ first, because it is in brackets, and multiply the answer by 5.

Manasa adds up the numbers 1342, 774, 8611, 9055 and 1022. After five minutes she gets 11749, and then she sees that she forgot the fourth number, 9055. Does she have to start again? No. The terms can be added in any order, so she adds the missing one last: $11749 + 9055 = 20804$.

The order of actions matters in daily life too. Putting on a watch and a cap can be done in either order. But you must put on your socks before your shoes!

More expressions and their terms

Example 7

Amu, Charan, Madhu and John order four dosas at ₹23 each, and leave the waiter a tip of ₹5. Write an expression for the total cost.

The dosas cost $4 \times 23$ rupees. Adding the tip gives $4 \times 23$ $+$ $5$ $= 92 + 5 = 97$. They pay ₹97.

If 7 friends order a dosa each and tip ₹5, what do they pay? Name the terms.

Example 8

A class plays “Fire in the mountain, run, run, run!”. The teacher calls out a number, the children form groups of that size, and anyone left over is out. Ruby is resting, so 33 children are playing. The teacher calls out “5”. Ruby writes $6 \times 5 + 3$. Why?

33 children make 6 groups of 5, with 3 children left over. So $33 = 6 \times 5 + 3$, which means 3 more than $6 \times 5$. As a sum of terms it is $6 \times 5$ $+$ $3$.

What would Ruby write if the teacher called out 4, or 7? Try it for your own class.
