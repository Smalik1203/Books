Example 4

Mallesh brings 30 marbles to the playground. Arun brings 5 bags with 4 marbles in each bag. How many marbles did they bring altogether?

Solution.

Step 1 Mallesh writes the expression $30 + 5 \times 4$
Step 2 Purna adds 30 and 5 first to get 35, and then multiplies 35 by 4 he gets 140
Step 3 Mallesh multiplies 5 by 4 first to get 20, and then adds 20 to 30 he gets 50
Answer For this story, Mallesh is right: the bags hold $5 \times 4 = 20$ marbles, and $30 + 20 = 50$

Purna did nothing silly. Looking only at $30 + 5 \times 4$, we cannot tell whether to add first or to multiply first. We need a way to write the expression so that there is no doubt.

Brackets in expressions

In the marbles story, we have to multiply 5 by 4 first and then add the answer to 30. Brackets show this order: $30 + (5 \times 4)$. We always work out what is inside the brackets before doing anything else. Brackets can hold any part of an expression, and one expression can have several pairs of brackets. Each pair is worked out before its answer is used.

$30 + (5 \times 4) = 30 + 20 = 50$

Example 5

Irfan buys a packet of biscuits for ₹15 and a packet of toor dal for ₹56. He gives the shopkeeper ₹100. Write an expression for the change he should get.

Solution.

Step 1 He spends $15 + 56$ rupees, and his change is 100 minus that
Step 2 Could we write $100 - 15 + 56$? If we subtract 15 from 100 and then add 56, we get 141. Irfan cannot get back more than he paid!
Step 3 $100 - (15 + 56) = 100 - 71 = 29$ brackets fix this
Answer Irfan gets back ₹29

Terms in expressions

What if $30 + 5 \times 4$ has no brackets at all? It still has a meaning. When an expression has several operations and no brackets, we use its terms to decide the order.

The terms of an expression are the parts joined by + signs. In $12 + 7$, the terms are 12 and 7. In this chapter we will shade each term, like this: $12$ $+$ $7$. Books do not usually do this; it is only to help you see the terms while they are new.
