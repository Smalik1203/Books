Using What You Know

The chapter gave you terms, brackets, the freedom to add terms in any order, and the distributive property. Every question below needs only those. What is new is that the question does not tell you which one to use.

Without working out either one, which is larger: $3 \times (48 + 52)$ or $3 \times 48 + 52$?

In $3 \times (48 + 52)$, both 48 and 52 are multiplied by 3, because a multiple of a sum is the sum of the multiples. In $3 \times 48 + 52$, only 48 is multiplied by 3, and 52 is added once. So the first is larger. It is larger by two more 52s, which is 104. The values are 300 and 196, and $300 - 196 = 104$. The difference came from the bracket, not from any calculation.

Riya writes $100 - 45 - 25 = 100 - (45 - 25)$. Is she right?

Remove the bracket on the right. It has a minus sign in front, so the terms inside change sign: $100 - (45 - 25) = 100 - 45 + 25$. That is not what Riya started with. The values are 30 on the left and 80 on the right. The bracket that works is $100 - (45 + 25)$, because removing it gives back $100 - 45 - 25$.

Work out $99 \times 37$ in your head.

99 is $100 - 1$, and a multiple of a difference is the difference of the multiples.

$99 \times 37 = 100 \times 37 - 1 \times 37 = 3700 - 37 = 3663$

Finding 100 times something is easy. Taking away one more 37 is easy too. The hard multiplication never had to be done.

Put brackets into $8 - 5 - 2 + 1$ so that its value is 6. Can you also make its value 0?

Without brackets the value is 2. To reach 6 we need to add 4 more. A bracket after a minus sign changes the signs of the terms inside it. If $-2$ becomes $+2$, the value goes up by 4. So we put 5 and 2 together behind the minus sign: $8 - (5 - 2) + 1 = 8 - 3 + 1 = 6$.
