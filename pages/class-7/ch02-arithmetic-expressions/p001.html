Terms and Brackets

2.1Simple Expressions

You have met phrases like $13 + 2$, $20 - 4$, $12 \times 5$ and $18 \div 3$ many times. Each of them is an arithmetic expression. An expression works out to a number, and that number is its value. The value of $13 + 2$ is 15. We can read $13 + 2$ as “13 plus 2” or “the sum of 13 and 2”.

The equals sign joins an expression to its value: $13 + 2 = 15$. We read this as “13 plus 2 equals 15”, or “the value of 13 plus 2 is 15”.

Example 1

Mallika spends ₹25 on lunch at school every day from Monday to Friday. Write an expression for what she spends on lunch in one week.

She spends ₹25 on each of 5 days, so the expression is $5 \times 25$. We read it as “5 times 25” or “the product of 5 and 25”. If she also ate lunch at school on Saturday, the expression would be $6 \times 25$.

Many different expressions can have the same value. Here are four expressions whose value is 12:

$10 + 2$,   $15 - 3$,   $3 \times 4$,   $24 \div 2$

Choose a number you like. Write as many expressions as you can whose value is that number. For example, 7 is $10 - 3$, and it is also $14 \div 2$.

Comparing expressions

We compare numbers with the signs =, < and >. We can compare expressions in the same way, by comparing their values. For example, $10 + 2 \gt 7 + 1$, because 12 is greater than 8. And $13 - 2 \lt 4 \times 3$, because 11 is less than 12. When the numbers are large, finding both values can take a long time.
