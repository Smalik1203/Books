Problem 4

Which expression has the same value as $6 \times 13 + 6 \times 7$?

  1. $6 \times 13 + 7$
  2. $(6 + 6) \times (13 + 7)$
  3. $6 \times (13 + 7)$
  4. $6 + 13 \times 6 + 7$
Solution

Both terms are 6 times something, so the sum of the multiples is a multiple of the sum.

$6 \times 13 + 6 \times 7 = 6 \times (13 + 7) = 6 \times 20 = 120$

The answer is (c). Option (a) multiplies only 13 by 6, and has value 85. Option (b) doubles the 6 and has value 240. Option (d) has value 91: its terms are 6, $13 \times 6$ and 7.

Taking a number outside a bracket is the distributive property read from right to left. Look for it whenever every term of a sum has the same number in it, as $6 \times 13$ and $6 \times 7$ both have 6.

Problem 5

Kiran had ₹500. He bought a book for ₹185 and a pen for ₹45. Then his aunt gave him ₹100. Which expression gives the money he has now?

  1. $500 - 185 + 45 + 100$
  2. $500 - (185 + 45) + 100$
  3. $500 - (185 - 45) + 100$
  4. $500 - 185 - 45 - 100$
Solution

He spends $185 + 45$ rupees, which comes off 500, and then 100 is added.

$500 - (185 + 45) + 100 = 500 - 230 + 100 = 370$

The answer is (b), and Kiran has ₹370. Options (a) and (c) both have value 460: they treat the ₹45 for the pen as money he received. Option (d) takes away the aunt’s ₹100 instead of adding it, and gives 170.
