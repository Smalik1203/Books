Removing brackets — II

Example 15

Lhamo and Norbu go to a hotel. Each of them orders a vegetable cutlet for ₹43 and a rasgulla for ₹24. Write an expression for what they pay together.

Solution.

Step 1 Each person’s share is $43 + 24$ a cutlet, a rasgulla
Step 2 Can the total be written as $2 \times 43 + 24$? As a sum of terms, that is $2 \times 43$ $+$ $24$, which means 24 more than $2 \times 43$
Step 3 We want double of $43 + 24$, so we need brackets: $2 \times (43 + 24)$
Answer $2 \times (43 + 24) = 2 \times 43 + 2 \times 24$ two cutlets, two rasgullas

Example 16

In a Republic Day parade, the scouts march in 4 rows with 5 in each row, and the guides march in 3 rows with 5 in each row (Fig. 2.4). How many are marching?

Solution.

Step 1 There are $4 \times 5$ scouts and $3 \times 5$ guides, so $4 \times 5 + 3 \times 5$ children in all
Step 2 We can also count the rows first, $4 + 3$, and multiply by the 5 children in each row: $(4 + 3) \times 5$
Step 3 $4 \times 5 + 3 \times 5 = 20 + 15 = 35$ group by group
Answer $(4 + 3) \times 5 = 7 \times 5 = 35$ rows first

Why is $5 \times 4 + 3$ not equal to $5 \times (4 + 3)$? Is $5 \times (4 + 3)$ equal to $5 \times (3 + 4)$ and to $(3 + 4) \times 5$?

Take $10 \times 98 + 3 \times 98$. That is 98 added 10 times, and then 98 added 3 more times. Altogether 98 is added $10 + 3 = 13$ times, so $10 \times 98 + 3 \times 98 = (10 + 3) \times 98$. Swapping the numbers in each product, $98 \times (10 + 3) = 98 \times 10 + 98 \times 3$.

Subtraction works in the same way: 14 tens with 6 tens taken away leaves $14 - 6 = 8$ tens, so $14 \times 10 - 6 \times 10 = (14 - 6) \times 10$.
