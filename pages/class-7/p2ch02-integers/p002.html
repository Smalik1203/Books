Carrom coin integers

A carrom coin starts at 0 on a line marked in units. A strike moves the coin to the right by some number of units, depending on how hard it is struck. If the first strike moves it 4 units and the second moves it 3 units, the coin ends up $4 + 3 = 7$ units from 0. In general, if the first strike moves the coin $a$ units to the right and the second moves it $b$ units to the right, its final position is $P = a + b$.

Now suppose the coin can be struck to the left as well as to the right. Can we still give one formula for the final position after two strikes? We could look at four cases separately: both strikes to the right, both to the left, the first to the right and the second to the left, and the first to the left and the second to the right.

There is a quicker way. Treat the line as a number line, and count a movement to the right as positive and a movement to the left as negative. If the first strike moves the coin 5 units to the right, the first movement is 5. If the second moves it 7 units to the left, the second movement is $-7$. The final position is the sum of the two movements, $5 + (-7) = -2$, which is 2 units to the left of 0 (Fig. 2.1).

So whatever the directions, if the two movements are $a$ and $b$, each positive for a strike to the right and negative for a strike to the left, the final position is again $P = a + b$. Use this to answer two questions. If the first movement is $-4$ and the final position is 5, what is the second movement? If the movements are $1, -2, 3, -4, \ldots, -10$, in that order, where does the coin end up?

A movement written as an integer carries two facts: how far the coin goes, called the magnitude, and in which direction. A movement of $-4$ has magnitude 4 and goes to the left.
