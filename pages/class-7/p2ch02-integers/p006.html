Will this always happen? The magnitude of the product depends only on the magnitudes of the two numbers, and swapping two positive numbers does not change their product. The sign does not change either: if both numbers have the same sign, the product is positive before and after the swap, and if their signs are different, it is negative before and after. So the product does not change when the numbers are swapped, whatever their signs.

Multiplication is commutative

For any two integers $a$ and $b$, $a \times b = b \times a$.

Brahmagupta’s rules

Brahmagupta, in his Brahmasphutasiddhanta of 628 CE, gave rules for adding and subtracting positive and negative numbers, and also for multiplying and dividing them. He called a positive number a fortune (dhana) and a negative number a debt (rina). His rules say that the product or quotient of two fortunes is a fortune, of two debts is a fortune, and of a debt and a fortune, in either order, is a debt. This was the first time such rules were written down, and it was an important step in the growth of arithmetic and algebra.

Example 1

A test has 50 multiple-choice questions. A correct answer earns 5 marks and a wrong answer earns $-2$ marks. Mala answered 30 questions correctly and 20 wrongly. What was her score?

Solution.

Step 1 $30 \times 5 + 20 \times (-2)$ correct and wrong marks
Step 2 $= 150 + (-40)$
Step 3 $= 110$
Answer Mala scored 110 marks.
