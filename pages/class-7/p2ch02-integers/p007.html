Brahmagupta’s rules

Brahmagupta, in his Brahmasphutasiddhanta of 628 CE, gave rules for adding and subtracting positive and negative numbers, and also for multiplying and dividing them. He called a positive number a fortune (dhana) and a negative number a debt (rina). His rules say that the product or quotient of two fortunes is a fortune, of two debts is a fortune, and of a debt and a fortune, in either order, is a debt. This was the first time such rules were written down, and it was an important step in the growth of arithmetic and algebra.

Example 1

A test has 50 multiple-choice questions. A correct answer earns 5 marks and a wrong answer earns $-2$ marks. Mala answered 30 questions correctly and 20 wrongly. What was her score?

$30 \times 5 + 20 \times (-2)$

$= 150 + (-40)$

$= 110$

Mala scored 110 marks.

What is the highest score possible in this test? What is the lowest?

Example 2

A lift in a mine shaft moves above and below the ground. Positions above the ground are positive and positions below it are negative. The lift moves at 3 metres per minute. (a) It goes down from ground level, 0. Where is it after one hour? (b) It starts at 15 m above the ground and goes down. Where is it after 45 minutes?

(a) In one hour, 60 minutes, the lift moves $60 \times 3 = 180$ m. Going down from 0 means subtracting: $0 - 180 = -180$. We can also give the movement a sign: going down, it moves $-3$ m every minute, so in 60 minutes it moves $60 \times (-3) = -180$ m. Either way, it is 180 m below the ground.

(b) Using the second way, it moves $45 \times (-3)$ m from 15 m.

$15 + 45 \times (-3) = 15 + (-135)$

$= -120$

The lift is 120 m below the ground. Solve part (b) by the first way too.
