Tokens

In Class 6 we also used tokens for integers: a token marked + stands for 1, and a token marked − stands for $-1$. A + token and a − token together make 0, so we call them a zero pair.

Let us find $7 - 18$. We have to remove 18 + tokens from 7 + tokens, and there are not enough. So we first put in zero pairs: we need 11 more + tokens, so we put in 11 zero pairs. Now we can remove 18 + tokens. What is left is 11 − tokens, so $7 - 18 = -11$.

We also saw that subtracting a number is the same as adding its additive inverse. Use tokens to explain why $7 - 18 = 7 + (-18)$, and why $4 - (-12) = 4 + 12$. The additive inverse of an integer $a$ is written $-a$. So the additive inverse of 18 is $-18$, and the additive inverse of $-18$ is $-(-18) = 18$.

2.2Multiplication of Integers

Tokens helped us add and subtract integers. Can they help us multiply? Start with an empty bag, and put 2 + tokens into it 4 times. The bag now holds 8 + tokens. This is the multiplication $4 \times 2 = 8$: the multiplier 4 says how many times, and the multiplicand 2 says what is put in each time.

What could $4 \times (-2)$ mean? In the same way, put 2 − tokens into an empty bag 4 times. The bag holds 8 − tokens, so $4 \times (-2) = -8$. Find $4 \times (-6)$ and $9 \times (-7)$ in the same way.

Now for $(-4) \times 2$. When the multiplier is positive, we put tokens into the bag. When it is negative, we take tokens out. So $(-4) \times 2$ means taking 2 + tokens out of the bag, 4 times. Why + tokens and not − tokens? Because the multiplicand is 2, not $-2$.

But every multiplication starts with an empty bag, and there are no + tokens to take out. As with subtraction, we first put in zero pairs: to take out 2 + tokens we put in 2 zero pairs, and then take the + tokens out. Doing this 4 times leaves 8 − tokens in the bag, so $(-4) \times 2 = -8$.

What if both numbers are negative? $(-4) \times (-2)$ means taking 2 − tokens out of the bag, 4 times. Again we put in 2 zero pairs each time and take out their − tokens. What is left is 8 + tokens, so $(-4) \times (-2) = 8$. Fig. 2.4 shows all four multiplications.
