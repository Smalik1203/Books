2.3Division of Integers

A division can be turned into a multiplication. For example, $(-100) \div 25$ asks: what should 25 be multiplied by to give $-100$? Since $25 \times (-4) = -100$, we get $(-100) \div 25 = -4$.

In the same way, $(-100) \div (-4)$ asks what $-4$ should be multiplied by to give $-100$. Since $(-4) \times 25 = -100$, $(-100) \div (-4) = 25$. Division undoes multiplication, so each division here comes from a multiplication we already know. And since $(-25) \times (-2) = 50$, we get $50 \div (-25) = -2$. What rules for dividing integers do these examples suggest?

Signs of a quotient

For any positive integers $a$ and $b$: $a \div (-b) = -(a \div b)$, $(-a) \div b = -(a \div b)$, and $(-a) \div (-b) = a \div b$. The quotient is positive when the dividend and the divisor have the same sign, and negative when their signs are different.
