Using What You Know

The chapter gave you the sign rules for products and quotients, and three properties of multiplication: it is commutative, associative and distributive over addition. The questions below need only those. What is new is choosing the rule that makes the working short.

Without multiplying, is $(-7) \times 13 \times (-2) \times (-1)$ positive or negative?

Multiply in pairs. Two negative numbers give a positive product, so every pair of negative factors can be put aside as positive. Here there are three negative factors: one pair, and one left over. So the product is negative. Its magnitude is $7 \times 13 \times 2 \times 1 = 182$, and the product is $-182$. The sign depended only on how many factors were negative.

The product of two integers is $-24$ and their sum is 5. Find the integers.

The product is negative, so one integer is positive and the other negative. Their sum is positive, so the positive one has the larger magnitude. The pairs of magnitudes with product 24 are 24 and 1, 12 and 2, 8 and 3, and 6 and 4. The differences of these magnitudes are 23, 10, 5 and 2, and only 8 and 3 differ by 5. So the integers are 8 and $-3$.

Find $(-25) \times 37 \times (-4)$ quickly.

Multiplication can be done in any order, so multiply $-25$ and $-4$ first: $(-25) \times (-4) = 100$. Then $100 \times 37 = 3700$. Choosing the order turned a hard product into an easy one.

Find $17 \times (-99)$ without long multiplication.

Write $-99$ as $(-100) + 1$ and use the distributive property, so that each of the two products is easy to find in your head.

$17 \times (-99) = 17 \times (-100) + 17 \times 1$

$= -1700 + 17$

$= -1683$
