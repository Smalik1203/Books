We can also multiply 5 and 4 first: $5 \times 4 = 20$, and $20 \times (-3) = -60$. In how many orders can $5 \times (-3) \times 4$ be worked out? Is the product the same every time? Work out $25 \times (-6) \times 12$ in every order and check. When three or more numbers are multiplied, the product is the same in any order. Changing the order and the grouping is often the quickest way to a product.

Now look at products of $-1$: $(-1) \times (-1) = 1$, $(-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) = -1$, $(-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) = 1$ and $(-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) \times (-1) = -1$. With two or four factors of $-1$ the product is positive; with three or five it is negative. Can you say this for any number of factors? Use it to give a simple rule for the sign of a product of many integers. Test your rule on ten factors of $-1$, and on eleven.

Is $5 \times (4 + (-2))$ equal to $5 \times 4 + 5 \times (-2)$? The first is $5 \times 2 = 10$ and the second is $20 + (-10) = 10$, so it is. This is the distributive property we met for positive numbers. Check it for $(-2) \times (4 + (-3))$ and a few expressions of your own. It seems to hold for integers too. Will it always?

For positive numbers, a rectangle of objects showed why the distributive property holds. Tokens do the same for integers. Fig. 2.5 has 4 rows, each with 2 + tokens and 3 − tokens. The whole rectangle is $4 \times (2 + (-3))$, and it is also the block $4 \times 2$ together with the block $4 \times (-3)$.

Can you show the distributive property with tokens for $(-4) \times (2 + (-3))$? (Hint: multiplying a number by $-4$ is adding its additive inverse 4 times.)

The distributive property

For any integers $a$, $b$ and $c$, $a \times (b + c) = a \times b + a \times c$.
