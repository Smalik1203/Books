Game: Terhuchu

Terhuchu is a game played in Assam and Nagaland. Its board, often scratched on a large stone or drawn in mud, is a square of 16 small squares with lines across it and a small triangle at each corner (Fig. 2.12). Two players each have 9 coins that look different from the other player’s, set out as in the figure.

The aim is to capture all the other player’s coins, or to leave them with no move. If neither player can win, the one with more coins wins. Players take turns. In a turn, a player moves one coin along a line to the next empty point, in any direction. If the next point along a line has the other player’s coin and the point beyond it is empty, the player may jump over that coin and capture it. One move may make several jumps, changing direction after each. Inside a corner triangle, a coin may also jump over an empty point to the one beyond it.
