Signs of a product

Changing the signs of the multiplier and the multiplicand does not change the magnitude of the product. The product is positive when both numbers are positive or both are negative. It is negative when one is positive and the other is negative.

Multiplying by 1 and by $-1$

For a positive integer $a$, we know that $1 \times a = a$. Is this true when $a$ is negative? $1 \times a$ means putting $a$ into the bag once. If $a$ is $-5$, the bag then holds 5 − tokens, which is $-5$. So $1 \times a = a$ for every integer $a$.

What is $(-1) \times a$? When $a$ is positive, the product has the same magnitude as $a$ and is negative. When $a$ is negative, the product has the same magnitude and is positive. Either way, the product is the additive inverse of $a$.

$(-1) \times a = -a$, for every integer $a$
