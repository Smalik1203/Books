We found $(-4) \times 2$ by taking 2 + tokens out of the bag 4 times. Taking a number away is the same as adding its additive inverse. So taking out 2 + tokens has the same effect as putting in 2 − tokens, and $(-4) \times 2$ can also be found by putting 2 − tokens into the bag 4 times. The answer is still $-8$.

Patterns in integer multiplication

Look at the products $4 \times 3 = 12$, $3 \times 3 = 9$, $2 \times 3 = 6$, $1 \times 3 = 3$ and $0 \times 3 = 0$. Each time the multiplier goes down by 1, the product goes down by the multiplicand, 3. Does the pattern continue when the multiplier becomes negative? It does: $(-1) \times 3 = -3$, $(-2) \times 3 = -6$ and $(-3) \times 3 = -9$.

Now let the multiplicand be negative: $4 \times (-3) = -12$, $3 \times (-3) = -9$, $2 \times (-3) = -6$, $1 \times (-3) = -3$ and $0 \times (-3) = 0$. Each time the multiplier goes down by 1, the product goes up by 3. Does this pattern continue when the multiplier becomes negative? It does again: $(-1) \times (-3) = 3$, $(-2) \times (-3) = 6$ and $(-3) \times (-3) = 9$. The token model and the patterns agree.

Signs of a product

Changing the signs of the multiplier and the multiplicand does not change the magnitude of the product. The product is positive when both numbers are positive or both are negative. It is negative when one is positive and the other is negative.
