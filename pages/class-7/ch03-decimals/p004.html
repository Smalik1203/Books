3.3A Hundredth Part

A strip of paper is $8\tfrac{9}{10}$ units long. We fold it in half along its length. How long is it now?

On a ruler marked in tenths, the folded strip ends between $4\tfrac{4}{10}$ and $4\tfrac{5}{10}$. There are no finer marks, so we cannot say exactly where. Earlier we split a unit into 10 tenths to measure smaller lengths. Now we can split each tenth into 10 even smaller parts.

There are 10 tenths in a unit, and each tenth has 10 small parts, so a unit has $10 \times 10 = 100$ small parts. Each small part is $\tfrac{1}{100}$ of a unit, a hundredth. With these marks, the folded strip ends at 4 units, 4 tenths and 5 hundredths. We write this as $4\tfrac{4}{10}\tfrac{5}{100}$, meaning $4 + \tfrac{4}{10} + \tfrac{5}{100}$.

One length can be written in several ways. A wire that is 1 unit, 1 tenth and 4 hundredths long is also 1 unit and 14 hundredths long, and it is also 114 hundredths long:

$1\dfrac{1}{10}\dfrac{4}{100} = 1\dfrac{14}{100} = \dfrac{114}{100}$

We read these as “one and one-tenth and four-hundredths”, “one and fourteen-hundredths” and “one hundred fourteen hundredths”.
