Adding and subtracting tenths

Example 1

Sonu’s lower arm is $2\tfrac{7}{10}$ units long, and his upper arm is $3\tfrac{6}{10}$ units long. How long is his whole arm?

Add the units and the tenths separately. There are $2 + 3 = 5$ units and $7 + 6 = 13$ tenths. But 13 tenths is 1 unit and 3 tenths.

$2\dfrac{7}{10} + 3\dfrac{6}{10} = 5\dfrac{13}{10} = 5 + \dfrac{10}{10} + \dfrac{3}{10} = 6\dfrac{3}{10}$

Another way is to change both lengths to tenths first: $\dfrac{27}{10} + \dfrac{36}{10} = \dfrac{63}{10}$. And 63 tenths is 60 tenths and 3 tenths, which is $6\tfrac{3}{10}$. His arm is $6\tfrac{3}{10}$ units long.

Example 2

Shylaja’s hand is $12\tfrac{4}{10}$ units long, and her palm is $6\tfrac{7}{10}$ units long. How long is her middle finger?

We need $12\tfrac{4}{10} - 6\tfrac{7}{10}$. As with whole numbers, it is easiest to start with the smallest parts. We cannot take 7 tenths from 4 tenths, so we split one of the 12 units into 10 tenths. That leaves 11 units and 14 tenths.

$12\dfrac{4}{10} - 6\dfrac{7}{10} = 11\dfrac{14}{10} - 6\dfrac{7}{10} = 5\dfrac{7}{10}$

Her middle finger is $5\tfrac{7}{10}$ units long. Try the subtraction again by changing both lengths to tenths.
