Writing and reading decimal numbers

We write 456, not $4 \times 100 + 5 \times 10 + 6 \times 1$, because each place already says what its digit is worth. Can we also leave out “tenths” and “hundredths”? Suppose we write $4\tfrac{2}{10}$ as 42. How would anyone know whether 42 means 4 tens and 2 ones, or 4 ones and 2 tenths?

The same trouble comes with 705. It could mean any of these:

7 hundreds, 0 tens and 5 ones: $700 + 0 + 5$

7 tens, 0 ones and 5 tenths: $70 + 0 + \tfrac{5}{10}$

7 ones, 0 tenths and 5 hundredths: $7 + 0 + \tfrac{5}{100}$

These are three different quantities, so they need three different ways of being written. We mark the place where the whole number ends and the parts smaller than one begin. The mark is a dot, called the decimal point. The digit just after the point is the tenths digit.

7 hundreds and 5 ones, $700 + 0 + 5$705
7 tens and 5 tenths, $70 + 0 + \tfrac{5}{10}$70.5
7 ones and 5 hundredths, $7 + 0 + \tfrac{5}{100}$7.05

So decimal notation is our place value system carried on past the ones place. Just as 705 means $7 \times 100 + 5 \times 1$, the number 70.5 means $7 \times 10 + 5 \times \tfrac{1}{10}$, and 7.05 means $7 \times 1 + 5 \times \tfrac{1}{100}$. On both sides of the point, each place is worth 10 times the place on its right.
