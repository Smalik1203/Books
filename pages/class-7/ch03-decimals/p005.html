Adding and subtracting with hundredths

Example 3

Find $15\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{4}{100} + 2\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{8}{100}$.

Add the units, the tenths and the hundredths separately. Then use the fact that 10 hundredths make 1 tenth, and 10 tenths make 1 unit.

$(15 + 2) + \left(\dfrac{3}{10} + \dfrac{6}{10}\right) + \left(\dfrac{4}{100} + \dfrac{8}{100}\right) = 17 + \dfrac{9}{10} + \dfrac{12}{100}$

$= 17 + \dfrac{9}{10} + \dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{2}{100} = 17 + \dfrac{10}{10} + \dfrac{2}{100} = 18\dfrac{2}{100}$

We could also change both numbers to hundredths: $\dfrac{1534}{100} + \dfrac{268}{100} = \dfrac{1802}{100} = 18\dfrac{2}{100}$.

Compare this with adding whole numbers. For $483 + 268$ we add hundreds, tens and ones separately: $600 + 140 + 11$. Then 11 ones become 1 ten and 1 one, and 150 tens become 1 hundred and 50: the total is 751. Carrying works the same way with tenths and hundredths.

Example 4

Find $25\tfrac{9}{10} - 6\tfrac{4}{10}\tfrac{7}{100}$.

The first number has no hundredths, so we cannot take away 7 hundredths. Split one of its 9 tenths into 10 hundredths.

$25\dfrac{8}{10}\dfrac{10}{100} - 6\dfrac{4}{10}\dfrac{7}{100} = 19\dfrac{4}{10}\dfrac{3}{100}$

Sometimes we must borrow twice. In $15\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{4}{100} - 2\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{8}{100}$, a tenth becomes 10 hundredths, giving $15\tfrac{2}{10}\tfrac{14}{100}$. Then a unit becomes 10 tenths, giving $14\tfrac{12}{10}\tfrac{14}{100}$. The difference is $12\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{6}{100}$.

Do the first subtraction again by changing both numbers to hundredths. Then compare with $653 - 268$, where we also borrow twice: a ten becomes 10 ones, and a hundred becomes 10 tens, and the answer is 385.
