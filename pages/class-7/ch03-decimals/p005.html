One length can be written in several ways. A wire that is 1 unit, 1 tenth and 4 hundredths long is also 1 unit and 14 hundredths long, and it is also 114 hundredths long:

$1\dfrac{1}{10}\dfrac{4}{100} = 1\dfrac{14}{100} = \dfrac{114}{100}$

We read these as “one and one-tenth and four-hundredths”, “one and fourteen-hundredths” and “one hundred fourteen hundredths”.

Adding and subtracting with hundredths

Example 3

Find $15\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{4}{100} + 2\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{8}{100}$.

Add the units, the tenths and the hundredths separately. Then use the fact that 10 hundredths make 1 tenth, and 10 tenths make 1 unit.

$(15 + 2) + \left(\dfrac{3}{10} + \dfrac{6}{10}\right) + \left(\dfrac{4}{100} + \dfrac{8}{100}\right) = 17 + \dfrac{9}{10} + \dfrac{12}{100}$

$= 17 + \dfrac{9}{10} + \dfrac{1}{10} + \dfrac{2}{100} = 17 + \dfrac{10}{10} + \dfrac{2}{100} = 18\dfrac{2}{100}$

We could also change both numbers to hundredths: $\dfrac{1534}{100} + \dfrac{268}{100} = \dfrac{1802}{100} = 18\dfrac{2}{100}$.
