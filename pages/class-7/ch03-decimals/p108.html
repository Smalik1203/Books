Why the other options are wrong

B1 $0.8 = 0.80$, which is 80 hundredths, and 0.75 is 75 hundredths, so A is true. R is false: 0.3 also has fewer digits than 0.75, and it is smaller. The answer is (c).
B6 0.5 is 5 tenths and 0.05 is 5 hundredths, so 0.05 mg is one-tenth of 0.5 mg, not ten times it. A is false. R is true, and it is the very reason A is false. The answer is (d).
B7 750 g is 0.75 kg, and $1.5 + 0.75 + 0.25 = 2.5$. The answer is (c). Option (b) writes 750 g as 0.075 kg, and (a) does not change 750 g into kilograms at all.
C1 $4.1 = 4.10$ and $4.2 = 4.20$. The numbers between them with two digits after the point are 4.11, 4.12, and so on up to 4.19, which is 9 numbers. The answer is (c). Option (a) also counts 4.10 or 4.20, which are not between.
C3 $20 - 4.75 = 15.25$, then $15.25 - 6.8 = 8.45$, then $8.45 + 3.5 = 11.95$. The answer is (b). Option (c) takes the 3.5 L away instead of adding it.
C5 3.4 overs is 3 overs and 4 balls, and 2.5 overs is 2 overs and 5 balls. Together they make 5 overs and 9 balls. Six of the balls make one more over, so the total is 6 overs and 3 balls, shown as 6.3. The answer is (c). Option (a) adds them as if they were decimals.
C6 7.009 is 9 thousandths above 7, and 6.989 is 11 thousandths below it. 7.02 is 20 thousandths away, and 6.9 is 100 thousandths away. The answer is (a). Option (b) looks just as close, until the distances are counted.
C7 The largest such number is 97.52 and the smallest is 25.79, and $97.52 - 25.79 = 71.73$. The answer is (d). Option (a) takes the smaller digit from the larger in each place, (c) forgets a borrow, and (b) adds.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. A digit’s worth comes from its place, not from how many digits there are, so write zeros at the end to line numbers up. Change quantities to the same unit before adding them. And an hour has 60 minutes and an over has 6 balls, so the digits after the point in 2.25 hours are not minutes, and the 5 in 5.5 overs is not tenths.
