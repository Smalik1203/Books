Rupees and paise

You may have heard of the paisa. There are 100 paise in 1 rupee. Not long ago there were coins of 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 25 and 50 paise. Coins of 25 paise and less stopped being used in 2011, but we still see paise on bills and bank statements.

A rupee is split into 100 paise, so a paisa is one-hundredth of a rupee: 1 paisa is $\tfrac{1}{100}$ rupee, or ₹0.01. So 75 paise is ₹0.75, because

$\dfrac{75}{100} \text{ rupee} = \left(\dfrac{70}{100} + \dfrac{5}{100}\right) \text{rupee} = \left(\dfrac{7}{10} + \dfrac{5}{100}\right) \text{rupee}$

3.6Locating and Comparing Decimals

Take the decimal number 1.4. It is 1 one and 4 tenths. So the unit from 1 to 2 is split into 10 equal parts, and we take 4 of them. This means 1.4 lies between 1 and 2.
