3.7Adding and Subtracting Decimals

Priya needs 2.7 m of cloth for a skirt, and Shylaja needs 3.5 m for a kurti. How much cloth do they need together? We need $2.7 + 3.5$. Earlier we added $2\tfrac{7}{10} + 3\tfrac{5}{10}$ with tenths. Decimal notation lets us add in columns, exactly as with whole numbers, keeping the points in a line.

$2\dfrac{7}{10} + 3\dfrac{5}{10} = 5\dfrac{12}{10} = 6\dfrac{2}{10}$

$\begin{array}{r} 2.7 \\ +\,3.5 \\ \hline 6.2 \end{array}$

Seven tenths and 5 tenths make 12 tenths. We write 2 in the tenths column and carry 1 one into the ones column. So they need 6.2 m of cloth.

How much longer is Shylaja’s cloth than Priya’s? We need $3.5 - 2.7$. We cannot take 7 tenths from 5 tenths, so we split one of the 3 ones into 10 tenths. That leaves 2 ones and 15 tenths, and $15 - 7 = 8$ tenths.

$3\dfrac{5}{10} - 2\dfrac{7}{10} = 2\dfrac{15}{10} - 2\dfrac{7}{10} = \dfrac{8}{10}$

$\begin{array}{r} 3.5 \\ -\,2.7 \\ \hline 0.8 \end{array}$

Shylaja’s cloth is 0.8 m longer. The column methods we use for whole numbers work for decimals too, as long as each digit stays in its own place.
