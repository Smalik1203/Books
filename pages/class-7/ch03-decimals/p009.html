Reading decimal numbers

We read 705 as “seven hundred and five”. We read 70.5 as “seventy point five”, which is short for seventy and five-tenths. We read 7.05 as “seven point zero five”, short for seven and five-hundredths.

After the point, each digit is read on its own. So 0.274 is “zero point two seven four”. It is not “zero point two hundred and seventy-four”, because 0.274 is 2 tenths, 7 hundredths and 4 thousandths.

Many tenths, many hundredths

In the chapter on large numbers, we wrote numbers like 23 hundreds. Twenty hundreds are 2000 and three hundreds are 300, so 23 hundreds is 2300. The 2 moves into the thousands place. In the same way, 23 tens is $200 + 30 = 230$.

The same thing happens on the other side of the point. Every 10 tenths make a one, so 230 of the 234 tenths are 23 ones, and 4 tenths are left over:

$\dfrac{234}{10} = \dfrac{200}{10} + \dfrac{30}{10} + \dfrac{4}{10} = 20 + 3 + \dfrac{4}{10} = 23.4$
