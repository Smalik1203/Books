Example 4

Find $25\tfrac{9}{10} - 6\tfrac{4}{10}\tfrac{7}{100}$.

The first number has no hundredths, so we cannot take away 7 hundredths.

Solution.

Step 1 $25\dfrac{9}{10} = 25\dfrac{8}{10}\dfrac{10}{100}$ split one tenth
Step 2 $25\dfrac{8}{10}\dfrac{10}{100} - 6\dfrac{4}{10}\dfrac{7}{100} = 19\dfrac{4}{10}\dfrac{3}{100}$
Answer $19\dfrac{4}{10}\dfrac{3}{100}$

Sometimes we must borrow twice. In $15\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{4}{100} - 2\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{8}{100}$, a tenth becomes 10 hundredths, giving $15\tfrac{2}{10}\tfrac{14}{100}$. Then a unit becomes 10 tenths, giving $14\tfrac{12}{10}\tfrac{14}{100}$. The difference is $12\tfrac{6}{10}\tfrac{6}{100}$.

Do the first subtraction again by changing both numbers to hundredths. Then compare with $653 - 268$, where we also borrow twice: a ten becomes 10 ones, and a hundred becomes 10 tens, and the answer is 385.
