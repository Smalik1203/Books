Zooming in on the number line

Fig. 3.5 finds the number 4.185 in three steps. First, 4.185 lies between 4.1 and 4.2. That stretch is magnified and split into hundredths, and 4.185 lies between 4.18 and 4.19. That stretch is magnified again and split into thousandths, and 4.185 is the fifth mark.

Now try it the other way. A number is found by zooming in: it lies between 0.6 and 0.7, then between 0.63 and 0.64, and then it is the seventh mark after 0.63. What is the number? Draw zooming number lines like Fig. 3.5 for 9.876 and for 0.407.

A number line does not always go up in tenths. In Fig. 3.6 (i), the 5 units from 5 to 10 are split into 10 equal parts, so each part is half a unit, or 0.5. Point b is 5 parts after 5, so it is $5 + 2.5 = 7.5$.

Which is larger?

Which is larger, 6.456 or 6.465? We could find both on a number line. We can also compare the digits place by place, as in Table 3.8.

Both numbers have 6 ones, so we look at the tenths. Both have 4 tenths, so we look at the hundredths. The first has 5 hundredths and the second has 6. So 6.465 is larger, and we do not need to look at the thousandths at all.
