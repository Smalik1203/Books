The chapter showed that 0.1 hour is 6 minutes, because an hour split into 10 parts gives parts of 6 minutes. So 0.2 hour is 12 minutes. That leaves 0.05 hour, which is half of 0.1 hour, or 3 minutes.

$0.25 \text{ hour} = 0.2 \text{ hour} + 0.05 \text{ hour} = 12 \text{ minutes} + 3 \text{ minutes} = 15 \text{ minutes}$

The journey takes 2 hours and 15 minutes, so Ravi is wrong. He read the digits after the point as minutes, but an hour has 60 minutes, not 100.

Find a decimal number between 2.3 and 2.31.

At first there seems to be no room. In hundredths, the two numbers are 230 and 231 hundredths, with nothing in between. So split each hundredth into 10 thousandths. Now the numbers are $2.300$ and $2.310$, and there are nine numbers between them: 2.301, 2.302, and so on up to 2.309. Any of these is an answer, such as 2.305.

The same trick works for any two different decimals. When there is no room in one place, we move to the next place to the right, where every part is split into 10 again.

Type 1 · Tenths and hundredths

Example 1

Write $5\tfrac{3}{10}$ cm as a number of tenths of a centimetre. Then write $\tfrac{47}{10}$ as units and tenths.

Solution.

Step 1 5 units $= 5 \times 10 = 50$ tenths 10 tenths make 1
Step 2 $5\tfrac{3}{10} = 50 + 3 = 53$ tenths $= \tfrac{53}{10}$
Step 3 $\tfrac{47}{10} = \tfrac{40}{10} + \tfrac{7}{10} = 4\tfrac{7}{10}$ 40 tenths make 4
Answer $\tfrac{53}{10}$ cm; $4\tfrac{7}{10}$
Example 2

Write $2\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{6}{100}$ as a number of hundredths. Then write $\tfrac{507}{100}$ as units, tenths and hundredths.

Solution.

Step 1 2 units $= 200$ hundredths; 3 tenths $= 30$ hundredths
Step 2 $200 + 30 + 6 = 236$, so $2\tfrac{3}{10}\tfrac{6}{100} = \tfrac{236}{100}$
Step 3 $\tfrac{507}{100} = \tfrac{500}{100} + \tfrac{7}{100} = 5\tfrac{7}{100}$ no tenths left
Answer $\tfrac{236}{100}$; 5 units, 0 tenths and 7 hundredths
