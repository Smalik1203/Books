A line parallel to a given line

Copying an angle has an important use: it lets us construct parallel lines with only a ruler and a compass. Earlier, we used a ruler and a set square to make equal corresponding angles, and that gave parallel lines. Let us do the same with a compass. Remember the fact behind that method: when a transversal makes equal corresponding angles with two lines, the two lines are parallel. So all we need is a way to make a corresponding angle equal to a given one, and copying an angle gives exactly that.

Suppose we need a line parallel to a line m. Draw a line l that crosses m at A. Then l is a transversal of m and of the parallel line we are about to draw. Choose a point B on l for the new line to pass through. The new line must make the same corresponding angle with l as m does, and we can make it by copying the angle at A to B. The point B can be anywhere on l, and the arcs can have any radius, as long as the arc from B has the same radius as the arc from A. A larger radius makes the construction more accurate.

  1. Draw an arc from A that cuts m at C and l at D. With the same radius, draw an arc from B that cuts l at F.
  2. Open the compass to the length CD. With its point on F, cut the arc through F at E.
  3. Draw the line n through B and E. Then n is parallel to m.
