6.1Geometric Constructions

Eyes

In Class 6 we constructed a pair of eyes. An eye can be drawn freehand, but we wanted its upper and lower arcs to match exactly. So we guessed two centres, A and B, and drew the lower arc from A and the upper arc from B. The eyes looked right, but the two centres were only good guesses, not exact points.

The two arcs meet at X and Y. The line XY is a supporting line: it helps us draw the figure, but it is not part of the finished eye. Can we start from the supporting line, and find A and B by a method instead of by guessing? A method would give exactly the same eye every time, whoever draws it.

For the eye to be symmetrical about XY, the two arcs must have the same radius, so $AX = BX$. If the radii were different, one arc would be flatter than the other and the eye would look lopsided. Each arc passes through both X and Y, so $AX = AY$ and $BX = BY$. Putting these together,

$AX = AY = BX = BY$

How can we find two such points? Draw arcs from X and from Y with the same radius, above XY and below it. The arcs above meet at a point, which is A, and the arcs below meet at another point, which is B. Use this method to construct an eye. Keep the compass open to the same radius throughout.
