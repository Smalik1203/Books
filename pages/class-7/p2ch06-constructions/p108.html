C1 Each angle of a regular octagon is $135^\circ$. Two make $270^\circ$ and three make $405^\circ$, so octagons cannot fill the space around a point: (d). Squares ($4 \times 90^\circ$), triangles ($6 \times 60^\circ$) and hexagons ($3 \times 120^\circ$) all make $360^\circ$.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Two points at equal distances from X and Y fix the perpendicular bisector, with no measuring. Angles we can construct come from $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, by bisecting and by adding. And when a tiling seems impossible, colour the region: if the colours do not balance, no tiling exists.
