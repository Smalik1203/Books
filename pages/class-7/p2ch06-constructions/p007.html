Take an angle XOY. Suppose we can draw two congruent triangles OBC and OAC, with A on OY, B on OX and C inside the angle. Then $\angle BOC = \angle AOC$, and OC bisects the angle. How do we construct such triangles? If $OA = OB$ and C is chosen with $AC = BC$, then OC is a common side, and $\triangle OBC \cong \triangle OAC$ by the SSS condition. This gives the method.

  1. With the compass point on O, draw an arc that cuts OY at A and OX at B, so that $OA = OB$.
  2. Choose a radius long enough, and draw arcs from A and from B with this same radius. Name the point where they meet C.
  3. Draw the ray OC. It bisects $\angle XOY$.

So a $45^\circ$ angle can be constructed by constructing a $90^\circ$ angle and bisecting it. Bisecting a $45^\circ$ angle in turn gives an angle of $22.5^\circ$.
