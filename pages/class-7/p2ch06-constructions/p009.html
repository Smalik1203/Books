For the copies to be exact, every unit must have the same length of arms and the same angle between them. A compass gives equal lengths easily, but how do we make equal angles? Draw an angle, and try to make a copy of it using only a ruler and a compass. Here is one way to do it. The method uses only arcs and the congruence of triangles, just as bisecting an angle did.

  1. Let the given angle be at A. Draw a ray with its starting point X; the copy will be made on it.
  2. Draw an arc from A that cuts the two arms at B and C. Now A, B and C are the corners of an isosceles triangle ABC.
  3. With the same radius, draw an arc from X that cuts the ray at Z.
  4. Open the compass to the length BC. With its point on Z, cut the arc through Z at Y, so that $YZ = BC$.
  5. Draw the ray XY. Then $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$ by the SSS condition, so $\angle X = \angle A$.
