Type 6 · Tiling the plane

Example 14

Copies of which regular polygon can fit around a point with no gap and no overlap?

  1. a regular pentagon, each angle $108^\circ$
  2. a regular hexagon, each angle $120^\circ$
  3. a regular octagon, each angle $135^\circ$
  4. a regular decagon, each angle $144^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 $3 \times 120^\circ = 360^\circ$ a complete turn
Step 2 $3 \times 108^\circ = 324^\circ$, $2 \times 135^\circ = 270^\circ$, $2 \times 144^\circ = 288^\circ$ gaps left
Step 3 one corner more: $432^\circ$, $405^\circ$, $432^\circ$ too much
Answer (b)
Example 15

At a vertex of a tiling, two squares and one equilateral triangle meet. What angle is left to fill? Which regular polygons could fill it?

Solution.

Step 1 angle left $= 360^\circ - 90^\circ - 90^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$ a complete turn
Answer $120^\circ$: one regular hexagon, or two equilateral triangles ($60^\circ + 60^\circ$)
Example 16

Can two regular octagons, each angle $135^\circ$, and a square meet at a vertex of a tiling?

Solution.

Step 1 $135^\circ + 135^\circ + 90^\circ = 360^\circ$ the angles at the vertex
Answer Yes: exactly $360^\circ$, with no gap and no overlap
