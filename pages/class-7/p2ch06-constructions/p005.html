A right angle at a given point

Can we use the same idea to construct a $90^\circ$ angle at a given point on a line? Draw a line and mark a point O on it. We want a line through O perpendicular to it.

First find a segment of the line that has O as its midpoint. With the compass point on O, mark two points X and Y on the line, one on each side, at the same distance from O. Now O is the midpoint of XY, so the perpendicular bisector of XY passes through O and is perpendicular to the line.

Do we now need two pairs of arcs to draw the perpendicular bisector of XY? No. We already know one point on it, O. One pair of arcs from X and Y, meeting at A, gives a second point, and OA is the line we want.

Constructions in the Sulba-sutras

Mathematicians in many ancient civilisations, including India, knew exact methods for constructing perpendiculars and perpendicular bisectors.

In India, the oldest texts we know with these methods are the Sulba-sutras. They are geometry texts of the Vedic period about building fire altars for rituals, and they belong to one of the six Vedangas, a word that means "limbs of the Vedas". The Sulba-sutras contain the methods we have just found for the perpendicular and the perpendicular bisector.
