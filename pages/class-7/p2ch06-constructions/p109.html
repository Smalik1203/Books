1. Assertion (A): A $3 \times 4$ grid can be tiled with $2 \times 1$ tiles.

    Reason (R): Any region of unit squares with an even number of squares can be tiled with $2 \times 1$ tiles.

Very short answer
  1. What angle does the perpendicular bisector of a segment make with the segment?
  1. What is a vertex of a tiling?
  1. Angles of $100^\circ$, $85^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ fit around a point. What angle fills the gap?
  1. How many $2 \times 1$ tiles are needed to tile a $6 \times 7$ grid?
Short answer
  1. Draw a segment PQ of 7 cm and construct its perpendicular bisector. Measure the two parts of PQ.
  1. Draw an angle of $110^\circ$ with a protractor, and construct its bisector. Measure the two angles you get.
  1. Construct an angle of $67.5^\circ$ at a point O on a line XY, and say why it is $67.5^\circ$.
  1. Can a $5 \times 5$ grid with its centre square removed be tiled with $2 \times 1$ tiles? Give a reason.
  1. A rangoli has 16 petals, spaced evenly around its centre, one along each half of 8 lines through the centre. What angle do neighbouring lines make? Which constructions give this angle?
Long answer
  1. Construct $\triangle ABC$ with AB $=$ 6 cm, BC $=$ 5 cm and CA $=$ 4 cm, and the perpendicular bisectors of AB and BC, meeting at O. Measure OA, OB and OC. What do you notice, and why?
  1. Construct a regular hexagon ABCDEF with sides of 3.5 cm. Join AC, CE and EA.
    1. Measure AC, CE and EA.
    2. What kind of triangle is ACE? Explain why, using congruent triangles.
