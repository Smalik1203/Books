1. The floor of a school verandah is marked out as a grid of 4 rows and 9 columns of squares. It is to be covered with stone slabs, and each slab covers two squares next to each other.

    1. How many slabs cover the whole floor?
    2. Two pillars stand on the corner squares at the two ends of one long side. Can the rest of the floor be covered? Give a reason.
    3. Instead, the two pillars stand on the corner squares at the two ends of one short side. Can the rest of the floor be covered?
    4. For part (c), say how the slabs can be laid.
4
Answers
  1. 1–9 1 (c)   2 (c)   3 (b)   4 (d)   5 (a)   6 (b)   7 (d)   8 (a)   9 (b)
  2. 10–13 10 (a)   11 (d)   12 (b)   13 (c)
14 $90^\circ$
15 A point where corners of tiles meet; their angles there add up to $360^\circ$.
16 $360^\circ - 100^\circ - 85^\circ - 90^\circ = 85^\circ$
17 $6 \times 7 = 42$ squares, so $42 \div 2 = 21$ tiles
18 Drawing. Each part measures 3.5 cm, and the bisector meets PQ at $90^\circ$.
19 Drawing. Each angle measures $55^\circ$.
20 Construct $\angle AOY = 60^\circ$ and bisect it twice, to get $\angle COY = 15^\circ$. XOY is straight, so $\angle COX = 180^\circ - 15^\circ = 165^\circ$.
21 Yes. With dark corners the grid has 13 dark and 12 white squares, and the centre is dark, so 12 of each are left. Tile the blocks rows 1–2 × columns 1–3, rows 1–3 × columns 4–5, rows 4–5 × columns 3–5 and rows 3–5 × columns 1–2 with three tiles each.
22 $360^\circ \div 12 = 30^\circ$. Construct a $60^\circ$ angle and bisect it.
23 Drawing. $OA = OB = OC$, about 3.0 cm each: O is on the perpendicular bisector of AB, so $OA = OB$, and on that of BC, so $OB = OC$.
24 (a) about 6.9 cm each (b) equilateral: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle CDE \cong \triangle EFA$ by SAS, each with sides of 4 cm and 4 cm and $120^\circ$ between them, so $AC = CE = EA$.
25 (a) W is 5 km from both villages, the same distance. (b) two, one on each side of the road (c) 4 cm, so 4 km (d) No. Such a point would give a path of $2 + 2 = 4$ km between the villages, shorter than the 6 km road.
26 (a) 18 (b) No. The corners at the two ends of a long side have the same colour, leaving 16 of that colour and 18 of the other. (c) Yes. The corners at the two ends of a short side differ in colour, leaving 17 of each. (d) One slab upright in column 1 over rows 2 and 3; the other 8 columns, a $4 \times 8$ grid, take 4 slabs across in each row: 17 slabs.
