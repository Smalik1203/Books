To see this, look first at $\triangle ABX$ and $\triangle ABY$. Here $AX = AY$, $BX = BY$, and AB is common, so $\triangle ABX \cong \triangle ABY$ by the SSS condition. Their corresponding angles are equal, so $\angle XAB = \angle YAB$, which is the same as $\angle XAO = \angle YAO$. So $\triangle AOX \cong \triangle AOY$, and AB is the perpendicular bisector of XY.

Eyes come in different shapes. How can we draw them? One way is to choose two other points C and D with $CX = CY = DX = DY$, and draw the arcs from C and D instead.

Will C and D lie on the line AB? C and D are both at the same distance from X and from Y. We have just shown that joining two such points, one on each side of XY, gives the perpendicular bisector of XY. A segment has only one perpendicular bisector, the line AB, so C and D must lie on AB.

Points at equal distances

Any point that is at the same distance from X and from Y lies on the perpendicular bisector of XY. Use the facts we have proved to explain why.

So we can draw eyes of different shapes by choosing different pairs of points on the perpendicular bisector as the centres of the arcs.

Constructing a perpendicular bisector

How do we draw the perpendicular bisector of a segment XY using only an unmarked ruler and a compass? We have seen that joining any two points on opposite sides of XY that are at equal distances from X and Y gives the perpendicular bisector. That gives us a method. It needs no measuring at all, so it works in exactly the same way for a segment of any length, anywhere on the page.
