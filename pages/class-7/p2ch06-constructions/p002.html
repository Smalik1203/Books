In Fig. 6.2, A and B are joined. Where does AB cross XY, and what angle does it make with XY? Measure and see.

AB passes through the midpoint of XY, and it is perpendicular to XY. Dividing a line segment, or any geometric figure, into two identical parts is called bisection. A line that bisects a segment and is perpendicular to it is called the perpendicular bisector of the segment.

Will this always happen, whatever the length of XY and whatever radius we choose for the arcs? We can answer this using congruence.

Take a segment XY and points A and B with $AX = AY = BX = BY$. Join AB, AX, AY, BX and BY, and let AB cross XY at O. For AB to be the perpendicular bisector of XY, O must be the midpoint of XY and AB must be perpendicular to XY. Which two triangles should be congruent for this to be true?

Suppose we can show that $\triangle AOX \cong \triangle AOY$. Then $OX = OY$, and $\angle AOX = \angle AOY$, since they are corresponding parts of congruent triangles. These two angles together make a straight angle, so $\angle AOX + \angle AOY = 180^\circ$, and each of them is $90^\circ$. So O is the midpoint of XY, and AB is perpendicular to XY.

In $\triangle AOX$ and $\triangle AOY$, we already know that $AX = AY$ and that the side AO is common. If we also knew that $\angle XAO = \angle YAO$, the SAS condition would make the triangles congruent.
