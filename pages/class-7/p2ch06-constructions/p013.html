To answer this, we turn the question around. Can six congruent equilateral triangles be placed together as in Fig. 6.19? If they can, is the result a regular hexagon?

If they can, the six outer sides are equal, and each angle of the hexagon is made of two $60^\circ$ angles, so it is $60^\circ + 60^\circ = 120^\circ$. So what we must check is whether six equilateral triangles fit around the centre without overlapping and without leaving a gap.

We measure angles by taking the complete turn around a point as $360^\circ$, so the angles around the centre must add up to $360^\circ$. Look at Fig. 6.20. Will the $70^\circ$ angle fit into the gap? What is the gap angle GOA?

$40^\circ + 60^\circ + 50^\circ + 30^\circ + 40^\circ + 90^\circ + \text{gap angle} = 360^\circ$

Use this to decide whether the $70^\circ$ angle fits the gap.

So if some angles add up to $360^\circ$, their corners can be placed together at one point so that the angles do not overlap and they cover the whole region around the point. Each angle of an equilateral triangle is $60^\circ$, and six of them add up to $360^\circ$. So six congruent equilateral triangles can be arranged as in Fig. 6.19.
