Regular hexagons

A regular polygon has all its sides equal and all its angles equal. A regular polygon with 3 sides is an equilateral triangle, and one with 4 sides is a square. We have constructed both of these before.

How do we construct a regular pentagon, with 5 sides, or a regular hexagon, with 6 sides? To begin with, try to draw a pentagon and a hexagon whose sides are all equal. Are their angles equal too?

To construct a regular pentagon, we first need to know more about triangles and pentagons, and we will learn this in later classes. But a regular hexagon is within our reach. Can we break a regular hexagon into smaller pieces that we know how to construct? Triangles are the pieces we know best, since we can already construct a triangle from its three sides.

What happens when we join the opposite corners of a regular hexagon? Since its sides and angles are all equal, can we expect a figure like Fig. 6.19? Will all six triangles in it be equilateral? Measure the sides and angles of the triangles in a regular hexagon you have drawn. Measuring can only suggest an answer, because a drawing is never perfectly exact, so we also need a reason that works for every regular hexagon, whatever its size.
