Type 4 · Angles around a point

Example 10

Angles of $80^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $110^\circ$ are placed around a point without overlapping. What angle fills the gap? Could an angle of $60^\circ$ fill it?

Solution.

Step 1 $80^\circ + 95^\circ + 110^\circ = 285^\circ$ the angles placed
Step 2 gap $= 360^\circ - 285^\circ = 75^\circ$ a complete turn
Answer $75^\circ$. A $60^\circ$ angle leaves $75^\circ - 60^\circ = 15^\circ$ uncovered.
Example 11

Construct an angle of $157.5^\circ$ at a point O on a line XY.

Solution.

Step 1 the perpendicular OA to XY at O $\angle AOX = 90^\circ$
Step 2 bisect $\angle AOX$; name the bisector OC $\angle COY = 135^\circ$
Step 3 bisect $\angle COX$; name the bisector OB $\angle BOC = 22.5^\circ$
Answer $\angle BOY = 135^\circ + 22.5^\circ = 157.5^\circ$
Example 12

Construct a regular hexagon whose six corners lie on a circle of radius 3 cm.

Solution.

Step 1 circle with centre O, radius 3 cm; mark a point A on it
Step 2 same radius from A, cutting the circle at B OA $=$ OB $=$ AB
Step 3 $\triangle OAB$ is equilateral, so $\angle AOB = 60^\circ$ three equal sides
Step 4 same radius from B, C, D and E in turn, giving C, D, E and F four more equal turns
Step 5 $6 \times 60^\circ = 360^\circ$, so the arc from F lands on A; join ABCDEF a complete turn
Answer Every side is 3 cm, and every angle is $60^\circ + 60^\circ = 120^\circ$: a regular hexagon.
