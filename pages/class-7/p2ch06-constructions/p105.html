Type 5 · Tiling a grid

Example 11

A verandah floor is marked out as a $5 \times 6$ grid of squares. A tulsi pot stands on one square and cannot be moved. Can the rest of the floor be covered with $2 \times 1$ stone slabs, without cutting a slab?

Solution.

Step 1 squares in the grid $= 5 \times 6 = 30$
Step 2 squares to cover $= 30 - 1 = 29$ one square taken
Step 3 slabs cover 2, 4, 6, … squares always an even number
Answer No. 29 is odd, wherever the pot stands.
Example 12

From a $4 \times 5$ grid, the square in row 1, column 1 and the square in row 2, column 2 are removed. The 18 squares left are an even number. Can they be tiled with $2 \times 1$ tiles?

Solution.

Step 1 full grid: 10 dark and 10 white squares chessboard colouring
Step 2 $1 + 1 = 2$ and $2 + 2 = 4$, both even both removed squares dark
Step 3 left: 8 dark and 10 white squares
Step 4 each tile covers 1 dark and 1 white colours must balance
Answer No. $8 \ne 10$, so no tiling exists.
Example 13

One unit square is removed from a $7 \times 9$ grid. Which square could be removed, so that the rest can possibly be tiled with $2 \times 1$ tiles?

  1. the square in row 1, column 2
  2. the square in row 2, column 3
  3. a corner square
  4. any square at all

Solution.

Step 1 63 squares: 32 of the corner colour, 31 other chessboard colouring
Step 2 remove a square of the corner colour leaves 31 of each
Step 3 (a) $1 + 2 = 3$, (b) $2 + 3 = 5$: odd sums not the corner colour
Step 4 corner: 4 tiles in row 1, 27 in the $6 \times 9$ grid a tiling exists
Answer (c)
