1. Choose a radius. With it, draw two long arcs above XY, one from X and one from Y. Name the point where they meet A.
  2. With the same radius, draw two long arcs below XY, from X and from Y. Name the point where they meet B.
  3. Draw the line AB. It is the perpendicular bisector of XY.

So the perpendicular bisector can be constructed with the simplest tools there are: an unmarked ruler and a compass. In the rest of this chapter we use only these two tools, unless we need a line of a particular length in centimetres.

This construction is exact, and it is also practical. Finding the midpoint of a segment this way is more accurate than measuring the segment with a marked ruler and halving the length.
