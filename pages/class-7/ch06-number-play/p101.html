Using What You Know
The chapter’s ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter gave you parity, the reasoning that builds a magic square, the rule for the Virahanka numbers, and place value in cryptarithms. Every question below needs only those. The new part is deciding which idea a question is really about, so before you calculate, ask what kind of question it is. None of them needs long arithmetic; each needs the right idea, used once or twice.

Without adding, is $37 + 58 + 91 + 12$ odd or even?

Only the odd numbers matter, because even numbers never leave anything over. There are two odd numbers, 37 and 91, and their two leftovers make a pair. So the sum is even. (It is 198.) Adding first would have given the answer too, but counting the odd numbers works just as well for a sum of a hundred numbers. The even numbers can be ignored altogether.

Six children in a line call out 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. What can you say about their heights?

The last child calls 5, and there are exactly 5 children in front, so every one of them is taller. The fifth child calls 4 with 4 in front, so all of those are taller too, and so on down the line. Each child is shorter than everyone in front: the line goes from tallest at the front to shortest at the back. The numbers alone fixed the whole order. The list 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0 fixes it too, the other way round: each child is taller than everyone in front.
