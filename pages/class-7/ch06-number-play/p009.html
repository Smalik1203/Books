A $4 \times 4$ magic square from Khajuraho

The oldest known $4 \times 4$ magic square is carved in a 10th-century inscription at the Parshvanath Jain temple in Khajuraho. It is called the Chautisa Yantra; chautisa means thirty-four (Fig. 6.15). Each of the numbers 1 to 16 appears in it exactly once, just as 1 to 9 do in a $3 \times 3$ square.

Check that every row, column and diagonal adds up to 34. Then look for other groups of four numbers that make 34: try the four corners, or any $2 \times 2$ block of squares. How many $2 \times 2$ blocks are there, and do they all work?
