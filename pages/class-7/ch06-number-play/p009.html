The general $3 \times 3$ magic square

Take a magic square of consecutive numbers, and call the number in its centre $m$. Every other number is some amount more or less than $m$. In the square of 1 to 9, with 5 in the centre, 8 is $m + 3$ and 1 is $m - 4$. Writing every number in this way gives Fig. 6.13. Try it with your square of 2 to 10, where $m = 6$: there 9 is $m + 3$ and 2 is $m - 4$, just as before.

Check that every row, column and diagonal of Fig. 6.13 adds up to $3m$, whatever number $m$ is. In the top row, $(m + 3) + (m - 4) + (m + 1) = 3m$, because 3, $-4$ and 1 add up to 0. Check the other rows, the columns and both diagonals in the same way; each time, the three amounts added to $m$ come to 0.

Think and Reflect

In any line through the centre of Fig. 6.13, the two outer numbers, such as $m + 3$ and $m - 3$, add up to $2m$. Why does that make the line add up to $3m$? Which lines do not pass through the centre?
