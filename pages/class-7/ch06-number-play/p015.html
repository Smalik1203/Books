What this chapter showed
  1. Numbers can describe an order, such as the order of heights in a line, without giving any measurements.
  2. Even numbers can be put in pairs; odd numbers have one left over. Whether a number is even or odd is its parity.
  3. A sum is odd exactly when it has an odd number of odd numbers in it. A product is odd only when every number multiplied is odd.
  4. The $n$th even number is $2n$ and the $n$th odd number is $2n - 1$.
  5. In a $3 \times 3$ magic square of the numbers 1 to 9, the magic sum is 15, the centre is 5, and 1 and 9 sit in middle squares.
  6. Each Virahanka number is the sum of the two before it: 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, …
  7. In a cryptarithm, letters stand for digits, and place value and carrying reveal them.

To decide whether a sum is odd or even, do not add it up: count how many odd numbers are in it.
