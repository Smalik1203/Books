Now look at the parity of each number: 1 is odd, 2 is even, 3 is odd, 5 is odd, 8 is even, and so on. Do you see a pattern? Can you tell whether the number after 89 is odd or even without adding?

The Virahanka numbers appear in drumming, in architecture and all through nature. A daisy, for example, usually has 13, 21 or 34 petals.

6.5Letters for Digits

In the sums below, each letter stands for one digit from 0 to 9, and different letters stand for different digits. Can you find the digits?

$\begin{array}{r} \mathrm{A} \\ \mathrm{A} \\ +\,\mathrm{A} \\ \hline \mathrm{BA} \end{array}$

Three copies of the one-digit number A add up to a two-digit number that ends in A. Try A $= 2$: the sum 6 has only one digit. Try A $= 4$: the sum 12 does not end in 4. Only A $= 5$ works, since $5 + 5 + 5 = 15$. So B $= 1$.

Now try P3 $+$ P3 $=$ RSS. Here P3 is a two-digit number with 3 in the ones place and P in the tens place.

$\begin{array}{r} \mathrm{P}3 \\ +\,\mathrm{P}3 \\ \hline \mathrm{RSS} \end{array}$

In the ones place, $3 + 3 = 6$, so S $= 6$. The tens digit of the sum is also 6, so P $+$ P ends in 6, and P is 3 or 8. With P $= 3$ the sum $33 + 33 = 66$ has only two digits, so P $= 8$. Then $83 + 83 = 166$, and R $= 1$.

Solve these, and explain how you thought about each one to a classmate. Puzzles like these are called cryptarithms.
