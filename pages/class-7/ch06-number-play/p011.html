Write 5 as a sum of 1s and 2s in every possible way; you should find 8 ways. Here is a method that finds them without guessing. Every rhythm of 5 beats begins either with a short syllable or with a long one. After a short syllable 4 beats are left, so we write $1 + {}$ in front of each of the 5 rhythms of 4 beats. After a long syllable 3 beats are left, so we write $2 + {}$ in front of each of the 3 rhythms of 3 beats (Table 6.19).

The Virahanka numbers

In the sequence 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55, …, each number is the sum of the two before it. The $n$th number is the number of rhythms of $n$ beats.

The Prakrit scholar Virahanka gave this rule, in the form of a verse, around 700 CE. He built on the work of Pingala, who lived around 300 BCE, and the numbers were later written about by Gopala, around 1135 CE, and Hemachandra, around 1150 CE. In Europe they are named after Fibonacci, who described them in 1202 CE, about 500 years after Virahanka. They are called the Virahanka numbers, or the Virahanka–Fibonacci numbers.

So there are 34 rhythms of 8 beats, since 34 is the 8th number in the sequence. The number after 55 is $34 + 55 = 89$. Write the next three numbers after 89.
