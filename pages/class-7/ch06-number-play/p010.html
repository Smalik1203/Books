Magic squares around the world

The oldest recorded magic square is the Lo Shu square of China, more than 2000 years old. A story tells of a turtle that came out of the Lo River with the square on its shell. Magic squares were later studied in India, Japan, Central Asia and Europe, and Indian mathematicians found general ways of building squares of size $5 \times 5$ and larger. Magic squares are carved on a pillar of an 8th-century temple at Palani in Tamil Nadu, and they are found in homes and shops as yantras, such as the Kubera Yantra in Fig. 6.16. 6.4The Virahanka Numbers

The numbers 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, … turn up all over mathematics, science and art. Surprisingly, they were first found in the study of poetry, and a rule about rhythm is what produces them.

In Sanskrit, Prakrit, Marathi, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu and many other languages, every syllable of a poem is either short or long, and a long syllable lasts exactly twice as long as a short one. When the poem is sung, a short syllable takes one beat and a long syllable takes two. Poets asked: how many different rhythms of 8 beats can be made from short and long syllables?
