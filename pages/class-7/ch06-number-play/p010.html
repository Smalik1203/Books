6.4The Virahanka Numbers

The numbers 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, … turn up all over mathematics, science and art. Surprisingly, they were first found in the study of poetry, and a rule about rhythm is what produces them.

In Sanskrit, Prakrit, Marathi, Malayalam, Tamil, Telugu and many other languages, every syllable of a poem is either short or long, and a long syllable lasts exactly twice as long as a short one. When the poem is sung, a short syllable takes one beat and a long syllable takes two. Poets asked: how many different rhythms of 8 beats can be made from short and long syllables? Fig. 6.16 shows four of them. Can you find others?

In the language of numbers: in how many ways can 8 be written as a sum of 1s and 2s, if the order matters? Table 6.2 lists every way for the numbers 1 to 4.

Every rhythm of 5 beats begins either with a short syllable or with a long one. After a short syllable 4 beats are left, so we write $1 + {}$ in front of each of the 5 rhythms of 4 beats.
