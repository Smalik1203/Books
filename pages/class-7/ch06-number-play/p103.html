Example 3

Ravi says that a line of 5 children called out 0, 1, 2, 3, 5. Show that he has made a mistake.

Solution.

Step 1 The 5th child has 4 children in front
Step 2 The 5th child can call at most 4 only those in front
Step 3 $5 \gt 4$
Answer No child in a line of 5 can call 5, so Ravi is wrong.

Example 4

The sum of 7 odd numbers and 4 even numbers is

  1. always even
  2. always odd
  3. sometimes even and sometimes odd
  4. impossible to decide without the numbers

Solution.

Step 1 4 even numbers: even no leftovers
Step 2 7 odd numbers: 7 leftovers $=$ 3 pairs and 1 single, so odd
Step 3 even $+$ odd $=$ odd whatever the numbers
Answer (b)
Example 5

Priya buys 7 pencils at ₹5 each and 4 erasers at ₹3 each. The shopkeeper asks for ₹48. Without working out the bill, show that this is wrong.

Solution.

Step 1 $7 \times 5$ is odd $\times$ odd, so odd
Step 2 $4 \times 3$ has an even number in it, so even
Step 3 bill $=$ odd $+$ even $=$ odd
Step 4 ₹48 is even cannot be the bill
Answer The shopkeeper is wrong. The bill is $35 + 12 = 47$ rupees.
