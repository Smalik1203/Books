An even number of objects can be put in pairs with nothing left over, as in Fig. 6.4. If we add even numbers together, every group is still in pairs, so the total can be put in pairs too. A sum of even numbers is always even. Join the pictures of 4 and 6, for example, and you get 5 pairs, which is 10.

An odd number is a set of pairs with one object left over (Fig. 6.5). We could also think of it as a set of pairs with one object missing.

What happens when we add two odd numbers? In Fig. 6.6 the two leftover objects join to make a new pair, so the whole total is in pairs. The sum of two odd numbers is always even. The picture works for any two odd numbers, not only 7 and 5, so it is a proof.

Now add three odd numbers. Two of the leftovers make a pair, but the third is left on its own, so the sum is odd. What is the parity of a sum of 4 odd numbers? Of 5? Of 6?

Back to Kishor. Every card is odd, and he needs five of them. Five leftovers make two pairs and one single, so any five odd numbers add up to an odd number. But 30 is even, so no choice of five cards will work.

Martin and Maria were born exactly one year apart, and today is their birthday. Maria says that their ages add up to 112. Can she be right? Their ages are two consecutive numbers, such as 51 and 52. The counting numbers go odd, even, odd, even, and so on, so of two consecutive numbers one is odd and the other is even. Together they have just one leftover, so their sum is odd. Since 112 is even, Maria has made a mistake.

Parity

Whether a number is even or odd is called its parity. Even $+$ even and odd $+$ odd are even, and even $+$ odd is odd. A sum is odd exactly when an odd number of the numbers in it are odd.
