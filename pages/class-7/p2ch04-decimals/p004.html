Multiplying decimals is multiplying their fractions. The product of the numerators is the product of the two numbers with their decimal points removed. The denominators are 1, 10, 100, 1000 and so on, so their product is also 1 followed by zeros, and it has as many zeros as the two denominators together. That number of zeros decides where the decimal point goes in the product.

Multiplying decimals

Multiply the two numbers as if they had no decimal points. The product has as many digits after the decimal point as the two numbers have together. For example, $596 \times 248 = 147{,}808$, and 5.96 and 24.8 have $2 + 1 = 3$ digits after their points, so $5.96 \times 24.8 = 147.808$.

Example 5

Find $5.8 \times 1.24$.

Solution.

Step 1 $58 \times 124 = 7192$ as if no points
Step 2 5.8 and 1.24 have $1 + 2 = 3$ digits after their decimal points
Answer $5.8 \times 1.24 = 7.192$

Check this by writing both numbers as fractions.

Is the product always larger?

When we multiply two fractions, the product is not always larger than both of them. The same is true of decimals. $2.25 \times 8 = 18$ is larger than both numbers. $0.25 \times 8 = 2$ is larger than 0.25 but smaller than 8. And $0.25 \times 0.8 = 0.2$ is smaller than both. When is the product larger than both numbers, and when is it smaller than both? Decimals are fractions written another way, so the answer is the one we found for fractions (Table 4.4).
