What this chapter showed
  1. To divide by 10, 100, 1000 and so on, move the decimal point left by as many places as there are zeros; to multiply, move it right.
  2. To multiply decimals, multiply as counting numbers; the product has as many digits after the decimal point as the two numbers together.
  3. Long division carries on past the ones place: ones regroup as tenths, tenths as hundredths, and so on. The decimal point goes into the quotient when ones are regrouped as tenths.
  4. To divide by a decimal, multiply the divisor and the dividend by the same power of ten to make the divisor a counting number.
  5. Some divisions never end, such as $10 \div 3 = 3.333\ldots$; the remainders, and the digits, repeat.
  6. A leap year has 366 days: a year divisible by 4 is a leap year, except one divisible by 100 that is not divisible by 400.

Before you place the decimal point, estimate. $5.96 \times 24.8$ is about $6 \times 25 = 150$, so 147.808 is right and 14.7808 cannot be.
