Example 7

Neenu has 29 m of red ribbon and shares it equally with Anu. How much does each get?

Each gets $29 \div 2$ m. If each takes 14 m, 1 m is left, and sharing it gives each another $\frac{1}{2}$ m. To write $\frac{1}{2}$ as a decimal, find an equal fraction with denominator 10, 100, 1000 and so on: $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{1 \times 5}{2 \times 5} = \frac{5}{10} = 0.5$. So each gets $14 + 0.5 = 14.5$ m.

Shared among four friends, each gets $\frac{29}{4}$ m. 4 is not a factor of 10, but $4 \times 25 = 100$.

$\dfrac{29}{4} = \dfrac{29 \times 25}{4 \times 25} = \dfrac{725}{100} = 7.25$

Each of the four friends gets 7.25 m.

Dividing by place value

So far we have divided by writing the division as a fraction and finding an equal fraction whose denominator is 10, 100, 1000 and so on. That cannot always be done: no such fraction is equal to $\frac{10}{3}$. We need a method that works for any two counting numbers. Let us recall dividing by place value, also called long division.

Example 8

Find $1324 \div 4$, sharing 1 thousand, 3 hundreds, 2 tens and 4 ones into 4 equal parts.

1 thousand cannot be shared without regrouping: it becomes 10 hundreds, making 13 hundreds.

13 hundreds $\div$ 4: each part gets 3 hundreds, and 1 hundred is left.

Regroup 1 hundred as 10 tens, making 12 tens. 12 tens $\div$ 4: each part gets 3 tens.

4 ones $\div$ 4: each part gets 1 one.

So $1324 \div 4 = 3$ hundreds $+ 3$ tens $+ 1$ one $= 331$.

A quotient with a decimal part

Now find $1325 \div 4$ in the same way. As before, each part gets 3 hundreds, 3 tens and 1 one, but this time 1 one is left over. How can 1 one be shared into 4 equal parts? Regroup it as 10 tenths. When we move from ones to tenths, we put a decimal point in the quotient.

10 tenths $\div$ 4: each part gets 2 tenths, and 2 tenths are left.

Regroup 2 tenths as 20 hundredths. 20 hundredths $\div$ 4: each part gets 5 hundredths.
