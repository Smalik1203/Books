Dividing by a decimal

Example 12

Ravi rode his scooter 126 km from Pune to Matheran in 2.5 hours. What was his average speed?

Solution.

Step 1 $126 \div 2.5$ distance divided by time
Step 2 $126 \div \dfrac{25}{10}$ divisor as a fraction
Step 3 $= 126 \times \dfrac{10}{25} = \dfrac{1260}{25} = 50.4$ multiply by its reciprocal
Answer His average speed was 50.4 km per hour

The last step is done by long division.

Example 13

Find $4.68 \div 1.3$ and $4.68 \div 0.13$.

Solution.

Step 1 $4.68 \div 1.3 = 4.68 \times \dfrac{10}{13} = \dfrac{46.8}{13} = 3.6$ reciprocal of 1.3
Step 2 $4.68 \div 0.13 = 4.68 \times \dfrac{100}{13} = \dfrac{468}{13} = 36$ reciprocal of 0.13
Answer 3.6 and 36

What do you notice? When the divisor is a decimal, multiply both numbers by 10, 100 or 1000 to make the divisor a counting number: $\frac{4.68}{0.13} = \frac{4.68 \times 100}{0.13 \times 100} = \frac{468}{13}$. Then divide by long division.

Does this ever end?

Find $10 \div 3$ by long division. 10 ones $\div$ 3 gives 3 ones, with 1 one left. That becomes 10 tenths, and 10 tenths $\div$ 3 gives 3 tenths, with 1 tenth left. That becomes 10 hundredths: 3 hundredths, with 1 hundredth left. Every step leaves a remainder of 1, so the division never ends. We write $10 \div 3 = 3.333\ldots$; it cannot be written as a decimal with a fixed number of digits. You will study such numbers in a later class.

Find $10 \div 9$ and $100 \div 11$. Now find $1 \div 7$. Does it end? Keep track of the remainders: 1, 3, 2, 6, 4, 5, and then 1 again, so they repeat in the same order for ever. Can you explain why the division can never end? Because the remainders repeat, the digits of the quotient repeat too: $1 \div 7 = 0.142857\,142857\ldots$
