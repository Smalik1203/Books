Can you write another expression for the same number? The Earth takes $100 \times 365.2422 = 36{,}524.22$ days to go round the Sun 100 times. So the calendar now has too many days: we have corrected too much.

Second adjustment: no extra day in the 100th year

So the calendar makers decided that a year divisible by 100 does not get the extra day. The rule became: if the year is divisible by 100, it has 365 days; if not, it has 366 days when it is divisible by 4, and 365 days otherwise. Can you write an expression for the days in 100 calendar years now?

Of the 25 years divisible by 4, one is the 100th year, so only 24 years have 366 days, and the other 76 have 365.

$24 \times 366 + 76 \times 365 = 36{,}524$ days

This is close to 36,524.22, but is it close enough? In 1000 years the calendar has $36{,}524 \times 10 = 3{,}65{,}240$ days, while the Earth takes $1000 \times 365.2422 = 3{,}65{,}242.2$ days. The calendar is now 2.2 days short.

Third adjustment: an extra day every 400th year

To close this gap, every year divisible by 400 was made a leap year after all. The full rule is set out below.

If the year is divisible by 400, it has 366 days.

If not, but it is divisible by 100, it has 365 days.

If neither, but it is divisible by 4, it has 366 days.

Otherwise it has 365 days.

Let us count the days in 1000 calendar years. Two years are divisible by 400, and they have 366 days. $10 - 2 = 8$ years are divisible by 100 but not by 400, and they have 365. $250 - 10 = 240$ years are divisible by 4 but not by 100, and they have 366. The other $1000 - (2 + 8 + 240) = 750$ years have 365. Check that the four groups add up to 1000 years.

$750 \times 365 + 240 \times 366 + 8 \times 365 + 2 \times 366 = 3{,}65{,}242$ days

The Earth needs 3,65,242.2 days for 1000 trips, so in 1000 years the calendar is short by only 0.2 of a day. The calendar makers did not trouble themselves over so small a difference, and the rule has stayed as it is.
