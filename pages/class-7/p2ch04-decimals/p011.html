4.4Look Before You Leap

The Earth takes about 365.2422 days to go round the Sun, not exactly 365. Our calendar, the Gregorian calendar, gives a year 365 days to keep things simple. So after one calendar year, the Earth still needs 0.2422 of a day to finish its trip. That is not much, but after 100 calendar years it adds up to $0.2422 \times 100 = 24.22$ days.

You know from science that the seasons come from the tilt of the Earth and its journey round the Sun. If the calendar drifts away from the Earth’s position, the seasons slowly stop matching the months. To fix this, a leap year of 366 days is used: every fourth year gets one extra day.
