Using What You Know
The chapter’s methods, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter gave you a rule for placing the decimal point in a product, long division past the ones place, and a way to divide by a decimal. What is new is using a fact you already know instead of starting a long calculation.

Without calculating, which is larger: $48 \times 0.98$ or $48 \div 0.98$?

Multiplying by 0.98, a number between 0 and 1, gives less than 48. Dividing by it gives more than 48, because we are asking how many times 0.98 fits into 48, and it fits more than 48 times. So $48 \div 0.98$ is larger. We knew the answer before finding either number.

$23 \times 47 = 1081$. Find $2.3 \times 0.047$.

The digits of the product are 1081. The numbers 2.3 and 0.047 have $1 + 3 = 4$ digits after their decimal points, so the product has 4 as well: $2.3 \times 0.047 = 0.1081$. A quick estimate agrees: $2 \times 0.05 = 0.1$.

How many 0.25 L glasses can be filled from a 3.5 L jug?

We want $3.5 \div 0.25$. Multiply both numbers by 100 to make the divisor a counting number: $350 \div 25 = 14$. So 14 glasses. Since four glasses make 1 L, $3.5 \times 4 = 14$ gives the same answer.

Write $\frac{1}{8}$ as a decimal without long division.

Since $8 \times 125 = 1000$, $\frac{1}{8} = \frac{125}{1000} = 0.125$. This works whenever the denominator divides 10, 100, 1000 or some such number.
