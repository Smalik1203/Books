In the same way, $24 \div 100 = 0.24$, $678 \div 1000 = 0.678$, $12 \div 1000 = 0.012$ and $12345 \div 1000 = 12.345$. Dividing by 10, 100 or 1000 makes a number smaller, so the decimal point moves to the left. Multiplying by them makes it larger, and the point moves to the right, as we shall see.

4.2Multiplying Decimals

Example 1

Arshad buys 5 pens at ₹9.50 each. How much does he pay?

Solution.

Step 1 $9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 = 47.5$ adding 9.5 five times
Step 2 $9.5 \times 5 = \dfrac{95}{10} \times \dfrac{5}{1}$ both as fractions
Step 3 $= \dfrac{95 \times 5}{10 \times 1} = \dfrac{475}{10} = 47.5$ multiply numerators and denominators
Answer He pays ₹47.50

A quick check: 5 pens at ₹10 each would cost ₹50, so ₹47.50 is sensible.

Example 2

A car goes 12.5 km on a litre of petrol. How far does it go on 7.5 litres?

Solution.

Step 1 $12.5 \times 7.5 = \dfrac{125}{10} \times \dfrac{75}{10}$ both as fractions
Step 2 $= \dfrac{125 \times 75}{10 \times 10} = \dfrac{9375}{100} = 93.75$
Answer It goes 93.75 km

An estimate agrees: about 12 km a litre for about 8 litres is about 96 km.

Can the product of two decimals be a counting number? Can the product of a decimal and a counting number be a counting number? Try $2.5 \times 0.4$ and $1.5 \times 4$ before you answer.
