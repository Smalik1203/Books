In the same way, $24 \div 100 = 0.24$, $678 \div 1000 = 0.678$, $12 \div 1000 = 0.012$ and $12345 \div 1000 = 12.345$. Dividing by 10, 100 or 1000 makes a number smaller, so the decimal point moves to the left. Multiplying by them makes it larger, and the point moves to the right, as we shall see.

4.2Multiplying Decimals

Example 1

Arshad buys 5 pens at ₹9.50 each. How much does he pay?

We multiply 9.5 by 5, which is adding 9.5 five times: $9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 + 9.5 = 47.5$. We can also write both numbers as fractions and multiply the numerators and the denominators.

$9.5 \times 5 = \dfrac{95}{10} \times \dfrac{5}{1} = \dfrac{95 \times 5}{10 \times 1} = \dfrac{475}{10} = 47.5$

He pays ₹47.50. A quick check: 5 pens at ₹10 each would cost ₹50, so ₹47.50 is sensible.

Example 2

A car goes 12.5 km on a litre of petrol. How far does it go on 7.5 litres?

$12.5 \times 7.5 = \dfrac{125}{10} \times \dfrac{75}{10} = \dfrac{125 \times 75}{10 \times 10} = \dfrac{9375}{100} = 93.75$

It goes 93.75 km. An estimate agrees: about 12 km a litre for about 8 litres is about 96 km.

Can the product of two decimals be a counting number? Can the product of a decimal and a counting number be a counting number? Try $2.5 \times 0.4$ and $1.5 \times 4$ before you answer.

Example 3

Ajay’s school is 827 m from his home. He walks there and back once a day, 6 days a week. How far does he walk in a week, in kilometres?

Each way is 827 m, which is 0.827 km.

in a day: $0.827 \times 2 = \dfrac{827 \times 2}{1000} = \dfrac{1654}{1000} = 1.654$

in a week: $1.654 \times 6 = \dfrac{1654 \times 6}{1000} = \dfrac{9924}{1000} = 9.924$

He walks 9.924 km in a week.
