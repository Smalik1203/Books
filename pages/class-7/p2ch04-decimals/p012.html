4.4Look Before You Leap

The Earth takes about 365.2422 days to go round the Sun, not exactly 365. Our calendar, the Gregorian calendar, gives a year 365 days to keep things simple. So after one calendar year, the Earth still needs 0.2422 of a day to finish its trip. That is not much, but after 100 calendar years it adds up to $0.2422 \times 100 = 24.22$ days.

You know from science that the seasons come from the tilt of the Earth and its journey round the Sun. If the calendar drifts away from the Earth’s position, the seasons slowly stop matching the months. To fix this, a leap year of 366 days is used: every fourth year gets one extra day. Which month gets it?

First adjustment: an extra day every 4 years

The rule is: if the year is divisible by 4, it has 366 days; otherwise it has 365. So four calendar years have $4 \times 365 + 1 = 1461$ days, while the Earth takes $4 \times 365.2422 = 1460.9688$ days to go round the Sun four times.

With this rule, how many days are there in 100 calendar years? Can you write an expression for it? Here is one way. Every year has 365 days, and each of the $100 \div 4 = 25$ years divisible by 4 has one more.

$100 \times 365 + 25 \times 1 = 36{,}525$ days

Can you write another expression for the same number? The Earth takes $100 \times 365.2422 = 36{,}524.22$ days to go round the Sun 100 times. So the calendar now has too many days: we have corrected too much.

Second adjustment: no extra day in the 100th year

So the calendar makers decided that a year divisible by 100 does not get the extra day. The rule became: if the year is divisible by 100, it has 365 days; if not, it has 366 days when it is divisible by 4, and 365 days otherwise. Can you write an expression for the days in 100 calendar years now?

Of the 25 years divisible by 4, one is the 100th year, so only 24 years have 366 days, and the other 76 have 365.

$24 \times 366 + 76 \times 365 = 36{,}524$ days

This is close to 36,524.22, but is it close enough? In 1000 years the calendar has $36{,}524 \times 10 = 3{,}65{,}240$ days, while the Earth takes $1000 \times 365.2422 = 3{,}65{,}242.2$ days. The calendar is now 2.2 days short.
