The triangle inequality

Three lengths satisfy the triangle inequality when each length is less than the sum of the other two. The lengths 3, 4 and 5 satisfy it; 10, 15 and 30 do not. Lengths that do not satisfy it cannot be the sides of a triangle.

Do 4 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm make a triangle? The longest length is less than the sum of the other two: $8 \lt 4 + 5 = 9$. The other two comparisons need no checking, because 4 and 5 are each less than 8 on its own; can you see why that is enough? So every straight path is shorter than the roundabout one. But this only shows that nothing is wrong; it does not yet show that the triangle exists. For that, the two arcs in the construction must actually cross.

Picturing the circles

Take the longest length, 8 cm, as the base AB. Draw the whole circle of radius 4 cm with centre A, and the whole circle of radius 5 cm with centre B. The first circle meets AB at X, with AX $=$ 4 cm, so BX $= 8 - 4 = 4$ cm. The circle round B has radius 5 cm, which is more than BX, so it reaches past X, and the two circles cross at two points (Fig. 7.9). Either crossing point, together with A and B, gives a triangle with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm.
