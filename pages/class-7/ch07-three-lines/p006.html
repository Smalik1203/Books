Picturing the circles

Take the longest length, 8 cm, as the base AB. Draw the whole circle of radius 4 cm with centre A, and the whole circle of radius 5 cm with centre B. The first circle meets AB at X, with AX $=$ 4 cm, so BX $= 8 - 4 = 4$ cm. The circle round B has radius 5 cm, which is more than BX, so it reaches past X, and the two circles cross at two points (Fig. 7.9). Either crossing point, together with A and B, gives a triangle with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 8 cm.

Does the triangle inequality guarantee a triangle?

In every example so far, lengths that satisfy the triangle inequality have made a triangle. Will they always? To be sure, we must show that the two circles always cross. Draw the longest length as the base AB, and use the two shorter lengths as the radii of the circles. Fig. 7.10 shows the three things that can happen.
