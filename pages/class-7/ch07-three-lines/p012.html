7.4Altitudes

We often speak of heights: the height of a person, of a tree or of a building. What is the height of a vertex of a triangle above the opposite side, and how can it be measured? In Fig. 7.19, AD is drawn from A perpendicular to BC. Its length is the height of A above BC, and the segment AD is called an altitude of the triangle.

Every triangle has three altitudes, one from each vertex to the opposite side. In Fig. 7.20 they are AD, BE and CF. When we speak of the height of a triangle, we mean the altitude to whichever side we take as the base.

What is the altitude from A to BC in a triangle like Fig. 7.21? The perpendicular from A does not meet the side BC itself, so we extend BC and draw the perpendicular from A to the extended line.
