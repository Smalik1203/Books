B6 A is true because one angle is $90^\circ$. R is true too, but having two acute angles is not what makes a triangle right-angled; every triangle has two. The answer is (b).
B8 The arms stop meeting when $\angle A + \angle B$ reaches $180^\circ$, that is, when $\angle B = 140^\circ$. The answer is (b). With $139^\circ$ the arms still meet, far away.
C1 $\angle B + \angle C = 110^\circ$. Take the extra $20^\circ$ away, and two equal parts make $90^\circ$, so $\angle C = 45^\circ$ and $\angle B = 65^\circ$. The answer is (a). Option (c) is half of $110^\circ$.
C2 The third side is less than $1 + 6 = 7$ cm and more than $6 - 1 = 5$ cm, so it must be 6 cm. The answer is (c). With 5 cm or 7 cm the vertices would lie on one line.
C3 The angle beside the exterior angle is $180^\circ - 80^\circ = 100^\circ$, which is obtuse. The answer is (a).
C7 Add $30^\circ$ to the smallest angle: the three angles would then be equal and make $210^\circ$, so each of the equal angles is $70^\circ$ and the third is $40^\circ$. The answer is (d). Option (a) is the smallest angle.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. To test three lengths, compare only the longest with the sum of the other two. An exterior angle hands you the angle beside it at once, through the straight angle. And when angles are described by how they compare, fill in the differences first, so that what is left splits into equal parts.
