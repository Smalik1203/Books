Short answer
  1. Can 4.5 cm, 6 cm and 10 cm be the sides of a triangle? Show the comparison you make.
  1. Which of these can be the three angles of a triangle? Name each such triangle by its angles.
    1. $45^\circ$, $65^\circ$, $70^\circ$
    2. $90^\circ$, $45^\circ$, $55^\circ$
    3. $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$, $90^\circ$
  1. An isosceles triangle has sides of 4 cm and 9 cm. Find its third side, with a reason.
  1. In △ABC, $\angle C = 60^\circ$, and $\angle A$ is twice $\angle B$. Find $\angle A$ and $\angle B$.
  1. In △PQR, side QR is extended to S. $\angle PRS = 112^\circ$ and $\angle Q = 48^\circ$. Find $\angle P$ and $\angle PRQ$.
  1. Construct △ABC with AB $=$ 6 cm and $\angle A = \angle B = 60^\circ$. What kind of triangle is it?
  1. Construct an isosceles triangle with two sides of 5 cm and a base of 8 cm. Measure its angles, and name it by its angles.
Long answer
  1. Two sides of a triangle are 7 cm and 12 cm, and the third side is a whole number of centimetres.
    1. List every length the third side can have.
    2. For which of these lengths is the triangle isosceles?
    3. For which of them is the third side the longest side?
  1. Construct a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm.
    1. Measure its largest angle, and name the triangle by its angles.
    2. Construct the altitude to the 10 cm side, and measure it.
  1. Draw a triangle. Extend each side at one end, going round the triangle, so that there is one exterior angle at each vertex. Explain why the three exterior angles add up to $360^\circ$.
  1. In Fig. 7.16 the line through A was drawn parallel to BC. Draw △ABC again, extend BC beyond C to D, and draw the line CE through C parallel to BA, on the same side of BD as A. Use it to show that $\angle ACD = \angle A + \angle B$, and then that $\angle A + \angle B + \angle ACB = 180^\circ$.
Case-based questions

  1. Three villages, Amli, Bamni and Chandur, are joined by straight roads, and they are not on one straight line. Amli is 12 km from Bamni, and Bamni is 9 km from Chandur.

    1. Can the road from Amli to Chandur be 25 km long? Give a reason.
    2. That road is a whole number of kilometres long. How many lengths are possible?
    3. The road from Amli to Chandur is in fact 12 km long. Name the triangle by its sides.
    4. A road from Bamni meets the Amli–Chandur road at right angles. What is it called?
