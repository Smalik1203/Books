Can any three lengths make a triangle?

Try to construct a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 8 cm. What goes wrong? Try 2 cm, 3 cm and 6 cm too. Can you find more such sets, and a pattern in them?

We could test every set by trying to construct it, but that is slow. Is there a quicker test?

The triangle inequality

Can a triangle have sides of 10 cm, 15 cm and 30 cm? Think first about paths. A tent, a pole and a tree stand on a flat field (Fig. 7.7). To walk from the tent to the tree, you could go straight there, or go to the pole first and then on to the tree. The straight path is shorter. A straight path is the shortest path between two points, so going by way of a third point is always longer.

Now suppose a triangle with sides 10 cm, 15 cm and 30 cm did exist. We are not sure that it does; we only suppose so, and draw a rough sketch (Fig. 7.8). Between any two vertices, the side joining them is the straight path, and it must be shorter than the path through the third vertex. Check each pair.

B to C: straight, 10 cm. By way of A, $15 + 30 = 45$ cm. The straight path is shorter.

A to B: straight, 15 cm. By way of C, $30 + 10 = 40$ cm. The straight path is shorter.

C to A: straight, 30 cm. By way of B, $10 + 15 = 25$ cm. The straight path is longer!

The last line is impossible, because no roundabout path is shorter than the straight one. So there is no triangle with sides 10 cm, 15 cm and 30 cm. We found this without any construction, by reasoning about paths, just as we reasoned about lines in Chapter 5.
