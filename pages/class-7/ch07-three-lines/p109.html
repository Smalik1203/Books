Assertion and reason

In Questions 15 to 18, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): Lengths of 5 cm, 6 cm and 11 cm can be the sides of a triangle.

    Reason (R): The longest side of a triangle is less than the sum of the other two sides.

  1. Assertion (A): No triangle has two obtuse angles.

    Reason (R): The angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$, and two obtuse angles already add up to more than $180^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): A triangle with angles $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ is right-angled.

    Reason (R): A right-angled triangle has two acute angles.

  1. Assertion (A): In △ABC, the exterior angle made at C by extending BC is greater than $\angle A$.

    Reason (R): An exterior angle of a triangle is equal to the angle of the triangle next to it.
