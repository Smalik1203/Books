Can a triangle have two right angles?

Two right angles add up to $180^\circ$, and two angles of a triangle must add up to less than $180^\circ$. So no. In a construction, the arms at A and B would both be perpendicular to AB, so they would be parallel and would never meet.

Problem 1

Which set of lengths can be the sides of a triangle?

  1. 3 cm, 5 cm, 9 cm
  2. 4 cm, 4 cm, 8 cm
  3. 6 cm, 7 cm, 12 cm
  4. 2 cm, 3 cm, 6 cm
Solution

It is enough to compare the longest length with the sum of the other two.

(a) $9 \gt 3 + 5 = 8$   (b) $8 = 4 + 4$   (c) $12 \lt 6 + 7 = 13$   (d) $6 \gt 2 + 3 = 5$

The answer is (c). In (b) the two circles would only touch, and the three vertices would lie on one straight line. Options (a) and (d) fail by more.

Problem 2

Two angles of a triangle are $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$. The third angle is

  1. $115^\circ$
  2. $65^\circ$
  3. $75^\circ$
  4. $113^\circ$
Solution

The three angles add up to $180^\circ$.

third angle $= 180^\circ - (48^\circ + 67^\circ) = 180^\circ - 115^\circ = 65^\circ$

The answer is (b). Option (a) is the sum of the two given angles. Option (c) adds $48^\circ$ and $67^\circ$ wrongly, as $105^\circ$. Option (d) takes away only $67^\circ$.
