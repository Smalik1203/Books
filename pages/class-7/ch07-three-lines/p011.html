The angle sum property

The same idea works for every triangle. Take any △ABC and draw XY through A parallel to BC (Fig. 7.16).

$\angle B = \angle XAB$ and $\angle C = \angle YAC$, because they are pairs of alternate angles.

So $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C = \angle BAC + \angle XAB + \angle YAC$.

These three angles make the straight angle at A, so the sum is $180^\circ$.

The angle sum property

The three angles of any triangle add up to $180^\circ$.

At first there was no clear link between the third angle and the other two. One extra line, through the top vertex and parallel to the base, made the link plain. This idea appears in the Elements, a book of geometry by the Greek mathematician Euclid, who lived around 300 BCE; it has been one of the most influential books in the history of mathematics. Seeing which line to draw is creative work.

Paper folding gives a quick check. Cut out a paper triangle, fold its top vertex down to the base, and then fold the other two vertices in to meet it (Fig. 7.17). The three angles fit together along a straight line.
