The third angle

Construct a triangle with angles of $60^\circ$ and $70^\circ$ and an included side of 5 cm, and another with the same angles and a side of 7 cm. Measure the third angle each time. Try other pairs of angles and other lengths. Does the third angle depend on the length of the side?

Your measurements should show that it does not, apart from small errors. So there should be a way to find the third angle without drawing. Take △ABC with $\angle B = 50^\circ$ and $\angle C = 70^\circ$, and draw the line XY through A parallel to BC (Fig. 7.14).

AB is a transversal of the parallel lines XY and BC, so $\angle XAB$ and $\angle B$ are alternate angles, and $\angle XAB = 50^\circ$. In the same way, with AC as the transversal, $\angle YAC = \angle C = 70^\circ$. The three angles $\angle XAB$, $\angle BAC$ and $\angle YAC$ together make the straight angle at A.

$50^\circ + \angle BAC + 70^\circ = 180^\circ$, so $\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 120^\circ = 60^\circ$.

Construct the triangle to check.
