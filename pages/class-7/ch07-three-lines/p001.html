A Tale of Three Intersecting Lines

A triangle is the simplest closed shape that can be made with straight lines. It has three corner points, called its vertices, and three sides, the line segments that join each pair of vertices. Fig. 7.1 shows triangles of several shapes.

We write △ABC for the triangle with vertices A, B and C. The vertices may be named in any order, so △ABC, △BCA and △CAB are the same triangle. At each vertex two sides meet and make an angle. The three angles of △ABC are $\angle CAB$, $\angle ABC$ and $\angle BCA$, which we shorten to $\angle A$, $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.

What happens if the three vertices lie on one straight line?

7.1Equilateral Triangles

A triangle whose three sides all have the same length is called an equilateral triangle. It is the most symmetric of all triangles. Draw an equilateral triangle with sides of 4 cm. Which tools did you use? Could you do it with only a marked ruler and a pencil?

A ruler alone will do it, but only by trial. Draw a base AB of 4 cm and mark a point C that is 4 cm from A, as in Fig. 7.2. There is no reason for BC to come out as 4 cm too, so C has to be moved and measured again and again.

A compass makes the construction exact. In Class 6 you found the top point of a house shape that was 5 cm from two other points, and the same method works here. Draw AB of 4 cm first.
