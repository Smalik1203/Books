Altitudes by folding

Cut out a paper triangle and choose one side as the base. Fold the triangle so that the crease passes through the opposite vertex and the base folds onto itself. The crease is the altitude. Why must the crease be perpendicular to the base? Think about the two angles the crease makes with the base.

Constructing an altitude

Draw any triangle ABC, with BC as the base. An altitude drawn with a ruler alone is not accurate, because the right angle is only guessed. A ruler and a set square together give a true right angle (Fig. 7.22).

Step 1: Lay a ruler along BC. Put a set square against it, with one edge of its right angle on the ruler.

Step 2: Slide the set square along the ruler until its upright edge touches A.

Step 3: Draw along the upright edge from A to BC.
