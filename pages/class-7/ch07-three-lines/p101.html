Using What You Know

The chapter gave you the triangle inequality, the rule for two angles, the angle sum property, and the idea of drawing a parallel line through a vertex. Every question below needs only those. The new part is choosing which one.

Two sides of a triangle are 6 cm and 9 cm, and the third side is a whole number of centimetres. What can it be?

The third side must be less than $6 + 9 = 15$ cm. It must also be long enough that 9 cm is less than the other two sides together, so the third side and 6 cm must add up to more than 9 cm; the third side is more than 3 cm. So it is 4, 5, 6, and so on up to 14 cm: eleven lengths. Both checks were needed, because the third side could be the longest or not.

A triangle has one angle of $100^\circ$, and its other two angles are equal. What are they?

The other two angles add up to $180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ$, so each is $40^\circ$. Could the $100^\circ$ angle be one of the two equal angles instead? Then two angles would add up to $200^\circ$, which is more than $180^\circ$. So there is only one answer.

An exterior angle of a triangle is $125^\circ$, and one of the two angles inside the triangle away from it is $70^\circ$. Find the third angle.

The angle beside the exterior angle makes a straight angle with it, so it is $180^\circ - 125^\circ = 55^\circ$. The three angles add up to $180^\circ$, so the third is $180^\circ - 55^\circ - 70^\circ = 55^\circ$. Notice that $70^\circ + 55^\circ = 125^\circ$: the exterior angle is the sum of the two angles inside the triangle away from it.

Can a triangle have two right angles?

Two right angles add up to $180^\circ$, and two angles of a triangle must add up to less than $180^\circ$. So no. In a construction, the arms at A and B would both be perpendicular to AB, so they would be parallel and would never meet.
