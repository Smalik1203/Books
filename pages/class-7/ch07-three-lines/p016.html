Why is an acute-angled triangle not defined as a triangle with one acute angle? How many acute angles does every triangle have?

Exercise Set 7.9
  1. Construct △ABC with BC $=$ 5 cm, AB $=$ 6 cm and CA $=$ 5 cm. Then construct the altitude from A to BC. Measure its length, and say whether it falls inside or outside the triangle.
  1. Construct △TRY with RY $=$ 4 cm, TR $=$ 7 cm and $\angle R = 140^\circ$. Then construct the altitude from T to RY. Where does it meet the line of RY? Why does RY have to be extended first?
  1. Construct a right-angled triangle ABC with $\angle B = 90^\circ$ and AC $=$ 5 cm. How many different triangles have these measurements? (Hint: take AC as the base. What can $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ be, so that $\angle B$ is $90^\circ$?)
  1. By constructing, find out whether an equilateral triangle can be right-angled, or obtuse-angled. Then construct an isosceles triangle that is right-angled, and one that is obtuse-angled.

The shortest path on a box

A spider sits at corner S of a closed box and wants to reach corner T, the corner farthest from it (Fig. 7.25). It cannot fly, so it must walk over the faces of the box. Which path is the shortest? Mark the path you think is shortest on a cardboard box, and compare its length with your classmates’ paths. It may help to cut the box open and lay it flat.
