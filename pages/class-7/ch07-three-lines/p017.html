What this chapter showed
  1. With a compass, a triangle can be constructed from the lengths of its three sides.
  2. Three lengths satisfy the triangle inequality when each is less than the sum of the other two. They are the sides of a triangle exactly when they satisfy it.
  3. A triangle can be constructed from two sides and the included angle, or from two angles and the included side.
  4. Two angles can belong to one triangle only when their sum is less than $180^\circ$.
  5. The angles of any triangle add up to $180^\circ$.
  6. An altitude is the perpendicular segment from a vertex to the line of the opposite side.
  7. By their sides, triangles are equilateral, isosceles or scalene; by their angles, they are acute-angled, right-angled or obtuse-angled.

Before constructing a triangle, check that it can exist: the longest side must be shorter than the other two together, and two given angles must add up to less than $180^\circ$.
