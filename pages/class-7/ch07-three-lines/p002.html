A compass makes the construction exact. In Class 6 you found the top point of a house shape that was 5 cm from two other points, and the same method works here. Draw AB of 4 cm first.

Step 1: With the compass opened to 4 cm and its point on A, draw a long arc. C lies somewhere on this arc.

Step 2: Keep the compass at 4 cm, put its point on B, and draw an arc that crosses the first. The crossing point is C.

Step 3: Join AC and BC.

Why is △ABC equilateral? C lies on the first arc, so it is 4 cm from A, and it lies on the second arc, so it is 4 cm from B.

7.2A Triangle From Its Three Sides

Now construct a triangle with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm. Once again, a ruler alone would need many trials.

Choose one length for the base, say 4 cm, and draw AB. Call the third vertex C, with AC $=$ 5 cm and BC $=$ 6 cm. Every point that is 5 cm from A lies on the circle with centre A and radius 5 cm, so C is somewhere on that circle. C is also 6 cm from B, so it lies on the circle with centre B and radius 6 cm. C must be a point where the two circles cross. We do not need the whole circles, only the parts near C.

Step 1: Draw the base AB $=$ 4 cm (Fig. 7.4 (i)).

Step 2: With centre A, draw a long arc of radius 5 cm (Fig. 7.4 (ii)).

Step 3: With centre B, draw an arc of radius 6 cm that crosses the first arc. The crossing point is C (Fig. 7.4 (iii)).

Step 4: Join AC and BC.
