Two sides and an angle that is not included between them make up the SSA (side, side, angle) condition. We have just seen that the SSA condition does not always give congruent triangles, so measurements of this kind cannot be trusted to fix a triangle.

So far we have used two sides and one angle. Can we use two angles and one side? There are two cases, and we look at them one at a time. Take first the case where the side lies between the two angles.

Two angles and the included side

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle XYZ$, $BC = YZ = 5$ cm, $\angle B = \angle Y = 50^\circ$ and $\angle C = \angle Z = 30^\circ$. Here the equal side lies between the two equal angles. Are the triangles congruent? Could two triangles with these measurements fail to be congruent? Construct and find out.

You have constructed triangles like this before. Draw BC, then draw an angle of $50^\circ$ at B and an angle of $30^\circ$ at C. The vertex A is where the arms of the two angles meet, and they can meet at only one point. Drawing it in another place would only move the triangle or turn it round. So every triangle with these measurements is congruent to every other, and $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$.

The ASA condition

If two angles and the included side of one triangle are equal to two angles and the included side of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. This is called the ASA (angle, side, angle) condition for congruence.
