Writing a congruence

The triangles in Fig. 1.9 are congruent. Sides marked with the same number of ticks are equal. To place one triangle on the other, which vertex of $\triangle ABC$ should go on which vertex of $\triangle XYZ$? The equal sides have to lie on each other. Work out how.

Place A on X, B on Y and C on Z. Then each side lies on the side equal to it, and the triangles fit exactly. Is there any other way of placing the vertices that works?

Because the triangles fit exactly, their angles fit too: $\angle A = \angle X$, $\angle B = \angle Y$ and $\angle C = \angle Z$. When two congruent triangles are placed one on the other, the parts that lie on each other are called corresponding parts. For these triangles they are:

corresponding vertices: A and X, B and Y, C and Z

corresponding sides: AB and XY, BC and YZ, CA and ZX

corresponding angles: $\angle A$ and $\angle X$, $\angle B$ and $\angle Y$, $\angle C$ and $\angle Z$

We write the congruence of the two triangles as

$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$

and read it as “triangle ABC is congruent to triangle XYZ”. The order of the letters shows which vertices correspond. The first vertex named on the left corresponds to the first vertex named on the right, the second to the second, and the third to the third.

So for these two triangles it is wrong to write $\triangle ACB \cong \triangle XYZ$, because C does not correspond to Y. But $\triangle ACB \cong \triangle XZY$ is correct.

Can you find two congruent triangles in Fig. 1.10? Why are they congruent?

Look at $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$. Since ABCD is a rectangle, its opposite sides are equal, so AB = CD and AD = CB. The third side of each triangle is BD, which the two triangles share. So the SSS condition holds, and the triangles are congruent.
