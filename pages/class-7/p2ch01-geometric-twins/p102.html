In the four-sided figure ABCD, AB = CD and AD = CB. Show that $\angle A = \angle C$.

Draw the diagonal BD to make two triangles. In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle CDB$, AB = CD, AD = CB, and BD is a side of both. By SSS, $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle CDB$. In this congruence A corresponds to C, so $\angle A = \angle C$.

Problem 1

If $\triangle PQR \cong \triangle STU$, which of these must be true?

  1. PQ = TU
  2. $\angle Q = \angle U$
  3. QR = TU
  4. PR = ST
Solution

Read the vertices in order: P corresponds to S, Q to T, and R to U.

sides: PQ and ST, QR and TU, PR and SU

angles: $\angle P$ and $\angle S$, $\angle Q$ and $\angle T$, $\angle R$ and $\angle U$

The answer is (c). Options (a), (b) and (d) each pair a part with the wrong partner.

Problem 2

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$, AB = DE, $\angle B = \angle E$ and BC = EF. Which condition shows that they are congruent, and how is the congruence written?

  1. SSS, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$
  2. ASA, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$
  3. SAS, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle EDF$
  4. SAS, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$
Solution

$\angle B$ lies between AB and BC, and $\angle E$ between DE and EF: this is the SAS condition. B matches E, A matches D, and C matches F.

$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$

The answer is (d). We are not given the parts that (a) and (b) need, and (c) matches A with E.
