Solved Examples

Type 1 · Congruent figures

Example 1

Which of these pairs of figures are congruent? (a) two line segments, each 5 cm long (b) a square of side 4 cm, and a square whose perimeter is 16 cm (c) a square of side 4 cm, and a square of side 5 cm

Solution.

Step 1 (a) $5$ cm $= 5$ cm: congruent a segment has only length
Step 2 (b) side $= 16 \div 4 = 4$ cm: congruent four equal sides
Step 3 (c) $4$ cm $\ne 5$ cm: not congruent same shape, not size
Answer (a) and (b)
Example 2

Two tick-shaped symbols have arms $AB = PQ = 3$ cm and $BC = QR = 6$ cm. In the first symbol $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$, and in the second $\angle PQR = 110^\circ$. Are the symbols congruent? What would make them congruent?

Solution.

Step 1 $AB = PQ$ and $BC = QR$ arms equal
Step 2 $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$, $\angle PQR = 110^\circ$, and $70^\circ \ne 110^\circ$ angles not equal
Answer Not congruent. They would be congruent if $\angle PQR$ were $70^\circ$.
Example 3

In one tick-shaped symbol, AB = 5 cm, BC = 9 cm and $\angle ABC = 75^\circ$. In another, PQ = 9 cm, QR = 5 cm and $\angle PQR = 75^\circ$. Are the symbols congruent? If they are, which arm of the second symbol goes on AB?

Solution.

Step 1 $AB = QR = 5$ cm short arms
Step 2 $BC = PQ = 9$ cm long arms
Step 3 $\angle ABC = \angle PQR = 75^\circ$ angle between the arms
Answer Congruent, after a turn or a flip. QR goes on AB.
