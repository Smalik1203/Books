Why the other options are wrong

B1 $\angle B = \angle C$, and together they make $180^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ$, so each is $65^\circ$. The answer is (b). Option (c) forgets to share the $130^\circ$ between the two angles.
B3 $\angle B = \angle Q = 50^\circ$, so $\angle C = 180^\circ - 70^\circ - 50^\circ = 60^\circ$, and $\angle R = \angle C$. The answer is (a). Option (d) adds $70^\circ$ and $50^\circ$ instead of taking them away from $180^\circ$.
B4 AB = CD, BC = DA and AC = CA, so A matches C, B matches D and C matches A. The answer is (c). Option (a) matches AB with AC, a side with the diagonal.
B6 Fig. 1.14 shows triangles with the same angles and different sizes, so A is false. R is true. The answer is (d).
B8 The angle between AB and AC is $\angle A$, not $\angle B$, so this is SSA and not SAS. The answer is (a).
C1 $\angle C = \angle B$, so the angles are $\angle A$, twice $\angle A$ and twice $\angle A$: five times $\angle A$ makes $180^\circ$, and $\angle A = 36^\circ$. The answer is (a). Option (b) is $\angle B$.
C3 An isosceles triangle with AB = AC fits itself as $\triangle ABC$ and as $\triangle ACB$. An equilateral triangle fits itself in six ways, and a scalene one in only one. The answer is (b).
C5 AB = BC = BE and $\angle ABE = 60^\circ + 90^\circ = 150^\circ$. $\triangle ABE$ is isosceles, so its other two angles share $30^\circ$, and $\angle BAE = 15^\circ$. The answer is (d). Option (a) forgets to share the $30^\circ$.
C7 $\triangle ADB \cong \triangle EDC$ by SAS: AD = ED, BD = CD, and the angles at D are vertically opposite. So AB = EC. The answer is (d).

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Mark the equal parts on a sketch before naming a condition, and check that an equal angle really lies between the equal sides. When no triangles are drawn, draw a line, such as a diagonal, that makes two. And read a congruence letter by letter: the order of the vertices tells you which sides and angles are equal.
