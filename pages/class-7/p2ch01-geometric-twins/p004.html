Are $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle ABF$ congruent? You could trace one and place the tracing over the other, or cut one out and flip it over. You can also see it from the construction: everything below AB is drawn in exactly the same way as everything above it, so AB is a line of symmetry of the figure. Folding the paper along AB puts E exactly on F, and the whole of one triangle on the other. The two triangles are congruent. Try at least one of these ways yourself.

The same happens for any three lengths that make a triangle. Try it with sides of 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm: draw the base, draw the two circles, and cut out the triangles at both crossing points. Then choose a different side as the base and do it again. Every triangle you cut out fits exactly on every other one, whichever side you started with. All triangles with the same three side lengths are congruent, so Meera was right.

The SSS condition

If the three sides of one triangle are equal to the three sides of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. This is called the SSS (side, side, side) condition for congruence. The angles need not be measured: when the sides are equal, the angles are equal too.
