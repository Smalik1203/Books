Example 10

$\triangle ABC$ is right-angled at B, and $\triangle DEF$ is right-angled at E. AC = DF = 10 cm and AB = EF = 6 cm. Are the triangles congruent? If they are, write the congruence.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle B = \angle E = 90^\circ$ right angles
Step 2 $AC = DF = 10$ cm and $AB = EF = 6$ cm hypotenuse and a side
Step 3 B corresponds to E, A to F, and C to D equal parts matched
Answer Congruent by RHS: $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle FED$

Type 5 · Angles of isosceles triangles

Example 11

One angle of an isosceles triangle is $100^\circ$. The other two angles are

  1. $80^\circ$ and $80^\circ$
  2. $40^\circ$ and $40^\circ$
  3. $45^\circ$ and $35^\circ$
  4. $50^\circ$ and $50^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 $100^\circ + 100^\circ = 200^\circ$, more than $180^\circ$ not a base angle
Step 2 $180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ$ angles add to $180^\circ$
Step 3 $80^\circ \div 2 = 40^\circ$ base angles equal
Answer (b)
Example 12

In $\triangle ABC$, AB = AC, and D is the midpoint of BC. Which of these is not always true?

  1. $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$
  2. $\angle ADB = 90^\circ$
  3. $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$
  4. AD = BD

Solution.

Step 1 $AB = AC$, $BD = CD$, $AD = AD$: $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$ SSS
Step 2 $\angle ADB = \angle ADC = 180^\circ \div 2 = 90^\circ$ a straight angle
Step 3 $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$ corresponding angles
Step 4 AD = BD only when $\angle BAC = 90^\circ$ not always
Answer (d)
