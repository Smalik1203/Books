Problem 3

One angle of an isosceles triangle is $100^\circ$. The other two angles are

  1. $80^\circ$ and $80^\circ$
  2. $40^\circ$ and $40^\circ$
  3. $45^\circ$ and $35^\circ$
  4. $50^\circ$ and $50^\circ$
Solution

The two angles opposite the equal sides are equal. They cannot both be $100^\circ$, because two angles of $100^\circ$ already add up to more than $180^\circ$. So $100^\circ$ is the third angle, and the equal angles share what is left.

$180^\circ - 100^\circ = 80^\circ$

$80^\circ \div 2 = 40^\circ$

The answer is (b). Option (a) gives each angle the whole $80^\circ$, the angles in (c) are not equal, and (d) adds up to $200^\circ$.

Problem 4

The segments AB and CD cross at O, and O is the midpoint of both. Which of these must be true?

  1. AB = CD
  2. OA = OC
  3. AC = BD
  4. AC = AB
Solution

Draw a rough sketch and look at $\triangle AOC$ and $\triangle BOD$.

OA = OB, since O is the midpoint of AB

OC = OD, since O is the midpoint of CD

$\angle AOC = \angle BOD$, since they are vertically opposite angles

By SAS, $\triangle AOC \cong \triangle BOD$, so AC = BD. The answer is (c). Nothing given makes the parts in (a), (b) or (d) equal.

In Problem 4 the triangles to compare were not drawn for us. A rough sketch showed two triangles meeting at O, the midpoint gave two pairs of equal sides, and the vertically opposite angles gave the equal angle between them. Choosing the right pair of triangles was most of the work; once that was done, the condition followed from what was given. Problem 5 asks for the same kind of choice.
