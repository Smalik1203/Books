Example 1

Since O is the midpoint of AD, AO = OD. Since O is the midpoint of BC, BO = OC. Are any angles equal? Yes: $\angle AOB = \angle DOC$, because they are vertically opposite angles.

So in $\triangle AOB$ and $\triangle DOC$, two sides and the included angle are equal, and by the SAS condition the triangles are congruent.

Which vertices correspond? AO has to lie on DO, and BO on CO. So A corresponds to D, O to O, and B to C.

$\triangle AOB \cong \triangle DOC$

AB and DC are corresponding sides, so they are equal: AB = CD.
