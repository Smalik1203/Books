Geometric Twins

1.1Congruent Figures

A signboard carries a tick-shaped symbol, and the same symbol has to be painted on a second board. How can we make an exact copy of it? The copy has to match the first symbol in shape and in size.

We could trace the symbol on tracing paper, but that is hard when the symbol is large. Could we take a few measurements instead and draw the copy from them? Which measurements would we need? Name the corner points of the symbol A, B and C, as in Fig. 1.1.

Suppose we measure the two arms and find AB = 4 cm and BC = 8 cm. Are these two lengths enough? Every figure in Fig. 1.2 has arms of 4 cm and 8 cm, and yet the four figures are not the same.

The arms can open by different amounts, so we also need the angle between them. The two lengths AB and BC, together with $\angle ABC$, fix both the shape and the size of the figure. Draw the symbol with AB = 4 cm, BC = 8 cm and $\angle ABC = 80^\circ$.

Figures that are exact copies of each other, with the same shape and the same size, are called congruent figures. One of two congruent figures can be placed exactly over the other; we say it can be superimposed on the other. Trace the first figure in Fig. 1.3 and place your tracing on the second. It fits exactly.
