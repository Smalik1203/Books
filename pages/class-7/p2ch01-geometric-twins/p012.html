In the same way, $\angle Y = 70^\circ$, so $\angle B = \angle Y$. Now the triangles have $\angle B = \angle Y$, $BC = YZ$ and $\angle C = \angle Z$. These are two angles and the side between them, so by the ASA condition, $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$. Finding the third angle first turned the question into one we had already answered.

The AAS condition

If two angles of one triangle, and a side that is not between them, are equal to the corresponding two angles and side of another triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. This is called the AAS (angle, angle, side) condition. It works because the third angles must be equal too, and that gives the ASA condition.

Two sides of a right-angled triangle

The SSA condition does not always give congruent triangles, but there is one important case in which it does. In it, the equal angle is a right angle that is not between the two equal sides. $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle XYZ$ are right-angled at B and Y, with $BC = YZ = 4$ cm and $AC = XZ = 5$ cm.
