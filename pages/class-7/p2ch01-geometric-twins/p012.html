$\triangle PQR$ is the triangle we wanted. Now continue the line $l$ below QR. The arc from R cuts it there as well, at a point S, and $\triangle SQR$ also has the given measurements. Is its shape or size different from that of $\triangle PQR$? It is not: $\triangle SQR$ is $\triangle PQR$ flipped over QR, so the two are congruent. Why? So all triangles with these measurements are congruent, and $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$.

In this case, the parts that were equal were the right angle and two sides, one of which is the side opposite the right angle. In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is called the hypotenuse.

The RHS condition

If the hypotenuse and one other side of a right-angled triangle are equal to the hypotenuse and one side of another right-angled triangle, then the two triangles are congruent. This is called the RHS (right angle, hypotenuse, side) condition.

Five conditions that are enough

From everything so far, two triangles are congruent if any one of these conditions holds: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS or RHS. Two conditions are not enough: three equal angles, and SSA in general.
