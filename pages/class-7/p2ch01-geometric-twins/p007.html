Measuring the angles

Suppose Meera and Rabia measure the three angles of the frame instead of its three sides. Can they make an exact copy from the angles alone? Say the angles are $30^\circ$, $70^\circ$ and $80^\circ$.

Fig. 1.14 shows three triangles, and each of them has these three angles. They have the same shape, but not the same size, so they are not congruent. Two triangles with the same three angles need not be congruent.

Two sides and the included angle

In $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle XYZ$, $AB = XY = 6$ cm, $AC = XZ = 5$ cm and $\angle A = \angle X = 30^\circ$. Are the triangles congruent? To find out, ask whether two triangles that are not congruent could both have these measurements.

The equal angle lies between the two equal sides: it is the included angle. You have already constructed a triangle from two sides and the included angle. Construct a triangle with these measurements, and compare it with the triangles your classmates drew. Are they all congruent? Explain why every triangle with these measurements must be congruent to every other.

Once the $30^\circ$ angle at A is drawn and the two sides are marked off along its arms, the points B and C are fixed, and so is the side BC. There is only one triangle to draw.
