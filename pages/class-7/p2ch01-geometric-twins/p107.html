In Questions 13 to 17, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): In $\triangle ABC$, if AB = BC, then $\angle A = \angle C$.

    Reason (R): In a triangle, the angles opposite equal sides are equal.

  1. Assertion (A): Two equilateral triangles with sides of 4 cm are congruent.

    Reason (R): Every angle of an equilateral triangle is $60^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): If $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DEF$, then BC = EF.

    Reason (R): Two triangles with the same perimeter are always congruent.

  1. Assertion (A): If the three angles of one triangle are equal to the three angles of another, the triangles are congruent.

    Reason (R): The three angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): Two triangles with two pairs of equal sides and one pair of equal angles are always congruent.

    Reason (R): The SAS condition needs the equal angle to be included between the equal sides.
