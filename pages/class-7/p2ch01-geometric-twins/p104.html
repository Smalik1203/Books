Problem 5

In $\triangle ABC$, AB = AC, and D is the midpoint of BC. Which of these is not always true?

  1. $\triangle ABD \cong \triangle ACD$
  2. $\angle ADB = 90^\circ$
  3. $\angle BAD = \angle CAD$
  4. AD = BD
Solution

In $\triangle ABD$ and $\triangle ACD$, AB = AC, BD = CD and AD is a side of both, so by SSS the triangles are congruent. That makes (a) true, and gives two more facts.

$\angle ADB = \angle ADC$, and together they make a straight angle, so each is $90^\circ$

$\angle BAD = \angle CAD$, since they are corresponding angles

So (b) and (c) are true as well. Nothing makes AD equal to BD: in a tall, narrow triangle AD is much longer than BD. The answer is (d).
