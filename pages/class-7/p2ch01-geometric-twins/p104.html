Example 7 · Multiple correct

Which pairs of figures are congruent?

Select all correct options.

  1. Two line segments, each 5 cm long
  2. A square of side 4 cm and a square whose perimeter is 16 cm
  3. A square of side 4 cm and a rectangle 4 cm by 5 cm
  4. Two circles, each of radius 3 cm

Solution.

Step 1Segments of equal length, and circles of equal radius, fit exactly on each other. A square of perimeter 16 cm has side 4 cm.
Step 2A square and a 4 cm by 5 cm rectangle cannot be placed exactly on each other.
Answer(a), (b), (d)
Example 8 · Multiple correct

Which of these facts about two triangles are always enough to show that they are congruent?

Select all correct options.

  1. All three pairs of sides are equal.
  2. Two pairs of sides and the angles between them are equal.
  3. All three pairs of angles are equal.
  4. Two pairs of sides and an angle not between them are equal.

Solution.

Step 1SSS and SAS always give congruent triangles.
Step 2Equal angles fix the shape but not the size. Two sides and an angle not between them can give two different triangles.
Answer(a), (b)
Example 9 · Multiple correct

In △ABC, AB = AC, and D is the midpoint of BC. Which statements are always true?

Select all correct options.

  1. △ABD ≅ △ACD
  2. ∠ADB = 90°
  3. ∠BAD = ∠CAD
  4. AD = BD

Solution.

Step 1By SSS, △ABD ≅ △ACD, so ∠BAD = ∠CAD and ∠ADB = ∠ADC = 90°. AD = BD fails for a tall triangle.
Answer(a), (b), (c)
