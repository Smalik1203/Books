1. Find the angles marked with letters in Fig. 1.29. Sides marked with the same number of ticks are equal, and in (iii) ABCD is a square. Give a reason for each angle you find, naming the congruence or the fact about isosceles triangles that you use.
What this chapter showed
  1. Figures with the same shape and the same size are congruent. One can be placed exactly on the other, after turning it round or flipping it over if needed.
  2. Two triangles are congruent if three sides are equal (SSS), two sides and the included angle are equal (SAS), two angles and the included side are equal (ASA), or two angles and a side that is not included are equal (AAS).
  3. In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse. Two right-angled triangles are congruent if their hypotenuses and one other pair of sides are equal (RHS).
  4. Two sides and an angle that is not included (SSA), or three equal angles, do not always give congruent triangles.
  5. In $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle XYZ$, the order of the letters shows which vertices correspond. Corresponding sides and angles of congruent triangles are equal.
  6. In a triangle, the angles opposite equal sides are equal. Every angle of an equilateral triangle is $60^\circ$.
