Using What You Know

The chapter gave you names for large numbers, two ways to place commas, rounding, and quick ways to multiply. The questions below use only those ideas. The difference is that no question tells you which idea it needs.

Can Chitti make 5072 with exactly 20 presses?

The fewest presses for 5072 is $5 + 0 + 7 + 2 = 14$. Any other way uses a smaller button in place of a bigger one. Trading one press of +1000 for ten presses of +100 keeps the number the same and adds 9 presses. Every trade of this kind adds 9 presses, and undoing one takes 9 away. So the possible numbers of presses are 14, 23, 32, 41 and so on. The number 20 is not in that list, so Chitti cannot do it. We did not try a single way of pressing the buttons. We only asked how the count of presses can change.

Without multiplying, how many digits does $4832 \times 67{,}915$ have?

A 4-digit number times a 5-digit number has 8 or 9 digits, so there are only two choices. Now look at the first digits. 4832 is more than 4000, and 67,915 is more than 60,000.

$4000 \times 60{,}000 = 24{,}00{,}00{,}000$

That already has 9 digits, and the real product is bigger still. It cannot have more than 9 digits, so it has exactly 9. The first digits decided between the two choices.

Riya rounds 3,48,000 and 2,46,000 to the nearest lakh, and adds: $3{,}00{,}000 + 2{,}00{,}000 = 5{,}00{,}000$. Is 5,00,000 the nearest lakh of the exact sum?

The exact sum is 5,94,000, so its nearest lakh is 6,00,000. Riya rounded both numbers down, by 48,000 and by 46,000. Together that is 94,000, which is nearly a whole lakh. So rounding first can give a different answer from adding first.
