Working in steps like this, first one year and then ten, keeps a large calculation easy to check. You can use steps for any large calculation.

Exercise Set 1.13
  1. You travel 1000 km every day. Could you reach the Sun in your lifetime? Use the distance you found for the Sun above.
  1. A sheet of paper weighs 5 g. Could you lift one lakh sheets of paper at the same time?
  1. About 250 babies are born in the world every minute. Are a million babies born in one day?
  1. You can count one coin every second. Could you count a million coins in one day?
  1. Make up a question like these, and say what you assumed.
End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. Use each of the digits 0 to 9 exactly once to make a 10-digit number. The number cannot start with 0.
    1. What is the largest such number that is a multiple of 5?
    2. What is the smallest such number that is even?
  1. In words, 10,30,285 is “ten lakh thirty thousand two hundred eighty five”. Its name has 41 letters. Find a 7-digit number whose name has as many letters as possible.
  1. Write a 9-digit number where swapping any two of its digits gives a bigger number. How many such numbers are there?
  1. Cross out 10 digits of the number 12345123451234512345 so that the number left is as large as possible. Then cross out 10 digits so that the number left is as small as possible.
  1. The names zero and one share the letters e and o. The names one and two share the letter o, and two and three share the letter t. How far must you count to find two numbers in a row whose names share no letter?
  1. Write the counting numbers one after another: 1, 2, 3, …, 9, 10, 11, and so on. The tenth digit you write is 1 and the eleventh is 0, both from the number 10.
    1. What is the 1000th digit you write? In which number does it come?
    2. Which number holds the millionth digit?
    3. When do you write the digit 5 for the 5000th time?
