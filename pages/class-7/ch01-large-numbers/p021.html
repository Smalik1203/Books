Chapter 1 at a glance
What this chapter showed
  1. A lakh has 5 zeros, a crore has 7 and an arab has 9. A million is ten lakh, and a billion is a hundred crore.
  2. The Indian system groups digits from the right as 3, 2, 2, …; the American system groups them in threes.
  3. The successor of a number is 1 more than it, and its predecessor is 1 less.
  4. The expanded form of a number is the sum of the place values of its digits.
  5. A number with more digits is greater. With the same number of digits, the first place from the left where the digits differ decides.
  6. To round off, look at the next digit to the right: 5 or more rounds up, and less than 5 rounds down.
  7. Regrouping makes many products quick: $824 \times 25 = 206 \times 100$. An $m$-digit number times an $n$-digit number has $m + n - 1$ or $m + n$ digits.
  8. A sensible assumption and a calculation in steps can answer questions such as whether a city fits into a lakh buses.

Put the commas in first; they name every group of digits.
