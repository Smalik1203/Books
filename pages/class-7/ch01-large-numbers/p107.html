Example 14 · Matching

Match List I with List II.

List IList II
(P) 1 million(1) 100 thousand
(Q) 1 billion(2) 10 million
(R) 1 lakh(3) 10 lakh
(S) 1 crore(4) 100 crore

Each entry has exactly one match.

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–1, S–2
  3. P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
  4. P–2, Q–4, R–1, S–3

Solution.

Step 11 million = 10,00,000 = 10 lakh, and 1 billion = 1,00,00,00,000 = 100 crore.
Step 21 lakh = 1,00,000 = 100 thousand, and 1 crore = 1,00,00,000 = 10 million.
Answer(c) P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
Example 15 · Matching

Match List I with List II.

List IList II
(P) 6,48,572 to the nearest hundred(1) 6,49,000
(Q) 6,48,572 to the nearest thousand(2) 6,50,000
(R) 6,48,572 to the nearest ten thousand(3) 6,00,000
(S) 6,48,572 to the nearest lakh(4) 6,48,600

Each entry has exactly one match.

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–4, Q–1, R–2, S–3
  2. P–1, Q–4, R–2, S–3
  3. P–4, Q–2, R–1, S–3
  4. P–3, Q–1, R–2, S–4

Solution.

Step 172, 572 and 8,572 are past 50, 500 and 5,000, so they round up: 6,48,600, 6,49,000, 6,50,000. But 48,572 is short of 50,000, so it rounds down to 6,00,000.
Answer(a) P–4, Q–1, R–2, S–3
