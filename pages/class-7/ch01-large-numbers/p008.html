The fewest presses

Systematic Sippy is another calculator. Its buttons are +1, +10, +100, +1000, +10000 and +100000. Sippy wants to press as few buttons as possible.

Here is one way for Sippy to make 5072. It presses +1000 five times, +10 six times and +1 twelve times.

$(5 \times 1000) + (6 \times 10) + (12 \times 1) = 5072$

Presses $= 5 + 6 + 12 = 23$

Can Sippy make 5072 with fewer than 23 presses? Find a way, and write its expression.

Now go back to Chitti, which has a +1000000 button. Suppose it presses that button ten times. The screen shows 1 followed by seven zeros. That is a hundred lakh, and it has its own name: one crore. We write one crore as 1,00,00,000.

1.3Crores, Millions and Billions

There are two common ways to name large numbers. In India we use the Indian system. Many other countries use the American system, which is also called the International system. The two systems give the same number different names, and they put the commas in different places.

Table 1.3 shows the same numbers in both systems. Look at where the commas go. In the Indian system, the digits are grouped from the right as 3, then 2, then 2, and so on: thousands, lakhs, crores, arabs. In the American system, the digits are grouped in threes all the way: thousands, millions, billions.

Did you know?

Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, the Maldives, Afghanistan and Myanmar also use the Indian system. The words lakh and crore come from the Sanskrit words lakṣa and koṭi.
