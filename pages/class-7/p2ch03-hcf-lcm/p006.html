Example 5

Find the HCF of 225 and 750. Here $225 = 3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5$ and $750 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 \times 5$.

The fewest 3s is one, in 750, and the fewest 5s is two, in 225, so the HCF is $3 \times 5 \times 5 = 75$.

The same method finds the HCF of three or more numbers.

3.2The Lowest Common Multiple

Torans of the same length

Anshu and Guna make torans by placing strips of cloth end to end. Anshu’s strips are 6 cm long and Guna’s are 8 cm long. They want to make torans of the same length. What is the shortest toran they can both make?

Any toran Anshu makes is a multiple of 6 cm long: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48 cm, … Any toran Guna makes is a multiple of 8 cm: 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 48 cm, … A toran both can make must be a common multiple of 6 and 8, such as 24 cm or 48 cm (Fig. 3.4). The smallest common multiple is 24, so the shortest toran is 24 cm. Is there a largest common multiple?
