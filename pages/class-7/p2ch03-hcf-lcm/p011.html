1. A cowherd took his cows to graze. At a crossing with 3 gates, an equal number of cows went through each gate. At a second crossing with 5 gates, and at a third with 7 gates, the same happened. He had fewer than 200 cows. How many did he have? (This problem comes from the folk mathematics of Karnataka.)
  1. A box is 12 cm long, 18 cm wide and 36 cm high. Which of these cubes can fill the box with no gaps?
    1. 9 cm
    2. 6 cm
    3. 4 cm
    4. 3 cm
    5. 2 cm
  1. Which of these is the largest number that divides both 306 and 36 exactly?
    1. 36
    2. 612
    3. 18
    4. 3
    5. 2
    6. 360
  1. Find the smallest number that is divisible by 3, 4, 5 and 7 but leaves a remainder of 10 when divided by 11.
  1. Children are playing Fire in the Mountain. When 6 is called out, nobody is out. When 9 is called out, nobody is out. When 10 is called out, some children are out. How many children could have been playing at the start?
    1. 72
    2. 90
    3. 45
    4. 3
    5. 36
    6. none of these
  1. $m$ and $n$ are two different primes. Which statements about their LCM can be true?
    1. It is less than both numbers.
    2. It is between the two numbers.
    3. It is greater than both numbers.
    4. It is less than $m \times n$.
    5. It is greater than $m \times n$.
  1. A dog chases a rabbit that is 150 feet ahead. Each time the rabbit jumps 7 feet, the dog jumps 9 feet. After how many jumps does the dog catch the rabbit?
  1. What is the smallest number that is a multiple of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10? You may remember the answer from Class 6.
  1. Mahaviracharya, a mathematician of about 850 CE, set this problem: add together $\frac{8}{15}$, $\frac{1}{20}$, $\frac{7}{36}$, $\frac{11}{63}$ and $\frac{1}{21}$. What do you get? How can you find the sum quickly?
