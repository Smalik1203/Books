Example 14

Is the HCF of two numbers always a factor of their sum? Try one pair, and then give a reason that holds for every pair.

Solution.

Step 1 12 and 18: HCF 6, and $12 + 18 = 30 = 6 \times 5$ one example
Step 2 Call the HCF $h$. The numbers are $h \times p$ and $h \times q$ the HCF divides both
Step 3 $h \times p + h \times q = h \times (p + q)$ distributive property
Answer Yes. The sum is a multiple of $h$, whatever the two numbers are.

Example 15

The largest number that divides 70 and 125, leaving remainders 5 and 8, is

  1. 65
  2. 5
  3. 13
  4. 117

Solution.

Step 1 70 is a multiple plus 5, so the number divides $70 - 5 = 65$ no remainder now
Step 2 In the same way, it divides $125 - 8 = 117$ no remainder now
Step 3 HCF of $65 = 5 \times 13$ and $117 = 3 \times 3 \times 13$ is 13 largest such number
Step 4 $70 = 13 \times 5 + 5$ and $125 = 13 \times 9 + 8$ check
Answer (c)
Example 16

The smallest number greater than 1 that leaves a remainder of 1 when divided by each of 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 is

  1. 61
  2. 31
  3. 121
  4. 59

Solution.

Step 1 Take 1 away, and 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 each divide what is left no remainder now
Step 2 So it is a common multiple, and the smallest is the LCM smallest wanted
Step 3 $\text{LCM} = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 60$
Step 4 $60 + 1 = 61$ smallest above 1
Answer (a)
