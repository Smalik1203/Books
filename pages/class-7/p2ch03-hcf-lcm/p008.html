Finding the LCM

Write the prime factorisation of each number. Take every prime that appears in any of them, as many times as it appears in the factorisation where it appears the most times. The product of these primes is the LCM. This works for two or more numbers.

3.3Patterns and Properties

The HCF of 6 and 18 is 6, which is one of the two numbers. Find more pairs whose HCF is one of the numbers. How would you describe such pairs? This happens exactly when one number is a factor of the other, which also means that the other number is a multiple of the first.

A statement that describes a pattern or a property holding in every case is a general statement, and finding one is called generalisation. We can write such a statement with letter-numbers. A multiple of a number $n$ is a positive integer times $n$. For example, $5n$ is a multiple of $n$, and $n$ is a factor of $5n$, so the HCF of $n$ and $5n$ is $n$.

For pairs of this kind, in which one number is the HCF: (a) if one number is $m$, what could the other be? (b) if one number is $7k$, what could the other be?
