Example 4

Find the HCF of 30 and 72.

$30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$

$72 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$

The HCF is the largest part common to both. It can contain only primes that appear in both factorisations, here 2 and 3. How many 2s? 30 has only one 2, so the largest common part has one 2. How many 3s? 30 has only one 3, so it has one 3. The HCF is $2 \times 3 = 6$. Any larger common part would need a second 2 or a second 3, and 30 has neither.

Finding the HCF

Write the prime factorisation of each number. Take each prime that appears in all of them, as many times as it appears in the factorisation where it appears the fewest times. The product of these primes is the HCF.

Example 5

Find the HCF of 225 and 750. Here $225 = 3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5$ and $750 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 \times 5$.

The fewest 3s is one, in 750, and the fewest 5s is two, in 225, so the HCF is $3 \times 5 \times 5 = 75$.

The same method finds the HCF of three or more numbers.
