Doubling both numbers

What happens to the HCF of two numbers if both are doubled? Try some pairs. Can you see why the HCF doubles too? Doubling gives each number one more 2 in its prime factorisation, and that 2 joins the largest common part. For example:

$270 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$ and $50 = 2 \times 5 \times 5$, so the HCF is $2 \times 5 = 10$

$540 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$ and $100 = 2 \times 2 \times 5 \times 5$, so the HCF is $2 \times 2 \times 5 = 20$

Now take two multiples of 14: $14 \times 6$ and $14 \times 9$. Clearly 14 is a common factor. Is it the highest? In prime factors, $14 \times 6 = (2 \times 7) \times 2 \times 3$ and $14 \times 9 = (2 \times 7) \times 3 \times 3$, so the HCF is $14 \times 3 = 42$.

Find the HCF of each pair of multiples: (a) $18 \times 10$ and $18 \times 15$, (b) $10 \times 38$ and $10 \times 21$, (c) $5 \times 13$ and $5 \times 20$, (d) $12 \times 16$ and $12 \times 20$. In which of them is the HCF equal to the common multiplier, as in (b), where it is 10? Try more examples to find out when this happens.

A quicker way to find the HCF and the LCM

Fig. 3.5 shows a procedure for 84 and 180 together. Can you explain it? It is like the division method, but at each step both numbers are divided by a prime that is a factor of both, and the two quotients are written on the next line. We stop when the two numbers have no common prime factor. How can we use it to find the HCF of 84 and 180?
