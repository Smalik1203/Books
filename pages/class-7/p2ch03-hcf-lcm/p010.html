The HCF, the LCM and the product

Which is greater, the LCM of two numbers or their product? Try examples, and then explain why the LCM is never greater than the product. (Hint: is the product a common multiple of the two numbers?)

There is a neat link between the product of two numbers and their HCF and LCM. Take 105 and 95. Since $105 = 3 \times 5 \times 7$ and $95 = 5 \times 19$, the LCM is $3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 19$. The product is $105 \times 95 = 3 \times 5 \times 5 \times 7 \times 19$. So the LCM is a factor of the product, and $105 \times 95 = \text{LCM} \times 5$.

Check whether the LCM is a factor of the product for (a) 45 and 105, (b) 275 and 352, (c) 222 and 370. When it is, find what the LCM must be multiplied by to give the product. Do you see a pattern? In each case the multiplier is the HCF.

HCF, LCM and product

For any two numbers, $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$ is equal to the product of the two numbers.
