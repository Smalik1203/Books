Type 5 · The HCF, the LCM and the product

Example 11

The product of two numbers is 1080 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is

  1. 6480
  2. 180
  3. 90
  4. 1074

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = 1080$ equals the product
Step 2 $\text{LCM} = 1080 \div 6 = 180$
Step 3 30 and 36: HCF 6, LCM 180, $30 \times 36 = 1080$ check
Answer (b)
Example 12

How many different pairs of numbers have an HCF of 5 and an LCM of 60? Count a pair only once, whichever number is written first.

  1. 1
  2. 2
  3. 3
  4. 4

Solution.

Step 1 Divide both numbers by 5. The two left have no common factor but 1. dividing together
Step 2 Their product $= 60 \div 5 = 12$ LCM is 5 times both
Step 3 $12 = 1 \times 12 = 3 \times 4$ not 2 and 6: they share 2
Step 4 The pairs are 5 and 60, and 15 and 20.
Answer (b)

Type 6 · Conjectures and general statements

Example 13

Kiran makes a conjecture: “If $a$ and $b$ are co-prime, and $b$ and $c$ are co-prime, then $a$ and $c$ are co-prime.” Is Kiran right?

Solution.

Step 1 $4 = 2 \times 2$, $9 = 3 \times 3$: the HCF of 4 and 9 is 1 try 4, 9 and 10
Step 2 $10 = 2 \times 5$, so the HCF of 9 and 10 is 1 no common prime
Step 3 The HCF of 4 and 10 is 2 2 in both
Answer No. 4, 9 and 10 are a counterexample.
