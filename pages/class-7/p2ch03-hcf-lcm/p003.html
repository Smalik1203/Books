Factors from a prime factorisation

A prime factorisation shows every factor of a number. Take $840 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7$. Is $2 \times 2 \times 7 = 28$ a factor of 840? Reorder the primes, which does not change the product:

$840 = (2 \times 2 \times 7) \times (2 \times 3 \times 5) = 28 \times 30$

So 28 is a factor of 840, and $28 \times 30 = 840$. Any product of some of the primes in the factorisation, a part of it, is a factor. Is $2 \times 7 = 14$ a factor of 840? Is $2 \times 2 \times 2$? Is $3 \times 3 \times 3$? Give a reason each time.

Let us list all the factors of 225 in this way. By the division method (Fig. 3.3), $225 = 3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5$. Take the parts of the factorisation one size at a time.

one prime: 3, 5

two primes: $3 \times 3 = 9$, $5 \times 5 = 25$, $3 \times 5 = 15$

three primes: $3 \times 3 \times 5 = 45$, $3 \times 5 \times 5 = 75$

four primes: $3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 5 = 225$

Adding 1, the factors of 225 are 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 25, 45, 75 and 225. Check that no factor is missing.
