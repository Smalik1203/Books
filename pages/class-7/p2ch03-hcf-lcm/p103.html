Problem 3

The product of two numbers is 1080 and their HCF is 6. Their LCM is

  1. 6480
  2. 180
  3. 90
  4. 1074
Solution

$\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$ is the product, so the LCM is $1080 \div 6 = 180$. The answer is (b). Option (a) multiplies instead of dividing, and option (d) subtracts. Check with 30 and 36: their HCF is 6, their LCM is 180, and $30 \times 36 = 1080$.

Problem 4

Three tanks hold 45 L, 60 L and 75 L. What is the largest jug that can measure each of them exactly, using whole jugfuls?

  1. 5 L
  2. 3 L
  3. 900 L
  4. 15 L
Solution

$45 = 3 \times 3 \times 5$, $60 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$, $75 = 3 \times 5 \times 5$

$\text{HCF} = 3 \times 5 = 15$

A 15 L jug fills the tanks in 3, 4 and 5 jugfuls. The answer is (d). Options (a) and (b) are common factors but not the highest, and option (c) is the LCM, a jug larger than every tank.

Problem 5

Which of these cannot be the HCF and the LCM of two numbers?

  1. 6 and 36
  2. 5 and 40
  3. 4 and 30
  4. 1 and 35
Solution

The HCF must be a factor of the LCM. 6 divides 36, 5 divides 40 and 1 divides 35, but 4 does not divide 30. The answer is (c). The others are possible: for example 6 and 36, 5 and 40, and 5 and 7. So checking whether the HCF divides the LCM is enough.
