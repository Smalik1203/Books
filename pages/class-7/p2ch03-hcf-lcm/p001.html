Finding Common Ground

3.1The Highest Common Factor

Tiles for a room

Sameeksha is building a new house. Its main room is 12 ft by 16 ft, and she wants to cover the floor with square tiles that are all the same size. She wants as few tiles as possible, and the side of each tile must be a whole number of feet. Which size should she buy?

For the tiles to fit the breadth of 12 ft exactly, the side of a tile must be a factor of 12. For them to fit the length of 16 ft exactly, it must be a factor of 16. So the side must be a factor of both 12 and 16.

factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12

factors of 16: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16

common factors: 1, 2, 4

So the tiles could have sides of 1 ft, 2 ft or 4 ft. To use as few tiles as possible she should choose the largest of these. Why? She needs tiles with sides of 4 ft (Fig. 3.1). How many tiles should she buy? If the side of a tile did not have to be a whole number of feet, would the answer change?

The highest common factor (HCF) of two or more numbers is the largest of their common factors. It is also called the greatest common divisor (GCD). The HCF of 12 and 16 is 4. A rough drawing like Fig. 3.1 often helps us see what a problem is asking.
