What this chapter showed
  1. Every number greater than 1 has one prime factorisation, apart from the order of the primes; the division method finds it.
  2. Every factor of a number is a part of its prime factorisation, so the factors can be listed from it.
  3. The HCF is the largest common factor. It takes each common prime the fewest number of times it appears.
  4. The LCM is the smallest common multiple. It takes each prime the largest number of times it appears.
  5. Dividing the numbers together by common factors gives the HCF (the divisors) and the LCM (the divisors and the last numbers).
  6. For two numbers, $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$ equals their product.
  7. A conjecture is a claim not yet proved; one counterexample shows it is false. A general statement holds in every case.

The largest prime known today, found on 12 October 2024, has 4,10,24,320 digits. If you started writing it down, one digit a second, how long would it take?

Write both prime factorisations in a line, one under the other, with equal primes lined up. The HCF is what the two lines share, and the LCM is everything in either line, with shared primes counted once.
