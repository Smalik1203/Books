$\dfrac{14}{15} \times \dfrac{25}{42} = \dfrac{14 \times 25}{15 \times 42} = \dfrac{1 \times 5}{3 \times 3} = \dfrac{5}{9}$

Dividing a numerator and a denominator by a common factor before multiplying is called cancelling the common factor. Reducing a fraction to its lowest terms, called apavartana, was so familiar in India that the Jaina scholar Umasvati, around 150 CE, used it as a comparison in a work of philosophy.

Is the product always larger?

Multiplying by 1 leaves a number unchanged, so leave 1 aside. When we multiply two counting numbers larger than 1, such as 3 and 5, the product 15 is larger than both. But $\frac{1}{4} \times 8 = 2$, which is larger than $\frac{1}{4}$ but smaller than 8. And $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{6}{20}$. Writing $\frac{3}{4}$ as $\frac{15}{20}$ and $\frac{2}{5}$ as $\frac{8}{20}$ shows that this product is smaller than both numbers.
