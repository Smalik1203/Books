Example 4 · Single correct

A ribbon $7\frac{1}{2}$ m long is cut into pieces $\frac{3}{4}$ m long. How many pieces are there?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 10
  2. $\frac{45}{8}$
  3. $\frac{1}{10}$
  4. 30

Solution.

Step 1Pieces = $7\frac{1}{2}$ ÷ $\frac{3}{4}$ = $\frac{15}{2}$ × $\frac{4}{3}$.
Step 2= $\frac{60}{6}$ = 10.
Answer(a) 10
Example 5 · Single correct

Half of the students in a class are girls, and $\frac{2}{5}$ of the girls play football. What fraction of the class are girls who play football?

Choose one correct option.

  1. $\frac{9}{10}$
  2. $\frac{3}{7}$
  3. $\frac{1}{5}$
  4. $\frac{4}{5}$

Solution.

Step 1“$\frac{2}{5}$ of the girls” means $\frac{2}{5}$ × $\frac{1}{2}$ of the class.
Step 2$\frac{2}{5}$ × $\frac{1}{2}$ = $\frac{2}{10}$ = $\frac{1}{5}$.
Answer(c) $\frac{1}{5}$
Example 6 · Single correct

Tara drinks $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{1}{3}$ litre of juice. Ravi drinks $\frac{1}{3}$ of $\frac{1}{2}$ litre. Who drinks more?

Choose one correct option.

  1. Both drink the same amount.
  2. Tara
  3. Ravi
  4. It cannot be decided.

Solution.

Step 1Tara: $\frac{1}{2}$ × $\frac{1}{3}$ = $\frac{1}{6}$ litre. Ravi: $\frac{1}{3}$ × $\frac{1}{2}$ = $\frac{1}{6}$ litre.
Step 2The order of multiplying does not change a product.
Answer(a) Both drink the same amount.
