$\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{2}{5} = 3 \times \dfrac{2}{20} = \dfrac{6}{20} = \dfrac{3}{10}$

This is the same method we used for a fraction and a whole number: divide the multiplicand by the denominator of the multiplier, then multiply by the numerator.

Use it to find $\frac{5}{4} \times \frac{3}{2}$. The multiplicand $\frac{3}{2}$ is one whole and a half. In Fig. 8.5 it is drawn as a unit square cut into 2 rows, each row a half, with one more row of the same size below. Cutting the rows into 4 equal columns divides $\frac{3}{2}$ into 4 equal parts, and one part is the shaded column. The unit square is now cut into 2 rows and 4 columns, 8 equal cells, and the shaded column is 3 of those cells, so it is $\frac{3}{8}$. Multiplying by 5:

$\dfrac{5}{4} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = 5 \times \dfrac{3}{8} = \dfrac{15}{8}$

Fractions and the area of a rectangle

In Fig. 8.6 a unit square, with sides of 1 unit, is cut into 2 rows and 4 columns. The shaded rectangle is $\frac{1}{4}$ unit long and $\frac{1}{2}$ unit broad. Eight such rectangles fill the square, whose area is 1 square unit, so the area of the rectangle is $\frac{1}{8}$ square unit. That is exactly $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{2}$.
