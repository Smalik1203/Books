Using What You Know

The chapter gave you the rule for multiplying fractions, cancelling, reciprocals, the rule for dividing, and a way to tell whether an answer should be larger or smaller. Every question below needs only those. The new part is deciding whether a question is asking you to multiply or to divide.

What is $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ of 24?

We could take $\frac{3}{4}$ of 24, which is 18, and then $\frac{2}{3}$ of 18, which is 12. Or we could multiply the fractions first: $\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$ after cancelling, and $\frac{1}{2}$ of 24 is 12. The order of multiplication does not matter, so both ways must agree.

Without calculating, is $\frac{5}{6} \div \frac{2}{3}$ greater or less than $\frac{5}{6}$?

The divisor $\frac{2}{3}$ is between 0 and 1, so the quotient is greater than the dividend. Check: $\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5}{4}$, which is more than 1 and so more than $\frac{5}{6}$. Knowing the answer should be larger catches a slip, such as multiplying by $\frac{2}{3}$ instead.

A recipe for 6 people uses $\frac{3}{4}$ cup of rice. How much rice is needed for 10 people?

For one person it is $\frac{3}{4} \div 6 = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{8}$ cup. For 10 people it is $10 \times \frac{1}{8} = \frac{5}{4}$ cups, or $1\frac{1}{4}$ cups. First we divided to find one share, then we multiplied.

Two numbers multiply to give 1. One of them is $2\frac{1}{3}$. What is the other?

As a fraction, $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$, and the number that gives 1 when multiplied by it is its reciprocal, $\frac{3}{7}$. The reciprocal of the mixed number is not $2\frac{3}{1}$; the mixed number must first be written as one fraction. Check the answer by multiplying: $\frac{7}{3} \times \frac{3}{7} = \frac{21}{21} = 1$.
