Multiplying two fractions

The tortoise walks $\frac{1}{4}$ km in 1 hour. How far does it walk in half an hour? The distance is $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4}$ km: the length we get when $\frac{1}{4}$ km is divided into 2 equal parts.

To see this, let a unit square stand for the whole. In Fig. 8.3, a quarter of the square is shaded. Cutting the square across the middle divides that quarter into 2 equal parts. The whole is now in 8 equal parts, and one of them is shaded darker, so it is $\frac{1}{8}$ of the whole. The tortoise walks $\frac{1}{8}$ km in half an hour, and $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8}$.

Suppose instead that the tortoise walks $\frac{2}{5}$ km in an hour. How far does it walk in $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour? The distance is $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5}$ km, and we find it in the same two steps as before.

Step 1: find the distance in $\frac{1}{4}$ of an hour, by dividing $\frac{2}{5}$ into 4 equal parts.

Step 2: multiply that distance by 3.

In Fig. 8.4 the unit square is cut into 5 rows and 4 columns, which makes $5 \times 4 = 20$ equal parts. Two rows make $\frac{2}{5}$ of the square. Dividing those two rows into 4 equal parts gives the dark part, 2 of the 20 small parts, so the distance in $\frac{1}{4}$ of an hour is $\frac{2}{20}$ km. Three such parts, the dark and light parts together, are 6 of the 20. That is the distance the tortoise walks in $\frac{3}{4}$ of an hour.

$\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{2}{5} = 3 \times \dfrac{2}{20} = \dfrac{6}{20} = \dfrac{3}{10}$

This is the same method we used for a fraction and a whole number: divide the multiplicand by the denominator of the multiplier, then multiply by the numerator.
