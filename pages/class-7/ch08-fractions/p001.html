8

Working with Fractions

8.1Multiplying Fractions

Aaron walks 3 km in 1 hour. How far does he walk in 5 hours? We multiply: in 5 hours he walks $5 \times 3 = 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 15$ km. The same idea, repeated addition, still works when the distance is a fraction.

Aaron’s pet tortoise is much slower. It walks only $\frac{1}{4}$ km in 1 hour. How far does it walk in 3 hours? The distance in 1 hour is now a fraction, but that changes nothing: we still multiply (Fig. 8.1).

$3 \times \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{3}{4}$

Now let the time be a fraction of an hour. How far does Aaron walk in $\frac{1}{5}$ of an hour? Again we multiply: the distance is $\frac{1}{5} \times 3$ km. In $\frac{1}{5}$ of an hour he covers one of 5 equal parts of the 3 km he covers in a whole hour, so the distance is $\frac{3}{5}$ km (Fig. 8.2). So $\frac{1}{5} \times 3 = \frac{3}{5}$.

How far does he walk in $\frac{2}{5}$ of an hour? This time is twice $\frac{1}{5}$ of an hour, so he walks twice as far.
