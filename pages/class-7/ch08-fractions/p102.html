As a fraction, $2\frac{1}{3} = \frac{7}{3}$, and the number that gives 1 when multiplied by it is its reciprocal, $\frac{3}{7}$. The reciprocal of the mixed number is not $2\frac{3}{1}$; the mixed number must first be written as one fraction. Check the answer by multiplying: $\frac{7}{3} \times \frac{3}{7} = \frac{21}{21} = 1$.

Problem 1

$\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{10}{9}$ is equal to

  1. $\dfrac{13}{14}$
  2. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
  3. $\dfrac{27}{50}$
  4. $\dfrac{3}{2}$
Solution

Cancel before multiplying: 3 and 9 have the common factor 3, and 10 and 5 have the common factor 5.

$\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{10}{9} = \dfrac{3 \times 10}{5 \times 9} = \dfrac{1 \times 2}{1 \times 3} = \dfrac{2}{3}$

The answer is (b). Option (a) adds the numerators and adds the denominators. Option (c) divides instead of multiplying. Option (d) is the reciprocal of the answer.

Problem 2

$\frac{3}{4} \div \frac{1}{8}$ is equal to

  1. $\dfrac{3}{32}$
  2. 6
  3. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
  4. $\dfrac{3}{8}$
Solution

Multiply the dividend by the reciprocal of the divisor.

$\dfrac{3}{4} \div \dfrac{1}{8} = \dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{8}{1} = 6$

The answer is (b). Since $\frac{1}{8}$ is between 0 and 1, the quotient must be greater than $\frac{3}{4}$, which rules out (a), (c) and (d) at once. Option (a) multiplies, and (c) turns the dividend upside down instead of the divisor.
