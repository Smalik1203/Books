B7 Each kilometre uses $\frac{1}{12}$ litre, so 30 km uses $30 \times \frac{1}{12} = \frac{5}{2}$ litres. The answer is (c). Option (b) divides 12 by 30.
C2 $\frac{1}{2} \div \frac{1}{8} = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 = 4$. The answer is (c). Option (b) counts the pieces a whole rope would make.
C3 In 1 hour, tap A fills the tank $1 \div \frac{1}{3} = 3$ times and tap B 6 times, so together 9 times, and once in $\frac{1}{9}$ hour. The answer is (a). Option (b) adds the two times.
C5 Multiplying by $\frac{5}{7}$ and then by its reciprocal $\frac{7}{5}$ multiplies by 1 in all. The answer is (c).
C6 Since $q$ is greater than 1, $p \times q$ is greater than $p$; since $p$ is less than 1, it is less than $q$. The answer is (d). Option (c) fails for $p = \frac{1}{10}$ and $q = 2$.
C7 Each tile covers $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{16}$ square metre, and $3 \div \frac{1}{16} = 48$. The answer is (c). Option (a) forgets that a tile has two sides of $\frac{1}{4}$ m.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Decide first whether a question asks for a part of something, which is a multiplication, or for how many times one amount fits into another, which is a division. Before calculating, say whether the answer should be larger or smaller. And write mixed numbers as single fractions before you multiply, divide or take a reciprocal.
