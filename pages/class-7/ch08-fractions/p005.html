Exercise Set 8.2
  1. Find each product, using a unit square as the whole.
    1. $\dfrac{1}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{5}$
    2. $\dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{3}$
    3. $\dfrac{1}{5} \times \dfrac{1}{2}$
    4. $\dfrac{1}{6} \times \dfrac{1}{5}$

Now find $\frac{1}{12} \times \frac{1}{18}$. Drawing it is slow, so look at what happened in Question 1. Each time, the whole was cut into rows and columns. The number of rows is the denominator of the multiplicand, here 18, and the number of columns is the denominator of the multiplier, here 12 (Fig. 8.7). The whole is cut into $18 \times 12$ equal parts, and the product is one of them.

$\dfrac{1}{12} \times \dfrac{1}{18} = \dfrac{1}{12 \times 18} = \dfrac{1}{216}$

A product of two unit fractions is 1 over the product of their denominators: $\frac{1}{b} \times \frac{1}{d} = \frac{1}{b \times d}$.

Multiplying numerators and denominators

Now find $\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{7}{18}$, step by step. Cut the whole into 18 rows and 12 columns, $12 \times 18$ equal parts. Dividing $\frac{7}{18}$ into 12 equal parts gives $\frac{7}{12 \times 18}$. Multiplying this by 5 gives the product.

$\dfrac{5}{12} \times \dfrac{7}{18} = \dfrac{5 \times 7}{12 \times 18} = \dfrac{35}{216}$

Multiplying fractions

$\dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a \times c}{b \times d}$

Brahmagupta first stated this rule in this general form, in his Brahmasphutasiddhanta of 628 CE.
