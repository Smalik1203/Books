Example 5

Chaturveda Prithudakasvami, writing about Brahmagupta’s book around 860 CE, asked this. Four fountains fill a tank. The first fills it in a day, the second in half a day, the third in a quarter of a day and the fourth in a fifth of a day. If all four run together, how long do they take to fill the tank?

Count how many times each fountain fills the tank in one day.

Solution.

Step 1 First: $1 \div 1 = 1$. Second: $1 \div \frac{1}{2} = 2$. times in a day
Step 2 Third: $1 \div \frac{1}{4} = 4$. Fourth: $1 \div \frac{1}{5} = 5$. times in a day
Step 3 Together they fill the tank $1 + 2 + 4 + 5 = 12$ times in a day all four running
Answer They fill it once in $\frac{1}{12}$ of a day.

Fractions of fractions

In Fig. 8.10, what fraction of the whole square is shaded? There are several ways to find it. Here is one. Let the area of the whole square be 1 square unit.

The small square at the top right is $\frac{1}{4}$ of the whole.

The triangle above its diagonal is half of that small square: $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{8}$.

The shaded parts are 3 of the 4 equal triangles in that triangle: $\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{8} = \frac{3}{32}$.

So $\frac{3}{32}$ of the square is shaded. Draw a figure of your own like this, and ask a classmate to find the shaded fraction.

A donation in drammas

This puzzle comes from the Lilavati, written by Bhaskaracharya (Bhaskara II) in 1150 CE. A miser gave a beggar $\frac{1}{5}$ of $\frac{1}{16}$ of $\frac{1}{4}$ of $\frac{1}{2}$ of $\frac{2}{3}$ of $\frac{3}{4}$ of a dramma, a silver coin. One dramma was worth 1280 cowrie shells. How many cowrie shells did the beggar get?

$\dfrac{3}{4} \times \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{16} \times \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{6}{7680} = \dfrac{1}{1280}$
