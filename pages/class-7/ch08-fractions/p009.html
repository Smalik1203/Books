8.3Problems With Fractions

Example 3

Leena makes 5 cups of tea with $\frac{1}{4}$ litre of milk (Fig. 8.9). How much milk is in each cup?

The milk in one cup is $\frac{1}{4} \div 5$ litre, since $5 \times (\text{milk in one cup}) = \frac{1}{4}$. The reciprocal of the divisor 5 is $\frac{1}{5}$.

$\dfrac{1}{4} \div 5 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{1}{20}$

Each cup has $\frac{1}{20}$ litre of milk.

Example 4

Some of the oldest problems with fractions like $\frac{2}{3}$ or $\frac{7}{5}$ come from the Sulbasutras, early Indian books of geometry. This one is from Baudhayana’s Sulbasutra, of about 800 BCE. How many square bricks with sides of $\frac{1}{5}$ unit are needed to cover an area of $7\frac{1}{2}$ square units?

Each brick has area $\frac{1}{5} \times \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{25}$ square unit, and the area to cover is $\frac{15}{2}$ square units. Since number of bricks $\times$ area of one brick $=$ total area,

number of bricks $= \dfrac{15}{2} \div \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{15}{2} \times 25 = \dfrac{375}{2}$

That is $187\frac{1}{2}$ bricks: 187 whole bricks and half a brick.

Example 5

Chaturveda Prithudakasvami, writing about Brahmagupta’s book around 860 CE, asked this. Four fountains fill a tank. The first fills it in a day, the second in half a day, the third in a quarter of a day and the fourth in a fifth of a day. If all four run together, how long do they take to fill the tank?

Count how many times each fountain fills the tank in one day.

First: $1 \div 1 = 1$. Second: $1 \div \frac{1}{2} = 2$. Third: $1 \div \frac{1}{4} = 4$. Fourth: $1 \div \frac{1}{5} = 5$.

Together they fill the tank $1 + 2 + 4 + 5 = 12$ times in a day, so they fill it once in $\frac{1}{12}$ of a day.
