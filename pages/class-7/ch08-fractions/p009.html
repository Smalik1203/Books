8.3Problems With Fractions

Example 3

Leena makes 5 cups of tea with $\frac{1}{4}$ litre of milk (Fig. 8.10). How much milk is in each cup?

Solution.

Step 1 The milk in one cup is $\frac{1}{4} \div 5$ litre, since $5 \times (\text{milk in one cup}) = \frac{1}{4}$ shared equally
Step 2 The reciprocal of the divisor 5 is $\frac{1}{5}$ divide by multiplying
Step 3 $\dfrac{1}{4} \div 5 = \dfrac{1}{4} \times \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{1}{20}$
Answer Each cup has $\frac{1}{20}$ litre of milk.
Example 4

Some of the oldest problems with fractions like $\frac{2}{3}$ or $\frac{7}{5}$ come from the Sulbasutras, early Indian books of geometry. This one is from Baudhayana’s Sulbasutra, of about 800 BCE. How many square bricks with sides of $\frac{1}{5}$ unit are needed to cover an area of $7\frac{1}{2}$ square units?

Solution.

Step 1 Each brick has area $\frac{1}{5} \times \frac{1}{5} = \frac{1}{25}$ square unit a square brick
Step 2 The area to cover is $\frac{15}{2}$ square units as a fraction
Step 3 number of bricks $\times$ area of one brick $=$ total area so divide
Step 4 number of bricks $= \dfrac{15}{2} \div \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{15}{2} \times 25 = \dfrac{375}{2}$ divide by multiplying
Answer That is $187\frac{1}{2}$ bricks: 187 whole bricks and half a brick.
