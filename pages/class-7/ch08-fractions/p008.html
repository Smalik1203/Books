8.2Dividing Fractions

You know that $12 \div 4 = 3$. Here 12 is the dividend, 4 is the divisor and 3 is the quotient. The same question can be asked as a multiplication: what must 4 be multiplied by to give 12? That is, $4 \times ? = 12$. Asking it this way lets us divide fractions.

What is $1 \div \frac{2}{3}$? As a multiplication, it asks: $\frac{2}{3} \times ? = 1$. If we multiply by $\frac{3}{2}$, the 2s and the 3s cancel and leave 1.

$\dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{3}{2} = 1$, so $1 \div \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{3}{2}$.

What is $3 \div \frac{2}{3}$? It asks $\frac{2}{3} \times ? = 3$. We already know what gives 1, and three times that gives 3.

$\dfrac{2}{3} \times \left(\dfrac{3}{2} \times 3\right) = 3$, so $3 \div \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{3}{2} \times 3 = \dfrac{9}{2}$.

What is $\frac{1}{5} \div \frac{1}{2}$? It asks $\frac{1}{2} \times ? = \frac{1}{5}$. Multiplying $\frac{1}{2}$ by 2 gives 1, and then multiplying by $\frac{1}{5}$ gives $\frac{1}{5}$.

$\dfrac{1}{2} \times \left(2 \times \dfrac{1}{5}\right) = \dfrac{1}{5}$, so $\dfrac{1}{5} \div \dfrac{1}{2} = 2 \times \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{2}{5}$.

What is $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{3}{5}$? It asks $\frac{3}{5} \times ? = \frac{2}{3}$.

$\dfrac{3}{5} \times \left(\dfrac{5}{3} \times \dfrac{2}{3}\right) = \dfrac{2}{3}$, so $\dfrac{2}{3} \div \dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{5}{3} \times \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{10}{9}$.

Look at what we did each time. First we found the number that gives 1 when it is multiplied by the divisor. For the divisor $\frac{3}{5}$ that number is $\frac{5}{3}$: its numerator is the divisor’s denominator, and its denominator is the divisor’s numerator. Then we multiplied that number by the dividend.

Reciprocals and division

The reciprocal of $\frac{c}{d}$ is $\frac{d}{c}$, and a fraction multiplied by its reciprocal gives 1. To divide by a fraction, multiply the dividend by the reciprocal of the divisor:

$\dfrac{a}{b} \div \dfrac{c}{d} = \dfrac{a}{b} \times \dfrac{d}{c} = \dfrac{a \times d}{b \times c}$

This rule for dividing fractions was also first stated in general form by Brahmagupta, in 628 CE. With it, $\frac{2}{3} \div \frac{3}{5} = \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{5}{3} = \frac{10}{9}$ in one line.

Dividend, divisor and quotient

When we divide whole numbers, as in $6 \div 3 = 2$, the quotient is less than the dividend. But $6 \div \frac{1}{4} = 24$, and the quotient is greater than the dividend. So is $\frac{1}{8} \div \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{2}$, because $\frac{1}{2}$ is greater than $\frac{1}{8}$. When is the quotient less than the dividend, and when is it greater? Is there a similar pattern for the divisor and the quotient? Use what you found for multiplication: dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal.
