Not every three lengths make a triangle. Each side must be shorter than the other two sides added together. Rods of 3 cm, 4 cm and 9 cm cannot be joined into a triangle, since $3 + 4 = 7$, which is less than 9. But when three rods do make a triangle, they make only one.

The diagonal is also the cure. Nail a bar across a gate from corner to corner, and the gate becomes two triangles. Neither triangle can change shape, so the gate cannot either. Look at a roof frame, an electricity tower, a crane or a bicycle. Each is built from triangles, or from quadrilaterals with a diagonal added.

You can see this on any building site. Bamboo or steel scaffolding is tied together in squares and rectangles, and these could lean. So workers tie long poles across them from corner to corner. Each square becomes two triangles, and the scaffolding stands firm.

So the diagonal did three jobs in this chapter. It cut a quadrilateral into two triangles and gave the angle sum. It made the congruent triangles that proved the properties of a parallelogram. And it is the bar that stops a frame from leaning.

Rigid frames
  • Three side lengths fix a triangle. Four side lengths do not fix a quadrilateral.
  • A diagonal brace turns a quadrilateral frame into two triangles, so it cannot lean.
