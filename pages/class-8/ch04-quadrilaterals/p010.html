4.5A Family, Not a List

Quadrilaterals have names — trapezium, kite, parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle, square — and they are usually met as a list of six shapes to be learnt one at a time. They are not a list. Each name is a condition laid on the figure, and the conditions stack: adding one takes you a step down a family in which everything below inherits everything above it, and inherits it for free, without any further proof.

trapezium one pair of opposite sides parallel
kite two pairs of adjacent sides equal
parallelogram both pairs of opposite sides parallel
rhombus a parallelogram with all four sides equal
rectangle a parallelogram with all four angles right
square both at once — all sides equal, all angles right

Read down that list and the conditions only ever get stronger, so the shapes only ever get more particular. That has a consequence people find hard to accept the first time: every square is a rectangle, and every square is also a rhombus, and every rectangle and every rhombus is a parallelogram, and every parallelogram is a trapezium.

The objection is usually but a square is not a rectangle, it is a square. Compare it with people: a square is a rectangle in the way that a sparrow is a bird. Naming the narrower thing does not stop the wider name being true, and every fact proved about rectangles is therefore free for squares.

The only awkward member is the kite, which does not sit under the parallelogram at all — its equal sides are next to each other rather than opposite. A kite and a parallelogram overlap in exactly one shape, and it is the rhombus.
