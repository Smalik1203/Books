4.7Rhombus, Rectangle and Square

A rhombus, a rectangle and a square are all parallelograms. So all four properties of Section 4.6 are true for each of them. Each one also has an extra property, and in each case it is about the diagonals.

In a rhombus the diagonals cross at right angles. Let the diagonals of rhombus $ABCD$ meet at $O$. Compare triangles $AOB$ and $COB$. Here $AB = CB$, since all the sides of a rhombus are equal. $AO = CO$, since the diagonals bisect each other. The side $OB$ is common. By the SSS rule the triangles are congruent, so $\angle AOB = \angle COB$. These two angles lie on the straight line $AC$, so they add to $180^\circ$. Each of them is therefore $90^\circ$.

The same congruent triangles show one more fact: $\angle ABO = \angle CBO$. So each diagonal of a rhombus cuts the angles at its ends in half.

In a rectangle the diagonals are equal. Compare triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$ in rectangle $ABCD$. The side $AB$ is common. $BC = AD$, since opposite sides are equal. The angles at $B$ and $A$ are both right angles. By the SAS rule the triangles are congruent, so $AC = BD$.

In a square the diagonals are equal and cross at right angles. A square is both a rhombus and a rectangle, so it has both properties. Its diagonals also cut each corner angle in half, into two angles of $45^\circ$.

These facts also work as tests. If the diagonals of a quadrilateral bisect each other, it is a parallelogram. If they also cross at right angles, it is a rhombus. If they bisect each other and are equal, it is a rectangle.
