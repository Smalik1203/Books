4.6Properties of a Parallelogram

A parallelogram is defined by one condition only: both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. The definition says nothing about lengths or angles. Yet four more properties follow from it. We prove them with a diagonal, as we did for the angle sum.

Draw parallelogram $ABCD$ and its diagonal $AC$. Since $AB$ is parallel to $DC$, the diagonal $AC$ is a transversal of these two lines. So the alternate angles $\angle BAC$ and $\angle DCA$ are equal. Since $AD$ is parallel to $BC$, the alternate angles $\angle DAC$ and $\angle BCA$ are also equal.

Now compare triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$. They have two pairs of equal angles, and the side $AC$ between those angles is common to both. By the ASA rule, the two triangles are congruent. Each part of one triangle is equal to the matching part of the other.

In every parallelogram
  • Opposite sides are equal, and opposite angles are equal.
  • Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$, and the diagonals bisect each other.

Opposite sides are equal. In the congruent triangles, $AB$ matches $CD$ and $BC$ matches $DA$. The definition only said the sides were parallel, and now we know they are equal too.

Opposite angles are equal. $\angle B$ and $\angle D$ are matching angles of the two triangles, so they are equal. $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ are each made of two parts, and the parts are equal in pairs. So the whole angles are equal, and $\angle A = \angle C$.

Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$. $AD$ is parallel to $BC$, and $AB$ is a transversal. So $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are interior angles on the same side of the transversal, and they add to $180^\circ$.
