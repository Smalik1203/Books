4.2Polygons

A polygon is a closed figure made of straight sides. Each part of this matters. The sides are straight, so a circle is not a polygon. The figure is closed, so a path that stops short is not a polygon. The sides meet only at their ends, so a figure whose sides cross each other is not a polygon either.

A point where two sides meet is a vertex. A polygon always has as many vertices as sides. Polygons are named by the number of their sides.

After ten, we usually just count. A polygon with 15 sides is called a $15$-gon, and one with $n$ sides is called an $n$-gon.

4.2.1 Convex and concave

Pick any two points inside a polygon and join them with a straight line. In a convex polygon the line always stays inside, wherever the two points are. In a concave polygon you can find two points whose line goes outside the figure and comes back in.

In this chapter a polygon is convex unless we say otherwise. Most of the results work for concave polygons too, but the figures are easier to read when every corner points outwards.
