In a regular polygon all the turns are equal. So each exterior angle is $360^\circ$ shared equally among the $n$ corners.

$$\text{each exterior angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}$$

This rule is often quicker than the interior one. A regular hexagon turns $360^\circ \div 6 = 60^\circ$ at each corner, so each interior angle is $180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$. This is the same answer as in Section 4.3.

Example 4

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $30^\circ$. Find the number of sides and each interior angle.

Solution.

Step 1 $30n = 360$ the turns make a full turn
Step 2 $n = 12$ divide by 30
Step 3 $180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$ on a line
Answer 12 sides; each interior angle is $150^\circ$

Check with the angle sum: $(12 - 2) \times 180 = 1800$, and $1800 \div 12 = 150$.

Since $n$ must be a whole number, the rule also tells us when a regular polygon cannot exist. We only need to check whether the exterior angle divides $360^\circ$ exactly.

Example 5

Is there a regular polygon with an exterior angle of $26^\circ$?

Solution.

Step 1 $26n = 360$ if there were one
Step 2 $360 \div 26$ is between 13 and 14 $26 \times 13 = 338$
Answer No. The number of sides must be a whole number

For example, $360 \div 24 = 15$, so a regular polygon can have exterior angles of $24^\circ$. It cannot have exterior angles of $26^\circ$.
