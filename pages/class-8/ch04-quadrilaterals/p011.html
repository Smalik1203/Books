4.5Kinds of Quadrilaterals

Six quadrilaterals have names of their own: the trapezium, the kite, the parallelogram, the rhombus, the rectangle and the square. Each name is a condition on the figure. A figure that meets the condition gets the name.

trapezium at least one pair of opposite sides parallel
kite two pairs of adjacent sides equal
parallelogram both pairs of opposite sides parallel
rhombus a parallelogram with all four sides equal
rectangle a parallelogram with all four angles right angles
square a parallelogram with all sides equal and all angles right

Going down from parallelogram, each name adds a condition to the one above it. So each shape lower down is a special case of the shapes above it. This means that every square is a rectangle. Every square is also a rhombus. Every rectangle and every rhombus is a parallelogram, and every parallelogram is a trapezium.

Some people say, “A square is not a rectangle. It is a square.” But a square meets every condition for a rectangle: its opposite sides are parallel and all its angles are right angles. A square is a rectangle in the same way that a mango is a fruit. The narrower name does not make the wider name false. So every fact we prove about rectangles is also true for squares.

The kite is different. Its equal sides are next to each other, not opposite each other, so a kite need not be a parallelogram. Only one shape is both a kite and a parallelogram: the rhombus.
