4.2.2 Diagonals

A diagonal is a line segment that joins two vertices that are not next to each other. A triangle has no diagonals, because every pair of its vertices is already joined by a side. A quadrilateral has two. Draw one of them and the quadrilateral is cut into two triangles.

Each of the $n$ vertices of an $n$-gon can be joined to $n - 3$ other vertices: all of them except itself and the two next to it. That gives $n(n - 3)$ joins. But each diagonal has two ends, so it has been counted twice. We halve the count.

$$\text{number of diagonals of an } n\text{-gon} = \frac{n(n-3)}{2}$$

For a quadrilateral this gives $\dfrac{4 \times 1}{2} = 2$. For a pentagon it gives $\dfrac{5 \times 2}{2} = 5$. Both agree with Fig. 4.2.

4.2.3 Regular polygons

A polygon is regular when all its sides are equal and all its angles are equal. Both conditions are needed. A rhombus has equal sides but need not have equal angles. A rectangle has equal angles but need not have equal sides. The only regular quadrilateral is the square.

Polygons
  • An $n$-gon has $n$ sides, $n$ vertices and $\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}$ diagonals.
  • A regular polygon has all its sides equal and all its angles equal.
