Example 1

Three angles of a quadrilateral are $85^\circ$, $110^\circ$ and $47^\circ$. Find the fourth.

Solution.

Step 1 the four angles add to $360^\circ$ angle sum
Step 2 $85 + 110 + 47 = 242$ the three given
Step 3 $360^\circ - 242^\circ = 118^\circ$ what is left
Answer The fourth angle is $118^\circ$

The formula runs backwards just as easily. If you are told the angle sum, you can find the number of sides, because $(n-2) \times 180^\circ$ has only one $n$ that fits.

Example 2

The interior angles of a polygon add to $1440^\circ$. How many sides has it?

Solution.

Step 1 $(n-2) \times 180 = 1440$ the angle-sum rule
Step 2 $n - 2 = 8$ divide by 180
Answer $n = 10$: the polygon is a decagon

The division must come out exactly. An angle sum of $1500^\circ$ would give $n - 2 = 8\tfrac13$, and no polygon has $10\tfrac13$ sides — so a figure with that angle sum does not exist, and saying so is the whole answer.

In a regular polygon the angles are all the same, so each one is the total shared out equally.

$$\text{each angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{(n-2) \times 180^\circ}{n}$$

A regular hexagon therefore has angles of $\dfrac{720^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ$, and a regular pentagon $\dfrac{540^\circ}{5} = 108^\circ$ — a number worth remembering, because it is not one of the friendly ones and it decides which shapes will tile a floor.
