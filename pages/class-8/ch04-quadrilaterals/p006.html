Example 1

Three angles of a quadrilateral are $72^\circ$, $124^\circ$ and $95^\circ$. Find the fourth angle.

Solution.

Step 1 The four angles add to $360^\circ$ angle sum
Step 2 $72 + 124 + 95 = 291$ the three given
Step 3 $360 - 291 = 69$ what is left
Answer The fourth angle is $69^\circ$

The rule also works backwards. If you know the angle sum, you can find the number of sides: divide the angle sum by $180^\circ$, and then add 2 to the answer. This gives the number of sides.

Example 2

The angles of a polygon add to $1620^\circ$. How many sides does it have?

Solution.

Step 1 $(n - 2) \times 180 = 1620$ the angle-sum rule
Step 2 $n - 2 = 9$ divide by 180
Answer $n = 11$. The polygon has 11 sides

The division must come out exactly. An angle sum of $1700^\circ$ gives $n - 2 = 1700 \div 180$, which is not a whole number. So no polygon has that angle sum.

In a regular polygon all the angles are equal. So each angle is the angle sum divided by the number of angles.

$$\text{each angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{(n-2) \times 180^\circ}{n}$$

A regular hexagon has angles of $\dfrac{720^\circ}{6} = 120^\circ$. A regular pentagon has angles of $\dfrac{540^\circ}{5} = 108^\circ$. For a regular octagon, work in two steps.

angle sum $(8 - 2) \times 180^\circ = 1080^\circ$
each angle $1080^\circ \div 8 = 135^\circ$
