Summary
  1. A polygon is a closed figure made of straight sides that meet only at their ends. A polygon with $n$ sides is an $n$-gon, and it has $n$ vertices.
  2. In a convex polygon, the line joining any two points inside stays inside. A concave polygon has an angle greater than $180^\circ$.
  3. A diagonal joins two vertices that are not next to each other. An $n$-gon has $\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2}$ diagonals, so a hexagon has $\dfrac{6 \times 3}{2} = 9$.
  4. A regular polygon has all its sides equal and all its angles equal.
  5. A diagonal cuts a quadrilateral into two triangles, so the angle sum of a quadrilateral is $360^\circ$. The angles of an $n$-gon add to $(n - 2) \times 180^\circ$.
  6. An exterior angle and its interior angle add to $180^\circ$. The exterior angles of any polygon add to $360^\circ$, so each exterior angle of a regular $n$-gon is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. For example, a regular 12-gon has exterior angles of $30^\circ$.
  7. The trapezium, kite, parallelogram, rhombus, rectangle and square form one family. Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, and every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram.
  8. In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal, opposite angles are equal, adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$, and the diagonals bisect each other.
  9. The diagonals of a rhombus cross at right angles. The diagonals of a rectangle are equal. The diagonals of a square do both.
  10. A triangle made of rods cannot change shape, but a quadrilateral can. A diagonal brace makes a quadrilateral frame rigid.
