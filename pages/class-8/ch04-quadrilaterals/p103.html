A quadrilateral's diagonals bisect each other. Prove that it must be a parallelogram — the chapter proved this the other way round.

Let the diagonals of $ABCD$ meet at $O$ with $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$. Compare triangles $AOB$ and $COD$.

sides $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$, given
angle between $\angle AOB = \angle COD$, vertically opposite
so the triangles are congruent

That gives $\angle OAB = \angle OCD$ — alternate angles equal, so $AB$ is parallel to $CD$. The same argument on triangles $BOC$ and $DOA$ makes $BC$ parallel to $AD$, and the figure is a parallelogram.

Notice how little the argument used. Not one length was measured and not one angle was named a number; the whole of it ran on alternate angles are equal applied twice. A converse proved this way is worth more than the same fact checked on twenty drawings, because it covers the parallelograms nobody has drawn.

It is worth noticing which way round the statement runs. The chapter proved that a parallelogram's diagonals bisect each other; this proves the reverse, that a quadrilateral whose diagonals bisect each other must be a parallelogram. Neither follows from the other, and a great many wrong answers in geometry come from using one where the other was needed.

Regular polygons are laid round a point with no gaps and no overlaps: one equilateral triangle, one regular hexagon, and two copies of a third regular polygon. What is the third polygon?

Polygons meeting at a point fill it when their angles there add to exactly $360^\circ$. Each angle of a regular polygon is $180^\circ$ less its exterior angle, and the exterior angle is $360^\circ$ shared out between the sides.

triangle $180 - 360 \div 3 = 60$
hexagon $180 - 360 \div 6 = 120$
left over $360 - 60 - 120 = 180$
each of the two $180 \div 2 = 90$

An angle of $90^\circ$ leaves an exterior angle of $90^\circ$, and $360 \div 90 = 4$, so the third polygon is the square. Taken round the point in the order triangle, square, hexagon, square, the angles are $60 + 90 + 120 + 90 = 360$, and that pattern, repeated, can cover a whole floor.
