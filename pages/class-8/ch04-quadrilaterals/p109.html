Example 14 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) Each angle of a regular hexagon(1) 9
(Q) Each exterior angle of a regular octagon(2) $540^\circ$
(R) The angle sum of a pentagon(3) $120^\circ$
(S) The number of diagonals of a hexagon(4) $45^\circ$

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–2, S–1
  3. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  4. P–1, Q–4, R–2, S–3

Solution.

Step 1$720^\circ \div 6 = 120^\circ$ and $360^\circ \div 8 = 45^\circ$.
Step 2$3 \times 180^\circ = 540^\circ$, and a hexagon has $\frac{6 \times 3}{2} = 9$ diagonals.
Answer(a) P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1
Example 15 · Matching

Match each property of the diagonals of a quadrilateral with the quadrilateral it describes. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) Diagonals that bisect each other(1) rhombus
(Q) Diagonals that bisect each other and are equal(2) square
(R) Diagonals that bisect each other at right angles(3) parallelogram
(S) Diagonals that are equal and bisect each other at right angles(4) rectangle

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–1, S–2
  3. P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
  4. P–2, Q–4, R–1, S–3

Solution.

Step 1Bisecting diagonals give a parallelogram; making them equal gives a rectangle.
Step 2Making them perpendicular gives a rhombus, and both together a square.
Answer(c) P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
