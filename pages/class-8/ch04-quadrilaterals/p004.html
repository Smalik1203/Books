4.3The Angle Sum

One fact is assumed here and proved in Class 9: the three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$. Everything in this section is that fact, applied more than once.

4.3.1 A quadrilateral is two triangles

Draw a quadrilateral $ABCD$ and put in the diagonal $AC$. It cuts the figure into triangle $ABC$ and triangle $ACD$, and the thing to check — because the whole argument rests on it — is that between them those two triangles use up every one of the quadrilateral's four angles and nothing else. The angles at $B$ and at $D$ are taken whole, one by each triangle. The angles at $A$ and at $C$ are each split in two by the diagonal, and the two triangles take one piece apiece.

So add the two triangles' angle sums and you have added the quadrilateral's four angles and nothing else.

triangle $ABC$ $\angle B + \angle BAC + \angle BCA = 180^\circ$
triangle $ACD$ $\angle D + \angle DAC + \angle DCA = 180^\circ$
add them $\angle A + \angle B + \angle C + \angle D = 360^\circ$
