Exercise Set 7.1
  1. Find the area of each trapezium. Lengths are in centimetres.
    1. parallel sides $10$ and $6$, height $4$
    2. parallel sides $9$ and $15$, height $8$
    3. parallel sides $22$ and $18$, height $5$
  1. A trapezium of area $60$ cm2 has parallel sides $11$ cm and $9$ cm. Find the height.
  1. A trapezium of area $132$ m2 has height $8$ m and one parallel side $20$ m. Find the other.
  1. An L-shaped room is $6$ m by $5$ m with a $2$ m by $3$ m corner missing. Find its floor area two ways — by cutting it into two rectangles, and by taking the corner from a whole rectangle.
  1. A triangle has base $14$ cm and area $56$ cm2. Find its perpendicular height.
  1. Two triangles stand on the same base of $10$ cm between the same pair of parallel lines $6$ cm apart. One is upright and one leans far over. Which has the greater area?
  1. A field is a trapezium with parallel sides $40$ m and $60$ m, $30$ m apart. Turf costs ₹$45$ a square metre. What will it cost to turf the field?
  1. Show that when the two parallel sides of a trapezium are equal, the formula $\tfrac12(a+b)h$ becomes the formula for a parallelogram. What figure is it then?
  1. Somebody computes the area of a trapezium with sides $12$ and $8$ and height $5$ as $\tfrac12 \times 12 + 8 \times 5$. What answer do they get, what did they leave out, and why does the bracket matter?

7.4Any Quadrilateral

A quadrilateral with no parallel sides at all — no trapezium, no parallelogram, nothing with a name — still has an area, and there is still a way to it. The way is Chapter 5's diagonal, which has already done more work in this book than any other line. Draw one across the figure and the quadrilateral becomes two triangles, both of them standing on that same diagonal as their base.
