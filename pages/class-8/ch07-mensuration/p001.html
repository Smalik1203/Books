7

Covering and Filling

7.1Introduction

Two questions can be asked about the same cardboard box, and they are not the same question. How much paper would it take to wrap it? And how much rice would it hold?

The first is about the box's outside — its skin, the flat surface you would have to cover. The second is about its inside — the space you would have to fill. One is measured in square centimetres and the other in cubic centimetres, and there is no arithmetic anywhere that turns one into the other.

Keeping the two apart is the whole discipline of this chapter, and it is not as easy as it sounds. Both are worked out from the same three measurements of the same box, by formulas that look much alike, and a great deal of the marks lost here are lost by answering the wrong one of the two questions perfectly.

Two questions, two units

Area covers, and is counted in squares — cm2, m2. Volume fills, and is counted in cubes — cm3, m3. If your answer's unit is wrong, the answer is wrong, whatever the arithmetic did.

7.2Area, by Cutting It Up

There is only one method for finding the area of a flat figure, and every formula in the first half of this chapter is a case of it: cut the figure into pieces whose areas you already know, and add them. Sometimes it is easier to add a piece and take it away again afterwards, which is the same method with a minus sign.

Three areas are known already, and everything is built on them.

rectangle $\text{length} \times \text{breadth}$
square $\text{side}^2$
triangle $\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$
