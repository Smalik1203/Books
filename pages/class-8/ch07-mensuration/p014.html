None of this needs a new formula. It is $\text{area} = k^2$ and $\text{volume} = k^3$, which came from the fact that area is two lengths multiplied and volume is three — the same fact that gave Chapter 1 its two words, squared and cubed, and that has been sitting under this whole chapter since its first page.

Exercise Set 7.5
  1. A cube's edge is trebled. By what factor does each of these grow?
    1. the length of an edge
    2. the area of one face
    3. the volume
  1. A cube of side $2$ cm and a cube of side $6$ cm sit side by side. How many of the small ones would fill the large one? Check by dividing the volumes.
  1. A model of a building is made to a scale of $1 : 100$. The model's roof has area $40$ cm2. What is the real roof's area, in square metres?
  1. A tank holds $8$ times as much as a similar tank half its size. Explain why, in one sentence.
  1. A cube of side $1$ cm is cut into $1000$ cubes of side $1$ mm. Find the total surface area before and after, and say what has happened to the volume.
  1. Explain, using this section, why crushed ice cools a drink faster than a single block of the same weight.
  1. Two cylinders are similar, the larger having three times the radius and three times the height of the smaller. Find the ratio of their curved surface areas and of their volumes.
  1. A giant is built ten times as tall as a person, of the same proportions and the same materials. His weight goes up by one factor and the strength of his leg bones by another. Find both factors, and say what that predicts.
  1. Is there a cube whose surface area and volume are the same number? Find it, and then say why the question is really about units rather than about cubes.
