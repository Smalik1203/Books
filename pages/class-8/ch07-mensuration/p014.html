What this chapter established
  1. Two different questions can be asked about one solid. Area covers and is counted in squares; volume fills and is counted in cubes. No arithmetic turns one into the other, and a wrong unit is a wrong answer.
  2. There is one method for the area of a flat figure: cut it into pieces whose areas you know and add them, or add a piece and take it away again. Every formula below is that method tidied up.
  3. Two copies of a trapezium make a parallelogram of base $a + b$ and height $h$, so one trapezium is $\tfrac12 (a+b)h$. The height is always the perpendicular one.
  4. A diagonal cuts any quadrilateral into two triangles on the same base, so its area is $\tfrac12 d(h_1 + h_2)$ — half the diagonal times the sum of the two perpendiculars onto it.
  5. In a rhombus those two perpendiculars are each half the other diagonal, because the diagonals bisect each other at right angles. So its area is $\tfrac12 d_1 d_2$, and a square's is $\tfrac12 d^2$.
  6. A solid's net is its surface area laid flat. Add up the net and you have the surface area with no formula at all.
  7. A cuboid has three pairs of matching faces, so its surface area is $2(lb + bh + hl)$; a cube's is $6a^2$, the same formula with the three measurements equal.
  8. The lateral surface is the sides alone and the total is everything. Read the question: a closed box has six faces, an open tank five, a room to be painted four walls and a ceiling.
  9. Unrolled, a cylinder's curved surface is a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$. So the curved surface is $2\pi r h$, and with both ends the total is $2\pi r(h + r)$.
  10. Volume is the area of the base times the height, for all three solids — $lbh$, $a^3$ and $\pi r^2 h$ — because each was made by dragging a flat shape straight upwards.
  11. Capacity is volume in litres: $1$ cm3 is a millilitre, $1000$ cm3 a litre, and $1$ m3 a thousand litres.
  12. Multiply every length by $k$ and areas grow by $k^2$ while volumes grow by $k^3$ — because an area is two lengths multiplied and a volume is three. Big things are bulky for their skin, which is why a cake bakes slowly, crushed ice cools quickly, and an elephant's legs are not a mouse's scaled up.
