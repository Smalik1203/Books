Type 7 · The volume of a cylinder

Example 15

A cylindrical water tank is $2.8$ m across and $2$ m high. How many litres does it hold? Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $r = 1.4$ m half the width across
Step 2 $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4^2 \times 2 = 12.32$ m3 $\pi r^2 h$
Step 3 $12.32 \times 1000 = 12\,320$ into litres
Answer 12 320 litres
Example 16

A metal pipe is $70$ cm long. Its inner radius is $3$ cm and its outer radius is $4$ cm. Find the volume of metal in it. Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\pi \times 4^2 - \pi \times 3^2 = \pi (4^2 - 3^2)$ whole circle less the hole
Step 2 $\tfrac{22}{7} \times (16 - 9) = \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 = 22$ cm2 the ring's area
Step 3 $22 \times 70 = 1540$ base times height
Answer 1540 cm3

This is the chapter's first method again, used on a base: the ring is a whole circle with a smaller one taken away.

Type 8 · How area and volume grow

Example 17

Every edge of a cube of side $18$ cm is halved. Compare the new surface area and volume with the old.

Solution.

Step 1 $6 \times 18^2 = 1944$ and $6 \times 9^2 = 486$ the two surfaces
Step 2 $18^3 = 5832$ and $9^3 = 729$ the two volumes
Step 3 $486 \div 1944 = \tfrac14$ and $729 \div 5832 = \tfrac18$ $k = \tfrac12$
Answer The surface area is a quarter of the old, the volume an eighth
