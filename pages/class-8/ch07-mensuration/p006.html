7.5Solids, and What They Unfold Into

Three solids are enough for this chapter, and all three are made the same way: take a flat shape and drag it straight up. Drag a rectangle and you get a cuboid. Drag a square as far as its own side and you get a cube. Drag a circle and you get a cylinder.

Cut such a solid open along its edges and lay it flat, and what you have is its net. The net is the surface area made visible: it is exactly the material the solid is built from, spread out where it can be measured.

That last point is worth taking seriously. Every surface-area formula in the next section is only the net's pieces added up and tidied, and a reader who forgets a formula in an examination can draw the net and add the faces instead.
