7.6Covering: Surface Area

A cuboid $l$ long, $b$ broad and $h$ high has six faces in three matching pairs: two of $l \times b$, two of $b \times h$, two of $h \times l$. Add them.

$$\text{surface area of a cuboid} = 2(lb + bh + hl)$$

A cube is the case where all three are the same, so all six faces are $a \times a$ and the surface area is $6a^2$. There is no need to remember it separately; it is the cuboid formula with $l = b = h$.

Two more words are worth having. The lateral surface area is the sides alone, without the top and bottom — which is what you want for the walls of a room, or a label round a tin. The total surface area is everything. A question about painting the four walls of a hall is a lateral-surface question, and answering it with the total is the standing error of this section.

Read the question for what is actually being covered. A closed box needs six faces. An open tank needs five. A room to be painted needs four walls and a ceiling, but not the floor. The formula is never wrong. What goes wrong is using all of it when the question asked for part.

Example 4

A closed box measures $8$ cm by $5$ cm by $3$ cm. Find its total surface area.

Solution.

Step 1 $lb = 40$,   $bh = 15$,   $hl = 24$ the three pairs
Step 2 $2(40 + 15 + 24) = 2 \times 79$ each face twice
Answer $158$ cm2

7.6.1 The cylinder, unrolled

A tin's curved surface looks like nothing you have a formula for, until you cut it down its side and unroll it. What you get is a rectangle — and its two measurements are already known.

So the curved surface is a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$, and the two flat ends are circles of area $\pi r^2$ each.
