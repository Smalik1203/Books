Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

Four things came out of this chapter and nothing below needs a fifth. An area is found by cutting a figure into pieces you know. A surface area is the net added up. A volume is the area of the base times the height. And multiplying every length by $k$ multiplies areas by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.

What the questions below have in common is that none of them says which of the two measurements it wants, and several of them want both. Read each one twice before starting, and try it before turning the page.

The surface area of a cube is $216$ cm2. Find its volume — and then say why the answer turns out to be the same number as the surface area.

A block of wax $12$ cm by $6$ cm by $4$ cm is melted down and cast into small cubes of side $2$ cm. How many cubes are made?

A well $7$ m across is dug $10$ m deep, and the earth taken out is spread evenly over a field $22$ m by $14$ m. How deep is the layer? Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

A stone is dropped into a tank $50$ cm by $40$ cm holding water, and the level rises by $2$ cm. Find the volume of the stone.

A wire is bent into a square of side $22$ cm. It is straightened and bent into a circle instead. Which encloses the greater area, and by how much?
