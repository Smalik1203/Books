Using What You Know

Four things came out of this chapter and nothing below needs a fifth. An area is found by cutting a figure into pieces you know. A surface area is the net added up. A volume is the area of the base times the height. And multiplying every length by $k$ multiplies areas by $k^2$ and volumes by $k^3$.

What the questions below have in common is that none of them says which of the two measurements it wants, and several of them want both. Read each one twice before starting, and try it before reading what follows it.

A cylinder has radius $2$ cm and height $7$ cm. Find its volume and its curved surface area, taking $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$ — and then say why the two answers turn out to be the same number.

The volume is $\pi r^2 h$ and the curved surface is $2\pi r h$, so work out each in turn.

volume $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 2^2 \times 7 = 88$ cm3
curved surface $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 2 \times 7 = 88$ cm2

Both come to $88$ because $\pi r^2 h$ and $2\pi r h$ are equal exactly when $r = 2$. The numbers agree; the quantities do not, one being cubic centimetres and the other square.

A block of wax $12$ cm by $6$ cm by $4$ cm is melted down and cast into small cubes of side $2$ cm. How many cubes are made?

Melting does not create or destroy wax. So the volume of the block equals the volume of all the cubes together, and the count follows by dividing.

block $12 \times 6 \times 4 = 288$ cm3
one cube $2^3 = 8$ cm3
count $288 \div 8 = 36$

Thirty-six cubes. What did not survive is the surface area: the block had $288$ cm2 of skin and the cubes have $864$ between them. Cutting up leaves the volume alone and multiplies the surface.
