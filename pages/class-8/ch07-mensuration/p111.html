Why the other options are wrong

A1 $\tfrac12(10+6)\times 5 = 40$. Option (a) forgets to halve; (d) adds the two parallel sides and halves but never multiplies by the height.
A3 Six faces of $5 \times 5$. Option (b) is the volume of the same cube, and choosing it is the standing error of the whole chapter — the right arithmetic done to the wrong question.
A7 Unrolled, the curved surface is a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$. Option (a) is the volume, which has $r$ squared because a volume needs three lengths and a surface needs two.
A9 A square is a rhombus with equal diagonals, so its area is $\tfrac12 d^2 = \tfrac12 \times 64$. Option (a) treats the diagonal as though it were the side.
A10 The four walls come to $2(l+b)h = 2 \times 9 \times 3$. Option (b) is $lb + $ something and answers no question; (d) is the floor.
B1 $\tfrac12(10+18)h = 84$ gives $14h = 84$ and $h = 6$. Option (b) halves once too often, which happens when the $\tfrac12$ is applied and then the fourteen is halved again.
B3 $6a^2 = 96$ gives $a = 4$, so the volume is $4^3 = 64$. Option (b) answers with the surface area it was given; (d) takes $a = 8$ by forgetting the six.
B6 The cuboid is $6 \times 3 \times 3$, so its surface is $2(18 + 9 + 18) = 90$. Option (a) is twice one cube's surface area, which double-counts the two faces that are now glued together and hidden.
B9 Volume goes up by $k^3$, and $2^3 = 8$. Option (b) is what happens to the surface area, and option (a) to a single length.
B10 $2\pi r(h+r) = 2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 14 = 616$. Option (a) is the curved surface alone, which is what a label would need but not what the question asked.
C2 Melting keeps the amount of metal, so the volume survives; the shape and therefore the surface area do not. Option (c) is the answer people give without thinking, and the wax question of stage 1 shows how far the surface can change.
C5 The second statement is true, and it is exactly why the first is false: two lengths are multiplied to make an area, so doubling both multiplies it by four.
