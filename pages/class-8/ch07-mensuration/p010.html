7.7Filling: Volume

Volume is counted in cubes. A cubic centimetre is a cube one centimetre on every edge, and the volume of a solid is the number of such cubes that would fill it.

For a cuboid the count is easy to see. Lay one layer of unit cubes on the floor of the box: there are $l \times b$ of them, because the floor is a rectangle $l$ by $b$. Then stack $h$ such layers.

$$\text{volume} = \text{area of the base} \times \text{height}$$

That one line covers all three solids of this chapter, because all three were made by dragging a flat shape straight upwards. What changes between them is only the base.

cuboid base $lb$, so volume $lbh$
cube base $a^2$, so volume $a^3$
cylinder base $\pi r^2$, so volume $\pi r^2 h$

The middle line is where Chapter 1's word came from. A number multiplied by itself three times is cubed because that is the volume of a cube — and $a^2$ is called squared for exactly the same reason one line above it.
