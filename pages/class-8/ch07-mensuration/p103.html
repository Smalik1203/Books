The circle wins by $132$ cm2, over a quarter as much again from the same wire. Of every shape a fixed length can be bent into, the circle encloses the most; a corner is wire spent going in and out.

A closed cylindrical tank of radius $1.4$ m and height $3$ m is to be painted all over on the outside. Paint costs ₹$120$ a square metre. Find the cost, taking $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

All over on the outside means the curved surface and both ends, so the total surface area is what is wanted.

formula $2\pi r(h + r)$
substitute $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4 \times (3 + 1.4)$
area $8.8 \times 4.4 = 38.72$ m2
cost $38.72 \times 120 =$ ₹$4646.40$

A radius of $1.4$ is $\tfrac{7}{5}$, so the sevens cancel here as they did with a radius of $7$. Any radius that is a multiple or a tenth of seven is the question telling you to use $\tfrac{22}{7}$.

A photograph is enlarged so that its area is twenty-five times as large. By what factor have its lengths grown? If a sculpture were enlarged in the same proportion, by what factor would its weight grow?

Areas grow by $k^2$, so if the area is twenty-five times as large then $k^2 = 25$ and the lengths have grown by $5$.

lengths $k = 5$
weight $k^3 = 125$ times

A photograph has no weight worth speaking of, so nobody notices. A sculpture five times as tall in the same material weighs a hundred and twenty-five times as much. That is why a model cannot simply be scaled up. At some size the enlarged version has to be hollow, or braced, or made of something else.
