## 7.3 The Trapezium

### Exercise Set 7.1

1. Area $= \tfrac12 (a + b)h$.
   - (i) $\tfrac12 (10 + 6) \times 4 = 32$ cm²
   - (ii) $\tfrac12 (9 + 15) \times 8 = 96$ cm²
   - (iii) $\tfrac12 (22 + 18) \times 5 = 100$ cm²

2. $\tfrac12 (11 + 9) \times h = 60$, so $10h = 60$ and the height is **6 cm**.

3. $\tfrac12 (20 + b) \times 8 = 132$, so $4(20 + b) = 132$, $20 + b = 33$,
   and the other side is **13 m**.

4. **24 m²** both ways. Answers will vary in the cut; for example:
   - Two rectangles: with the 2 m side of the corner along the 6 m wall,
     cut across the room where the corner ends. That leaves a full-width
     strip $6 \times (5 - 3) = 12$ and a rectangle $(6 - 2) \times 3 = 12$,
     total $12 + 12 = 24$.
   - Whole less corner: $6 \times 5 - 2 \times 3 = 30 - 6 = 24$.

   The drawing must show an L with outer sides 6 m and 5 m, the missing
   corner 2 m by 3 m, and the cut line.

5. $\tfrac12 \times 14 \times h = 56$, so $7h = 56$ and the height is **8 cm**.

6. **Neither: they are equal.** Both have base 10 cm and perpendicular height
   6 cm, so each is $\tfrac12 \times 10 \times 6 = 30$ cm².

7. Area $\tfrac12 (40 + 60) \times 30 = 1500$ m²; cost
   $1500 \times 45 = 67\,500$ rupees, **₹67 500**.

8. With $a = b$: $\tfrac12 (a + a)h = ah$, which is base times height, the
   parallelogram's formula. The figure is then a **parallelogram** (two pairs
   of parallel sides).

9. They get $\tfrac12 \times 12 + 8 \times 5 = 6 + 40 = 46$ cm². They left out
   the bracket, so only the 12 was halved and only the 8 was multiplied by the
   height. The correct area is $\tfrac12 (12 + 8) \times 5 = 50$ cm²: the
   bracket makes the two sides add first.

## 7.4 Any Quadrilateral

### Exercise Set 7.2

1. $\tfrac12 \times 20 \times (7 + 5) = 120$ cm².

2. Area $= \tfrac12 d_1 d_2$.
   - (i) $\tfrac12 \times 8 \times 6 = 24$ cm²
   - (ii) $\tfrac12 \times 10 \times 24 = 120$ cm²
   - (iii) $\tfrac12 \times 16 \times 9 = 72$ cm²
   - (iv) $\tfrac12 \times 7 \times 4 = 14$ cm²

3. $\tfrac12 \times 16 \times d = 96$, so $8d = 96$ and the other diagonal is
   **12 cm**.

4. $\tfrac12 d^2 = \tfrac12 \times 10^2 = 50$ cm².

5. $\tfrac12 \times 50 \times (22 + 18) = 1000$ m², and
   $1000 \div 10\,000 = 0.1$ hectare.

6. Rectangle $12 \times 8 = 96$ m², triangle $\tfrac12 \times 12 \times 5 = 30$ m²,
   total **126 m²**.

7. Area $\tfrac12 \times 6 \times 8 = 24$ cm². The diagonals bisect each other
   at right angles (Chapter 5), so each side is the long side of a right
   triangle with legs 3 cm and 4 cm: the side is **5 cm** (by measuring an
   accurate drawing, or by Pythagoras, which Mathematics II teaches).

8. **No.** The area is $\tfrac12 d_1 d_2$, and the diagonals change as the
   rhombus is pushed over. Answers will vary; for example a square of side 10
   has area 100 cm², while a nearly flat rhombus of side 10 has one very short
   diagonal and an area close to 0. The drawings must show two rhombuses with
   all four sides 10 cm, one with angles near 90° and one with two angles
   very small.

9. The diagonals are both $s\sqrt{2}$, so
   $\tfrac12 \times s\sqrt{2} \times s\sqrt{2} = \tfrac12 \times 2s^2 = s^2$,
   which is the square's area.

## 7.6 Covering: Surface Area

### Think and Reflect (the label and the open tin)

- The label needs the **curved surface** only, $2\pi r h$; for the tin of
  Example 5 that is $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 10 = 440$ cm².
- Open at the top, the tin has the curved surface and **one** end:
  $2\pi r h + \pi r^2$. For Example 5's tin, $440 + 154 = 594$ cm².

### Exercise Set 7.3

1. Surface area $= 2(lb + bh + hl)$.
   - (i) $2(60 + 24 + 40) = 248$ cm²
   - (ii) $2(60 + 25 + 60) = 290$ cm²
   - (iii) $2(63 + 14 + 18) = 190$ cm²

2. $6a^2$.
   - (i) $6 \times 9 = 54$ cm²
   - (ii) $6 \times 49 = 294$ cm²
   - (iii) $6 \times 100 = 600$ cm²
   - (iv) $6 \times 2.25 = 13.5$ cm²

3. $6a^2 = 150$, so $a^2 = 25$ and the side is **5 cm**.

4. Four walls: $2(6 + 5) \times 4 = 88$ m². With the ceiling
   $6 \times 5 = 30$ m²: $88 + 30 = 118$ m².

5. Curved surface $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 20 = 880$ cm². Total:
   $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times (20 + 7) = 1188$ cm².

6. 14 cm across means $r = 7$ cm. Open at both ends, only the curved surface:
   $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 50 = 2200$ cm².

7. Base $3 \times 2 = 6$ m², walls $2(3 + 2) \times 1.5 = 15$ m², no lid:
   **21 m²**.

8. The cuboid is 8 cm by 4 cm by 4 cm:
   $2(32 + 16 + 32) = 160$ cm². Two cubes have $2 \times 96 = 192$ cm², but the
   two faces pressed together ($2 \times 16 = 32$ cm²) are hidden, and
   $192 - 32 = 160$.

9. Doubled: $2(2l \times 2b + 2b \times 2h + 2h \times 2l) = 4 \times 2(lb + bh + hl)$,
   **four times** as large, because every term is two lengths multiplied and
   $2 \times 2 = 2^2 = 4$ — Chapter 1's squaring.

## 7.7 Filling: Volume

### Exercise Set 7.4

1. $lbh$.
   - (i) $8 \times 5 \times 3 = 120$ cm³
   - (ii) $12 \times 10 \times 4 = 480$ cm³
   - (iii) $7 \times 7 \times 2 = 98$ cm³

2. $a^3$.
   - (i) $4^3 = 64$ cm³
   - (ii) $6^3 = 216$ cm³
   - (iii) $10^3 = 1000$ cm³
   - (iv) $0.5^3 = 0.125$ m³

3. $7^3 = 343$, so the side is **7 cm**; surface area $6 \times 49 = 294$ cm².

4. $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 49 \times 15 = 2310$ cm³.

5. - (i) $5 \times 1000 = 5000$ litres
   - (ii) $2500 \div 1000 = 2.5$ litres
   - (iii) $4.5 \times 1000 = 4500$ cm³

6. $3 \times 2 \times 2 = 12$ m³, which is **12 000 litres**.
   $12\,000 \div 50 = 240$ minutes, which is **4 hours**.

7. $360 \div (12 \times 5) = 360 \div 60 = 6$ cm.

8. **Four times**: the radius is squared, and $2^2 = 4$. Check with
   $\pi = 3.14$: $3.14 \times 3^2 \times 10 = 282.6$ and
   $3.14 \times 6^2 \times 10 = 1130.4$, and $1130.4 \div 282.6 = 4$.

9. $2\pi r = 22$, so $r = 3.5$ cm. The height is 10 cm:
   $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5^2 \times 10 = 385$ cm³.

10. **No.** $2\pi r = 10$ gives $r = \tfrac{35}{22}$ cm, and the height is 22 cm:
    $\tfrac{22}{7} \times \tfrac{35}{22} \times \tfrac{35}{22} \times 22 = 175$ cm³,
    less than 385 cm³. Roll the **longer edge round**: the radius is squared,
    so a wide short cylinder gains more than a narrow tall one.

11. Cuboid: $2(96 + 40 + 60) = 392$ cm², volume $12 \times 8 \times 5 = 480$ cm³.
    The cube of volume 480 has a side between 7 and 8 cm ($7^3 = 343$,
    $8^3 = 512$), about 7.83 cm, so its surface is less than
    $6 \times 8^2 = 384$ cm² (about 368 cm²). **The cube takes less material.**

## 7.8 Why the Two Do Not Grow Together

### Exercise Set 7.5

1. (i) **3** (ii) $3^2 = 9$ (iii) $3^3 = 27$.

2. $6 \div 2 = 3$ along each edge, so $3^3 = 27$ small cubes. Check:
   $216 \div 8 = 27$.

3. Areas grow by $100^2 = 10\,000$: $40 \times 10\,000 = 400\,000$ cm², and
   $400\,000 \div 10\,000 = 40$ m².

4. Its lengths are twice as long, and a volume is three lengths multiplied,
   so it holds $2^3 = 8$ times as much.

5. Before: $6 \times 1^2 = 6$ cm². After: each small cube has
   $6 \times 0.1^2 = 0.06$ cm², and $1000 \times 0.06 = 60$ cm² — ten times as
   much. The volume is unchanged: $1000 \times 0.1^3 = 1$ cm³.

6. Crushing does not change the volume (the weight is the same) but
   multiplies the surface, as in Q5. The drink takes heat in through the
   ice's surface, so more surface cools it faster.

7. Curved surfaces $3^2 = 9$ times, so **9 : 1**; volumes $3^3 = 27$ times, so
   **27 : 1**.

8. Weight goes up with volume: $10^3 = 1000$ times. Bone strength goes up with
   the area of the bone's cross-section: $10^2 = 100$ times. Each square
   centimetre of bone carries **ten times** the load, so the giant's legs would
   break — he could not stand.

9. **Yes, side 6**: $6a^2 = a^3$ gives $a = 6$, and $6 \times 36 = 216 = 6^3$.
   The question is about units: 216 cm² and 216 cm³ are different
   quantities. Measured in millimetres the same cube has side 60,
   surface $21\,600$ mm² and volume $216\,000$ mm³, and the match vanishes.

10. 1 litre is 1000 cm³. $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 25 \times h = 1000$ gives
    $h = \tfrac{140}{11} \approx 12.73$ cm. Surface:
    $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 5 \times (\tfrac{140}{11} + 5) = \tfrac{42\,900}{77} \approx 557.1$ cm².
    With radius 10 cm, $h = \tfrac{35}{11} \approx 3.18$ cm and the surface is
    $\tfrac{63\,800}{77} \approx 828.6$ cm²: it **increases**, by about
    271.4 cm², because the two ends grow fourfold while the curved surface
    shrinks by only half.
