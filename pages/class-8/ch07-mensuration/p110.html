Mark all three sets before reading on. The paragraphs that follow take the questions most often answered wrongly and say what each wrong answer was thinking, which is more use than being told again what the right one is.

One pattern accounts for a good many of them. A question gives a solid and asks for a number, and the number wanted is a surface area where the working produced a volume, or the other way about. Both are found from the same three dimensions, both come out of the same first line of working, and only the units tell them apart — which is why the units are worth writing at every step and not only at the end.

A second pattern is narrower and just as costly: answering for the whole solid when the question asked for part of it. An open tank has five faces, a room to be painted has four walls and a ceiling, and a pipe has no ends at all. The formula is never wrong in these; what goes wrong is using all of it.
