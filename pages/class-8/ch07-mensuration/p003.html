A parallelogram's area is base times height, and this one has base $a + b$ and height $h$. That is the area of two trapeziums, so one of them is half as much.

$$\text{area of a trapezium} = \tfrac12 (a + b) \, h$$
Example 1

A trapezium has parallel sides $12$ cm and $8$ cm, and the distance between them is $5$ cm. Find its area.

formula $\tfrac12 (a + b)h$
substitute $\tfrac12 (12 + 8) \times 5$
answer $\tfrac12 \times 20 \times 5 = 50$ cm2

Add the parallel sides before halving, not after. Half of twelve plus eight is fourteen, and fourteen times five is not the answer to anything here.

The formula runs backwards, and where it does the work is Chapter 6's: write down what you know, call the missing measurement $x$, and solve.

Example 2

A trapezium of area $84$ m2 has parallel sides $15$ m and $13$ m. How far apart are they?

let $h$ = the distance in metres
equation $\tfrac12 (15 + 13)h = 84$
simplify $14h = 84$
solve $h = 6$ m

The unit of the answer is metres, not square metres. The square metres were on the left of the equation and were divided away by the sides, which is what a unit does when the arithmetic is done properly.
