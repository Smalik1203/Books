Example 1

A trapezium has parallel sides $12$ cm and $8$ cm, and the distance between them is $5$ cm. Find its area.

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac12 (a + b)h$ the formula
Step 2 $\tfrac12 (12 + 8) \times 5$ substitute
Step 3 $\tfrac12 \times 20 \times 5 = 50$ add before halving
Answer 50 cm2

Add the parallel sides before halving, not after. Half of twelve plus eight is fourteen, and fourteen times five is not the answer to anything here.

The formula runs backwards, and when it does, the work is Chapter 6's: write down what you know, call the missing measurement $x$, and solve.

Example 2

A trapezium of area $84$ m2 has parallel sides $15$ m and $13$ m. How far apart are they?

Solution.

Step 1 $h$ = the distance in metres name the unknown
Step 2 $\tfrac12 (15 + 13)h = 84$ the area formula
Step 3 $14h = 84$ half of 28
Answer $h = 6$, so the sides are 6 m apart

The unit of the answer is metres, not square metres. The square metres were on the left of the equation and were divided away by the sides, which is what a unit does when the arithmetic is done properly.

That habit is worth keeping through the rest of the chapter, because the units are the cheapest check there is. An area that comes out in metres, or a volume in square centimetres, is wrong before the arithmetic is looked at. The mistake is nearly always a length used where an area was wanted, or a dimension left out.
