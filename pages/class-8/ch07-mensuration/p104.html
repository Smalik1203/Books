One wire, two shapes

The wire's length does not change, so the square's perimeter and the circle's circumference are the same number. Find it first.

the wire $4 \times 22 = 88$ cm
square $22^2 = 484$ cm2
circle's radius $2\pi r = 88$,   $r = 14$ cm
circle $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 14^2 = 616$ cm2

The circle wins by $132$ cm2, over a quarter as much again from the same wire. Of every shape a fixed length can be bent into, the circle encloses the most; a corner is wire spent going in and out.

Painting a tank, and paying for it

All over on the outside means the curved surface and both ends, so the total surface area is what is wanted.

formula $2\pi r(h + r)$
substitute $2 \times \tfrac{22}{7} \times 1.4 \times (3 + 1.4)$
area $8.8 \times 4.4 = 38.72$ m2
cost $38.72 \times 120 =$ ₹$4646.40$

A radius of $1.4$ is $\tfrac{7}{5}$, so the sevens cancel here as they did with a radius of $7$. Any radius that is a multiple or a tenth of seven is the question telling you to use $\tfrac{22}{7}$.

Nine times the area, a thousand times the weight

Areas grow by $k^2$, so if the area is nine times as large then $k^2 = 9$ and the lengths have grown by $3$.

lengths $k = 3$
weight $k^3 = 27$ times

A photograph has no weight worth speaking of, so nobody notices. A sculpture three times as tall in the same material weighs twenty-seven times as much, which is why a maquette cannot simply be scaled up: at some size the enlarged version has to be hollow, or braced, or of something else.
