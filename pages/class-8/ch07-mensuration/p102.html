A closed cylindrical tank of radius $1.4$ m and height $3$ m is to be painted all over on the outside. Paint costs ₹$120$ a square metre. Find the cost, taking $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

A photograph is enlarged so that its area is nine times as large. By what factor have its lengths grown? If a sculpture were enlarged in the same proportion, by what factor would its weight grow?

A closed box is to hold exactly $1000$ cm3. It could be $10 \times 10 \times 10$, or $20 \times 10 \times 5$, or $50 \times 20 \times 1$. Find the surface area of each. Which shape uses the least cardboard?

Three of these are questions where a volume is moved rather than measured — the wax, the well and the stone. In every case the trick is the same and worth saying once: the volume does not change when the shape does. Melting, digging, pouring and displacing all keep the amount of stuff the same, so the volume you work out at one end of the question is the volume you use at the other.

The stone is the only one where the volume is not measured directly at all. You cannot put a ruler to a stone. What you can measure is the water it pushed out of the way, and that is exactly as much as the stone.

The last question has an answer that will look like a coincidence and is not. Work out all three surface areas before deciding what you think, and then look at which of the three boxes is nearest to being a cube.

A cube whose two numbers agree

Surface area is $6a^2$, so find the side first and only then the volume.

side $6a^2 = 216$,   $a^2 = 36$,   $a = 6$ cm
volume $a^3 = 216$ cm3

Both come to $216$ because $6a^2 = a^3$ exactly when $a = 6$. The numbers agree; the quantities do not, one being square centimetres and the other cubic — and in millimetres the coincidence vanishes.
