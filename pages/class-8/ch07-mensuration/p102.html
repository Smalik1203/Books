A cylindrical tank $7$ m across holds $385\,000$ litres when full. How deep is it? Take $\pi = \tfrac{22}{7}$.

$7$ m across means a radius of $3.5$ m. Turn the litres into cubic metres first, so that every measurement is in metres, and then run the volume formula backwards.

the water $385\,000 \div 1000 = 385$ m3
the base $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 3.5^2 = 38.5$ m2
depth $385 \div 38.5 = 10$ m

The diameter given rather than the radius is the trap, and it is set on purpose: using $7$ as the radius gives four times the base and a depth of $2.5$ m, which no one would notice was wrong.

A tank $50$ cm by $40$ cm holds water. Another $4$ litres are poured in. How far does the level rise?

The water poured in does not stay where it lands. It spreads over the whole floor of the tank as a thin layer — a cuboid $50$ cm by $40$ cm — and the height of that layer is the rise.

the water in $4$ litres $= 4000$ cm3
the floor $50 \times 40 = 2000$ cm2
the rise $4000 \div 2000 = 2$ cm

Notice what the question never needed: how deep the water was already.

The last three run the chapter's formula backwards: a volume is known, and a count, a depth or a rise is missing. In each, everything goes into one unit first, and the missing number follows by dividing.

A wire is bent into a square of side $22$ cm. It is straightened and bent into a circle instead. Which encloses the greater area, and by how much?

The wire's length does not change, so the square's perimeter and the circle's circumference are the same number. Find it first.

the wire $4 \times 22 = 88$ cm
square $22^2 = 484$ cm2
circle's radius $2\pi r = 88$,   $r = 14$ cm
circle $\tfrac{22}{7} \times 14^2 = 616$ cm2
