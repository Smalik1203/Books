A Shortcut for Divisibility by 11

The shortcut for $11$ also comes from the remainders that the place values leave. Table 5.12 shows how each place value compares with a multiple of $11$.

Table 5.12 Place values and the multiples of 11

units ($1$)$11 \times 0 = 0$$1 = 11 \times 0 + 1$one more than a multiple of $11$
tens ($10$)$11 \times 1 = 11$$10 = 11 \times 1 - 1$one less than a multiple of $11$

This pattern — one more than a multiple of $11$, then one less — carries on for the higher place values.

Since $400$ is $4$ hundreds, it is $4$ more than a multiple of $11$ ($396 + 4$). Since $60$ is $6$ tens, it is $6$ less than a multiple of $11$ ($66 - 6$). And $2$ is $2$ units, so it is $2$ more than a multiple of $11$ ($0 + 2$).

Using these, can you tell whether $462$ is divisible by $11$? What could a general method, or shortcut, for checking divisibility by $11$ be?

The place values alternate between one more and one less than a multiple of $11$. Example 3 turns this into three steps.

Example 3

Find how far $320185$ is from a multiple of $11$, and so the remainder when it is divided by $11$.

Solution.

Step 1 places $1$, $100$, $10000$ hold $5$, $1$, $2$: excess $5 + 1 + 2 = 8$ one more each
Step 2 places $10$, $1000$, $100000$ hold $8$, $0$, $3$: short $8 + 0 + 3 = 11$ one less each
Step 3 $8 - 11 = -3$ excess minus short
Answer $320185$ is $3$ short of a multiple of $11$, which is $8$ more than the multiple before it. The remainder is $8$.
