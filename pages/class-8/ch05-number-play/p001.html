Sum of Consecutive Numbers

Anshu is writing numbers as sums of consecutive numbers — numbers that follow one another without a gap, such as $3, 4, 5$. Here is what he has so far.

$7 = 3 + 4$

$10 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4$

$12 = 3 + 4 + 5$

$15 = 7 + 8 = 4 + 5 + 6 = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5$

Looking at his list, Anshu has some questions.

Think and Reflect

Can every natural number be written as a sum of consecutive numbers?

Which numbers can be written as a sum of consecutive numbers in more than one way?

Every odd number is a sum of two consecutive numbers. Can every even number be written as a sum of consecutive numbers?

Can $0$ be written as a sum of consecutive numbers? Negative numbers may help.

Explore these questions, and any others you think of. Discuss them with your class.

Now take any four consecutive numbers, for example $3, 4, 5$ and $6$. Put a $+$ or a $-$ sign in each gap between them, like this:

$3 + 4 - 5 + 6 \qquad 3 - 4 - 5 - 6$

How many different expressions can you make? Write all of them.

There are three gaps, and each gap takes one of two signs, so there are $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ expressions. The tree in Fig. 5.1 lists them all without missing one. At each branch point it chooses the sign before the next number.
