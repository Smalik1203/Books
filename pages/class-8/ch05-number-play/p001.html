Sums of Consecutive Numbers

Numbers that follow one another with no gap, such as $6, 7, 8$, are called consecutive numbers. Meher likes to break a number into a sum of consecutive numbers. Here are some she has found.

$9 = 4 + 5 = 2 + 3 + 4$

$11 = 5 + 6$

$14 = 2 + 3 + 4 + 5$

$18 = 5 + 6 + 7 = 3 + 4 + 5 + 6$

Letters help us see what such sums have in common. If the smallest number is $n$, then

$n + (n + 1) = 2n + 1$

$n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) = 3n + 3 = 3(n + 1)$

So the sum of two consecutive numbers is always odd, and every odd number from $3$ on is such a sum: $13 = 6 + 7$. The sum of three consecutive numbers is always $3$ times the middle number: $5 + 6 + 7 = 3 \times 6$, and $9 + 10 + 11 = 3 \times 10$.

Meher could not find any way to break up $8$ or $16$. She wonders which other numbers cannot be broken up, and why.

Think and Reflect
  1. Try every number from $1$ to $20$. Which ones cannot be written as a sum of two or more consecutive numbers? What do they have in common?
  2. Find three different ways to write $30$ as a sum of consecutive numbers.
  3. Which numbers are sums of four consecutive numbers? Use letters to find out.
  4. Now allow $0$ and negative numbers as well. Is $8$ then a sum of consecutive integers?
