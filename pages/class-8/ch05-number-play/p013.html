Divisibility by 9

A number is divisible by $9$ if and only if the sum of its digits is divisible by $9$. Add the digits again and again until a single digit is left: that digit is the remainder when the number is divided by $9$, except that a final $9$ means a remainder of $0$.

Look at each of these statements. Which are correct, and why?

  1. If a number is divisible by $9$, then the sum of its digits is divisible by $9$.
  2. If the sum of the digits of a number is divisible by $9$, then the number is divisible by $9$.
  3. If a number is not divisible by $9$, then the sum of its digits is not divisible by $9$.
  4. If the sum of the digits of a number is not divisible by $9$, then the number is not divisible by $9$.

Learning mathematics is not only knowing shortcuts and following steps. It is understanding why a shortcut works.

A Shortcut for Divisibility by 3

All multiples of $9$ are also multiples of $3$. That is, if a number is divisible by $9$, it is also divisible by $3$. But some multiples of $3$ are not multiples of $9$ — for example $15$, $33$ and $87$.

The shortcut for divisibility by $3$ is like the one for $9$. Explore the remainders when the powers of $10$ are divided by $3$, and explain why this method works.

Divisibility by 3

A number is divisible by $3$ if the sum of its digits is divisible by $3$.
