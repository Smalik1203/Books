Divisibility by 9

A number is divisible by $9$ if and only if the sum of its digits is divisible by $9$. Keep adding the digits until one digit is left. That digit is the remainder on division by $9$, except that a final $9$ means a remainder of $0$.

Here are four claims about a number and the sum of its digits. Decide whether each one is true, and give a reason.

  1. Every multiple of $9$ has a digit sum that is a multiple of $9$.
  2. Every number whose digit sum is a multiple of $9$ is a multiple of $9$.
  3. A number that is not a multiple of $9$ never has a digit sum that is a multiple of $9$.
  4. A number whose digit sum is not a multiple of $9$ is never a multiple of $9$.

A test is only useful when you know why it works. Then you can trust it, and you can build new tests of your own.

A Test for 3

Every multiple of $9$ is also a multiple of $3$. But some multiples of $3$, such as $21$, $48$ and $75$, are not multiples of $9$. So we need a test of its own for $3$.

Find the remainders when $10$, $100$ and $1000$ are divided by $3$. Each is $1$, since $10 = 9 + 1$, $100 = 99 + 1$ and $1000 = 999 + 1$, and $9$, $99$ and $999$ are all multiples of $3$. So the same reasoning as for $9$ gives a test for $3$.
