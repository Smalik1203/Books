## 5.1 Reasoning About Multiples

### Think and Reflect (sums of consecutive numbers)

1. $1, 2, 4, 8$ and $16$ cannot be broken up. They are the **powers of 2**.
2. $30 = 9 + 10 + 11 = 6 + 7 + 8 + 9 = 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8$.
3. $n + (n + 1) + (n + 2) + (n + 3) = 4n + 6 = 4(n + 1) + 2$: the numbers
   $2$ more than a multiple of $4$, from $10$ on — $10, 14, 18, 22, \ldots$
4. **Yes**: $-7 + (-6) + \cdots + 7 + 8 = 8$; the numbers from $-7$ to $7$
   cancel in pairs.

### Signs between four consecutive numbers (Fig. 5.1, Table 5.2)

For $7, 8, 9, 10$ the eight values, in the order of Fig. 5.1, are
$34, 14, 16, -4, 18, -2, 0, -20$. For $12, 13, 14, 15$ they are
$54, 24, 26, -4, 28, -2, 0, -30$. Every value is even. For $3, 8, 10, 15$
(sum $36$) all eight are even; for $3, 8, 10, 14$ (sum $35$) all eight are odd.
Changing a $-$ sign to $+$ changes the value by $+2q$ (or $2r$, $2s$), again
even.

### Even Without Working It Out

Even: (i) $86$, (iv) $548$, (vii) $568$, (viii) $2664$. Odd: (ii) $331$,
(iii) $7665$, (v) $809$, (vi) $40005$.

Always even: $6p + 4q$, $8x - 2y = 2(4x - y)$, $9k - 3k = 6k$,
$c^2 + c = c(c + 1)$ (one of two consecutive numbers is even), and
$10r \times 3s = 30rs$. Not always even (counterexamples): $3a + 7b$
($a = 1, b = 0$ gives $3$), $5m + 5n$ ($m = 1, n = 0$ gives $5$), $4a - 5b$
($a = 0, b = 1$ gives $-5$), $y^2 + 4$ ($y = 3$ gives $13$). *Answers will
vary* for the reader's own expressions, e.g. $4a + 8b$, $2m - 6n$, $12k$.

### When Two Even Numbers Make a Multiple of 4

Third case: $4p + (4q + 2) = 4(p + q) + 2$, e.g. $8 + 6 = 14$ and
$12 + 10 = 22$: two dots are always left over, so never a multiple of $4$.

### True Always, Sometimes or Never

Statement 1 **always** (the difference too: $6a - 6b = 6(a - b)$, e.g.
$30 - 12 = 18$). Statement 2 **sometimes**. Statement 3 **always**.
Statement 4 **always**. Statement 5 **sometimes**. Statement 6 **always**:
the LCM of $5$ and $6$ is $30$. Statement 7 **sometimes**: the LCM of $10$ and
$4$ is $20$, and $20$ is divisible by both but not by $40$; $80$ is divisible
by all three. Statement 8 **never**.

### Think and Reflect (after Statement 8)

1. **20**, so Statement 7 is only sometimes true.
2. *Answers will vary.* For example, "a multiple of $5$": $2 + 3 = 5$ is,
   $2 + 5 = 7$ is not.
3. The product can count a shared factor twice. $10 \times 4 = 40$, but a
   number divisible by $10$ and $4$ need only be divisible by $20$.

### Numbers With a Given Remainder

$4, 10, 16, 22, 28, \ldots$ Only $6k + 4$ ($k = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$) and $6k - 2$
($k = 1, 2, \ldots$) describe them all. $4k + 6$ gives $14$; $6k$ gives the
multiples of $6$; $4k$ gives $8$; $6k - 4$ gives $2, 8, \ldots$ Other forms:
$6k + 10$ from $k = -1$, $6k - 8$ from $k = 2$, and so on.

## 5.2 Quick Tests for Divisibility

### The tests for 5, 4 and 8

$10b + 100c + \cdots$ is a multiple of $5$, so the number is divisible by $5$
exactly when $a$ is: $a = 0$ or $5$. From $100$ up every place value is a
multiple of $4$, so only $10b + a$ matters: the last two digits. From $1000$
up every place value is a multiple of $8$, so only the last three digits
matter.

### A Test for 9
