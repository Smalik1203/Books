Summary
  1. If $a$ is divisible by $b$, then every multiple of $a$ is divisible by $b$.
  2. If $a$ is divisible by $b$, then $a$ is divisible by every factor of $b$.
  3. If $a$ divides $m$ and $a$ divides $n$, then $a$ divides $m + n$ and $m - n$.
  4. If $a$ is divisible by $b$ and also by $c$, then $a$ is divisible by the LCM of $b$ and $c$.
  5. We learnt shortcuts to check divisibility by $3$, $9$ and $11$, and why they work.
  6. Along the way we used mathematical reasoning — algebra, pictures, examples and counterexamples — to show that a property holds for every number, not just for the few we tried.

Navakankari

Navakankari, also known as Sālu Mane Āṭa, Chār-Pār or Navkakri, is a traditional Indian board game. It is the same game as Nine Men’s Morris, or Mills, played in the West. It is a strategy game for two players. The aim is to make lines of three of your own pawns, so that you can remove your opponent’s pawns or block their moves.

How to play.

  1. Each player starts with $9$ pawns. The players take turns to place their pawns on the marked points. A point can hold at most one pawn.
  2. Once all the pawns are placed, the players take turns to move one of their pawns to a neighbouring empty point, to make a line of three. A line can be across or up and down.
  3. A player who makes a line of three may remove any one of the opponent’s pawns, as long as it is not part of one of the opponent’s own lines.

A player wins when the opponent has fewer than $3$ pawns left, or cannot make a move.
