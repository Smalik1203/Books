1. Tanvi buys some pencils at ₹6 each and some erasers at ₹9 each. The shopkeeper asks her for ₹100. Show, without knowing how many of each she bought, that the amount must be wrong. Also find the correct amount if she bought $7$ pencils and $5$ erasers.
  1. In the given figure, the left circle holds the multiples of $4$ from $1$ to $60$ and the right circle holds the multiples of $6$ from $1$ to $60$. Write the numbers that belong in the overlap, and explain why they are the multiples of $12$. Also find how many numbers from $1$ to $200$ would lie in the overlap.
  1. Find how many multiples of $11$ lie between $500$ and $700$. Also find the largest of them whose digits are all different.
  1. Find the digits $a$ and $b$ for which the number $7a5b$ is divisible by both $9$ and $10$.
  1. Is it possible for the sum of five consecutive numbers to be $101$? Justify your answer. Also find the five consecutive numbers whose sum is $105$.
Long answer
  1. Mr Iyer has between $100$ and $200$ chairs for a school function. When he sets them out in rows of $6$, $4$ are left over. In rows of $8$, $6$ are left over, and in rows of $9$, $7$ are left over. Find the number of chairs. Also find how many more chairs he needs so that all of them can be set out in full rows of $9$.
  1. Show that for any three-digit number $abc$, the number $abc - (a + b + c)$ is a multiple of $9$. Using this, show that $abc$ is divisible by $9$ exactly when $a + b + c$ is. Hence find the digit $d$ for which $4d7$ is divisible by $9$.
  1. Show that for any three-digit number $abc$, the number $abc - (a - b + c)$ is a multiple of $11$. Hence find the remainders when $759$ and $846$ are divided by $11$.
  1. Solve the cryptarithm $\text{ABCD} \times 4 = \text{DCBA}$. Explain how you found each digit.
  1. For a three-digit number $abc$, show that $100a + 10b + c$ and $2a + 3b + c$ leave the same remainder when divided by $7$. Use this to test whether $581$ and $692$ are divisible by $7$.
