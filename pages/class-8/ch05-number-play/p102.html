Write the number in its general form: tens digit $a$ and units digit $b$ make $10a + b$. The condition is then an equation.

$10a + b = 4(a + b) = 4a + 4b$
so $6a = 3b$, that is, $b = 2a$

The units digit is twice the tens digit. The tens digit cannot be $0$, and $b$ must stay a single digit, so $a$ is $1$, $2$, $3$ or $4$. The numbers are $12$, $24$, $36$ and $48$. Check one: $4 \times (3 + 6) = 36$.

In the cryptarithm $\text{AB} + \text{BA} = 121$, find every possible number AB.

Again, write both numbers in general form.

$(10\text{A} + \text{B}) + (10\text{B} + \text{A}) = 11\text{A} + 11\text{B} = 11(\text{A} + \text{B})$

So $11(\text{A} + \text{B}) = 121$, and $\text{A} + \text{B} = 11$. Any two digits with a sum of $11$ will do. Neither can be $0$, since each starts a number, and they are different because $11$ is odd. So AB can be $29, 38, 47, 56, 65, 74, 83$ or $92$: eight answers.

Priya works out $347 \times 286$ and gets $99142$. Without doing the multiplication, show that her answer is wrong.

A number and its digital root leave the same remainder when divided by $9$, and remainders multiply along with the numbers. So the digital root of a product must match the digital root of the product of the two digital roots.

$347$ $3 + 4 + 7 = 14$, and $1 + 4 = 5$
$286$ $2 + 8 + 6 = 16$, and $1 + 6 = 7$
$5 \times 7$ $= 35$, and $3 + 5 = 8$
$99142$ $9 + 9 + 1 + 4 + 2 = 25$, and $2 + 5 = 7$

The product must have digital root $8$, and $99142$ has $7$, so it is wrong. The right product is $99242$, whose digits add to $26$, and $2 + 6 = 8$. The check can only catch a mistake, though. An answer with the right digital root may still be wrong: $99224$ also has digital root $8$.
