Example 6

Solve $\text{AB} \times 4 = \text{CA}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $25 \times 4 = 100$ has three digits, so AB is at most $24$ A is 1 or 2
Step 2 A is the units digit of $4 \times \text{B}$, which is even so A = 2
Step 3 $4 \times \text{B}$ ends in $2$, so B is $3$ or $8$; AB is $23$ or $28$ 28 is too big
Step 4 $23 \times 4 = 92$ so C = 9
Answer $23 \times 4 = 92$: $\text{A} = 2$, $\text{B} = 3$, $\text{C} = 9$.

Now try $\text{PQ} \times \text{Q} = 6\text{R}$. It has two solutions. Start by asking which digits Q can be, if the product is to lie in the $60$s. Then find P for each choice of Q. Notice that Q cannot be $1$, since then the answer would be PQ itself, and R would be the same digit as Q.

Reasoning with hidden digits
  • The units digit of a product depends only on the units digits multiplied.
  • The number of digits in an answer limits the first digit of each number.

Patterns, the properties of numbers and careful reasoning can solve every cryptarithm. Solve these. In each one, start with the units digits, and then use how many digits the answer has.
