The multiples of $6$ have the form $6k$. The numbers we want are $4$ more than a multiple of $6$, so they have the form $6k + 4$ (Fig. 5.8).

Such a number is also $2$ less than the next multiple of $6$. So the same numbers can be written as $6k - 2$, starting from $k = 1$. Table 5.9 puts the two forms side by side.

Table 5.9 Two expressions for the same numbers
$k$$0$$1$$2$$3$$4$
$6k + 4$$4$$10$$16$$22$$28$
$k$$1$$2$$3$$4$$5$
$6k - 2$$4$$10$$16$$22$$28$

The other four fail. For example, $4k + 6$ gives $14$ when $k = 2$, which leaves a remainder of $2$. Find other expressions that give exactly $4, 10, 16, 22, \ldots$

Numbers with a given remainder

The numbers that leave a remainder of $r$ when divided by $n$ have the form $nk + r$, where $k$ is a whole number.

Example 1

Two numbers each leave a remainder of $4$ when divided by $6$. Find the remainder when their sum is divided by $6$.

Solution.

Step 1 Write the numbers as $6a + 4$ and $6b + 4$.
Step 2 $(6a + 4) + (6b + 4) = 6a + 6b + 8$
Step 3 $= 6(a + b + 1) + 2$ $8$ is $6 + 2$
Answer The remainder is always $2$.
