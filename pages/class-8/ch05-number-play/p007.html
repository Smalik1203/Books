For example, $15 = 5 \times 3$, and its multiples $45 = 15 \times 3$ and $105 = 15 \times 7$ are both multiples of $5$. Statement 3 is always true.

Multiples of a multiple

If $A$ is divisible by $k$, then every multiple of $A$ is divisible by $k$.

Statement 4. If a number is divisible by $18$, then it is divisible by every factor of $18$.

A number divisible by $18$ can be written as $18m$, and

$18m = 2 \times 9 \times m = 3 \times 6 \times m$

Picture $18m$ as $m$ rows of eighteen. A factor of $18$ fills one row exactly, so it fills all $m$ rows exactly. For example, $36 = 18 \times 2$, and its factors $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18$ and $36$ include every factor of $18$. Statement 4 is always true.

Factors of a divisor

If $A$ is divisible by $k$, then $A$ is divisible by every factor of $k$.

Statement 5. If a number is divisible by $5$, then it is divisible by every multiple of $5$.

The number $30$ is divisible by $5$. It is also divisible by $10$ and by $15$, which are multiples of $5$. But it is not divisible by $20$, which is a multiple of $5$ too. So Statement 5 is sometimes true. In general, a multiple of $5$ written as $5k$ is divisible by $5m$ only when $m$ is a factor of $k$. If $k = ym$, then $5k \div 5m = y$.
