Sums and differences of multiples

If $a$ divides $M$ and $a$ divides $N$, then $a$ divides $M + N$ and $a$ divides $M - N$. In other words, if $M$ and $N$ are multiples of $a$, then $M + N$ and $M - N$ are also multiples of $a$.

Statement 2. If a number is divisible by $8$, then $8$ also divides each of any two numbers that add up to it.

A number divisible by $8$ is a multiple of $8$, written $8m$. It can be split into two multiples of $8$, $8m = 8a + 8b$. For example, $72 = 48 + 24$, which is $8 \times 9 = 8 \times 6 + 8 \times 3$. But it can also be split into two numbers $p$ and $q$ that are not multiples of $8$: $72 = 50 + 22$. So Statement 2 is sometimes true.

Statement 3. If a number is divisible by $7$, then every multiple of that number is divisible by $7$.

A number divisible by $7$ has $7$ as a factor, so it is $7j$ for some $j$: for example $14 = 7 \times 2$, $42 = 7 \times 6$ and $98 = 7 \times 14$. A multiple of it is $(7j) \times m$. That is $m$ blocks of $j$ rows of seven, which is $mj$ rows of seven in all (Fig. 5.8). So it is a multiple of $7$.

Some multiples of $14$ are $28 = (7 \times 2) \times 2$, $70 = (7 \times 2) \times 5$ and $154 = (7 \times 2) \times 11$. The number $7jm$, or $7 \times j \times m$, has $7$ as a factor. So Statement 3 is always true.

Multiples of a multiple

If $A$ is divisible by $k$, then every multiple of $A$ is divisible by $k$.

Statement 4. If a number is divisible by $12$, then it is also divisible by every factor of $12$.

A number divisible by $12$ is a multiple of $12$, such as $12, 24, 36, 48, 108$ or $132$. Write it as $12m$. Then

$12m = 2 \times 6 \times m = 3 \times 4 \times m$
