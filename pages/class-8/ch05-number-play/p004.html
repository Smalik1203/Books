Many problems in mathematics can be solved in more than one way. The method you found yourself may be the one you like best, but it is worth knowing how others thought about the same problem. So share your method, and listen to theirs.

Parity of sums and differences

An odd number plus or minus an odd number is even. An even number plus or minus an even number is even. An odd number plus or minus an even number is odd. So $a + b$ and $a - b$ always have the same parity, and all the expressions $a \pm b \pm c \pm d$ have the same parity.

Does this happen only when you take four numbers? What do you think? Asking what if…? and will it always happen?, and making a guess that you then test — a conjecture — is as much a part of mathematics as solving problems.

Breaking Even

You know how to tell whether a number is even. Without working them out, find which of these arithmetic expressions are even.

  1. $43 + 37$
  2. $672 - 348$
  3. $4 \times 347 \times 3$
  4. $708 - 477$
  5. $809 + 214$
  6. $119 \times 303$
  7. $543 - 479$
  8. $513^3$

Now use what you know about how parity behaves under the operations. Which of these algebraic expressions give an even number for every integer value of the letter-numbers?

  1. $2a + 2b$
  2. $3g + 5h$
  3. $4m + 2n$
  4. $2u - 4v$
  5. $13k - 5k$
  6. $6m - 3n$
  7. $x^2 + 2$
  8. $b^2 + 1$
  9. $4k \times 3j$

The expression $4m + 2q$ gives an even number for every pair of integers $m$ and $q$. There are two ways to justify this.

First, $4m$ is even and $2q$ is even for any integers $m$ and $q$, and the sum of two even numbers is even.

Second, $4m + 2q$ is equal to $2(2m + q)$, which means $2$ times $2m + q$. So $2$ is a factor of the expression, and it gives an even number for every $m$ and $q$.

For example, if $m = 4$ and $q = -9$, the expression $4m + 2q$ becomes $4 \times 4 + 2 \times (-9) = -2$, which is even.

In the expression $x^2 + 2$, $x^2$ is even if $x$ is even, and odd if $x$ is odd. So $x^2 + 2$ does not always give an even number. Here is an example and a non-example: if $x = 6$, then $x^2 + 2 = 38$; if $x = 3$, then $x^2 + 2 = 11$.

In the same way, find and explain which of the other expressions always give even numbers. For each one, write a couple of examples, and non-examples where there are any. Then write a few algebraic expressions of your own that always give an even number.
