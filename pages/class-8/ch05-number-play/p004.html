Take $6p + 4q$. There are two ways to see that it is always even. First, $6p$ and $4q$ are both even for any integers $p$ and $q$, and the sum of two even numbers is even. Second, $6p + 4q = 2(3p + 2q)$, so $2$ is a factor of it. For example, if $p = 5$ and $q = -7$, then $6p + 4q = 30 - 28 = 2$, which is even.

Now take $y^2 + 4$. If $y$ is even then $y^2$ is even, and if $y$ is odd then $y^2$ is odd. So $y^2 + 4$ is even for some values of $y$ and odd for others: $y = 4$ gives $20$, but $y = 3$ gives $13$. One value that breaks a rule is enough to show that the rule is not always true. Such a value is called a counterexample to the rule.

When Two Even Numbers Make a Multiple of 4

Add two even numbers. Sometimes the sum is a multiple of $4$, as in $6 + 10 = 16$, which is $4 \times 4$. Sometimes it is not, as in $8 + 6 = 14$, which is $4 \times 3 + 2$. Can we tell in advance which will happen?
