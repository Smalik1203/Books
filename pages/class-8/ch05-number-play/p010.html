Exercise Set 5.1
  1. The sum of four consecutive numbers is $34$. What are the numbers?
  1. Suppose $p$ is the greatest of five consecutive numbers. Describe the other four numbers in terms of $p$.
  1. For each statement below, decide whether it is always true, sometimes true or never true. Explain your answer, with examples and non-examples as needed, and justify your claim using algebra.
    1. The sum of two even numbers is a multiple of $3$.
    2. If a number is not divisible by $18$, then it is also not divisible by $9$.
    3. If two numbers are not divisible by $6$, then their sum is not divisible by $6$.
    4. The sum of a multiple of $6$ and a multiple of $9$ is a multiple of $3$.
    5. The sum of a multiple of $6$ and a multiple of $3$ is a multiple of $9$.
  1. Find a few numbers that leave a remainder of $2$ when divided by $3$ and a remainder of $2$ when divided by $4$. Write an algebraic expression that describes all such numbers.
  1. A riddle about pebbles. I hold some pebbles, not too many. When I group them in threes, one is left over. When I try to pair them up, one is still left over. When I group them in fives, one is left over again. But when I group them in sevens, none is left over. I hold no more than a hundred. How many pebbles do I hold?
  1. Tathagat has written several numbers that leave a remainder of $2$ when divided by $6$. He claims, “If you add any three such numbers, the sum will always be a multiple of $6$.” Is Tathagat’s claim true?
  1. When divided by $7$, the number $661$ leaves a remainder of $3$, and $4779$ leaves a remainder of $5$. Without calculating, can you say what remainder each of these leaves when divided by $7$? Show the solution both with algebra and with a picture.
    1. $4779 + 661$
    2. $4779 - 661$
  1. Find a number that leaves a remainder of $2$ when divided by $3$, a remainder of $3$ when divided by $4$, and a remainder of $4$ when divided by $5$. What is the smallest such number? Can you give a simple explanation of why it is the smallest?
