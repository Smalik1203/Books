Exercise Set 5.1
  1. The sum of three consecutive numbers is $72$. Find the numbers.
  1. The smallest of four consecutive numbers is $n$. Write the other three in terms of $n$. Show that the sum of all four is always even.
  1. Decide whether each statement is always, sometimes or never true. Give examples or a counterexample, and use algebra where you can.
    1. The sum of two odd numbers is a multiple of $4$.
    2. If a number is not divisible by $12$, then it is not divisible by $4$.
    3. If two numbers are not divisible by $5$, then their sum is not divisible by $5$.
    4. The sum of a multiple of $4$ and a multiple of $10$ is even.
    5. The sum of a multiple of $10$ and a multiple of $5$ is a multiple of $10$.
  1. Find some numbers that leave a remainder of $1$ when divided by $4$ and also when divided by $6$. Write one expression that describes all such numbers.
  1. Kiran has fewer than $50$ laddoos. When she packs them in boxes of $2$, $3$, $4$ or $6$, one laddoo is left over each time. When she packs them in boxes of $5$, none is left over. How many laddoos does she have?
  1. Farid writes numbers that leave a remainder of $3$ when divided by $4$. He says, “If I add any two of them, the sum is a multiple of $4$.” Is he right? What happens when he adds any four of them?
  1. When divided by $9$, the number $853$ leaves a remainder of $7$, and $1241$ leaves a remainder of $8$. Without dividing, find the remainder when each of these is divided by $9$. Show your reasoning with algebra.
    1. $1241 + 853$
    2. $1241 - 853$
  1. Find the smallest number that leaves a remainder of $1$ when divided by $2$, a remainder of $2$ when divided by $3$, and a remainder of $3$ when divided by $4$. Explain why no smaller number works.
