1. The digits of a two-digit number add up to $11$. When the digits are reversed, the new number is $27$ more than the original number. Taking the tens digit as $a$ and the units digit as $b$, form two equations and find the number. Also show that, for any two-digit number, the reversed number minus the original number is $9(b - a)$.
  1. The sum of four consecutive even numbers is $156$. Taking the smallest of them as $2n$, form an equation and find the numbers. Also show that the sum of any four consecutive even numbers is $4$ more than a multiple of $8$, and check that $156$ is such a number.
  1. Vikram has between $300$ and $400$ marbles. When he packs them in bags of $5$, $3$ are left over, and in bags of $7$, $3$ are left over. They can also be packed in bags of $4$ with none left over. Find the number of marbles. Also find how many are left over when he packs them in bags of $9$, and in bags of $11$.
  1. Show that a four-digit number and any number formed by rearranging its digits differ by a multiple of $9$. Hence show that $5283 - 3825$ is a multiple of $9$, and find the quotient when it is divided by $9$.
  1. Show that the product of any two consecutive numbers is even. Hence show that $n^2 + n + 1$ is odd for every whole number $n$, and find the remainder when $n^2 + 5n + 7$ is divided by $2$. Check your answer for $n = 4$.
Assertion and reason

Choose (a) if A and R are both true and R is the correct explanation of A; (b) if A and R are both true but R is not the correct explanation of A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): $7425$ is divisible by $9$.

    Reason (R): The sum of the digits of $7425$ is $18$, a multiple of $9$.

  1. Assertion (A): Every number divisible by both $4$ and $10$ is divisible by $40$.

    Reason (R): A number divisible by $k$ and by $m$ is divisible by the LCM of $k$ and $m$.

  1. Assertion (A): The sum of two odd numbers is even.

    Reason (R): The difference of two odd numbers is even.

  1. Assertion (A): $90816$ is divisible by $3$.

    Reason (R): A number is divisible by $3$ when its units digit is divisible by $3$.

  1. Assertion (A): $6k + 4$ leaves a remainder of $1$ when divided by $3$, for every whole number $k$.

    Reason (R): $6k$ is a multiple of $3$, and $4 = 3 + 1$.
