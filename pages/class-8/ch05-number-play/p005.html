In the first case, $p$ rows of four and $q$ rows of four make $p + q$ rows of four. So the sum is always a multiple of $4$.

In the second case, each number has two dots left over. The two pairs of leftover dots make one more full row, as Fig. 5.5 shows. So the sum is again a multiple of $4$.

In the third case, one number is a multiple of $4$ and the other is not. Write the explanation, give examples and draw the rows yourself. The algebra is

$4p + (4q + 2) = 4(p + q) + 2$

So this sum always has two dots left over, and it is never a multiple of $4$. Algebra and pictures of rows let us settle every case at once, instead of checking a few numbers.
