Pairs to Make Fours

Take a pair of even numbers and add them. Is the sum divisible by $4$? Try this with several pairs of even numbers. When is the sum a multiple of $4$, and when is it not? Is there a rule, or a pattern?

Even numbers are of two kinds, by the remainder they leave when divided by $4$. Set them out in rows of four, as in Fig. 5.4.

When will two even numbers add up to a multiple of $4$? The question is like asking when two numbers add up to an even number. Can you see why? There are three cases to look at. Table 5.5 sets out the first two.

In the first case, $p$ rows of four and $q$ rows of four make $p + q$ rows of four, so the sum is always a multiple of $4$. Some numbers of this kind are $4, 12, 16, 24$ and $36$.

In the second case the sum is always a multiple of $4$ too, because the two remainders of $2$ add up to $4$ and make one more full row. Some numbers of this kind are $2, 6, 10, 18, 22$ and $42$. Fig. 5.6 shows the rows.
