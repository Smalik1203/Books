Last year we met cryptarithms: puzzles in which each letter stands for a digit, each digit is shown by at most one letter, and the first digit of a number is never $0$.

Now let us try some cryptarithms that use multiplication. Guna solved the first one like this.

Example 5

Solve $\text{PQ} \times 8 = \text{RS}$.

Solution.

Step 1 a two-digit number times $8$ gives a two-digit number
Step 2 $10 \times 8 = 80$: the units digits of $10$ and $80$ are the same Q and S must differ
Step 3 $\text{PQ}$ cannot be $11$ P and Q must differ
Step 4 $12 \times 8 = 96$ fits every condition
Step 5 $13 \times 8 = 104$ three digits, as for every larger PQ
Answer $12 \times 8 = 96$: $\text{P} = 1$, $\text{Q} = 2$, $\text{R} = 9$, $\text{S} = 6$.
