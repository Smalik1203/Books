Exercise Set 5.4
  1. If $4k38$ is a multiple of $3$, where $k$ is a digit, find every possible value of $k$.
  1. Aarav says, “Take a number that leaves a remainder of $5$ when divided by $8$, and another that is $3$ less than a multiple of $8$. Their sum is always a multiple of $8$.” Examine his claim and justify your answer.
  1. Ishita says, “If a number is divisible by $6$, then the number made by reversing its digits is also divisible by $6$.” Is she right? Would her claim be true for $9$ in place of $6$? Give reasons.
  1. If $72a4b$ is a multiple of $15$, list all the possible pairs of values of $a$ and $b$.
  1. If $5m4n$ is divisible by $22$, list all the possible pairs of values of $m$ and $n$.
  1. Decide whether each statement is always, sometimes or never true. Explain your reasoning.
    1. The product of a multiple of $4$ and a multiple of $6$ is a multiple of $24$.
    2. The sum of four consecutive numbers is divisible by $4$.
    3. $6(2c + 5) - 4(3c - 1)$ is a multiple of $17$.
    4. If a number is a multiple of $12$, then the number formed by its last two digits is a multiple of $4$.
  1. Show that one of any three consecutive numbers is a multiple of $3$. Hence explain why the product of any three consecutive numbers is a multiple of $6$.
  1. Which of the Venn diagrams in Fig. 5.14 shows how the multiples of $3$, the multiples of $6$ and the multiples of $18$ are related?
