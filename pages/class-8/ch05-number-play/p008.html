Decide for yourself whether the next two statements are always, sometimes or never true.

Statement 6. If a number is divisible by both $5$ and $6$, then it is divisible by $30$.

Statement 7. If a number is divisible by both $10$ and $4$, then it is divisible by $40$.

Divisible by two numbers

If $A$ is divisible by $k$ and by $m$, then $A$ is divisible by the LCM of $k$ and $m$. This is because $A$ is a common multiple of $k$ and $m$, so its prime factorisation contains the prime factorisation of their LCM.

Statement 8. The sum of an odd number and an even number is a multiple of $4$.

Every multiple of $4$ is even. But an odd number plus an even number is odd. So Statement 8 is never true. Algebra says the same. Write the even number as $2n$ and the odd number as $2m + 1$, and suppose their sum is a multiple of $4$, say $4j$:

$2n + (2m + 1) = 4j$
then $2n + 2m = 4j - 1$
so $2(n + m) = 4j - 1$

The left side is even and the right side is odd. An even number can never equal an odd number, so no such sum exists.
