Example 14 · Matching

Match each number in List I with the remainder it leaves when divided by 9 in List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) 4512(1) 5
(Q) 8807(2) 0
(R) 7777(3) 3
(S) 36036(4) 1

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
  2. P–1, Q–3, R–4, S–2
  3. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  4. P–2, Q–1, R–4, S–3

Solution.

Step 1The digit sums are 12, 23, 28 and 18, with digital roots 3, 5, 1 and 9.
Step 2A digital root of 9 means a remainder of 0; the others are the remainders.
Answer(c) P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
Example 15 · Matching

Match each test in List I with the number whose divisibility it checks in List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) The number formed by the last three digits is a multiple of 8(1) 4
(Q) The digit sum is a multiple of 3 and the units digit is even(2) 11
(R) Adding and subtracting the digits in turn gives a multiple of 11(3) 6
(S) The number formed by the last two digits is a multiple of 4(4) 8

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–2, S–1
  3. P–4, Q–2, R–3, S–1
  4. P–1, Q–3, R–2, S–4

Solution.

Step 1The last three digits test 8 and the last two digits test 4, as in the shortcuts you learnt earlier.
Step 2Divisible by 2 and by 3 is divisible by 6, and the alternating sum tests 11.
Answer(b) P–4, Q–3, R–2, S–1
