Think of a number. Double it, add ten, halve the result, then take away the number you first thought of. Try it with $7$ and with $100$. Then show that the answer is always the same, whatever you started with.

The sixth question is the one worth slowing down for. Ravi has done nothing that looks illegal — dividing both sides by the same thing is one of the four operations — and yet his conclusion is wrong. The rule he broke has a condition attached to it, and it is the only one of the four that has.

For the questions that arrive as sentences, write down what your letter stands for before anything else, and give it units. In the purse question there are two kinds of coin and only one letter is needed: if $x$ of them are five-rupee coins then the rest are not some second letter, they are $30 - x$.

The last question is not arithmetic and cannot be settled by trying numbers, however many you try. What settles it is doing the whole thing to a letter instead of to a number, and then looking at what is left.
