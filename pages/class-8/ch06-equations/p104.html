The fraction is $\frac{3}{7}$, and adding one to each part gives $\frac48$, which is $\frac12$. The brackets in the third line are the fraction bars doing their work; without them the line reads $2d - 3 = d + 1$ and the answer comes out as $4$.

Where Ravi's division went wrong

Try $x = 0$ in $7x = 4x$: the left side is $0$ and so is the right. It is a solution, so the equation certainly has one, and Ravi's conclusion is false.

The step that failed is the division. Both sides may be divided by the same number provided it is not zero — and dividing by $x$ is dividing by a number nobody has checked. If $x$ happens to be zero, the step is not allowed; and it is precisely that value which then goes missing from the answer.

instead $7x - 4x = 0$
so $3x = 0$,   $x = 0$

Subtracting $4x$ carries no condition and keeps the solution. This is the one place where the small print on a rule decides the answer: never divide by something that contains the unknown.
