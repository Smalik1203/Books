Example 10

A father is four times as old as his son. In six years he will be three times as old. How old are they now?

Solution.

Step 1 $x$ = the son's age now, in years name the unknown
Step 2 the father is $4x$
Step 3 son $x + 6$,   father $4x + 6$ in six years
Step 4 $4x + 6 = 3(x + 6)$ the sentence
Step 5 $4x + 6 = 3x + 18$ expand
Step 6 $x = 12$ gather and solve
Answer The son is $12$ and the father is $48$

Read it back against the words: in six years they will be $18$ and $54$, and $54$ is three times $18$. Notice that both ages had six added — a father ages at the same rate as his son, and an equation that forgets this is the usual wrong answer.

Where several quantities are described in terms of one another, name the smallest or simplest of them and write the rest in terms of it. Which one you choose only changes how much arithmetic there is at the end.

Example 11

Three numbers are consecutive, and they add to $87$. Find them.

Consecutive means each is one more than the last, so one letter describes all three.

Solution.

Step 1 $n$ = the smallest of the three name the unknown
Step 2 $n$,   $n+1$,   $n+2$ the three
Step 3 $n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 87$ the sentence
Step 4 $3n + 3 = 87$ gather
Step 5 $n = 28$ solve
Answer The numbers are $28$, $29$ and $30$

They do add to $87$. Naming the middle one instead gives $3n = 87$ straight away, which is quicker — worth knowing, and worth noticing that both routes end in the same place.

Geometry and money questions are the same job with a different sentence. What changes is only where the equality comes from: a perimeter, a total cost, a stated comparison.
