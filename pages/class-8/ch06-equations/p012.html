6.6From Words to an Equation

Everything so far started with an equation already written. Most real questions do not: they arrive as a sentence, and the work is in getting from the sentence to the equation. Once it is written, the last three sections solve it.

Four steps, and the first is the one people skip.

Turning a sentence into an equation

Say in writing what your letter stands for, including its units. Write down the one sentence that says two things are equal. Solve it. Then read the answer back against the words, not against your working.

Naming the unknown properly is not a formality. Let $x$ be the son's age in years is a promise you can be held to; let $x$ be the age is not, and by the third line you will not remember whose age it was.

Example 10

A father is four times as old as his son. In six years he will be three times as old. How old are they now?

let $x$ = the son's age now, in years
then the father is $4x$
in six years son $x + 6$,   father $4x + 6$
the sentence $4x + 6 = 3(x + 6)$
expand $4x + 6 = 3x + 18$
solve $x = 12$

So the son is $12$ and the father is $48$. Read it back against the words: in six years they will be $18$ and $54$, and $54$ is three times $18$. Notice that both ages had six added — a father ages at the same rate as his son, and an equation that forgets this is the usual wrong answer.
