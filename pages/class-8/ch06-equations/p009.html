The check here is worth reading twice. It went back to the bracketed form, which is the form that was given, and so it tested the expansion as well as the answer.

Example 7

Solve $5(2x - 1) - 3(x - 4) = 24$.

The second bracket has a minus in front of it, so all of it changes sign.

Solution.

Step 1 $5(2x-1) = 10x - 5$ first bracket
Step 2 $-3(x-4) = -3x + 12$ second bracket
Step 3 $7x + 7 = 24$ gather
Step 4 $7x = 17$ take 7 from both
Answer $x = \dfrac{17}{7}$

The $-4$ inside became $+12$ and not $-12$. Had it gone the other way the answer would have come out as $\tfrac{41}{7}$, and the check against the original would have caught it.

6.5.2 Fractions

Fractions in an equation are cleared in one stroke: multiply every term on both sides by a number that all the denominators divide into — their lowest common multiple is the tidiest choice. Every fraction then cancels and the equation has whole numbers in it.

The words every term are the ones that get skipped. Multiplying both sides means multiplying all of both sides, including any term that had no fraction in it to begin with.

Example 8

Solve $\dfrac{x}{2} + \dfrac{x}{3} = 10$.

The denominators are $2$ and $3$, so multiply everything by $6$.

Solution.

Step 1 $3x + 2x = 60$ $\times 6$ every term
Step 2 $5x = 60$ gather
Step 3 $x = 12$ $\div 5$ both sides
Check $6 + 4 = 10$ true
Answer $x = 12$

The $10$ became $60$. A reader who multiplies only the two fractions gets $5x = 10$ and an answer of $2$, which fails the check at once.
