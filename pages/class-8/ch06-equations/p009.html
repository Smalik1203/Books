6.5Brackets and Fractions

Two things can stand between an equation and the methods of the last two sections, and both are cleared before solving rather than during it. Brackets are removed first; fractions are removed first. After that the equation is an ordinary one and nothing new is needed.

6.5.1 Brackets

Expand them, using the distributive law of Chapter 4 — and remembering that a minus in front of a bracket reverses the sign of everything inside, which is where the marks are lost.

Example 6

Solve $3(x + 4) = 2(x + 9)$.

expand both $3x + 12 = 2x + 18$
$-2x$ $x + 12 = 18$
$-12$ $x = 6$
check $3(10) = 2(15) = 30$  true

The check here is worth reading twice. It went back to the bracketed form, which is the form that was given, and so it tested the expansion as well as the answer.

Example 7

Solve $5(2x - 1) - 3(x - 4) = 24$.

The second bracket has a minus in front of it, so all of it changes sign.

first bracket $5(2x-1) = 10x - 5$
second $-3(x-4) = -3x + 12$
gather $7x + 7 = 24$
solve $7x = 17$,   $x = \dfrac{17}{7}$

The $-4$ inside became $+12$ and not $-12$. Had it gone the other way the answer would have come out as $\tfrac{41}{7}$, and the check against the original would have caught it.
