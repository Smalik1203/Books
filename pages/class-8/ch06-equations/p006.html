6.4Unknowns on Both Pans

So far the letter has sat on one side and the numbers on the other, and every step was aimed at getting it alone. Most equations do not arrive so conveniently arranged. In $3x + 2 = x + 8$ there are bags on both pans, and until they are gathered onto one there is nothing to get alone: subtracting the $2$ leaves $3x$ facing $x + 6$, which is no nearer an answer than what you began with.

The balance says how. Taking one bag off the left pan tips the beam, unless a bag comes off the right pan at the same moment — and the two bags weigh the same, whatever that is, because both are bags of $x$.

In symbols that is subtracting $x$ from both sides, and it is allowed for exactly the reason the four operations were: what is taken from one side is taken from the other. From there the equation is the kind already solved.
