C2 Taking $5x$ from both sides leaves $-2 = 6$, which is false whatever $x$ was, so nothing solves it. Option (d) comes from treating the false statement as though it were an equation still to be solved.
C4 All three can occur: $x = 0$ solves $x = 0$, $-7$ solves $x + 7 = 0$, and $\frac52$ solves $2x = 5$. Whole-number coefficients do not force a whole-number answer, which is what the seventh question of stage 1 showed.
C7 Subtracting $3$ from both sides leaves $kx = 7x$, which holds for every $x$ only when $k = 7$. Option (d) forgets that a single wrong value of $k$ is enough to break every.
C8 Multiplying by $6$ gives $3x = 2x$, so $x = 0$. Option (a) is Ravi's error from stage 1 — cancelling the $x$ and declaring the survivors unequal — and it loses the one solution there is.
C11 The equation $2x + 600 = 500$ has the perfectly good solution $x = -50$. What has no solution is the sharing: a share of $-50$ rupees is not a share. Option (d) confuses an equation without an answer with an answer that cannot be used.

What to carry forward

One rule solved every equation in this chapter: do the same to both sides. Every step in every worked answer was an instance of it, and the only small print is that the thing you divide by must not be zero — which is the whole of what went wrong for Ravi, and worth more attention than its one line suggests.

The harder half was never the solving. It was reading a sentence closely enough to write the equation: naming what the letter stands for and in what units, noticing that thirty coins means the second kind is $30 - x$ and not a second letter, and remembering that everybody in a question about ages gets older at the same rate. An equation solved perfectly from a sentence read carelessly gives a wrong answer with no working to blame.

And keep the two odd cases. When the letter cancels, nothing has failed: you have found either that the two sides can never agree, or that they always did. Both are answers, and outside a textbook they are often the answer somebody actually wanted.
