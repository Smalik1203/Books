Where several quantities are described in terms of one another, name the smallest or simplest of them and write the rest in terms of it. Which one you choose only changes how much arithmetic there is at the end.

Example 11

Three numbers are consecutive, and they add to $87$. Find them.

Consecutive means each is one more than the last, so one letter describes all three.

let $n$ = the smallest of the three
the three $n$,   $n+1$,   $n+2$
the sentence $n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 87$
gather $3n + 3 = 87$
solve $n = 28$

The numbers are $28$, $29$ and $30$, and they do add to $87$. Naming the middle one instead gives $3n = 87$ straight away, which is quicker — worth knowing, and worth noticing that both routes end in the same place.

Geometry and money questions are the same job with a different sentence. What changes is only where the equality comes from: a perimeter, a total cost, a stated comparison.

Example 12

A rectangle is $5$ cm longer than it is wide, and its perimeter is $46$ cm. Find its sides.

let $w$ = the width in centimetres
length $w + 5$
perimeter $2\big(w + (w+5)\big) = 46$
simplify $2(2w + 5) = 46$,   $4w + 10 = 46$
solve $w = 9$

The rectangle is $9$ cm by $14$ cm, and its perimeter is $2(9+14) = 46$. Answering with $w = 9$ alone would not have answered the question, which asked for both sides.
