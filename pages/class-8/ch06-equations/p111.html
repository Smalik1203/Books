Why the other options are wrong

A4 (a) has $x$ squared, (b) has two letters, and (c) has the letter in a denominator. Each breaks one of the three conditions, and each behaves quite differently from a linear equation.
A5 Adding $4$ to both sides of $x - 4 = -9$ gives $x = -5$. Option (b) adds the two numbers instead of undoing the subtraction; (a) and (d) drop the sign.
A8 The $-2$ multiplies both terms, and $-2 \times -3$ is $+6$. Option (a) is the standing error of the section: only the first sign changed.
A9 $12$ is the lowest number both $3$ and $4$ divide into. Option (a) adds the denominators, which is not a thing that helps anywhere; (b) and (c) clear one fraction and leave the other.
A12 Division by zero is barred everywhere, and here it would turn the equation into nonsense. Subtracting $x$ in (d) is allowed and is the whole of Section 6.4.
B2 Expanding gives $3x - 3 = 2x + 8$, so $x = 11$. Option (d) comes from expanding the left as $3x - 1$, which multiplies the bracket's first term only.
B3 Multiplying through by $6$ gives $3x + 2x = 30$, so $x = 6$. Option (d) multiplies only the fractions and leaves the $5$ alone; (c) adds the denominators.
B6 $5 - 2x = 3x - 10$ gives $15 = 5x$ and $x = 3$. Option (d) is what comes out if the letters are gathered on the left and the sign of the coefficient is then dropped.
B7 The perimeter is $2(w + w + 4) = 4w + 8 = 36$, so $w = 7$. Option (b) is the length rather than the width, and answers a question that was not asked.
B10 $4x + 5 = 3(x + 5)$ gives $x = 10$. Option (a) comes from adding five only to the mother's age — but a son ages at the same rate as his mother, and both sides of the sentence move together.
