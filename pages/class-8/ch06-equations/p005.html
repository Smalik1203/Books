Negative numbers and fractions come out of the same steps without any special handling. There is no rule saying an answer has to be a whole number, and an answer that is not one is not a sign that something has gone wrong.

Example 3

Solve $5 - 2x = 13$.

given $5 - 2x = 13$
$-5$ $-2x = 8$
$\div (-2)$ $x = -4$
check $5 - 2(-4) = 5 + 8 = 13$  true

The term is $-2x$, so what divides out is $-2$ and not $2$. The check is worth doing here more than anywhere: a sign lost in the last step is the commonest error in the whole chapter, and it is caught in one line.

Think and Reflect

You will often see the step from $x + 7 = 12$ to $x = 12 - 7$ described as moving the $7$ across and changing its sign.

Nothing moves anywhere. Say what is really being done to both sides, and then say why the rule about the sign changing comes out true anyway.
