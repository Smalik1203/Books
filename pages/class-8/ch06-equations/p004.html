add the same number to both sides
subtract the same number from both sides
multiply both sides by the same number
divide both sides by the same number, not zero

Dividing by zero is barred because it is barred everywhere, and the reason bites here in particular: multiplying both sides by zero turns every equation into $0 = 0$, which is true and tells you nothing at all. An operation that throws the question away is not a step towards its answer.

The aim of every step is the same — get the letter by itself on one side — and the way to choose a step is to look at what is standing in the way and undo it.

Example 1

Solve $x + 7 = 12$.

What stands in the way is the $+7$. Undo it by subtracting $7$ — from both sides.

given $x + 7 = 12$
$-7$ both sides $x + 7 - 7 = 12 - 7$
so $x = 5$
check $5 + 7 = 12$  true

The middle line is written out here and will not be again. Once the habit is formed, it is enough to write what you did in the margin and give the result.

Example 2

Solve $7x + 4 = 25$.

given $7x + 4 = 25$
$-4$ $7x = 21$
$\div 7$ $x = 3$
check $7(3) + 4 = 25$  true

Two things stood in the way and came off in reverse order — the $+4$ first, then the $\times 7$. To dress, socks then shoes; to undress, shoes then socks.
