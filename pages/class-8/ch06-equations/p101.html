Using What You Know

The chapter leaves you with three things and nothing below needs a fourth. Anything may be done to an equation provided it is done to both sides, which is how every one of them is solved. A sentence becomes an equation by naming the unknown and then writing the one thing that is said to be equal to another. And if the letter cancels, the equation is not broken — it is one of the two unusual answers, no solution or every number.

Several of the questions below arrive as sentences rather than equations, and in those the work is nearly all done before any solving starts. Try each one before reading what follows it.

For what value of $k$ does $4x + k = 7x - 9$ have the solution $x = 5$?

Here $x$ is known and $k$ is not, which is the reverse of every question so far. Put $x = 5$ in and the equation becomes one in $k$ alone.

substitute $20 + k = 35 - 9$
so $k = 6$

Check it: $4x + 6 = 7x - 9$ gives $15 = 3x$ and $x = 5$. Nothing new was needed, only the willingness to treat whichever letter is unknown as the unknown.

The average of five consecutive whole numbers is $23$. Find the five, without adding anything up.

Name the middle number rather than the smallest, and the five become $n-2$, $n-1$, $n$, $n+1$, $n+2$. Their total is $5n$, because the twos and the ones cancel in pairs.

average $\dfrac{5n}{5} = n = 23$

So the numbers are $21, 22, 23, 24, 25$. The average of a run of consecutive numbers is just its middle number, because everything on one side of the middle is cancelled by something the same distance on the other.
