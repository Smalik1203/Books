Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter leaves you with three things and nothing below needs a fourth. Anything may be done to an equation provided it is done to both sides, which is how every one of them is solved. A sentence becomes an equation by naming the unknown and then writing the one thing that is said to be equal to another. And if the letter cancels, the equation is not broken — it is one of the two unusual answers, no solution or every number.

Several of the questions below arrive as sentences rather than equations, and in those the work is nearly all done before any solving starts. Try each one before turning the page.

For what value of $k$ does $4x + k = 7x - 9$ have the solution $x = 5$?

The average of five consecutive whole numbers is $23$. Find the five, without adding anything up.

A purse holds thirty coins, some of five rupees and the rest of two, and they come to ₹$99$ altogether. How many of each are there?

The digits of a two-digit number add to nine. Reversing them makes the number larger by $27$. What was it?

The numerator of a fraction is four less than its denominator. Adding one to both makes the fraction $\frac12$. Find the fraction.
