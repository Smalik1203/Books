Exercise Set 6.5
  1. Write an equation and solve it. In each case say first what your letter stands for.
    1. Seven more than twice a number is $31$.
    2. A number less than $40$ by $13$.
    3. A third of a number, increased by $4$, is $10$.
  1. The sum of two consecutive numbers is $75$. Find them.
  1. The sum of three consecutive even numbers is $84$. Find them.
  1. A mother is three times as old as her daughter. In eight years she will be twice as old. Find both ages now.
  1. The length of a rectangle is twice its width, and its perimeter is $54$ cm. Find its area.
  1. Two angles of a triangle are equal and the third is $30^\circ$ more than each of them. Find all three.
  1. Pens cost ₹$8$ and pencils ₹$3$. Aarav buys twice as many pencils as pens and spends ₹$98$. How many of each did he buy?
  1. The angles of a quadrilateral are $x$, $x + 10$, $x + 20$ and $x + 30$ degrees. Find them, using a result from Chapter 5.
  1. A number is as much above $30$ as it is below $58$. Find it two ways — with an equation, and without one.
  1. A sum of ₹$720$ is shared between two people so that one gets ₹$60$ more than the other. How much does each get? Then say what happens to the answer if the difference is ₹$800$ instead, and why.

6.7No Answer, and Every Answer

Section 6.2 promised that a linear equation in one variable has exactly one solution, and every equation in this chapter's exercises has kept that promise. The promise can be broken, though, and it can be broken in two ways. Neither announces itself: both look like perfectly ordinary questions until the last line of working, at which point the letter vanishes from the page and there is apparently nothing left to solve for.
