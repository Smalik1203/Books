One equation is enough to sink it

A claim about every equation is disproved by one that fails, and the smallest one will do.

take $2x = 5$
solution $x = \dfrac{5}{2}$, which is not a whole number

The coefficients are whole and the solution is not, so the claim is false. That is the whole answer: disproving a general statement takes one case, while proving one takes an argument covering all of them.

Something weaker is true. If $ax = b$ with $a$ and $b$ whole and $a$ not zero, the solution is $\frac{b}{a}$ — always a rational number, however awkward the coefficients.

The trick that is not a trick

With $7$: double to $14$, add ten to $24$, halve to $12$, take away $7$, and the answer is $5$. With $100$: $200$, $210$, $105$, take away $100$, and the answer is $5$ again. Two cases are a suspicion, not a reason.

Do the whole thing to a letter and the reason appears.

start $n$
double $2n$
add ten $2n + 10$
halve $n + 5$
subtract $n$ $5$

The $n$ cancels at the last step, and once it has gone there is nothing left for the starting number to affect. This is Section 6.7 in a party trick: an identity, not an equation with a solution. The halving is what makes it work, turning $2n$ back into one $n$ ready to be taken away.
