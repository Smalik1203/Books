What this chapter established
  1. An equation is two expressions with an equals sign between them, claiming that the left side and the right side come to the same number. A value that makes them agree is a solution.
  2. An equation is linear in one variable when it has one letter, to the first power, and nowhere in a denominator. Ordinarily such an equation has exactly one solution.
  3. Checking costs one line: put the answer back into the equation you were given, not into a line of your own working, which may already carry the mistake.
  4. An equation may be added to, subtracted from, multiplied or divided — provided the same is done to both sides. Division by zero is barred, and multiplying by zero turns any equation into $0 = 0$.
  5. Each step undoes what stands between the letter and being alone, and the undoing runs in reverse order: socks then shoes to dress, shoes then socks to undress.
  6. Moving a term across and changing its sign is not a separate rule. It is what subtracting that term from both sides looks like when the middle line is not written down.
  7. When letters stand on both sides, gather them onto one — preferably the side that already has more, so the coefficient stays positive. Two identical unknowns come off the two pans together without anyone knowing what they weigh.
  8. Brackets are expanded before solving, and a minus in front of a bracket reverses the sign of everything inside it.
  9. Fractions are cleared by multiplying every term on both sides by a common multiple of the denominators. A fraction bar acts as a bracket even when none is written.
  10. To turn a sentence into an equation: say what your letter stands for and in what units, write the one sentence that says two things are equal, solve it, then read the answer back against the words. Where several quantities are described in terms of one another, name one and write the rest in terms of it.
  11. If the letter cancels and what is left is false, the equation has no solution. If the letter cancels and what is left is true, every number is a solution — the two sides were one expression written twice, which is Chapter 4's identity wearing an equals sign.
