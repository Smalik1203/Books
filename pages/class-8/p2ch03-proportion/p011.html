Example 9

Two pumps fill a tank in $18$ hours. How long with four pumps of the same kind?

Solution.

Step 1 $2 \times 18 = 4 \times x$ inverse: same product
Step 2 $x = 9$ divide by four
Answer $9$ hours
Example 10

A school has food for $80$ students for $15$ days. Twenty more students arrive. How long will the food last now?

Solution.

Step 1 there are now 100 students twenty more than 80
Step 2 $80 \times 15 = 100 \times x$ inverse: same product
Step 3 $x = 12$ divide by 100
Answer $12$ days

A quarter more mouths, and the food lasts a fifth less time. Those two fractions are different and both are right — the first is measured against $80$ and the second against $15$, which is Chapter 1's warning about bases turning up in new clothes.

One kind of question needs both ideas at once, and it is the one people find hardest.

3.7.1 Working together

Ram takes an hour to cut a basket of vegetables and Shyam takes an hour and a half. Working side by side, how long do the two of them take?

The tempting answer is the average, an hour and a quarter, and it cannot be right: two people must finish sooner than either alone. Ask instead how much each does in an hour, taking the whole job as one unit of work.

Ram, in an hour $1$ unit
Shyam, in an hour $\dfrac{1}{1.5} = \dfrac23$ of a unit
together, in an hour $1 + \dfrac23 = \dfrac53$ units
