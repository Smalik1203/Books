Work done and time taken are in direct proportion: twice the time, twice the work. So the last step is the rule of three.

as a proportion $\dfrac56 : 1 \ :: \ 1 : x$
so $x = \dfrac65$ of an hour, that is $72$ minutes

Rates add; times do not. That is why the average was wrong. Turn each time into the work done in one hour, add these, and turn the total back into a time.

A drain works against a tap, so its work is taken away. Suppose a tap fills a tank in $3$ hours, and a drain empties the full tank in $6$ hours. Both are open, and the tank starts empty.

tap, in an hour $\dfrac13$ of the tank filled
drain, in an hour $\dfrac16$ of the tank emptied
both, in an hour $\dfrac13 - \dfrac16 = \dfrac16$ of the tank, so $6$ hours to fill

The tank still fills, but it takes twice as long as with the tap alone.

Example 11

Irfan and Deepa together paint a wall in $4$ hours. Irfan alone takes $6$ hours. How long would Deepa take alone?

Solution.

Step 1 together they paint $\dfrac14$ of the wall in an hour the joint rate
Step 2 Deepa paints $\dfrac14 - \dfrac16 = \dfrac{1}{12}$ of it in an hour take away Irfan's rate
Check $\dfrac16 + \dfrac{1}{12} = \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac14$
Answer $12$ hours
