3.1Introduction

Rose sherbet is made from rose syrup and cold water. Kavitha mixes $2$ cups of syrup with $8$ cups of water. Anwar mixes $3$ cups of syrup with $12$ cups of water. Anwar has made more sherbet. Do the two jugs taste the same?

They do. The amounts are different, but in both jugs there is $4$ times as much water as syrup. That relation is the same in both jugs, and it is what decides the taste. A ratio records this relation and nothing else. So both jugs are written $1 : 4$, syrup to water, and the ratio carries no units.

This chapter is about quantities that change together. In each case, something stays the same while they change. In a recipe, a map or a mix of colours, the ratio stays the same. In the last section, something else stays the same. Finding what stays the same tells you what kind of relation you are looking at.

What a ratio records

A ratio compares two quantities: how many times one is of the other. It does not say how much there is of each.
