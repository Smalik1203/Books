Example 9

Three pumps of the same kind empty a pond in $16$ hours. How long would four such pumps take?

Solution.

Step 1 $3 \times 16 = 4 \times x$ inverse: same product
Step 2 $x = 48 \div 4 = 12$ divide by four
Answer $12$ hours
Example 10

A hostel has food for $120$ students for $20$ days. Forty more students arrive. How long will the food last now?

Solution.

Step 1 there are now $160$ students forty more than 120
Step 2 $120 \times 20 = 160 \times x$ inverse: same product
Step 3 $x = 2400 \div 160 = 15$ divide by 160
Answer $15$ days

A third more students, and the food lasts a quarter less time. The two fractions are different, and both are right. The first is measured against $120$ students and the second against $20$ days.

3.7.1 Working together

This kind of question needs both ideas at once. Lalita takes $2$ hours to paint a fence on her own. Nandini takes $3$ hours to paint the same fence. Working side by side, how long do the two of them take?

The tempting answer is the average, two and a half hours, and it cannot be right. Two people must finish sooner than either of them alone. Instead, ask how much of the fence each paints in one hour, taking the whole fence as one job.

Lalita, in an hour $\dfrac12$ of the job
Nandini, in an hour $\dfrac13$ of the job
together, in an hour $\dfrac12 + \dfrac13 = \dfrac56$ of the job
