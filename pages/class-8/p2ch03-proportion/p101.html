Using What You Know

Two things came out of this chapter and everything below is one of them. In a direct proportion the quotient holds still, so both quantities grow together. In an inverse proportion the product holds still, so one grows as the other shrinks. The whole difficulty is deciding which you are looking at, and no formula will decide it for you.

So none of the questions below says which kind it is, and two of them are not proportional at all until you have made them so. Try each before reading what follows it.

Each explanation begins the same way: decide, in a sentence, whether the two quantities grow together or pull apart. Everything after that is a single multiplication or a single division, and getting the first sentence wrong is the only way to get the answer wrong.

A recipe for six people uses $750$ g of rice. How much rice for eight?

Twice the eaters, twice the rice: direct. The quotient — grams of rice per person — is what stays the same.

per person $750 \div 6 = 125$ g
for eight $125 \times 8 = 1000$ g, that is $1$ kg

Or in one step: $750 \times \tfrac86 = 1000$. Going by way of one person is slower and safer, and it is the same move as finding the value of one part.

Twelve workers build a wall in ten days. How long would fifteen workers take?

The wall does not get bigger when more people build it, so the two quantities pull apart: inverse. The product is the size of the job.

the job $12 \times 10 = 120$ worker-days
so $15 \times y = 120$, giving $y = 8$ days

A quarter more workers, and a fifth off the time. Those two fractions differ, and both are right, for the reason Chapter 1 gave: they are measured against different starting numbers.
