3.4Dividing a Whole

Another kind of question gives the total and asks how it is shared. Share $20$ pencils between two children in the ratio $3 : 2$. There are $3 + 2 = 5$ parts in all, so one part is $20 \div 5 = 4$ pencils. The two shares are $3 \times 4 = 12$ and $2 \times 4 = 8$ pencils. Adding the terms first is the whole method, and it works for any number of terms.

Dividing a whole in a ratio
  • Add the terms. The whole divided by this total is one part.
  • Each share is its term times one part; the shares add back to the whole.
Example 5

A floor needs $75$ bags of the dry mix of Example 4, cement : sand : stone chips $= 1 : 1.5 : 2.5$. How many bags of each go into it?

Solution.

Step 1 $1 + 1.5 + 2.5 = 5$ total parts
Step 2 $75 \div 5 = 15$ bags one part
Step 3 $15$ cement, $22.5$ sand, $37.5$ stone chips 1, 1.5 and 2.5 parts
Step 4 $15 + 22.5 + 37.5 = 75$ the check
Answer $15$ bags of cement, $22.5$ of sand and $37.5$ of stone chips
Example 6

The three angles of a triangle are in the ratio $2 : 3 : 7$. Find them.

Here the whole comes from geometry: the angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.

Solution.

Step 1 $2 + 3 + 7 = 12$ total parts
Step 2 one part is $180^\circ \div 12 = 15^\circ$ one part
Step 3 $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $105^\circ$ 2, 3 and 7 parts
Answer The angles are $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $105^\circ$

Any triangle with its angles in this ratio has these angles, whatever its size. A ratio of sides, such as $5 : 6 : 7$, fixes the shape but not the lengths.
