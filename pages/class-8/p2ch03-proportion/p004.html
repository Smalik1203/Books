With more than two terms there is no single multiplication across. So this is the test: work out the quotients and see whether they agree. If even one of them is different, the two mixes are not the same mix.

A ratio can also be reduced, just as a fraction can, by dividing every term by a number that divides them all. $6 : 3 : 2 : 1$ has no such number, so it is already in its simplest form. $12 : 9 : 6 : 3$ can be divided by $3$, and it becomes $4 : 3 : 2 : 1$. Reducing does not change the relation, and it makes the arithmetic easier. This is why Section 3.6 reduces a ratio before it uses it.

Example 3

A rangoli colour is mixed yellow : red : white in the ratio $3 : 2 : 4$. Meher has $600$ g of white powder and wants to use all of it. How much yellow and red does she need, and how much colour will she have?

Solution.

Step 1 white is $4$ parts, and those four parts are $600$ g the amount given
Step 2 one part is $600 \div 4 = 150$ g divide by four
Step 3 yellow: $3$ parts $= 450$ g; red: $2$ parts $= 300$ g
Step 4 $450 + 300 + 600 = 1350$ g add all three
Answer $450$ g of yellow and $300$ g of red, making $1350$ g of colour

Finding the value of one part is the step that answers nearly every question of this kind. It turns a ratio into a list of amounts, and it needs only one amount to be known.

Example 4

A dry mix for a floor is cement : sand : stone chips in the ratio $1 : 1.5 : 2.5$. A builder has $4$ bags of cement. How many bags of dry mix can he make?

Solution.

Step 1 cement is $1$ part, so one part is $4$ bags the amount given
Step 2 $4 : 6 : 10$ bags each term times four
Step 3 $4 + 6 + 10 = 20$ bags add the three
Answer $20$ bags of dry mix

The terms of a ratio need not be whole numbers. $1 : 1.5 : 2.5$ is the same relation as $2 : 3 : 5$, and a builder who likes whole numbers would use the second.
