3.5A Ratio on a Map

Look in the corner of a map of a state, and you will often find a ratio with $1$ on the left and a very large number on the right, such as $1 : 40{,}00{,}000$. It is called the representative fraction, or the scale of the map. It says that one unit of length on the paper stands for forty lakh of the same unit on the ground.

So one centimetre on that map is $40{,}00{,}000$ cm on the ground. Divide by $100$ to get metres, and by another $1{,}000$ to get kilometres. One centimetre on the map stands for $40$ km.

Example 7

On a map with a scale of $1 : 40{,}00{,}000$, two cities are $6.5$ cm apart. How far apart are they on the ground? On a second map, the same two cities are $13$ cm apart. What is the scale of the second map?

Solution.

Step 1 $6.5 \times 40 = 260$ km 1 cm is 40 km
Step 2 $13$ cm is $260$ km, so $1$ cm is $20$ km the same two cities
Step 3 $1 : 20{,}00{,}000$ 20 km in centimetres
Answer $260$ km apart; the second map is drawn at $1 : 20{,}00{,}000$

On the second map every length is twice as long, and the number in its scale is half as large. A smaller number in the scale means a larger drawing. This is the first sign of what Section 3.7 is about.

A map's scale has no units, and that is on purpose. It holds whether the paper is measured in centimetres or in inches, since both sides of the ratio are then in the same unit. A scale also measures distance in a straight line, not along the road. A road that bends through hills can be much longer than the straight line between its ends.

The scale of a map
  • A scale of $1 : n$ means $1$ unit on the map stands for $n$ of the same unit on the ground.
  • Ground distance $=$ map distance $\times\, n$; map distance $=$ ground distance $\div\, n$.
