3.6A Slice of the Pie

Forty students sat an assessment and were given grades A to E. The counts were $12$, $10$, $8$, $6$ and $4$. A table says that plainly enough, but a picture says at a glance which grade was commonest and roughly what share of the class it took.

A pie chart is a circle cut into slices, one for each part, with each slice's angle proportional to what it stands for. So the question is how to divide $360^\circ$ in the ratio $12 : 10 : 8 : 6 : 4$ — which is the previous section's arithmetic with a whole that happens to be an angle.

Reducing the ratio first makes the numbers kinder. All five terms share a factor of $2$, so the ratio is $6 : 5 : 4 : 3 : 2$, and those add to $20$. One part is therefore $360 \div 20 = 18^\circ$.

A · 12 students $6 \times 18 = 108^\circ$
B · 10 students $5 \times 18 = 90^\circ$
C · 8 students $4 \times 18 = 72^\circ$
D · 6 students $3 \times 18 = 54^\circ$
E · 4 students $2 \times 18 = 36^\circ$

The five angles come to $360^\circ$, and if they had not, something would be wrong — that is the check, and it is worth doing before picking up a protractor.
