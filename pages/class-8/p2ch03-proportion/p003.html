3.3More Than Two Terms

Harpreet makes a snack mix for a school trip. She uses $6$ cups of roasted peanuts, $3$ cups of roasted chana, $2$ cups of raisins and $1$ cup of cashews. As a ratio, that is

$$\text{peanuts} : \text{chana} : \text{raisins} : \text{cashews} \ = \ 6 : 3 : 2 : 1.$$

A ratio may have as many terms as there are ingredients. It still records a relation and not amounts. It is proportional to another ratio when every term has been multiplied by the same number.

Tenzin wants the same mix, but he has only $1.5$ cups of chana. That is half of Harpreet's $3$ cups, so every other amount must be halved too: $3$ cups of peanuts, $1$ cup of raisins and half a cup of cashews. His mix is $3 : 1.5 : 1 : 0.5$. The two mixes are proportional, and we write $6 : 3 : 2 : 1 :: 3 : 1.5 : 1 : 0.5$ for this.

In general, if $a : b : c : d$ and $p : q : r : s$ are proportional, then every matching pair gives the same quotient:

$$\frac{a}{p} = \frac{b}{q} = \frac{c}{r} = \frac{d}{s}.$$
