Set C, 5. Options (a), (b) and (d) are the chapter's three standard inverse pairs. A square's side and its area grow together, so they are not inverse — though they are not directly proportional either, since doubling the side quadruples the area. Not every pair of quantities is one of the two kinds.

Set C, 12. Option (a) reads the ratio as though a bigger number meant a bigger map. It is the other way about: $1 : 2{,}00{,}000$ squeezes four times as much ground onto the same paper as $1 : 50{,}000$, so everything on it is four times smaller.

What to carry forward

Decide the kind before you calculate. Ask what happens to the second quantity when the first is doubled. If it doubles, the quotient is fixed and you have a direct proportion; if it halves, the product is fixed and you have an inverse one. Every question in this division was decided in that one sentence.

Find what one part is worth. A ratio becomes a set of amounts the moment you know the value of a single part, and one given amount is enough to find it. The same move handles paint, concrete, prize money and the angles of a pie chart.

Ratios survive multiplication and not addition. Multiply every term of a ratio by the same number and it is unchanged; add the same number to every term and it is not. Whenever a question adds something to both parts, write the quantities as $ax$ and $bx$ and turn it into an equation.
