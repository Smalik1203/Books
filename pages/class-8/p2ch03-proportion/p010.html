A · cricket, 24 $4 \times 30 = 120^\circ$
B · kabaddi, 18 $3 \times 30 = 90^\circ$
C · football, 12 $2 \times 30 = 60^\circ$
D · badminton, 12 $2 \times 30 = 60^\circ$
E · chess, 6 $1 \times 30 = 30^\circ$

The five angles add to $360^\circ$. If they did not, something would be wrong. That is the check, and it should be done before picking up a protractor.

Drawing the chart needs a compass and a protractor, and no more arithmetic. Draw a circle and one radius. Then measure each angle in turn, starting from the last radius drawn.

The chart shows shares. Cricket took a third of the circle, and cricket and kabaddi together took more than half. The chart does not show how many students there were: the same chart would fit a group of seven hundred and twenty. That is why a pie chart is nearly always labelled with its total.

Percentages give the same angles. Kabaddi was named by $\tfrac{18}{72} = 25\%$ of the students, and $25\%$ of $360^\circ$ is $90^\circ$. The arithmetic of Chapter 1 and of this chapter is the same, with a different whole: a hundred there, three hundred and sixty here.

Angles of a pie chart
  • A slice's angle is its fraction of the whole times $360^\circ$.
  • The angles of all the slices add to $360^\circ$; that is the check.
