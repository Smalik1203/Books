Here are four ways of covering the same $90$ km.

on foot, $5$ km/h $18$ hours    $5 \times 18 = 90$
by bicycle, $15$ km/h $6$ hours    $15 \times 6 = 90$
by motorcycle, $30$ km/h $3$ hours    $30 \times 3 = 90$
by car, $60$ km/h $1.5$ hours    $60 \times 1.5 = 90$

The two quantities do change by the same factor — a bicycle is three times a walking pace, and it takes a third of the time — but they change in opposite directions. Multiply the speed by $n$ and the time is multiplied by $\tfrac1n$.

Look at the right-hand column and the reason is plain. Speed times time is $90$ every time, because $90$ km is the distance and it is not changing. That product is what holds still here, exactly as the quotient held still in Section 3.2.

Two kinds of proportion

$x$ and $y$ are in direct proportion when their quotient is constant: $\ \dfrac{y}{x} = k$. Both grow together.

They are in inverse proportion when their product is constant: $\ xy = k$. One grows as the other shrinks. So for any two pairs of values, $\ x_1 y_1 = x_2 y_2$.

Nothing about a pair of quantities tells you which it is except thinking about the situation. More workers, more bricks moved — direct. More workers, fewer days to finish — inverse. The same word more in both, pulling opposite ways.

Example 8

Twenty workers take four days to lay a stretch of road. How long would ten workers take?

Fewer workers, more days: inverse. So the product stays the same.

Solution.

Step 1 $20 \times 4 = 80$ worker-days the constant
Step 2 $10 \times y = 80$ same product
Step 3 $y = 8$ divide by ten
Answer $8$ days

Halving the workers doubled the days, and the constant has a meaning worth keeping: eighty worker-days is the size of the job, however it is shared out.
