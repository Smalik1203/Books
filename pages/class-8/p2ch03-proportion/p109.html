Why the other options are wrong

Set A, 5. Option (b), $20$, is the answer to a direct proportion — double the $x$, double the $y$. But the question says inversely, and doubling $x$ from $6$ to $12$ must halve $y$ from $10$ to $5$. The product $6 \times 10 = 60$ is what had to be preserved, not the quotient.

Set A, 9. Option (a), $12$ days, doubles the days along with the workers. It is the commonest slip in the chapter, and a moment's thought catches it: more people cannot make a job take longer.

Set B, 7. Option (a), $9$ hours, averages the two times. Times cannot be averaged, and the answer must be under $6$ hours — two taps beat the faster one working alone. Adding the rates gives $\tfrac{1}{12} + \tfrac16 = \tfrac14$ of the tank an hour, so four hours.

Set B, 8. Option (a), $18$ and $30$, is in the ratio $3 : 5$ and differs by $12$. Both conditions have to hold at once, which is what writing the numbers as $3x$ and $5x$ arranges: their difference is $2x$, so $x = 8$.

Set B, 11. Option (b), $140$, scales the turns up with the radius. The bigger wheel covers more ground per turn, so it must turn fewer times over the same road — the answer had to be below $100$ before any arithmetic was done.
