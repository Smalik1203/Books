Patterns in cubes

The first ten cubes are $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729$ and $1000$, and between them they end in every digit there is. As with squares, the last digit of a cube depends only on the last digit of the number — but this time no digit is ruled out.

number ends in0123456789
its cube ends in0187456329

So the units digit can never show that a number is not a cube, as it could for squares. But it gives something squares never gave: each last digit comes from exactly one last digit of the number. A cube ending in 7 comes only from a number ending in 3, and one ending in 8 only from a number ending in 2 — useful, as we will see, for finding cube roots.

The cubes also thin out far faster than the squares. There are two cubes with one digit, 1 and 8; two with two digits, 27 and 64; and five with three digits, 125, 216, 343, 512 and 729. Among the numbers up to 1000 there are thirty-one squares but only ten cubes.
