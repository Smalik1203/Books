Problem 1 · Try it first

The smallest number by which $1176$ must be multiplied to give a perfect square is

  1. $2$
  2. $3$
  3. $6$
  4. $14$
Solution 1

Factorise, and see which primes are short of a partner.

$1176 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7 \times 7 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 7^2$

The $7$s pair. Of the three $2$s, two pair and one is left over. The single $3$ is left over as well. So two primes need partners, and the smallest multiplier that supplies both is $2 \times 3 = 6$, option (c). Then $1176 \times 6 = 7056 = \left(2^2 \times 3 \times 7\right)^2 = 84^2$. Options (a) and (b) each fix one prime and leave the other unpaired; (d) supplies a $2$ and a $7$, and the $7$ was already paired, so it leaves the $3$ alone and adds a new odd $7$.
