1.2Square Numbers

Why are 1, 4, 9, 16, … called squares? Because they are areas. The number of unit squares that cover a square is the product of its two sides, and the sides are equal: a square of side 3 units holds $3 \times 3 = 9$ unit squares, and one of side 4 holds 16 (Fig. 1.1).

We write the product of a number with itself with a small 2:

$1 \times 1 = 1^2 = 1, \quad 2 \times 2 = 2^2 = 4, \quad 3 \times 3 = 3^2 = 9, \quad 4 \times 4 = 4^2 = 16$

In general, for any number $n$, $n \times n$ is written $n^2$ and read $n$ squared. The side need not be a whole number. A square of side $\frac{3}{5}$ unit has area $\left(\frac{3}{5}\right)^2 = \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{9}{25}$, and one of side $2.5$ units has area $2.5^2 = 6.25$. So any number can be squared. The squares of the natural numbers — 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, … — are the ones this chapter is about, and they have a name of their own.

Perfect square

A perfect square is the square of a natural number: $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, \ldots$ Every perfect square is the area of a square whose side is a whole number of units.
