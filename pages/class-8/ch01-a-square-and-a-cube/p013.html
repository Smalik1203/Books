1.4Cubic Numbers

You know the word cube from geometry: a solid whose faces are all squares, meeting at right angles, with every edge the same length. How many cubes of side 1 cm make a cube of side 2 cm? Fig. 1.5 shows them — two layers of $2 \times 2$, eight in all. A cube of side 3 cm needs three layers of $3 \times 3$: twenty-seven.

The numbers $1, 8, 27, \ldots$ are called perfect cubes, and now you can see why. Each is obtained by multiplying a number by itself three times:

$1 = 1 \times 1 \times 1, \quad 8 = 2 \times 2 \times 2, \quad 27 = 3 \times 3 \times 3$

Is 9 a cube? We have $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ and $3 \times 3 \times 3 = 27$, and 9 falls between them. So 9 is not a perfect cube — and nor is any number from 10 to 26.

Can you estimate the number of unit cubes in a cube of edge 4 units? Look at it carefully: each layer is a $4 \times 4$ square of 16 unit cubes, and there are 4 such layers, so the total is $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$. In the same way, $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125$, so 125 is a cube.

For any number $n$, the product $n \times n \times n$ is written $n^3$ and read $n$ cubed. So $4^3 = 64$ and $5^3 = 125$.

Perfect cube

A perfect cube is the cube of a natural number: $1, 8, 27, 64, 125, \ldots$ Every perfect cube is the number of unit cubes in a cube whose edge is a whole number of units.
