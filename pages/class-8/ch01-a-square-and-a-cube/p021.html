Square pairs

Look at the row $3, \; 6, \; 10, \; 15, \; 1$. Each pair of neighbours adds up to a square: $3 + 6 = 9$, $6 + 10 = 16$, $10 + 15 = 25$, $15 + 1 = 16$.

  1. Arrange the numbers 1 to 17, each used once, in a row so that every pair of neighbours adds to a square.
  2. Can you do it in more than one way? If not, can you explain why not?
  3. Now try 1 to 32, each used once — but this time in a circle.

Which numbers from 1 to 17 have only one possible neighbour? Those must sit at the ends.
