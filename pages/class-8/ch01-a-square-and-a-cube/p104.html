Solution 2

The squares between the two bounds are $n^2$ for whole numbers $n$ in a run, so the question is really where that run starts and ends. $14^2 = 196$, which is below $200$, and $15^2 = 225$, which is above it. At the other end, $44^2 = 1936$ and $45^2 = 2025$.

$n$ runs from $15$ to $44$, so there are $44 - 15 + 1 = 30$ of them

The answer is (b). Option (a) comes from forgetting to add the $1$ — the count of numbers from $15$ to $44$ is not $44 - 15$. Option (c) comes from letting $n$ start at $14$, whose square is below $200$.

Problem 3 · Try it first

A cube of side $12$ cm is cut into cubes of side $2$ cm. How many are there, and how does the total surface area change?

  1. $36$; it is unchanged
  2. $216$; it is $6$ times bigger
  3. $216$; it is unchanged
  4. $1728$; it is $6$ times bigger
Solution 3

Along each edge, $12 \div 2 = 6$ small cubes fit. So the big cube is $6$ small cubes long, $6$ wide and $6$ tall.

$6 \times 6 \times 6 = 6^3 = 216$ small cubes

For the surface: the big cube has six faces of area $12^2 = 144$ cm², so $864$ cm² in all. Each small cube has six faces of area $2^2 = 4$ cm², so $24$ cm², and $216$ of them give $5184$ cm² — six times as much. The answer is (b). Every cut makes two new faces that were inside the solid before, so surface is created while volume is not.

Option (d) counts the volume in cubic centimetres, $12^3 = 1728$, rather than the number of $2$ cm cubes; option (a) divides the faces rather than the edges.
