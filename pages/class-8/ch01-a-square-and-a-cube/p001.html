1.1The Hundred Lockers

Queen Ratnamanjuri left her fortune of ratnas — precious stones — behind a puzzle. She wanted her son Khoisnam to have it all, but she knew that if she simply gave it to him, their ninety-nine relatives would pester him for ever. So her will invited all hundred of them to a reading, and said: if you all solve my puzzle at the same time, you will share the ratnas equally. If one of you is first, that one keeps everything.

The minister led them to a room with a hundred lockers, all closed, and gave each person a number from 1 to 100. Then he read the rules.

Person 1 opens every locker.

Person 2 toggles every 2nd locker — closes it if it is open, opens it if it is closed.

Person 3 toggles every 3rd locker: the 3rd, the 6th, the 9th, and so on.

Person 4 toggles every 4th locker: the 4th, the 8th, the 12th, and so on.

This goes on until all hundred have had their turn. At the end some lockers are open and the rest are closed, and the numbers of the open ones are the code to the safe.

Before anyone had moved, Khoisnam knew which lockers would be open. How?

Think and Reflect

Follow locker 6 through the whole process. Which people touch it, and does it end open or closed? Then do the same for lockers 1, 4 and 9.

Person $k$ touches locker $n$ exactly when $k$ is a factor of $n$.

A locker that is toggled an odd number of times ends open; toggled an even number of times, it ends closed. And the number of times locker $n$ is toggled is the number of factors of $n$, because Person $k$ touches it only when $k$ divides $n$.

Example 1

Locker 6 and locker 9, followed to the end.

locker 6 1 opens, 2 closes, 3 opens, 6 closes — four factors, so closed
locker 9 1 opens, 3 closes, 9 opens — three factors, so open
