Example 4

Given that $35^2 = 1225$, find $36^2$ without multiplying.

$1225$ is the sum of the first 35 odd numbers. To reach $36^2$ we add the 36th odd number, which is $2 \times 36 - 1 = 71$.

$36^2 = 1225 + 71 = 1296$

Check: $36 \times 36 = 1296$. It agrees.

Between two squares

How many numbers lie strictly between consecutive perfect squares? Between 1 and 4 there are two, 2 and 3. Between 4 and 9 there are four; between 9 and 16, six. The gap $(n+1)^2 - n^2$ is the odd number $2n + 1$, so the numbers strictly between $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$ are $2n$ in number — always even, and growing. Between $16^2 = 256$ and $17^2 = 289$ there are already 32. So the squares thin out: ten of them lie between 1 and 100, and every block of a hundred after that holds fewer.
