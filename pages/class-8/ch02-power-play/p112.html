That is the difference between arithmetic and mathematics, and this chapter is the first place in the book where the two really come apart. From here on, more and more of the questions will be ones no amount of calculating would answer — and the reward for the notation you have just learnt is that you will not need to calculate them.

It is worth ending where the chapter began. A sheet of paper is about a tenth of a millimetre thick and a fold is the least remarkable thing you can do to it. Nothing in the situation is large; nothing about it is surprising. What is surprising is only the arithmetic of doing a small thing forty-six times, and our failure to see it coming is not stupidity but the ordinary result of instincts trained on adding.

Whenever you next meet a quantity that doubles — a debt at compound interest, an infection in a population, the storage on a computer, the users of a new application — remember the sheet. The first ten steps will look like nothing at all. Then, quite suddenly, they will not.
