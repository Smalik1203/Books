round the Earth $4.0075 \times 10^7$ m
Earth to the Moon $3.844 \times 10^8$ m
Earth to the Sun $1.496 \times 10^{11}$ m

The exponents show the order at once: $7$, then $8$, then $11$. The Sun's exponent is $3$ more than the Moon's, so the Sun is hundreds of times further away. The coefficients then give a closer figure: about $390$ times.

So the exponent matters more than the coefficient. Suppose a city has $3 \times 10^6$ people. If the $3$ changes to a $4$, the city grows by a third. If the $6$ changes to a $7$, the city becomes ten times as large. The usual way of writing hides this most important digit in a row of zeros.

Standard form also makes multiplying easy. Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents, using the first law of § 2.3. So $(4 \times 10^6) \times (2 \times 10^3)$ is $8 \times 10^9$, done in one line.

Example 7

Write $73\,406$ and $0.00036$ in standard form.

Solution.

Step 1 $73406 = 7.3406 \times 10^4$ point moved $4$ places left
Step 2 $0.00036 = 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ point moved $4$ places right
Answer $7.3406 \times 10^4$ and $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$

Check the sign every time. A number larger than $10$ has a positive exponent. A number smaller than $1$ has a negative exponent.

Think and Reflect

A town counts its people as $2\,86\,417$. Written as $2.86417 \times 10^5$, this claims to be right to the last person. No count of a town is that exact.

What does $2.86 \times 10^5$ claim? What does $2.9 \times 10^5$ claim? What does the number of digits in the coefficient tell you?

The number of digits in the coefficient shows how well a number is known. If the count is sure to the nearest thousand, write $2.86 \times 10^5$. If it is sure only to the nearest ten thousand, write $2.9 \times 10^5$. Writing more digits than you are sure of is a false claim.

Small numbers work in the same way. A human hair is about $7 \times 10^{-5}$ m thick. The single digit $7$ says that this is a rough value. Writing $7.00 \times 10^{-5}$ m would claim far more than anyone measured.
