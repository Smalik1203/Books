$2^{-3}$ is $\tfrac18$, a small positive number. It is not $-8$, and it is not $-\tfrac18$. Compare $(-2)^3 = -8$, where the minus is on the base and does change the sign.

With that, the three laws hold for every integer exponent — positive, zero or negative — and they are the whole of the arithmetic of powers. There is nothing more to learn about them; the rest of this chapter is about using them.

Set the powers of $2$ out in a row and the whole system can be seen at once. Each step to the right multiplies by $2$; each step to the left divides by it. The exponent is simply how many steps you are from $1$, counted positive to the right and negative to the left.

Read the line and the laws become obvious instead of something to remember. Going from $2^1$ to $2^4$ is three steps right, which is multiplying by $2^3$; that is $2^1 \times 2^3 = 2^4$. Going from $2^4$ back to $2^1$ is three steps left, which is $2^4 \div 2^3 = 2^1$. And $2^0 = 1$ is not a special value but simply the place you happen to be standing when you have taken no steps at all.

Example 5

Simplify $2^{-4} \times 2^7$, $3^2 \times 3^{-5} \times 3^6$, $p^3 \div p^{10}$ and $(13^{-2})^{-3}$, leaving each answer as a single power with a positive exponent.

Solution.

Step 1 $2^{-4} \times 2^7 = 2^{-4+7} = 2^3$ add the exponents
Step 2 $3^2 \times 3^{-5} \times 3^6 = 3^{2-5+6} = 3^3$ add the exponents
Step 3 $p^3 \div p^{10} = p^{-7} = \dfrac{1}{p^7}$ subtract, then invert
Step 4 $(13^{-2})^{-3} = 13^{(-2) \times (-3)} = 13^6$ multiply the exponents
Answer $2^3 = 8$,  $3^3 = 27$,  $\dfrac{1}{p^7}$,  $13^6$

Every line is the same three laws. The only new thing is that the arithmetic on the exponents is now arithmetic with negative numbers, which you can already do.
