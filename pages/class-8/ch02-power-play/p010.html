2.4Zero and Negative Powers

The three laws were all argued from rows of factors, so they were only ever claims about counting numbers. But the laws themselves do not mention counting anywhere, and that suggests a question: if we insisted that they went on holding, what would $2^0$ have to be?

Take the division law and choose the two exponents to be equal. On the left it gives a number divided by itself, which is $1$; on the right it gives an exponent of zero.

by cancelling $\dfrac{2^4}{2^4} = 1$
by the law $\dfrac{2^4}{2^4} = 2^{4-4} = 2^0$

The two answers have to agree, so $2^0 = 1$. There was no choice about it: the moment we ask that the law keep working, the value is fixed.

Anything to the power zero

$n^0 = 1$ for every $n$ except $0$. It is not a special case to be remembered; it is what the division law forces.
