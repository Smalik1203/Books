Changing the units does not make a measurement more exact. A distance of $6$ km is known to the nearest kilometre. Writing it as $6000$ m does not make it known to the nearest metre, though the zeros suggest that. In standard form, $6 \times 10^3$ m keeps its one digit.

2.6Counting the Possibilities

Powers also appear when we count. Meera's bicycle lock has four dials. Each dial shows a digit from $0$ to $9$. She has forgotten her code. How many codes might she have to try?

Start with a smaller lock. Suppose it had only two dials. The first dial can show any of $10$ digits. For each of these, the second dial can show any of $10$ digits. So there are $10 \times 10 = 100$ codes: $00, 01, 02, \ldots, 99$.

With three dials, each of those $100$ codes can be followed by any of $10$ digits. That makes $100 \times 10 = 1000$ codes. The pattern is a power.
