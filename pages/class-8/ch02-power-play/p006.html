Suppose the greeting goes on for three more hours. That is three more factors of $3$, so in the seventh hour $3^7$ people get it.

We do not need to start again to find $3^7$. We already know $3^4 = 81$. Three more hours multiply by $3$ three more times, which is $3^3 = 27$. So

$3^7$ $= 3^4 \times 3^3 = 81 \times 27$ $2187$

Nothing is special about $4$ and $3$ here. Seven threes can be split in any way, such as $3^2 \times 3^5$ or $3^6 \times 3^1$. We are only cutting a row of seven threes into two shorter rows.

Multiplying powers of the same base

$n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b}$. A row of $a$ copies of $n$ next to a row of $b$ copies makes a row of $a + b$ copies.

Take care with two things. First, the law is about multiplying. So $n^a + n^b$ is not $n^{a+b}$. For example, $2^3 + 2^3 = 16$, but $2^6 = 64$. Second, the bases must be the same. $2^3 \times 5^4$ cannot be written as a single power, because no single number is repeated.

Example 3

Simplify $k^5 \times k^3$, and then $3^2 \times 3^4 \times 3$.

Solution.

Step 1 $k^5 \times k^3 = (k\,k\,k\,k\,k) \times (k\,k\,k) = k^8$ count the factors
Step 2 $3$ means $3^1$ a hidden exponent $1$
Step 3 $3^2 \times 3^4 \times 3^1 = 3^{2+4+1} = 3^7$ add the exponents
Answer $k^8$, and $3^7 = 2187$
