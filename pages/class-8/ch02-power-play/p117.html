Three habits will last longer than the questions that carried them. When two powers cannot be compared, force them to share a base or an exponent — that single move settled four of the eight questions in the first stage. When a number is too large to compute, trade it for a power of ten using $2^{10} \approx 10^3$, and be content with an answer that is right to one figure. And when a rule seems to run out, as it did at $n^0$, ask what value would let the existing laws keep working. That is how the boundaries of mathematics get extended, and it is nearly always more useful than treating the gap as a special case to memorise.

Whenever you next meet a quantity that doubles — a debt at compound interest, an infection in a population, the storage on a computer, the users of a new application — remember the sheet. The first ten steps will look like nothing at all. Then, quite suddenly, they will not.
