2.6Counting the Possibilities

Powers turn up in one more place, and it is the place where they do the most work outside a mathematics lesson: counting how many ways something can be done.

Two children find a safe with a five-digit combination lock and no idea of the code. They decide to try every possibility. How many is that?

Start smaller, which is nearly always the right move when a question is too big to see. Suppose the lock had two digits. The first can be any of $0$ to $9$, which is ten choices; and for each of those the second can again be any of ten. So there are $10 \times 10 = 100$ codes, and you can list them: $00, 01, 02, \ldots, 99$.
