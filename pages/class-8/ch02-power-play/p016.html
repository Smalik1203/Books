2.7Back to the Folded Sheet

With the notation in hand, the opening question can be answered properly rather than by reading a table. After $n$ folds the thickness is

$0.001 \times 2^{n}$ cm

and the only work left is arithmetic on powers. After $46$ folds it is $0.001 \times 2^{46}$ cm. We do not need to work out $2^{46}$ exactly to see what is happening — the laws will do it approximately in two lines. Since $2^{10} = 1024$, which is a little over $10^3$,

$2^{46}$ $= 2^{40} \times 2^6 = (2^{10})^4 \times 64$
$\approx (10^3)^4 \times 64 = 6.4 \times 10^{13}$

Multiply by $0.001$ and the thickness is about $6.4 \times 10^{10}$ cm, which is $6.4 \times 10^{5}$ km. The exact figure is $7.04 \times 10^5$ km, so the estimate is out by about a tenth — and we got it without multiplying anything larger than $64$.

That is what this chapter gives you. A number too big to write became a number you could reason about, and the reasoning was three laws and a rounding.
