$2$ dials $10 \times 10 = 10^2 = 100$
$3$ dials $10 \times 10 \times 10 = 10^3 = 1000$
$4$ dials $10^4$ $10\,000$
Independent choices multiply

Suppose something is made by $a$ choices in turn, and each choice has $n$ options whatever the earlier choices were. Then there are $n^a$ possibilities. Each new choice multiplies the count; it does not add to it.

Check that every choice has the same number of options. If the options change, the count is a product but not a power. Farida has $5$ kurtas, $3$ dupattas and $2$ pairs of sandals. She can dress in $5 \times 3 \times 2 = 30$ ways.

Example 8

A lock has five slots, and each slot takes a capital letter from A to Z. How many codes are there? How does this compare with Meera's four-dial lock?

Solution.

Step 1 letters: $26^5 = 11\,881\,376$ $\approx 1.2 \times 10^7$ 26 options, 5 slots
Step 2 digits: $10^4 = 10\,000$ $= 1 \times 10^4$ 10 options, 4 slots
Step 3 the exponents are $7$ and $4$ compare the exponents
Answer $11\,881\,376$ codes, about a thousand times as many

One more slot multiplies the count by $26$. One more letter multiplies it by only $(27/26)^5$, about $1.2$. The exponent has the larger effect.

This is why a long password is much safer than a short one. Each extra character multiplies the number of passwords that someone would have to try.

A count is a single power only when every slot has the same options. A four-digit PIN gives $10^4$ codes. A code of two letters followed by four digits gives $26^2 \times 10^4$: a product of two powers, not a single power.
