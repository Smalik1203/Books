Type 9 · Counting the possibilities

Example 17

A quiz has $6$ questions, and each has $3$ options. In how many ways can the answer sheet be filled in? If a fourth option is added to every question, by about what factor does the number grow?

Solution.

Step 1 $3^6 = 729$ $3$ options, $6$ times
Step 2 $4^6 = 4096$ $4$ options, $6$ times
Step 3 $4096 \div 729 \approx 5.6$ compare the two
Answer $729$ ways; the number grows about $5.6$ times
Example 18

A locker code is three letters, each from A to E, followed by two digits. How many codes are there? Is the count a single power?

Solution.

Step 1 letters: $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 5^3$ $5$ options, $3$ slots
Step 2 digits: $10 \times 10 = 10^2$ $10$ options, $2$ slots
Step 3 $5^3 \times 10^2 = 125 \times 100$ independent choices multiply
Answer $12\,500$ codes; a product of two powers, not a single power

Type 10 · Estimating in standard form

Example 19

Estimate $2^{45}$ in standard form, using $2^{10} \approx 10^3$.

Solution.

Step 1 $2^{45} = 2^{40} \times 2^5 = (2^{10})^4 \times 32$ split off the tens
Step 2 $\approx (10^3)^4 \times 32 = 32 \times 10^{12}$
Answer about $3.2 \times 10^{13}$

The exact value is about $3.5 \times 10^{13}$. The estimate is low because each $2^{10}$ is a little more than $10^3$.
