Why the other options are wrong

A1 (a) is $3 \times 4$, the commonest error in the chapter; (b) is $3^3$; (d) is $4^3$, the base and exponent swapped.
A2 (b) multiplies the exponents instead of adding them; (c) multiplies the bases as well, which no law permits; (d) subtracts.
A3 (a) adds the exponents where a power of a power multiplies them; (c) and (d) are $5^{2+3\times 2}$ and $5^{3^2}$, both invented.
A4 (b) adds, (d) multiplies. (c) is the classic slip of dividing the bases as well as subtracting the exponents — but $7 \div 7$ has already been used up in getting from $7^9$ to $7^3$.
A6 (a) and (b) put the minus sign on the answer, where it belongs on the exponent; a negative exponent means divide by. (d) has divided the base by the exponent.
A7 (a) forgets that four minus signs cancel in pairs. (c) and (d) multiply the base by the exponent.
A10 (c) is the right value but the wrong form: standard form needs the coefficient between $1$ and $10$, and $58$ is not. (d) has miscounted the places by one.
B2 $2^{10} = (2^2)^5 = 4^5$. (b) keeps the exponent while changing the base, which doubles the exponent's worth by mistake; (d) is a common instinct and simply wrong — any even power of $2$ is a power of $4$.
B3 Write them all as powers of $2$: $2^{16}$, $2^{14}$, $2^{15}$, $2^{12}$. That is the whole question, and once done it needs no arithmetic.
B4 (b) has added the coefficients instead of multiplying them; (c) has multiplied the exponents; (d) has the right value but is not in standard form, since $0.8$ is less than $1$.
