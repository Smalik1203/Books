Example 14 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) $8^4 \times 125^4$(1) 125
(Q) $\frac{15^4}{5^4 \times 3^2}$(2) 1024
(R) $\frac{5^8 \times 25^2}{125^3}$(3) $10^{12}$
(S) $2^{10}$(4) 9

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–1, S–2
  3. P–3, Q–1, R–4, S–2
  4. P–2, Q–4, R–1, S–3

Solution.

Step 1$8^4 \times 125^4 = 1000^4 = 10^{12}$, and $\frac{15^4}{5^4} = 3^4$, so the second is $3^2 = 9$.
Step 2$\frac{5^8 \times 5^4}{5^9} = 5^3 = 125$, and $2^{10} = 1024$.
Answer(a) P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2
Example 15 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) $0.000\,000\,052$(1) $1.35 \times 10^{8}$
(Q) $3\,250\,000\,000$(2) $6.048 \times 10^{5}$
(R) The number of seconds in a week(3) $5.2 \times 10^{-8}$
(S) $4.5 \times 10^5 \times 300$(4) $3.25 \times 10^{9}$

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–1, Q–4, R–2, S–3
  2. P–4, Q–3, R–2, S–1
  3. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  4. P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1

Solution.

Step 1Count the places the point moves. A week has $604\,800$ s, and $4.5 \times 10^5 \times 300 = 1.35 \times 10^8$.
Answer(d) P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1
