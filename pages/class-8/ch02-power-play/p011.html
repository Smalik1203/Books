Set out the powers of $2$ in a row, and the whole system can be seen at once. Each step to the right multiplies by $2$. Each step to the left divides by $2$. The exponent is the number of steps from $1$: positive to the right, negative to the left.

Read the line, and the laws are easy to see. Going from $2^1$ to $2^4$ is three steps right, which is multiplying by $2^3$: $2^1 \times 2^3 = 2^4$. Going back from $2^4$ to $2^1$ is three steps left: $2^4 \div 2^3 = 2^1$. And $2^0 = 1$ is simply where you stand when you have taken no steps at all.

Example 6

Simplify $3^{-5} \times 3^8$, $5^4 \times 5^{-6} \times 5^3$, $k^2 \div k^9$ and $(7^{-3})^{-2}$. Give each answer as a single power with a positive exponent.

Solution.

Step 1 $3^{-5} \times 3^8 = 3^{-5+8} = 3^3$ add the exponents
Step 2 $5^4 \times 5^{-6} \times 5^3 = 5^{4-6+3} = 5^1$ add the exponents
Step 3 $k^2 \div k^9 = k^{-7} = \dfrac{1}{k^7}$ subtract, then invert
Step 4 $(7^{-3})^{-2} = 7^{(-3) \times (-2)} = 7^6$ multiply the exponents
Answer $3^3 = 27$,  $5^1 = 5$,  $\dfrac{1}{k^7}$,  $7^6$

Every line uses the same three laws. The only new thing is arithmetic with negative numbers in the exponents, which you already know.
