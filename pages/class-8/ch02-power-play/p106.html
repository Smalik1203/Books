Why $0^0$ is left alone

Two rules are pulling in opposite directions, and there is no way to satisfy both.

$n^0 = 1$ for every $n$ we have allowed — so this wants $0^0 = 1$
$0^n = 0$ since $0^1 = 0$, $0^2 = 0$, $0^3 = 0$ — so this wants $0^0 = 0$

Approach the corner along one road and the answer looks like $1$; approach it along the other and it looks like $0$. Since no single value keeps both patterns intact, mathematicians decline to choose one, and $0^0$ is left undefined.

This is worth understanding rather than memorising, because it is how mathematics generally deals with a gap. Recall § 2.4: $n^0$ and $n^{-a}$ were not discovered, they were defined — and defined in the only way that let the existing laws go on holding. When there is such a way, the definition is made and nobody thinks of it as a choice. When there is no such way, as here, the case is left open. Leaving a thing undefined is not a failure; it is a refusal to write down something that would be wrong half the time.

The fifth power keeps the last digit

Try six and the pattern is unmistakable.

$2^5 = 32$ends in $2$
$3^5 = 243$ends in $3$
$4^5 = 1024$ends in $4$
$7^5 = 16807$ends in $7$
$8^5 = 32768$ends in $8$
$9^5 = 59049$ends in $9$

Every time, the fifth power ends in the digit it started with. The reason is the cycle of last digits, met two solutions ago. Every digit's last-digit cycle has length $1$, $2$ or $4$ — check them and see — and $5 - 1 = 4$ is a multiple of all three. So raising to the fifth power always lands exactly one step past a whole number of cycles, which is where it started.

Two things follow for free. The same is true of the ninth power, and the thirteenth, and every power of the form $4k + 1$. And the last digit of any power can be found by reducing the exponent modulo the length of its cycle, which is how the $7^{100}$ question was answered without a single multiplication of large numbers.
