2.2.1 Negative bases

The base can be a negative number. Look at what happens to the sign.

$(-3)^2$ $= (-3) \times (-3) = 9$
$(-3)^3$ $= (-3) \times (-3) \times (-3) = -27$
$(-1)^7$ $= -1$
$(-1)^{40}$ $= 1$

The minus signs cancel in pairs. So a negative base with an even exponent gives a positive number, and with an odd exponent a negative number. This is the rule you already know for squares and cubes, now for every exponent.

The brackets matter. $(-3)^2 = 9$, but $-3^2 = -9$. Without brackets, the power acts on the $3$ alone, and the minus sign comes after.

Example 2

Find the value of $(-2)^5$, $\;(-2)^4 \times (-1)^9$ and $-2^4$.

Solution.

Step 1 $(-2)^5 = -32$ odd exponent, so negative
Step 2 $(-2)^4 \times (-1)^9 = 16 \times (-1) = -16$ even, then odd
Step 3 $-2^4 = -(2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2) = -16$ no brackets
Answer $-32$, $\;-16$ and $-16$

The last two have the same value for different reasons. Always find the sign first, and then the size.

Notice that $3^2$ and $(-3)^2$ are both $9$. A square is never negative, whatever the sign of the number being squared.

What about the exponent $0$? What is $n$ multiplied by itself no times? We come back to this in § 2.4. The laws of the next section will decide it for us.
