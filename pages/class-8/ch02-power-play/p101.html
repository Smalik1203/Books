Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter left three laws and one habit. The laws: multiplying powers of the same base adds the exponents, raising a power to a power multiplies them, and dividing subtracts. The habit: when two powers cannot be compared as they stand, rewrite them until they share a base or an exponent, and then the comparison is free.

Every question below yields to that. Four of them look like they need a calculator and none of them does — in each case there is a way of rewriting that makes the answer visible. Try each before turning the page.

Which is larger, $2^{100}$ or $100^2$? Then find the smallest whole number $n$ for which $2^n$ is larger than $n^2$, and check that $2^n$ stays larger after that.

What is the last digit of $7^{100}$?

Show that $2^{10}$ is larger than $10^3$. Use it to decide which of $2^{100}$ and $10^{30}$ is larger, without evaluating either.

Which is larger, $3^{500}$ or $5^{300}$? Neither can be written out, and neither needs to be.

A rumour starts with one person. Every hour, everybody who knows it tells three people who do not. How many hours before a city of a million knows?
