Exercise Set 2.6
  1. Find the units digit of $2^{24} \times 4^{32}$ without evaluating either. Hint: $4 = 2^2$.
  1. Write each number as a product of two or more powers, in three different ways. The exponents may be any integers.
    1. $64^3$
    2. $192^8$
    3. $32^{-5}$
  1. Always true, sometimes true, or never true? Give a reason for each.
    1. A cube number is also a square number.
    2. A fourth power is also a square number.
    3. The fifth power of a number is divisible by its cube.
    4. The product of two cube numbers is a cube number.
    5. $q^{46}$ is both a fourth power and a sixth power, for $q$ prime.
  1. Given that $12^2 = 144$, write down each of these without multiplying.
    1. $(1.2)^2$
    2. $(0.12)^2$
    3. $(0.012)^2$
    4. $120^2$
  1. Three of these five are equal. Which three, and what is their common value as a power of $6$? $\;2^6 \times 3^6, \quad 6^4 \times 6^2, \quad 36^3, \quad 6^{12}, \quad 2^4 \times 3^6$.
  1. Which is greater in each pair?
    1. $4^3$ or $3^4$
    2. $2^8$ or $8^2$
    3. $100^2$ or $2^{100}$
  1. The world has about $10^9$ sheep and about $10^9$ goats. What is the total? It is not $10^{18}$ — explain the error and give the right answer in standard form.
  1. There are about $10^8$ bee colonies in the world and about $5 \times 10^4$ bees in a colony. Give the number of bees in standard form.
  1. The world has about $8 \times 10^9$ people. If each owns $30$ pieces of clothing, how many pieces are there altogether? Give the answer in standard form, and say how many of its digits you would defend.
  1. Show that $2^{30}$ is a little more than $10^9$ without evaluating either in full. Hint: $2^{10}$ is a little more than $10^3$.

The two lines of that estimate are worth reading again for what they did not need. No calculator, no long multiplication, and no exact value of $2^{46}$ — only the fact that $2^{10}$ is a little over $10^3$, which is the single most useful thing to know about powers of two. Anyone who remembers it can move between powers of two and powers of ten in their head, and that is most of what estimating large numbers ever asks of you.
