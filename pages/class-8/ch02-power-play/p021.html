Summary
  1. Adding the same amount again and again is additive growth. Multiplying by the same amount again and again is multiplicative or exponential growth, and it soon becomes very large: a sheet $0.1$ mm thick, folded $42$ times, would be thicker than the distance to the Moon.
  2. $n^a$ means $a$ copies of $n$ multiplied together. The number $n$ is the base and $a$ is the exponent, also called the power or the index. A number with no exponent has the exponent $1$.
  3. A negative base with an even exponent gives a positive number, and with an odd exponent a negative number: $(-3)^2 = 9$ and $(-3)^3 = -27$. Without brackets, $-3^2$ is $-9$, not $9$.
  4. The laws of indices: $n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b}$, $\;(n^a)^b = n^{ab}$, $\;n^a \div n^b = n^{a-b}$ and $m^a \times n^a = (mn)^a$. The first law does not apply to adding: $2^3 + 2^3$ is not $2^6$.
  5. For every $n$ other than $0$, $n^0 = 1$ and $n^{-a} = \dfrac{1}{n^a}$. The division law makes these values the only possible ones. A negative exponent gives a reciprocal; it never makes a number negative.
  6. Every column of a decimal numeral is a power of ten: $704.583$ has $7$ in the $10^2$ column and $3$ in the $10^{-3}$ column.
  7. A number is in standard form when it is written $x \times 10^y$, with $1 \le x \lt 10$ and $y$ an integer. The exponent shows the size at once, and the number of digits in $x$ shows how exactly the number is known.
  8. If something is made by $a$ choices in turn, each with $n$ options, there are $n^a$ possibilities. A four-dial lock with the digits $0$ to $9$ has $10^4$ codes.
  9. Since $2^{10} = 1024$ is a little more than $10^3$, ten doublings multiply a number by about a thousand. This helps to estimate large numbers: list what the answer depends on, estimate each part, multiply, and keep only the digits you trust.
