## 2.3 The Laws of Indices

### Exercise Set 2.2

The set was printed with two questions numbered 5; they are now 5 and 6, and
the last two are 7 and 8.

1. (a) $5^7$ (b) $p^8$ (c) $2^9$ (d) $3^8$ (e) $m^{15}$ (f) $10^5$

2. $8^6 = (8^2)^3 = (8^3)^2$, and as a power of 2,
   $8^6 = (2^3)^6 = 2^{18}$.

3. (a) $6^{8+3-9} = 6^2$ (b) $a^{12-7} = a^5$ (c) $4^5 \times 25^5 = 100^5$

4. (a) $2^7 \times 5^7 = 10^7$ (b) $(2^3)^2 \times 2 = 2^7 = 128$
   (c) $12^4 \div 4^4 = 3^4 = 81$

5. $2^{12} = (2^2)^6 = 4^6$ and $2^{12} = (2^3)^4 = 8^4$. The exponents
   possible are **6** (base 4) and **4** (base 8): $12$ has to be split into
   equal groups of 2 or of 3. There is no power of 3, because $2^{12}$ is made
   of the prime 2 alone and every power of 3 is made of the prime 3 alone.

6. Answers will vary in wording.
   - (a) Multiplying adds the exponents: $3^4 \times 3^2 = 3^6$, not $3^8$.
   - (b) Adding two equal powers doubles one of them:
     $2^5 + 2^5 = 2 \times 2^5 = 2^6$, not $2^{10}$.
   - (c) Dividing subtracts the exponents but leaves the base alone:
     $5^6 \div 5^2 = 5^4$.
   - (d) The bases differ and so do the exponents, so no law applies;
     $2^3 \times 5^4 = 5000$, and $10^7$ is ten million.

7. Doubling runs backwards as halving: half full on **day 29**, a quarter
   full on **day 28**.

8. $2^{20} = (2^{10})^2 = 1024^2$ and $10^6 = (10^3)^2 = 1000^2$. Same
   exponent, and $1024 \gt 1000$, so **$2^{20}$ is larger**.

## 2.4 Zero and Negative Powers

### Exercise Set 2.3

1. (a) $2^{-4} = \dfrac{1}{16}$ (b) $10^{-5} = \dfrac{1}{100000}$
   (c) $(-7)^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{49}$ (d) $5^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{5}$

2. (a) $10^{-7}$ (b) $9^{-11}$ (c) $4^{-6}$ (d) $5^3$ (e) $m^8$ (f) $2^6$

3. (a) $3^0 = 1$ (b) $(-8)^0 = 1$ (c) $4^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{16}$
   (d) $2^{-3} \times 2^5 = 4$

4. From $2^{-2}$ to $2^4$ is six steps right, so $2^4$ is $2^6 = 64$ times
   larger. The division law agrees: $2^4 \div 2^{-2} = 2^{4-(-2)} = 2^6$.

5. The base is more than 1, so the larger the exponent, the larger the number:
   **$3^{-5}$, $3^{-2}$, $3^{-1}$, $3^0$, $3^1$**.

6. $\dfrac{m^5 n^{12}}{m^9 n^9} = m^{-4} n^3 = \dfrac{n^3}{m^4}$

7. **$\dfrac{9}{4}$.** A negative exponent turns the fraction upside down:
   $\left(\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^{-2} = \left(\dfrac{3}{2}\right)^2 = \dfrac{9}{4}$.

## 2.6 Counting the Possibilities

### Exercise Set 2.5

1. (a) $10^4 = 10000$ (b) $36^5 = 60466176$ (c) $6^3 = 216$

2. **10 digits.** $8.5$ billion is $8.5 \times 10^9$. Nine digits give only
   $10^9$ codes, which is too few; ten digits give $10^{10}$, which is enough.

3. $3 \times 4 \times 2 = 24$ rolls. **Not a power**: the three choices offer
   different numbers of options, so different numbers are multiplied.

4. $26^4 = 456976$ passwords. With digits there are 36 options, so
   $36^4 = 1679616$; the number grows by $\left(\dfrac{36}{26}\right)^4$, a
   factor of **about 3.7**.

5. Estu added six tens instead of multiplying them. Each slot multiplies the
   count by 10, so the answer is $10^6 = 1000000$.

6. The first digit has 9 options and each of the other five has 10:
   $9 \times 10^5 = 900000$.

## 2.7 Back to the Folded Sheet

### Exercise Set 2.6

1. $2^{24} \times 4^{32} = 2^{24} \times 2^{64} = 2^{88}$. The last digits of
   powers of 2 run 2, 4, 8, 6, and $88 = 4 \times 22$, so the units digit is **6**.

2. Answers will vary. For example:
   - (a) $64^3 = 2^{18} = 2^9 \times 2^9 = 4^3 \times 4^6 = 8^2 \times 2^{12}$
   - (b) $192 = 2^6 \times 3$, so $192^8 = 2^{48} \times 3^8 = 4^{24} \times 3^8 = 16^{12} \times 9^4$
   - (c) $32^{-5} = 2^{-25} = 2^{-20} \times 2^{-5} = 4^{-10} \times 2^{-5} = 2^{-30} \times 2^5$

3. - (a) **Sometimes.** $8$ is a cube and not a square; $64$ is both.
   - (b) **Always.** $n^4 = (n^2)^2$.
   - (c) **Always**, for a whole number other than 0: $n^5 = n^3 \times n^2$.
   - (d) **Always.** $a^3 \times b^3 = (ab)^3$.
   - (e) **Never.** For a prime $q$, $q^{46}$ is a fourth power only if 46 is
     a multiple of 4, and it is not. (Nor is 46 a multiple of 6.)

4. (a) $1.44$ (b) $0.0144$ (c) $0.000144$ (d) $14400$ — each decimal place
   in the number puts two in the square.

5. **$2^6 \times 3^6$, $6^4 \times 6^2$ and $36^3$**, all equal to $6^6$
   ($46656$). $6^{12}$ is far larger, and $2^4 \times 3^6$ is smaller.

6. - (a) $3^4 = 81$ is greater than $4^3 = 64$.
   - (b) $2^8 = 256$ is greater than $8^2 = 64$.
   - (c) $2^{100}$ is far greater: $2^{100} = 1024^{10}$ is more than
     $10^{30}$, and $100^2 = 10^4$.

7. $10^9 + 10^9 = 2 \times 10^9$. Writing $10^{18}$ adds the exponents, which
   is what multiplying does, not adding.

8. $10^8 \times 5 \times 10^4 = 5 \times 10^{12}$ bees.

9. $8 \times 10^9 \times 30 = 240 \times 10^9 = 2.4 \times 10^{11}$ pieces.
   The inputs are known to about one figure, so only the first digit can be
   defended: **about $2 \times 10^{11}$**.

10. $2^{30} = (2^{10})^3 = 1024^3$ and $10^9 = 1000^3$. Since
    $1024 \gt 1000$, $2^{30}$ is larger — and only a little, because each of
    the three factors is only $2.4\%$ bigger. ($2^{30} = 1073741824$.)

## 2.8 A Note on Thought Experiments

### Exercise Set 2.7

1. Answers will vary. A worked instance: a 13-year-old, at 80 beats a minute.
   A year has $60 \times 24 \times 365 = 525600$ minutes, so
   $80 \times 525600 \times 13 = 546624000$, about $5 \times 10^8$ beats.
   The inputs are rough, so only the first digit is worth defending.

2. $1000 \div 0.02 = 50000$, about $5 \times 10^4$ grains.

3. Answers will vary. With about $1.4 \times 10^9$ people of average length
   $1.5$ m: $1.4 \times 10^9 \times 1.5 = 2.1 \times 10^9$ m, which is
   $2.1 \times 10^6$ km — about five and a half times the
   $3.844 \times 10^5$ km to the Moon.

4. Answers will vary. With $1.5$ hours a day of eating over 70 years of
   365 days: $1.5 \times 3600 \times 365 \times 70 = 137970000$ s, about
   $1.4 \times 10^8$ s. The lifetime is $24 \times 3600 \times 365 \times 70 = 2207520000$ s,
   so the fraction is $1.5 \div 24 = \dfrac{1}{16}$.

5. $10^9 \div (3.15 \times 10^7)$ is about 31.7 years. As in the Think and
   Reflect: from September 2026, about the end of **1994**. The exact date varies with
   the day it is worked.

6. The thickness is $0.001 \times 2^n$ cm, and 100 m is $10000$ cm. So
   $2^n$ must reach $10^7$: $2^{23} = 8388608$ is too small and
   $2^{24} = 16777216$ is enough, so **$n = 24$**.

7. **The first child wins.** $999999 \times 999999$ is less than
   $10^6 \times 10^6 = 10^{12}$, and the first number is $10^{13}$.
