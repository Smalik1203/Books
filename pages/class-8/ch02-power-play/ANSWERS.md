## 2.3 The Laws of Indices

### Think and Reflect

1. She multiplied the bases. The base stays $2$ and the exponents add:
   $2^3 \times 2^4 = 2^7 = 128$.
2. $2^{10} = (2^2)^5 = 4^5$. It is not a power of $8$: that needs an exponent
   of $2$ that is a multiple of $3$, and $10$ is not.
3. Three copies of $5^3$ multiplied is $(5^3)^3$ by the meaning of a power;
   both are $5^9$.

## 2.5 Powers of Ten

### Think and Reflect (the town's count)

$2.86 \times 10^5$ claims the count to the nearest thousand; $2.9 \times 10^5$
to the nearest ten thousand. The more digits in the coefficient, the more
exactly the number is claimed to be known.

## 2.7 Back to the Folded Sheet

### Think and Reflect

1. Ten more folds multiply the thickness by $2^{10} = 1024$, about a thousand.
2. $2^{20} \approx (10^3)^2 = 10^6$. The true value, $1048576$, is larger.

### Exercise Set 2.6

1. $3^8 \times 9^4 = 3^8 \times 3^8 = 3^{16}$. The last digits of the powers of
   $3$ run $3, 9, 7, 1$, and $16$ is a multiple of $4$, so the units digit
   is **1**.
2. (i) True: $n^6 = (n^3)^2$. (ii) False: $2^3 = 8$ is not a square.
   (iii) True: it is $(4n)^2$. (iv) True: $n^4 \div n^2 = n^2$.
3. $2^5 \times 5^5$, $10^2 \times 10^3$ and $10^{10} \div 10^5$ are all
   **$10^5$**. ($1000^2 = 10^6$, and $2^3 \times 5^5 = 25000$.)
4. He multiplied, adding the exponents. The total is
   $10^5 + 10^5 = 2 \times 10^5$.
5. $(4 \times 10^5) \times (2 \times 10^4) = 8 \times 10^9$ leaves.
6. $2^{10} = 1024$ is more than $10^3$, so $2^{20} = (2^{10})^2$ is more than
   $(10^3)^2 = 10^6$ — but only by about $5\%$, since $1.024^2$ is about
   $1.05$.
