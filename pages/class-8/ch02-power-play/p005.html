2.3The Laws of Indices

A king had three daughters. He gave each daughter three baskets; each basket held three keys; each key opened three rooms. How many rooms were there?

Three daughters, three baskets each, so nine baskets. Three keys to a basket, so twenty-seven keys. Three rooms to a key, so eighty-one rooms — and $81 = 3^4$, one factor of three for each layer of the story.

Now suppose each room held three tables, each table three necklaces, and each necklace three diamonds. That is three more levels, so the diamonds number $3^7$.

You could work $3^7$ out from scratch, but there is no need. We already know $3^4 = 81$ from the rooms. Three more levels multiplies by three three more times, which is $3^3 = 27$. So

$3^7$ $= 3^4 \times 3^3 = 81 \times 27$ $2187$
