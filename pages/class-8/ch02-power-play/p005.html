Now suppose each room held three tables, each table three necklaces, and each necklace three diamonds. That is three more levels, so there are $3^7$ diamonds.

You could work $3^7$ out from scratch, but there is no need. We already know $3^4 = 81$ from the rooms. Three more levels multiplies by three three more times, which is $3^3 = 27$. So

$3^7$ $= 3^4 \times 3^3 = 81 \times 27$ $2187$

Nothing about $4$ and $3$ was special. Seven threes can be split anywhere at all — $3^2 \times 3^5$, or $3^6 \times 3^1$ — because all that is happening is that a row of seven threes is being divided into two shorter rows.

Multiplying powers of the same base

$n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b}$. A row of $a$ copies of $n$ set beside a row of $b$ copies is a row of $a + b$ copies, and nothing else has happened.

Two warnings about that law, both of which cost marks every year. It is about multiplying, so $n^a + n^b$ is not $n^{a+b}$ and is not usually anything tidy at all. And the bases must match: $2^3 \times 5^4$ cannot be gathered into a single power, because there is no single number being repeated.

Example 2

Simplify $p^4 \times p^6$, and then $2^3 \times 2^5 \times 2$.

Solution.

Step 1 $p^4 \times p^6 = (p\,p\,p\,p) \times (p\,p\,p\,p\,p\,p) = p^{10}$ count the factors
Step 2 $2$ means $2^1$ an invisible exponent $1$
Step 3 $2^3 \times 2^5 \times 2^1 = 2^{3+5+1} = 2^9$ add the exponents
Answer $p^{10}$, and $2^9 = 512$
