What this chapter established
  1. Adding a fixed amount repeatedly is additive growth; multiplying by a fixed amount repeatedly is multiplicative or exponential growth. The second outruns the first by margins that no intuition prepares you for — a sheet folded $46$ times would reach the Moon.
  2. $n^a$ means $n$ multiplied by itself $a$ times. The $n$ is the base and the $a$ is the exponent, power or index.
  3. $3 \times 4$ and $3^4$ are different questions with different answers. Repeated addition is multiplication; repeated multiplication is a power.
  4. A negative base gives a positive answer to an even power and a negative answer to an odd one. The brackets matter: $(-4)^2 = 16$ but $-4^2 = -16$.
  5. The three laws, each of them a statement about rearranging rows of factors: $n^a \times n^b = n^{a+b}$, $\;(n^a)^b = n^{ab}$, and $\;n^a \div n^b = n^{a-b}$ for $n \ne 0$.
  6. When the exponents match, the bases may be gathered: $m^a \times n^a = (mn)^a$.
  7. $n^0 = 1$ and $n^{-a} = \dfrac{1}{n^a}$, for $n \ne 0$. Neither is a convention chosen for convenience — both are forced by insisting the division law keep working.
  8. A negative exponent means divide by, never make negative. $2^{-3}$ is $\tfrac18$.
  9. Every column of a decimal numeral is a power of ten, running down through the point without a break: $10^2, 10^1, 10^0$, point, $10^{-1}, 10^{-2}$.
  10. Standard form writes a number as $x \times 10^y$ with $1 \le x \lt 10$. It makes sizes comparable at a glance, and the exponent carries more meaning than the coefficient.
  11. The number of digits kept in the coefficient is a claim about how well the number is known, and writing more than you are sure of is a false claim rather than a precise one.
  12. If a thing is built from $a$ independent choices with $n$ options each, there are $n^a$ possibilities. Adding a slot multiplies the count by $n$, which is why the exponent is a far stronger lever than the base.
  13. Quantities nobody can count exactly can still be estimated: write down the assumptions, multiply, and keep only the digits the inputs earned.
