But the columns do not stop at the units. After the decimal point there are more columns. If the exponents count down, what comes after $0$?

$704.583 = (7 \times 10^2) + (0 \times 10^1) + (4 \times 10^0) + (5 \times 10^{-1}) + (8 \times 10^{-2}) + (3 \times 10^{-3})$

The exponents become negative, and they mean what § 2.4 said: $10^{-1}$ is $\tfrac{1}{10}$, $10^{-2}$ is $\tfrac{1}{100}$ and $10^{-3}$ is $\tfrac{1}{1000}$. The tenths, hundredths and thousandths columns are negative powers of ten. The decimal point marks the place where the exponent passes zero.

The decimal point is a marker

Every column of a decimal numeral is a power of ten. The exponents count down without a break: $\ldots, 10^2, 10^1, 10^0$, point, $10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, \ldots$

2.5.1 Standard form

Here are three distances, written in the usual way.

round the Earth $40\,075\,000$ m
Earth to the Moon $384\,400\,000$ m
Earth to the Sun $149\,600\,000\,000$ m

These are hard to read. It is not easy to see how much larger one is than another, and counting zeros by eye leads to mistakes. We want a way of writing a number that shows its size first.

Any number can be written as a number from $1$ up to $10$, multiplied by a power of ten. Take $7300$ as an example:

$7300$ $= 730 \times 10^1 = 73 \times 10^2 = 7.3 \times 10^3 = 0.73 \times 10^4$

All four are correct, but only one is in standard form. The first part must be at least $1$ and less than $10$. That picks out $7.3 \times 10^3$.

Standard form

A number is in standard form, also called scientific notation, when it is written $x \times 10^y$ with $1 \le x \lt 10$ and $y$ an integer. The $x$ is the coefficient and $y$ is the exponent.

Now the three distances are easy to read.
