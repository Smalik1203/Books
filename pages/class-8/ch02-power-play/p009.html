Example 4

Simplify $\dfrac{2^{100}}{2^{25}}$ and $\dfrac{7^9 \times 7^4}{7^{11}}$.

The first is a subtraction and nothing more; writing out a hundred twos would be a waste of an afternoon.

$2^{100} \div 2^{25}$ $= 2^{100-25} = 2^{75}$

In the second, gather the top first and then subtract.

top $7^9 \times 7^4 = 7^{13}$
then $7^{13} \div 7^{11} = 7^2$ $49$

So far the exponents have all been counting numbers, and every law has been argued by writing out rows of factors and rearranging them. That argument needs the exponents to be counts of something. The next section asks what happens when they are not — and the answer turns out to be forced.
