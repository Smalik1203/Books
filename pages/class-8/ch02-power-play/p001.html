Power Play

2.1Introduction

Take a sheet of paper and fold it in half. Fold it in half again, and then again. How many times can you do it?

Most people stop at six or seven folds. After that the paper is too thick to bend, and this has nothing to do with mathematics. So imagine a sheet that can be folded as many times as you like. It is $0.1$ mm thick, like a page of a notebook, and each fold doubles its thickness.

Make a guess before you read on. After thirty folds, how thick would the sheet be? As thick as a book? As tall as your school? As tall as a mountain?

Think and Reflect

Write down your guess for thirty folds. Then write a second guess for forty-two folds.

Keep both guesses, and compare them with the table below. Most guesses are far too small. This chapter explains why.

Here is what happens. The first few folds change very little. Then the numbers become very large, very quickly.

The last line is not a mistake. The Moon is about $384\,400$ km away. A sheet folded forty-two times would be thicker than that.

Nothing unusual was done here. Each fold only doubled the thickness. Doubling once is a small change. Doubling forty-two times is not.
