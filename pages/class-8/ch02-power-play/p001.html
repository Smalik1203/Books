When Multiplying Takes Over

2.1Introduction

Find the largest sheet of paper in the room and fold it in half. Fold it in half again, and again. How many times can you manage?

Most people get to six or seven and then find the paper will not take another fold, which has nothing to do with mathematics — it is the paper running out of give. So set the real paper aside and imagine one that folds as often as you like. Its thickness is a tenth of a millimetre, which is ordinary printing paper, and every fold doubles it.

Before reading on, guess. How thick would it be after thirty folds — the length of your arm? The height of the room? The height of a tall building?

Write your guess down before you turn the page. Then write a second guess for forty-six folds.

Whatever you have written, it is almost certainly too small — and the reason it is too small is the subject of this chapter.
