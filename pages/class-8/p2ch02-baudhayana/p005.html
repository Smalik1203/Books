2.4How Long Is the Diagonal?

Baudhāyana's construction makes the new square without ever saying how long its side is. On a building site that is useful: a cord and two pegs are all you need. But the length is still worth knowing, and we can find it with what we already have.

Take a square of side $1$ and cut it along a diagonal. Each half is a right triangle. Two of its sides have length $1$, and the third side is longer. The longest side, the one opposite the right angle, is called the hypotenuse. Call it $c$.

The word comes from Greek and means "stretching under": the side stretched under the right angle. Indian mathematicians called it the karṇa.

Before calculating, we can say roughly how long $c$ is. It is longer than each short side, so it is more than $1$. A straight line is the shortest path between two points, so $c$ is less than the two short sides together, which make 2.
