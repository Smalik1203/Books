Try this with paper. Cut out a square and fold each corner in to the centre. The creases mark the square of half the area. Then cut a second square along both its diagonals, and lay the four triangles around the first square. You have built the square of twice the area, using scissors instead of a cord.

Example 1

A square garden has an area of $72$ m2. A path joins the midpoints of its sides and makes a smaller square. Find the area and the side of the smaller square.

Solution.

Step 1 area $= 72 \div 2 = 36$ m2 joining midpoints halves the area
Step 2 side $= \sqrt{36} = 6$ m take the square root
Answer The smaller square has an area of $36$ m2 and a side of $6$ m.

We never needed the side of the garden itself, which is not a whole number.
