Two Squares Make One

2.1Introduction

Around 800 BCE, in a manual called the Śulbasūtra, Baudhāyana set down the geometry needed to lay out fire altars — how to mark a right angle on the ground, how to make a circle equal in area to a square, and how to build one shape out of another. The altars had to be built to a stated size, and a rule that was nearly right was no use at all.

One of his questions is easy to say and not at all easy to answer.

Baudhāyana's question

Given a square, construct a square of twice its area.

The first guess is to double the side, and the first guess is wrong. Doubling the side of a square gives four times the area, not twice — you can lay four copies of the original inside it and they fill it exactly.

So the side we want is somewhere between $s$ and $2s$, and it is not obvious where. Baudhāyana's answer is not a number at all. It is a construction, and it is one line long.

Śulbasūtra 1.9

The diagonal of a square produces a square of double the area of the original square.
