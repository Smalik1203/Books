The Baudhayana-Pythagoras Theorem

2.1Introduction

About 800 BCE, Baudhāyana wrote a manual called the Śulbasūtra. It gave the geometry needed to lay out fire altars on the ground. It showed how to mark a right angle, how to make a circle with the same area as a square, and how to turn one shape into another. Each altar had to have an exact size, so a rule that was only nearly right could not be used.

One of his questions is easy to ask and hard to answer.

Baudhāyana's question

Given a square, construct a square of twice its area.

The first guess is to double the side. That guess is wrong. A square with twice the side has four times the area, not twice. Four copies of the first square fit inside it exactly.

So the side we want is longer than $s$ and shorter than $2s$. Baudhāyana does not give it as a number. He gives a construction, in a single line.

Śulbasūtra 1.9

A cord stretched along the diagonal of a square makes a square of twice the area.

This chapter follows his question. We first double a square and then halve one. Next we find how long the diagonal of a square is. Then we join two different squares into one, which leads to the theorem named after Baudhāyana and Pythagoras. We use it to find lengths that cannot be measured directly, and to collect triangles whose sides are all whole numbers.
