Example 2

A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of $41$ m and one short side of $40$ m. Find the other.

Solution.

Step 1 $40^2 + b^2 = 41^2$ the theorem
Step 2 $b^2 = 1681 - 1600 = 81$ subtract the known square
Step 3 $b = 9$ m take the square root
Answer The other short side is $9$ m.

Here the known long side went on the right of the equals sign, and the working became a subtraction. Adding $40^2$ and $41^2$ would have answered a question nobody asked.

Bhāskarāchārya set this in his Līlāvatī, in the twelfth century. No depth is given.

Example 3

A lotus stands with the tip of its stem $1$ unit above the water. A breeze pushes it over until the tip touches the water $3$ units from where it stood. How deep is the lake?

Let $x$ be the depth. The stem is $x + 1$ long, and bending it over does not change its length, so the slanting stem is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with short sides $3$ and $x$.

Solution.

Step 1 $3^2 + x^2 = (x+1)^2$ the theorem
Step 2 $9 + x^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$ expand
Step 3 $9 = 2x + 1$ take $x^2$ off both sides
Step 4 $x = 4$ solve
Answer The lake is $4$ units deep.

The $x^2$ that vanished is the whole trick: the unknown appears squared on both sides, and once it goes what is left is a linear equation of the kind solved in Class 7.
