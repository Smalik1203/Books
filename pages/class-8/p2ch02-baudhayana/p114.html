Three habits from this division will last longer than the questions that carried them. Find the right angle before you find anything else. Most of these questions arrive without one drawn — two poles, a rhombus, a perpendicular dropped inside a triangle — and until it is on the page there is nothing to calculate. The theorem is about squares, not lengths. It hands you $c^2$, and the length comes afterwards, which is why the tidy route to a half-area square is to halve the area rather than to chase the side. Recognise the small triples. Spotting $(12,16,20)$ as four times $(3,4,5)$ saves the arithmetic; when the numbers are not a triple, expect a root that does not come out, and trap it rather than rounding early.
