Doubling ten times

Each step doubles the area, so after ten steps the area has been multiplied by $2$ ten times over.

area $2^{10} = 1024$
side $\sqrt{1024} = 32$

The side came out a whole number because $1024$ happens to be a perfect square. Every second square in the run has a whole-number side — $1$, $2$, $4$, $8$, $16$, $32$ — and the ones in between have sides $\sqrt2$ times as long, so they never will.

Two poles and the gap between them

Nothing in the question is a right-angled triangle until you draw one. Run a horizontal line from the top of the shorter pole across to the taller one: the line between the tops is now a hypotenuse.

across $12$ m, the distance between the poles
up $12 - 7 = 5$ m, the difference in height
so $144 + 25 = 169$, and the tops are $13$ m apart

The heights themselves never mattered, only the difference between them. Two poles of $107$ m and $102$ m would give the same answer.

Two sides that differ by one

Call the shorter side $a$; the other is $a + 1$, and the theorem gives an equation.

the theorem $a^2 + (a+1)^2 = 29^2 = 841$
expand $2a^2 + 2a + 1 = 841$, so $a^2 + a = 420$
try $20 \times 21 = 420$, so $a = 20$

The sides are $20$ and $21$, and $(20, 21, 29)$ is a Baudhāyana triple — the machine makes it from $m=5$, $n=2$. This is the one question here where the equation could not be finished by rearranging; it was finished by recognising a product.
