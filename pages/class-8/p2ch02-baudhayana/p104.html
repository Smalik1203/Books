Solved Examples

Type 1 · Doubling and halving a square

Example 1

A square has an area of $18$ cm². Find the side of the square built on its diagonal, and the side of the square made by joining the midpoints of its sides.

Solution.

Step 1 square on the diagonal: $2 \times 18 = 36$ cm² twice the area
Step 2 its side is $\sqrt{36} = 6$ cm
Step 3 midpoint square: $18 \div 2 = 9$ cm² half the area
Step 4 its side is $\sqrt{9} = 3$ cm
Answer $6$ cm and $3$ cm

The first square's own side, $\sqrt{18}$, was never needed.

Example 2

The side of a square is multiplied by $5$. By what is its area multiplied? And by what must the side be multiplied to make the area $49$ times as large?

Solution.

Step 1 side $5s$ gives area $5s \times 5s = 25s^2$ both sides grow
Step 2 area $49s^2 = 7s \times 7s$ undo the squaring
Answer The area is multiplied by $25$; the side must be multiplied by $7$

Type 2 · Trapping a square root

Example 3

Trap $\sqrt{3}$ between two numbers with one decimal place, and then between two with two decimal places.

Solution.

Step 1 $1.7^2 = 2.89$ and $1.8^2 = 3.24$ $3$ lies between
Step 2 $1.73^2 = 2.9929$ and $1.74^2 = 3.0276$ $3$ lies between
Answer $1.7 < \sqrt{3} < 1.8$ and $1.73 < \sqrt{3} < 1.74$
