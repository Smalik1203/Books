Doubling a square

The square built on the diagonal of a square has twice its area.

Once you can double a square, you can double it again, and again. Each new square stands on the diagonal of the one before. Each is turned through half a right angle from the last, and each has twice the area.

2.3Halving a Square

Baudhāyana's construction also works backwards, and Fig. 2.3 already shows how. To make a square with half the area of a given square, join the midpoints of its four sides.

Why is the inside square half? The four corners left over are four triangles. Each of them is a quarter of the inside square. Fold the four corners inwards and they cover the inside square exactly, with no gap and no overlap.

Notice what does not work. Halving the side does not halve the area. It makes the area a quarter, for the same reason that doubling the side made it four times as large. Four squares of half the side fit inside the first square.
