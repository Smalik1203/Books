2.3Halving a Square

Baudhāyana's construction runs backwards just as well, and the picture above has already given the answer away. To make a square of half the area of a given one, join the midpoints of its four sides.

Why is the inside square half? Because the four corners left over are four triangles, and each of them is a quarter of the inside square — fold the four corners inwards and they cover it exactly, with nothing over and nothing short.

It is worth pausing on what does not work. Halving the side does not halve the area; it quarters it, for the same reason that doubling the side quadrupled it. Four squares of half the side fit inside the original.
