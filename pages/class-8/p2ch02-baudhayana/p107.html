Type 6 · Testing for a right angle

Example 11

Which of these triangles is right-angled: sides $33$, $56$, $65$, or sides $6$, $7$, $9$?

Solution.

Step 1 $33^2 + 56^2 = 1089 + 3136 = 4225 = 65^2$ the two squares match
Step 2 $6^2 + 7^2 = 36 + 49 = 85$, but $9^2 = 81$ they do not
Answer The triangle $33$, $56$, $65$ is right-angled, with the right angle opposite $65$; the other is not

Always put the longest side on its own: it is the only one that can be the hypotenuse.

Example 12

A carpenter makes a rectangular frame $60$ cm by $80$ cm and measures its diagonal as $99$ cm. Is the corner square? What should the diagonal be?

Solution.

Step 1 $60^2 + 80^2 = 3600 + 6400 = 10000 = 100^2$ a square corner needs this
Step 2 $99^2 = 9801$, not $10000$ compare
Answer No. The diagonal should be $100$ cm

Type 7 · Using the theorem twice

Example 13

A box is $2$ cm by $3$ cm by $6$ cm. Find the length of the longest straight rod that fits inside it.

Solution.

Step 1 $d^2 = 2^2 + 3^2 = 13$ across the base
Step 2 $D^2 = d^2 + 6^2 = 13 + 36 = 49$ then up
Answer $D = 7$ cm

The base diagonal is $\sqrt{13}$, which is not a whole number, but only its square was used.
