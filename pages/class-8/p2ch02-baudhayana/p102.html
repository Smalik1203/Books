The eight are answered below in the order they were asked. In most of them the first move is to find the right-angled triangle that the question has not drawn: once it is on the page the arithmetic is short, and where it is not on the page there is nothing to do.

A half-area square, found without its side

Joining the midpoints of a square's sides halves the area, which Section 2.3 settled with the four folded corners. So there is no need to find the first square's side at all.

inner area half of $200 = 100$ cm2
inner side $\sqrt{100} = 10$ cm

Going the other way round — finding the outer side as $\sqrt{200}$ and then working with it — gives the same answer through a number that does not come out exactly. The tidy route was to halve the area first.

That is worth generalising. Areas in this chapter behave themselves and lengths often do not, so a question about lengths is frequently easier answered by working in areas for as long as possible and taking the square root once, at the end.
