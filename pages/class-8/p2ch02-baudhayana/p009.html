2.6Finding a Side You Cannot Reach

The theorem connects three lengths. So if we know any two of them, we can find the third. Very often the third is a length that a measuring tape cannot reach.

First decide which side is the hypotenuse. It is the side opposite the right angle, and it is always the longest side. If you know the hypotenuse, you subtract squares. If you are looking for it, you add squares.

Example 2

A right-angled triangle has short sides $16$ cm and $30$ cm. Find its hypotenuse.

Solution.

Step 1 $c^2 = 16^2 + 30^2 = 256 + 900 = 1156$ the theorem
Step 2 $c = \sqrt{1156} = 34$ cm take the square root
Check $34 > 30$, and $34 < 16 + 30$
Answer The hypotenuse is $34$ cm.
Example 3

A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of $53$ m and one short side of $45$ m. Find the other short side.

Solution.

Step 1 $45^2 + b^2 = 53^2$ the theorem
Step 2 $b^2 = 2809 - 2025 = 784$ subtract the known square
Step 3 $b = 28$ m take the square root
Answer The other short side is $28$ m.

Sometimes the unknown length is in a short side and in the hypotenuse. The theorem still finds it.
