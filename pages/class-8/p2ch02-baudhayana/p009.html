Example 3

A lotus stands with the tip of its stem $1$ unit above the water. A breeze pushes it over until the tip touches the water $3$ units from where it stood. How deep is the lake?

Let $x$ be the depth. The stem is $x + 1$ long, and bending it over does not change its length, so the slanting stem is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with short sides $3$ and $x$.

the theorem $3^2 + x^2 = (x+1)^2$
expand $9 + x^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$
take $x^2$ off both sides $9 = 2x + 1$, so $x = 4$

The lake is $4$ units deep. The $x^2$ that vanished is the whole trick: the unknown appears squared on both sides, and once it goes what is left is a linear equation of the kind Part I's Chapter 6 was about.

Example 4

The diagonals of a rhombus are $24$ and $70$ units long. Find the length of a side.

Part I's Chapter 5 established that the diagonals of a rhombus cut each other in half and cross at right angles. So each side of the rhombus is the hypotenuse of a right triangle whose short sides are half of each diagonal.

half-diagonals $12$ and $35$
so $s^2 = 12^2 + 35^2 = 144 + 1225 = 1369$
and $s = \sqrt{1369} = 37$

All four sides come to $37$, which they had to: the four right triangles have the same two short sides.
