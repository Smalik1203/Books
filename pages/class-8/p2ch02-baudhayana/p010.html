Example 4

A ladder leans with its top at the top of a wall and its foot $5$ m from the wall. Stood upright, it would reach $1$ m above the top. How high is the wall?

Solution. Let the wall be $x$ m high. The ladder is $x + 1$ m long, the hypotenuse of a right triangle with short sides $5$ and $x$.

Step 1 $5^2 + x^2 = (x+1)^2$ the theorem
Step 2 $25 + x^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$ expand
Step 3 $25 = 2x + 1$ take $x^2$ from both sides
Step 4 $x = 12$ solve
Answer The wall is $12$ m high, and the ladder is $13$ m long.

In Example 4 the unknown appeared squared on both sides. Once $x^2$ was taken away, what was left was a linear equation of the kind solved in Class 7.

Example 5

The diagonals of a rhombus are $42$ cm and $40$ cm long. Find the length of a side.

Solution. The diagonals of a rhombus cut each other in half at right angles (Chapter 4 of Part I). So each side is the hypotenuse of a right triangle, and the two half-diagonals are its short sides.

Step 1 half-diagonals $21$ cm and $20$ cm halve each diagonal
Step 2 $s^2 = 21^2 + 20^2 = 441 + 400 = 841$ the theorem
Step 3 $s = \sqrt{841} = 29$ take the square root
Answer Each side of the rhombus is $29$ cm.
