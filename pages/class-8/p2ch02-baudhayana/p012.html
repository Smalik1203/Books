2.7Triples

Most right-angled triangles have a side that is not a whole number: sides $1$ and $1$ give $\sqrt{2}$. The Śulbasūtra lists triangles whose sides are all whole numbers.

$(3, 4, 5)$ $9 + 16 = 25$
$(5, 12, 13)$ $25 + 144 = 169$
$(8, 15, 17)$ $64 + 225 = 289$
$(7, 24, 25)$ $49 + 576 = 625$
$(12, 35, 37)$ $144 + 1225 = 1369$
$(15, 36, 39)$ $225 + 1296 = 1521$

Three whole numbers with $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$ form a Baudhāyana triple, also called a Pythagorean triple.
