Example 9

A school playground is a rectangle 80 m long and 60 m wide. Children often walk across it along a diagonal instead of along two sides. A tap stands at the middle of the playground, where the two diagonals cross.

  1. The length of a diagonal is
    1. 140 m
    2. 100 m
    3. 70 m
    4. 120 m
  2. Numerical answer. How many metres shorter is the walk along a diagonal than the walk along two sides?
  3. Numerical answer. How far, in metres, is the tap from each corner?

Solution.

Step 1$80^2 + 60^2 = 6400 + 3600 = 10\,000$, so the diagonal is 100 m.
Step 2Two sides make $80 + 60 = 140$ m, so the diagonal saves $140 - 100 = 40$ m.
Step 3The diagonals of a rectangle are equal and cut each other in half, so the tap is $100 \div 2 = 50$ m from each corner.
Check$(60, 80, 100)$ is $(3, 4, 5)$ times 20.
Answer(i) (b) 100 m; (ii) 40; (iii) 50
