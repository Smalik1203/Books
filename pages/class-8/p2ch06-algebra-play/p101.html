Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter watched a trick being taken apart and then put back together. Almost every question below asks for the putting together: it hands you the finished behaviour — the answer a trick lands on, the top of a pyramid, the total of a square — and wants the design that produces it.

Every one of them yields to the same first move. Write the situation down in letters, exactly as it stands, and see what the writing leaves you with. Try each before turning the page.

A trick runs: think of a number, add $k$, double it, subtract $8$, halve it, subtract the number you first thought of. It ends at $6$ for everybody. What is $k$?

A friend works the date trick and tells you their answer is $1390$. What date did they think of?

A three-row pyramid has $100$ at the top, and its bottom row is three consecutive whole numbers. Find them.

Two $2 \times 2$ squares are drawn on the same calendar page. One totals $40$ and the other $68$. How far apart are their top-left dates, and where does that put one square in relation to the other?

A two-digit number is exactly four times the sum of its digits. Find every number for which this is true.
