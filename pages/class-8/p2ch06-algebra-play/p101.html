Beyond the Book

The chapter watched a trick being taken apart and then put back together. Almost every question below asks for the putting together: it hands you the finished behaviour — the answer a trick lands on, the top of a pyramid, the total of a square — and wants the design that produces it.

Every one of them yields to the same first move. Write the situation down in letters, exactly as it stands, and see what the writing leaves you with. Try each before reading what follows it.

Each question is explained straight after it. Watch how little of the work is arithmetic. In nearly every one, the hard part is over as soon as the situation has been written down properly, and what follows is a line or two of tidying.

A trick runs: think of a number, add $k$, double it, subtract $8$, halve it, subtract the number you first thought of. It ends at $6$ for everybody. What is $k$?

Follow the five steps on $x$, keeping $k$ as it stands. The doubling and the halving between them leave $x$ untouched, so the subtraction at the end removes it entirely and only the constants survive.

after doubling $2(x + k) - 8 = 2x + 2k - 8$
halving $x + k - 4$
subtracting $x$ $k - 4$, which is to be $6$, so $k = 10$

The $8$ was halved to $4$ on its way through and the $k$ was not, because $k$ was added before the doubling and $8$ after it. Where a constant is inserted decides how much of it survives, and that is the whole of the designer's control.

Notice the shape of the question, because six of the remaining seven share it. You are given what the trick does and asked for one of the numbers inside it, which is the reverse of the way the chapter presented every one of them.
