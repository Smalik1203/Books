Type 6 · Shapes that stand for numbers

Example 11

In a grid, the first row is a square and a star, totalling $12$. The second row is two squares and a star, totalling $17$. Find both shapes.

Solution.

Step 1 $s + t = 12$ and $2s + t = 17$ square $s$, star $t$
Step 2 $(2s + t) - (s + t) = s$, so $s = 17 - 12 = 5$ the rows differ by one square
Step 3 $t = 12 - 5 = 7$
Answer A square is $5$ and a star is $7$

Neither row has only one kind of shape, so the way in was to compare the two rows.

Type 7 · The largest product

Example 12

Arrange $2$, $7$ and $8$ in $\square\,\square \times \square$ to make the largest product. How close is the next best?

Solution.

Step 1 $p = 2$, $q = 7$, $r = 8$ in increasing order
Step 2 $qp \times r = 72 \times 8 = 576$ largest digit multiplies
Step 3 next best: $82 \times 7 = 574$
Step 4 $576 - 574 = 2 = 2 \times (8 - 7)$ the units parts differ
Answer $72 \times 8 = 576$, only $2$ ahead of $82 \times 7$

Type 8 · Reversing and cycling digits

Example 13

The digits of a two-digit number add to $12$. Reversing the digits gives a number $18$ less. Find the number.

Solution.

Step 1 $(10a + b) - (10b + a) = 9(a - b) = 18$ the reverse is smaller
Step 2 $a - b = 2$ and $a + b = 12$
Step 3 adding: $2a = 14$, so $a = 7$ and $b = 5$
Answer $75$, and indeed $75 - 57 = 18$
