Think and Reflect
  1. Put the same number $n$ in every box of the bottom row of a three-row pyramid. What is at the top? What if the pyramid has four rows?
  2. Why do the multipliers in each row add up to twice the total of the row before?
Exercise Set 6.2
  1. Without building the pyramid, find the top of the three-row pyramid with each bottom row.
    1. $6$, $11$, $9$
    2. $8$, $15$, $2$
    3. $12$, $7$, $20$
  1. Do the same for these four-row pyramids.
    1. $5$, $9$, $14$, $2$
    2. $10$, $4$, $8$, $3$
    3. $6$, $13$, $1$, $12$
  1. Write the top of a five-row pyramid in terms of its bottom row $a$, $b$, $c$, $d$, $e$. Find the multipliers by adding neighbours in the row $1$, $3$, $3$, $1$.
  1. Complete each three-row pyramid.
    1. Top $52$, bottom ends $5$ and $9$.
    2. Top $44$, bottom ends $8$ and $6$.
    3. Top $30$, bottom ends $11$ and $3$.
  1. Two three-row pyramids have the bottom rows $3$, $4$, $5$ and $2$, $6$, $2$. Both tops are $16$. Find a third bottom row of whole numbers with the top $16$, and explain how you found it.
  1. A four-row pyramid has $90$ at the top and the bottom row $4$, $x$, $x$, $8$. Find $x$. Then explain why no such pyramid can have $91$ at the top.
  1. In a five-row pyramid, which box of the bottom row has the most routes to the top, and how many? If you may add $1$ to just one bottom number, which one raises the top the most?
