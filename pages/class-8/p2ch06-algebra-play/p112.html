Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): A two-digit number minus its reverse can be $35$.

    Reason (R): A two-digit number minus its reverse is always a multiple of $9$.

  1. Assertion (A): The four dates of any $2 \times 2$ calendar square add to an even number.

    Reason (R): Their total is $4a + 16 = 4(a + 4)$, a multiple of $4$.

  1. Assertion (A): A two-digit number plus its reverse is always a multiple of $11$.

    Reason (R): $11$ is a prime number.

  1. Assertion (A): From the digits $1$, $2$ and $3$, the largest product of the form $\square\,\square \times \square$ is $21 \times 3 = 63$.

    Reason (R): The largest digit should always go in the tens place.

Very short answer
  1. The top-left date of a $2 \times 2$ calendar square is $9$. Find the total of its four dates without adding them.
  1. Write the two-digit number whose tens digit is $x$ and units digit is $y$, and the number made by reversing it.
  1. Find the top of the four-row pyramid whose bottom row is $1$, $1$, $1$, $1$.
Short answer
  1. A trick runs: think of a number, multiply by $6$, add $18$, divide by $3$, subtract twice the number you thought of. Show that the answer is always $6$. What should be added in place of $18$ to make the trick end at $10$?
  1. The bottom row of a three-row pyramid is $x$, $2x$, $x + 6$, and its top is $48$. Find $x$ and complete the pyramid.
  1. The digits of a two-digit number add to $11$. Reversing the digits gives a number $27$ more. Find the number.
  1. Riya spends half her money and then ₹$10$ more. She does the same again with what is left, and ends with ₹$6$. How much did she start with?
Long answer
  1. The nine dates of a $3 \times 3$ calendar square add to $117$. Find all nine dates and check their total. Then show, with the top-left date $a$, why the total of any such square is a multiple of $9$.
  1. A four-row pyramid has bottom row $x$, $3$, $5$, $x$ and top $60$. Find $x$ and build the whole pyramid to check. By how much does the top change if both end entries are raised by $1$?
