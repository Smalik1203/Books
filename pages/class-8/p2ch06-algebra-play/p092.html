  1. Deepa’s mother is $4$ times as old as Deepa. In $5$ years, her mother will be $3$ times as old as Deepa. Find their present ages. Also find how many years ago the mother was $7$ times as old as Deepa.
  1. Pooja designs a date trick: multiply the month by $2$, add $5$, multiply by $50$, and add the day. (i) Show that the answer is $100m + 250 + d$ for month $m$ and day $d$. (ii) Her friend’s answer is $1178$. Find the date. (iii) Explain why an answer of $1284$ must be wrong. (iv) Change one step of the trick so that the number to take away is $300$.
  1. In Sneha’s puzzle grid, two squares and three circles total $31$, and a square and two circles total $18$. Each shape stands for the same number wherever it appears. Find both shapes by writing one unknown in terms of the other. Also find the total of a row with three squares and one circle.
  1. Show that a three-digit number $abc$ and the two numbers $bca$ and $cab$ add to $111(a + b + c)$. Hence find the three-digit number whose digits are three consecutive numbers in decreasing order, if this total is $1332$.
  1. In the given figure, each number in the pyramid is the sum of the two numbers just below it. The bottom row is $3$, $x$, $2x$, $5$, and the top is $98$. Find $x$ and complete the pyramid. Check that the two numbers just below the top add to $98$.
Assertion and reason

Choose (a) if A and R are both true and R is the correct explanation of A; (b) if A and R are both true but R is not the correct explanation of A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): The sum of a two-digit number and its reverse can be $132$.

    Reason (R): The sum of a two-digit number and its reverse is always $11$ times the sum of its digits.

  1. Assertion (A): The four dates of a $2 \times 2$ square on a calendar can add to $70$.

    Reason (R): The four dates of a $2 \times 2$ square add to $4a + 16$, where $a$ is the top-left date.
