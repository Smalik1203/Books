Example 3

A three-digit number and the two numbers made by cycling its digits add to 2220. Which statements are correct?

  1. The sum is a multiple of 111.
  2. The digits add to 20.
  3. The digits add to 22.
  4. The number could have the digits 9, 9 and 2.

Solution.

Step 1$\overline{abc} + \overline{bca} + \overline{cab} = 111(a + b + c)$.
Step 2$2220 \div 111 = 20$, and $9 + 9 + 2 = 20$.
Answer(a), (b), (d)
Example 4

A $2 \times 2$ square of dates on a calendar adds to 104. Which statements are correct?

  1. The smallest date is 22.
  2. The largest date is 30.
  3. The sum is always 4 times the smallest date, plus 16.
  4. A $2 \times 2$ square could add to 105.

Solution.

Step 1The dates are $a$, $a + 1$, $a + 7$, $a + 8$, adding to $4a + 16 = 104$: $a = 22$ and the largest is 30.
Step 2$4a + 16$ is always even, so 105 is impossible.
Answer(a), (b), (c)
