Farhan started with $14$ tokens. Each step was undone in reverse order: a doubling by halving, and a fee by adding it back.

Algebra can do the same job going forwards. Call the starting number of tokens $x$, and follow the story.

after round 1 $2x - 16$
after round 2 $2(2x - 16) - 16 = 4x - 48$
the third doubling $2(4x - 48) = 8x - 96$, and this is $16$
so $8x = 112$, giving $x = 14$

Both ways give $14$. Here the backward way is shorter. But the forward way tells us more, because $2x - 16$, $4x - 48$ and $8x - 96$ describe the game for every player.

Is the game worth playing? After one round a player has $2x - 16$ in place of $x$. This is more than $x$ only when $x > 16$. So Farhan, with $14$ tokens, lost on every round.

Now let the fee be $c$. The same working gives $2x - c$ after one round and $4x - 3c$ after two. After $n$ rounds, the player has

$$2^{\,n}(x - c) + c.$$

If $x$ is more than $c$, the bracket is positive and the player gains every round. If $x$ equals $c$, nothing changes. If $x$ is less than $c$, as for Farhan, each round doubles how far behind the player is.

Two ways to undo
  • Backwards: undo each step, in reverse order.
  • Forwards: call the start $x$, and solve the equation at the end.
