The order matters, and choosing it is the whole skill. The top row contains only one kind of shape, so it is an equation in one unknown and gives up its answer at once. Once the triangle is known, the second row has one unknown too.

the top row three triangles make $27$, so a triangle is $9$
the second row two circles and a $9$ make $19$, so two circles make $10$
giving a circle is $5$

Written with letters, the two rows are $3t = 27$ and $2c + t = 19$, which is a pair of equations in two unknowns — and the pair was easy only because the first one had a single unknown to begin with. A grid in which every row mixes both shapes is a harder problem, and the way into it is the one used on the pyramid: write both rows down, use one to say what $t$ is in terms of $c$, and put that into the other.
