the difference $(10b + a) - (10a + b)$
collecting $10b - b - 10a + a = 9b - 9a$
factorising $9(b - a)$

So the difference is not merely a multiple of $9$ — it is $9$ times the gap between the digits, which is a second trick hidden inside the first. Shubham's $47$ gave $27$, and the quotient $3$ is exactly $7 - 4$. If $a$ is the larger digit the working is the same with the two lines the other way round, giving $9(a - b)$, and the quotient is again the gap. And if the digits are equal the gap is zero, the difference is zero, and Mukta's insistence that the digits differ was there to keep the trick from being dull rather than from being wrong.

Adding instead of subtracting gives a different divisor and the same kind of answer. Since $(10a + b) + (10b + a) = 11a + 11b = 11(a + b)$, the sum of a two-digit number and its reverse is always a multiple of $11$, and the quotient is the sum of the digits.

Three digits give more room to play in. Take any three-digit number $abc$ and make two more by cycling the digits round — $bca$ and $cab$ — then add all three.

written out $(100a + 10b + c) + (100b + 10c + a) + (100c + 10a + b)$
collecting $111a + 111b + 111c$
factorising $111(a + b + c)$

Each digit has taken every position exactly once, so each is counted once as a hundred, once as a ten and once as a unit — which is $111$ of it. And $111$ is $3 \times 37$, so the total is always divisible by $3$ and always divisible by $37$. Neither of those is a fact about the number chosen; both are facts about the cycling.

Example 6

Take a three-digit number, write it twice over to make a six-digit number — $482$ becomes $482482$ — and divide by $7$, then by $11$, then by $13$. Why does it come out exactly, and what is left at the end?

what repeating does $482482 = 482 \times 1000 + 482 = 482 \times 1001$
and $1001 = 7 \times 11 \times 13$
so the three divisions undo the $1001$, leaving $482$

The number you started with comes back, which is what makes this one feel like magic. Repeating a three-digit number is multiplying it by $1001$, and $1001$ happens to be the product of those three small primes — so the three divisions are just that multiplication taken apart again.
