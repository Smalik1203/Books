Example 2

Run the trick on Independence Day, the fifteenth of the eighth month, and check that it comes back.

the six steps $8 \to 40 \to 46 \to 184 \to 193 \to 965 \to 980$
subtract 165 $980 - 165 = 815$
read it $8$ hundreds and $15$ over — the eighth month, fifteenth day

The formula gives the same thing without the six steps: $100 \times 8 + 165 + 15 = 980$. Once a trick has been written in letters, it can be worked forwards in one line — which is a fair test of whether you have really understood it.

The number to subtract is not something to memorise. It is whatever the constants come to when the steps are carried out on $m$ and $d$, so if you change add 6 to add 7 the number to subtract changes with it, from $165$ to $185$. Work it out from the letters each time and there is nothing left to remember.
