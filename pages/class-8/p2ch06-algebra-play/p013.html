To compare the last two, split each product into a tens part and a units part.

$64 \times 7$ $(60 \times 7) + (4 \times 7) = 420 + 28$
$74 \times 6$ $(70 \times 6) + (4 \times 6) = 420 + 24$

The tens parts are equal. In both, the tens digit and the multiplier are the two largest digits, so both tens parts are $10 \times 6 \times 7$. So the answer depends on the units parts. There, the smallest digit $4$ is multiplied by $7$ or by $6$, and it is better to multiply it by the larger one.

This argument used only the order of the digits, so it works for any three digits. Let the digits be $p$, $q$ and $r$, with $p < q < r$. After dropping the smaller product in each pair, we are left with $rq \times p$, $rp \times q$ and $qp \times r$. Write each one out.

$rq \times p$ $10rp + qp$
$rp \times q$ $10rq + pq$
$qp \times r$ $10qr + pr$

The first two share the term $pq$. So we compare $10rp$ with $10rq$, and since $q > p$ the second is larger. The second and third share $10qr$. So we compare $pq$ with $pr$, and since $r > q$ the third is larger.

The largest product

To make $\square\,\square \times \square$ as large as possible, use the largest digit as the multiplier. Put the other two in decreasing order: $qp \times r$ when $p < q < r$.

Example 6

Arrange $2$, $5$ and $8$ to make the largest product, and compare it with the next largest.

Solution.

Step 1 $r = 8$, $q = 5$ and $p = 2$ by the rule
Step 2 $52 \times 8 = 416$ largest digit multiplies
Step 3 $82 \times 5 = 410$ the next largest
Answer $52 \times 8 = 416$ is the largest

Both have the tens part $10 \times 5 \times 8 = 400$. The difference of $6$ comes from $2 \times 8$ against $2 \times 5$, that is, $2 \times (8 - 5)$.
