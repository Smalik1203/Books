## 6.1 Think of a Number

### Think and Reflect

1. **$8$.** $2x + 16$ halved is $x + 8$, and $x + 8 - x = 8$.
2. **No.** Halving first gives $\dfrac{x}{2}$, then $\dfrac{x}{2} + 10$, and
   taking away $x$ leaves $10 - \dfrac{x}{2}$, which is different for every
   number.
3. Doubling first makes the number even, so the halving always comes out
   exactly and gives back $x$ plus a fixed number. Halving first can give a
   fraction, and the $x$ no longer cancels.

## 6.2 A Date in One Number

### Think and Reflect

1. **No.** $435 - 190 = 245$ would be month $2$, day $45$, and no month has
   $45$ days.
2. **Yes.** $(2m + 7) \times 5 = 10m + 35$, then $10m + 38$, then
   $(10m + 38) \times 10 = 100m + 380$. Take away **$380$**.

### Exercise Set 6.1

1. With *add ten* replaced by *add* $k$, the steps give
   $\dfrac{2x + k}{2} - x = \dfrac{k}{2}$, half of what is added.
   - (a) add $14$: **$7$**.
   - (b) add $7$: **$3.5$**. The trick still ends at one number, but $2x + 7$
     is odd, so the halving does not give a whole number.
   - (c) add $0$: **$0$**.

2. Answers will vary. One trick: *think of a number, double it, add $18$,
   halve it, take away the number you thought of.* In letters:
   $x \to 2x \to 2x + 18 \to x + 9$, and $x + 9 - x = 9$.

3. **$5$.** $x \to x + 6 \to 2x + 12 \to 2x + 10 \to x + 5$, and
   $x + 5 - x = 5$. The unknown disappears at the last step, when the number
   thought of is taken away.

4. **Take away $200$.** $(4m + 7) \times 5 = 20m + 35$, then
   $(20m + 35 + 5) \times 5 = 100m + 200$, and the day is added.

5. **The $13$th of August.** $1003 - 190 = 813 = 100 \times 8 + 13$.

6. Two dates with the same $20m + d$ give the same answer. Answers will vary;
   for example **21 January and 1 February**: $20 \times 1 + 21 = 41$ and
   $20 \times 2 + 1 = 41$. A day can be as large as $31$, so it spills into the
   month's place, which moves only in steps of $20$.

## 6.3 Number Pyramids

## 6.4 What the Top Counts

### Think and Reflect

1. **$4n$** for three rows and **$8n$** for four rows: the multipliers add to
   $1 + 2 + 1 = 4$ and $1 + 3 + 3 + 1 = 8$.
2. Each number in a row of multipliers is used twice in making the next row,
   once in the sum on its left and once in the sum on its right. So the total
   doubles.

   (right end).
   - (a) $5 + 2c + 9 = 52$, $c = 19$: bottom **$5, 19, 9$**, middle

     **$19, 11$**, top $30$.

5. Answers will vary. The top is $a + 2b + c = 16$, so choose the middle
   entry first and then two ends adding to $16 - 2b$. For example
   **$4, 5, 2$**: middle row $9, 7$, top $16$. (Others include $1, 7, 1$ and
   $6, 4, 2$.)

6. **$x = 13$.** $4 + 3x + 3x + 8 = 6x + 12 = 90$, so $6x = 78$. A top of
   $91$ would need $6x = 79$, and $79$ is not a multiple of $6$. (Put another
   way: $6x + 12$ is always even, and $91$ is odd.)

7. The fifth row of multipliers is **$1, 4, 6, 4, 1$**. The **middle** box has
   the most routes to the top, **$6$** of them, so adding $1$ to the middle

## 6.7 The Largest Product

### Think and Reflect

1. **$21 \times 3 = 63$.** No: the largest digit is the multiplier, not the
   tens digit.
2. Answers will vary. The largest and the next largest are $qp \times r$ and
   $rp \times q$, and they differ by $p(r - q)$. For a difference of $3$,
   take **$1, 4, 7$** ($41 \times 7 = 287$, $71 \times 4 = 284$) or
   **$3, 5, 6$** ($53 \times 6 = 318$, $63 \times 5 = 315$).

## 6.8 Turning a Number Round

### Think and Reflect

1. The total is $11(a + b)$, and $a + b$ runs from $10$ to $18$ for totals
   over $100$: **$110, 121, 132, 143, 154, 165, 176, 187, 198$**.
2. **Yes**, whenever $a + b$ is odd. For example, $23 + 32 = 55$.

### Exercise Set 6.4

1. The largest digit multiplies and the other two go in decreasing order.
   - (a) $41 \times 6 =$ **$246$**
   - (b) $53 \times 8 =$ **$424$**
   - (c) $72 \times 9 =$ **$648$**

2. **The smallest digit multiplies, and the other two go in increasing
   order:** $qr \times p$ for $p < q < r$.
   - (a) $46 \times 1 = 46$ (b) $58 \times 3 = 174$ (c) $79 \times 2 = 158$

   Why: in each pair sharing a multiplier the smaller two-digit number wins,
   leaving $qr \times p$, $pr \times q$ and $pq \times r$, that is
   $10pq + pr$, $10pq + qr$ and $10pr + qr$. The first two share $10pq$, and
   $pr < qr$ because $p < q$. The third exceeds the first by
   $10p(r - q) + r(q - p)$, which is positive. So $qr \times p$ is smallest.

3. The difference is $9$ times the gap between the digits, and
   $54 \div 9 = 6$, so **the digits differ by $6$**. Answers will vary:
   **$17$, $28$, $39$** (or $71$, $82$, $93$). Check: $71 - 17 = 54$.

4. The sum is $11(a + b)$, so **the digits add to $121 \div 11 = 11$**. The
   numbers are **$29, 38, 47, 56, 65, 74, 83, 92$**.

5. $(100a + 10b + c) - (100c + 10b + a) = 99a - 99c = 99(a - c)$, a multiple
   of $99$. The tens digit cancels.

6. The total is $111(a + b + c)$, so **the digits add to
   $1665 \div 111 = 15$**. Answers will vary: **$159$**, since
   $159 + 591 + 915 = 1665$.

7. Writing $\overline{ab}$ twice gives $100 \times (10a + b) + (10a + b) =
   101 \times (10a + b)$; for example $5252 = 52 \times 101$. **$101$ is a
   prime**: it is not divisible by $2$, $3$, $5$ or $7$, and $11 \times 11 =
   121$ is already bigger than $101$. So it cannot be split into smaller whole
   factors, whereas $1001 = 7 \times 11 \times 13$.

## 6.9 Undoing What Was Done

### Think and Reflect

- Nothing left after exactly three rounds: $2^{\,3}(x - c) + c = 0$, so
  $8x = 7c$ and **$c = \dfrac{8x}{7}$**. Check: $x = 14$ gives $c = 16$,
  Farhan's game.
- After exactly two rounds: $4(x - c) + c = 0$, so $4x = 3c$ and
  **$c = \dfrac{4x}{3}$**.

### Exercise Set 6.5

1. With $x$ stickers and $c$ given each day: $2x - c$, then $4x - 3c$, then
   $8x - 7c = 0$. So $8x = 7c$, and the smallest whole answer is **$7$
   stickers, with $8$ given to each friend**: $14 - 8 = 6$, $12 - 8 = 4$,
   $8 - 8 = 0$.

2. **$15$ cows** (and $25$ ducks).
   - (a) With $h$ cows: $4h + 2(40 - h) = 110$, so $2h + 80 = 110$ and
     $h = 15$.
   - (b) If all $40$ were ducks there would be $80$ legs. The other $30$ legs
     come two at a time from each cow, so there are $30 \div 2 = 15$ cows.

3. **$10$ years.** $3s + 10 = 2(s + 10)$, so $s = 10$. The aunt is $30$; in
   ten years they are $40$ and $20$.

4. **Harpreet $4$, Divya $12$.** With Harpreet's $h$: $3h - 4 = h + 4$, so
   $h = 4$. After the gift both have $8$.

5. - (a) Costs are $800 + 80 \times 15 = 2000$ rupees, so the sales must
     bring in $2000 + 1200 = 3200$: a price of **₹$40$**.
   - (b) Each glass then earns $35 - 15 = 20$ rupees, and $800 + 1200 = 2000$
     rupees are needed: **$100$ glasses**, since $2000 \div 20 = 100$.

6. Each is **$1$**: $16 - 15 = 1$, $64 - 63 = 1$ and $121 - 120 = 1$. Why:
   $n \times n - (n - 1)(n + 1) = n \times n - (n \times n - 1) = 1$.

7. **$21$ tokens.** After the third doubling he holds $8x - 6c$, and this is
   the fee: $8x - 6 \times 24 = 24$, so $8x = 168$. Check: $42 - 24 = 18$,
   $36 - 24 = 12$, $24 - 24 = 0$.

8. Four days: $16x - 15c = 0$, so the smallest start is **$15$ stickers, with
   $16$ to each friend**. For $n$ days: $2^{\,n}x = (2^{\,n} - 1)c$, and since
   $2^{\,n}$ and $2^{\,n} - 1$ share no factor, the smallest start is
   **$2^{\,n} - 1$ stickers, with $2^{\,n}$ to each friend**: one less than a
   power of two, and that power of two.
