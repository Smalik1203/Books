The twenty-fifth of December. The split is unambiguous only because $25$ is under $100$ — which every day of every month is, and which is the reason the multipliers were arranged to come to a hundred in the first place.

A three-row pyramid has $100$ at the top, and its bottom row is three consecutive whole numbers. Find them.

Three consecutive whole numbers are not three unknowns. Name the smallest $a$ and the other two are $a + 1$ and $a + 2$, so the pyramid has one unknown in it and the formula for the top does the rest.

the top $a + 2(a+1) + (a+2) = 4a + 4$
so $4a + 4 = 100$, giving $a = 24$
the row $24$, $25$, $26$ — and $49 + 51 = 100$

Notice that $4a + 4$ is a multiple of $4$, so a top of $102$ or $103$ would have had no answer at all. A formula tells you which questions are worth asking as well as answering the ones that are.

Two $2 \times 2$ squares are drawn on the same calendar page. One totals $40$ and the other $68$. How far apart are their top-left dates, and where does that put one square in relation to the other?

Each total is $4a + 16$ for its own top-left date, so each gives up that date at once.

first square $4a + 16 = 40$, so $a = 6$
second square $4a + 16 = 68$, so $a = 13$

The dates differ by $7$, and on a calendar a difference of $7$ is a difference of exactly one row. So the second square sits directly beneath the first, sharing its lower two dates as its own upper two — which is a fact about the page that arrived from the arithmetic without anyone looking at a calendar.

A two-digit number is exactly four times the sum of its digits. Find every number for which this is true.

Write the number as $10a + b$ and the sum of its digits as $a + b$. The condition is then a single equation, and collecting it is the whole argument.

the condition $10a + b = 4(a + b)$
expanding $10a + b = 4a + 4b$
collecting $6a = 3b$, so $b = 2a$
