at the end $8$ coins, just after the third doubling
before that doubling $8 \div 2 = 4$
before the second payment $4 + 8 = 12$, so before that doubling $6$
before the first payment $6 + 8 = 14$, so before that doubling $7$

Karim began with seven coins. Every step of that was an undoing: a doubling was undone by halving, a payment by adding back, and the steps were taken in reverse order because the last thing done is the first thing to unpick.

Algebra does the same job facing forwards. Call the starting number of coins $x$, follow the story through in order, and read off what the last line must equal.

after round 1 $2x - 8$
after round 2 $2(2x - 8) - 8 = 4x - 24$
the third doubling $2(4x - 24) = 8x - 48$, and this is $8$
so $8x = 56$, giving $x = 7$

Both roads reach seven, and for this question the backwards one is shorter. The forwards one is the one to have as soon as the question changes — because $2x - 8$, $4x - 24$, $8x - 48$ is not an answer about Karim but a description of the deal, and it can be asked things the story never asked.

Here is one. Was the deal ever worth taking? After a single round Karim holds $2x - 8$ where he held $x$, so he is better off exactly when $2x - 8 > x$, which is to say when $x > 8$. The bargain is good for anybody who starts with more than eight coins and bad for everybody else, and Karim, with seven, was one coin short of breaking even. Had he started with eight he would have walked round the tree all day and come away with eight.

Replace the eight by a charge of $c$ and the same working gives $2x - c$ after one round, $4x - 3c$ after two, and after $n$ rounds

$$2^{\,n}(x - c) + c.$$

Everything about the deal is in that expression. If $x$ is bigger than $c$ the bracket is positive and the doubling runs away with it, so Karim gets rich. If $x$ equals $c$ the bracket is zero and he stays where he is for ever. And if $x$ is smaller than $c$ — Karim's case — the bracket is negative, and each round doubles how far behind he is. The genie did not need luck. Knowing that Karim had seven coins, it had only to name a charge above seven, and eight was the smallest whole number that would do.
