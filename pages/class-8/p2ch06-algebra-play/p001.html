Why the Trick Always Works

6.1Introduction

Mukta says to Shubham: think of a number, and do not tell me what it is. Double it. Add four. Divide by two. Now subtract the number you started with. Shubham does all of it in his head, and before he can speak Mukta says two.

He tries again with a different number and gets two again. He tries $1{,}000$, and gets two. Something is going on, and it is not that Mukta can read minds — it is that she does not need to know the starting number at all. The trick was built so that the starting number would disappear.

This chapter is about how that is done, and the tool for it is one the last two years have already put in your hands. A letter-number stands for any number, so a calculation carried out in letters is every case of that calculation at once. That is what turns it worked for the six numbers I tried into it works, and here is why.

The tricks that follow are worth doing on somebody. But the useful half is the second half: once you can see why a trick works, you can take it apart and build another one that ends wherever you like.
