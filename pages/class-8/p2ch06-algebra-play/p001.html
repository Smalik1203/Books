Algebra Play

6.1Think of a Number

Tanvi says to her cousin Irfan, “Think of a number, and do not tell me what it is. Double it. Add ten. Halve the result. Now take away the number you first thought of.” Irfan works it out in his head. Before he can speak, Tanvi says, “Five.”

He tries again with another number, and again he gets five. He tries $1{,}000$, and he still gets five. Tanvi cannot read minds. She does not need to know his number at all, because the trick is built so that his number disappears.

This chapter shows how such tricks are built. The tool is the letter-number you met in earlier classes. A letter stands for any number, so working with a letter works every case at once. It turns it worked for the numbers I tried into it always works, and here is why.

Write Tanvi’s steps in a column. Beside them, carry out the same steps on the number $7$, and then on a letter-number $x$.

The stepStarting with $7$Starting with $x$
Think of a number$7$$x$
Double it$14$$2x$
Add ten$24$$2x + 10$
Halve it$12$$x + 5$
Take away the number you thought of$5$$5$

The middle column checks one case. The last column checks every case at once, and it shows where the answer comes from. Halving $2x + 10$ gives $x + 5$. The last step takes the $x$ away, and only the $5$ is left. That $5$ is half of the $10$ that was added.

A letter stands for every number
  • Trying a trick on a few numbers shows only that it worked for those numbers.
  • Working the trick with a letter shows that it works for every number.
