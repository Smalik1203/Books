Example 4

A friend says their square adds to $70$. Find the dates. Another says theirs adds to $50$. What do you tell them?

Solution.

Step 1 first friend: $4a + 16 = 70$, so $4a = 54$ not a whole number
Step 2 second friend: $4a + 16 = 50$, so $4a = 34$ not a whole number either
Answer Neither total can come from a square: both friends added wrongly

Both have added up wrongly, and you can say so without seeing the calendar. Every possible total is $4a + 16$, which is a multiple of $4$; a total of $70$ or $50$ cannot come from any square anywhere on any calendar page. The trick checks its own input, which no amount of trying out squares would have told you.

The same handful of lines settles a bigger square. A $3 \times 3$ block holds $a$, $a+1$, $a+2$ across the top, then the same three plus $7$, then plus $14$, and those nine add to $9a + 72$. That factorises as $9(a + 8)$ — and $a + 8$ is the date in the middle of the block. So the nine dates in any $3 \times 3$ square on a calendar come to nine times the one at its centre, which is quicker to say than to believe until you have seen the algebra.

Two things the algebra does not know: that a square must not run off the right-hand edge of the week, and that it must not run past the end of the month. The expression $4a + 16$ is happy with $a = 30$ in August and gives $136$, which no square can produce. A formula answers the question it was given, and the question of whether the square fits on the page has to be asked separately.
