The same few lines work for a bigger square. A $3 \times 3$ square holds $a$, $a + 1$ and $a + 2$ in its top row. The next row holds the same three dates plus $7$, and the last row the same three plus $14$. The nine dates add to $9a + 72$, which is $9(a + 8)$. And $a + 8$ is the date in the middle. So the nine dates in any $3 \times 3$ square add to nine times the middle date.

The algebra does not know that a square must not run off the edge of a week, or past the end of the month. The expression $4a + 16$ gives $136$ for $a = 30$, but no square in July can start on the $30$th. Check separately that the square fits on the page.
