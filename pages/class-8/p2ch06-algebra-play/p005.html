6.4Number Pyramids

In a number pyramid every number is the sum of the two directly below it. The pyramid on the chapter's opening page is built from the bottom row $1$, $9$, $4$: the middle row is $1 + 9 = 10$ and $9 + 4 = 13$, and the top is $10 + 13 = 23$.

Filled from the bottom upwards there is nothing to it — every step is an addition and there is never a choice. Filled from the top downwards it is a different puzzle, because now a number and one of its two children are known and the other has to be recovered.

That pyramid gave itself up because at every stage some box had both of its neighbours known. The next one does not.

The rule of the pyramid, written out for this one, says three things.

the top $a + b = 60$
the left $12 + c = a$
the right $c + 8 = b$
