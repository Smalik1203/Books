6.3Number Pyramids

In a number pyramid, each number is the sum of the two numbers just below it. The pyramid on the first page of this chapter has the bottom row $3$, $8$, $5$. Its middle row is $3 + 8 = 11$ and $8 + 5 = 13$, and its top is $11 + 13 = 24$.

Filling a pyramid from the bottom up is easy, because every step is an addition. Filling it from the top down is a different puzzle. Now you know a number and one of the two below it, and you must find the other.

In that pyramid there was always a box that could be filled next. The next pyramid is different.

The rule of the pyramid gives three equations.

the top $a + b = 50$
the left $9 + c = a$
the right $c + 7 = b$

The last two equations say what $a$ and $b$ are in terms of $c$. Put them into the first equation, and only $c$ is left.
