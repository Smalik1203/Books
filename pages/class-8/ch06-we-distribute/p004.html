By how much does the product of two numbers change if one of them is increased by $m$ and the other by $n$? If $a$ and $b$ are the numbers we start with, they become $a + m$ and $b + n$.

$(a + m)(b + n)$ $= (a + m)b + (a + m)n$
$= ab + mb + an + mn$

The increase is $an + bm + mn$. Look at where the four terms come from: each term of $(a + m)$ has been multiplied by each term of $(b + n)$, and the four products have been added.

$$\text{Identity 1}\qquad (a + m)(b + n) = ab + mb + an + mn$$

Identity 1 tells us how a product changes when the numbers are increased by any amount. It also works when a number is decreased, because taking away a number is the same as adding its negative.

Go back to one number increased by $1$ and the other decreased by $1$. Write $(a + 1)(b - 1)$ as $(a + 1)\big(b + (-1)\big)$ and take $m = 1$ and $n = -1$ in Identity 1:

$ab + (1) \times b + a \times (-1) + (1) \times (-1) = ab + b - a - 1$
