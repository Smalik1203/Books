Nothing is added or taken away when the block of dots is split, so the two parts together hold exactly as many dots as the whole block. This is the distributive property of multiplication over addition: the multiplication by $a$ is distributed over each number in the sum.

The distributive property

For any numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$, $\;a(b + c) = ab + ac$. To multiply a sum by a number, multiply each term of the sum by that number and add the products.

Put $a = 23$, $b = 27$ and $c = 1$ into it:

$23(27 + 1) = 23 \times 27 + 23$

The last term, $23$, is the increase in the product. Here $a(b + c)$ means $a \times (b + c)$, and $23(27 + 1)$ means $23 \times (27 + 1)$. We usually leave out the $\times$ sign next to a bracket, just as we write $5a$ and $xy$ without one.

The property works just as well with the sum on the left. Using the fact that the order of a multiplication does not matter, which is the commutative property, we can expand $(a + b)c$:

$(a + b)c$ $= c(a + b)$ commutativity
$= ca + cb$ distributivity
$= ac + bc$ commutativity

Now we can answer the question for any product. Suppose the two numbers are $a$ and $b$. If one of them, say $b$, is increased by $1$, then

$a(b + 1) = ab + a$

so the product increases by $a$, the other number.

What happens when both numbers are increased by $1$? The product $ab$ becomes $(a + 1)(b + 1)$. How do we expand this?

Treat $(a + 1)$ as a single term and use the distributive property once. Then use it again on each of the two pieces. Below the letters, the same steps are shown with $a = 23$ and $b = 27$.

$(a + 1)(b + 1)$ $= (a + 1)b + (a + 1)1$ $(a + 1)$ as one term
$= ab + (b + a + 1)$ distribute again
$(23 + 1)(27 + 1)$ $= (23 + 1)27 + (23 + 1)1$
$= 23 \times 27 + (27 + 23 + 1)$

So when each of $a$ and $b$ is increased by $1$, the product $ab$ increases by $a + b + 1$. For $23 \times 27$ the increase is $27 + 23 + 1$.
