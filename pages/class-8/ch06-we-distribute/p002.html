Nothing is added or taken away when the block of dots is split, so the two parts together hold exactly as many dots as the whole block. This is the distributive property of multiplication over addition: the multiplication by $a$ is shared out, or distributed, over each number in the sum.

The distributive property

For any numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$, $\;a(b + c) = ab + ac$. To multiply a sum by a number, multiply each term of the sum by that number and add the products.

Put $a = 24$, $b = 35$ and $c = 1$ into it:

$24 \times (35 + 1) = 24 \times 35 + 24$

The last term, $24$, is the increase in the product. In $a(b + c)$ there is no sign between $a$ and the bracket, but it means $a \times (b + c)$. We usually leave out the $\times$ sign next to a bracket, just as we write $5a$ and $xy$ without one.

The property works just as well with the sum on the left. The order of a multiplication does not matter; this is the commutative property. Using it, we can expand $(a + b)c$:

$(a + b)c$ $= c(a + b)$ commutativity
$= ca + cb$ distributivity
$= ac + bc$ commutativity

Now we can answer the question for any product. Call the two numbers $a$ and $b$. If one of them, say $b$, is increased by $1$, then

$a(b + 1) = ab + a$

so the product goes up by $a$, the other number.

What happens when both numbers are increased by $1$? The product $ab$ becomes $(a + 1)(b + 1)$. To expand it, treat $(a + 1)$ as a single term and use the distributive property once. Then use it again on each of the two pieces. The same steps are shown below with $a = 24$ and $b = 35$.

$(a + 1)(b + 1)$ $= (a + 1)b + (a + 1)1$ $(a + 1)$ as one term
$= ab + (b + a + 1)$ distribute again
$(24 + 1)(35 + 1)$ $= (24 + 1)35 + (24 + 1)1$
$= 24 \times 35 + (35 + 24 + 1)$
