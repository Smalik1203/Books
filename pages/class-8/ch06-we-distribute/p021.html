Summary
  1. We extended the distributive property to find the product of two expressions that each have two terms. In general, $(a + b) \times (c + d) = ac + ad + bc + bd$.
  2. We saw three special cases of this identity: $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, $\;(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ and $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$.
  3. We looked at different patterns and used algebra to understand them. There are often several ways to solve a problem and reach the same correct answer. Finding different ways to approach the same problem is a creative process.

Puzzle time: Coin Conjoin

Arrange $10$ coins in a triangle, as on the left of Fig. 6.17. The task is to turn the triangle upside down by moving one coin at a time. How many moves are needed? What is the smallest number of moves?

A triangle of $3$ coins can be turned upside down with a single move, and a triangle of $6$ coins by moving $2$ coins. The $10$-coin triangle can be turned with just $3$ moves. Can you find how? Then find the smallest number of moves that turns the next triangle, of $15$ coins. Try the same for larger triangular numbers. Is there a simple way to work out the smallest number of moves for any such triangle?
