Areas can be found in more than one way too. Find the area of the shaded square in Fig. 6.10. All four rectangles are $m$ by $n$.

Farah’s way. The whole figure is a square of side $m + n$, so its area is $(m + n)^2$. Taking away the four rectangles leaves the shaded square, so its area is $(m + n)^2 - 4mn$.

Kiran’s way. The shaded square has side $n - m$, so its area is $(n - m)^2$.

Expand both expressions and check that $(m + n)^2 - 4mn = (n - m)^2$. With $m = 2$ and $n = 5$, both give $9$.

Next, look at the square tile in Fig. 6.11. It has side $x$. Two painted strips, each $y$ wide, cross in its middle. Three students found the painted area in three different ways.

Neha’s way. Each strip has area $xy$. The small square where they cross has been counted twice, so take it away once.

painted area $= xy + xy - y^2 = 2xy - y^2$

Arjun’s way. Take one whole strip, of area $xy$. What is left of the other strip is two pieces, each $y$ wide and $\frac{x - y}{2}$ long.

painted area $= xy + 2 \times y \times \frac{x - y}{2} = xy + y(x - y)$

Sana’s way. Take the four unpainted corners away from the whole tile. Each corner is a square of side $\frac{x - y}{2}$, and four of them fit together into a square of side $x - y$.
