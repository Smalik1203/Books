Check that this is what Identity 1 gives with $m = u$ and $n = -v$. As before, the product is the sum of the products of each term of $a + u$ (that is, $a$ and $u$) with each term of $b - v$ (that is, $b$ and $-v$). The sign of each product comes from the usual rules for multiplying integers. A subtraction is an addition of a negative number, so this one identity covers every way of increasing or decreasing the two numbers.

Expand (i) $(a - u)(b + v)$ and (ii) $(a - u)(b - v)$ in the same way. We get

$(a - u)(b + v) = ab - ub + av - uv$

$(a - u)(b - v) = ab - ub - av + uv$

The distributive property is not limited to two terms inside a bracket. The number outside multiplies every term inside, however many there are.

Example 1

Expand $\dfrac{3a}{2}\left(a - b + \dfrac{1}{5}\right)$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\frac{3a}{2}\left(a - b + \frac{1}{5}\right) = \left(\frac{3a}{2} \times a\right) - \left(\frac{3a}{2} \times b\right) + \left(\frac{3a}{2} \times \frac{1}{5}\right)$
Step 2 $\frac{3a}{2} \times a = \frac{3}{2} \times (a \times a) = \frac{3}{2}a^2$ exponent notation
Step 3 $\frac{3a}{2} \times b = \frac{3}{2} \times (a \times b) = \frac{3}{2}ab$
Step 4 $\frac{3a}{2} \times \frac{1}{5} = \left(\frac{3}{2} \times \frac{1}{5}\right)a = \frac{3}{10}a$
Answer $\frac{3}{2}a^2 - \frac{3}{2}ab + \frac{3}{10}a$

Can any two of these terms be added to make a single term? Can $\frac{3}{2}a^2$ and $\frac{3}{10}a$ be added, for instance? No two of the terms have exactly the same letter-numbers, so none of them can be joined, and the expression cannot be simplified further. Recall that terms with exactly the same letter-numbers are called like terms; only like terms can be added into one.

Example 2

Expand $(a + b)(a + b)$.

Solution.

Step 1 $(a + b)(a + b) = (a + b)a + (a + b)b$ $(a + b)$ as one term
Step 2 $= a \times a + b \times a + a \times b + b \times b = a^2 + ba + ab + b^2$
Step 3 $ba + ab = ab + ab = 2ab$ like terms, since $ba = ab$
Answer $(a + b)(a + b) = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
