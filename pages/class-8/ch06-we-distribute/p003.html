Now let one number go up by $1$ and the other come down by $1$. Is there any change in the product? If $a$ is increased by $1$ and $b$ is decreased by $1$, the product becomes $(a + 1)(b - 1)$.

$(a + 1)(b - 1)$ $= (a + 1)b - (a + 1)1$
$= ab + b - (a + 1)$
$= ab + b - a - 1$ the increase is $b - a - 1$
$(23 + 1)(27 - 1)$ $= (23 + 1)27 - (23 + 1)1$
$= 23 \times 27 + 27 - (23 + 1)$
$= 23 \times 27 + 27 - 23 - 1$

What happens when $a$ and $b$ are negative integers? Check each of the cases above by putting in values such as $a = -5$, $b = 8$ and $a = -4$, $b = -5$.

Integers obey the distributive property too: if $x$, $y$ and $z$ are any three integers, then $x(y + z) = xy + xz$. So the expressions we found for the increase in a product still hold when the letter-numbers take negative integer values.

Recall that a letter standing for a number is a letter-number, usually called a variable. Two algebraic expressions are equal when they give the same value every time their letter-numbers are replaced by numbers, and those numbers may be any integers. A statement that two algebraic expressions are equal in this way is called an identity. For example,

$a(b + 8) = ab + 8a \qquad (a + 1)(b - 1) = ab + b - a - 1$

Identity

An identity is an equality of two algebraic expressions that is true for every value of the letter-numbers in it.

By how much does the product of two numbers change if one of them is increased by $m$ and the other by $n$? If $a$ and $b$ are the numbers we start with, they become $a + m$ and $b + n$.

$(a + m)(b + n)$ $= (a + m)b + (a + m)n$
$= ab + mb + an + mn$

The increase is $an + bm + mn$. Look at where the four terms come from: each term of $(a + m)$ has been multiplied by each term of $(b + n)$, and the four products added.
