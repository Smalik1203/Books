So when each of $a$ and $b$ is increased by $1$, the product $ab$ goes up by $a + b + 1$. For $24 \times 35$ the increase is $35 + 24 + 1 = 60$. Check it: $24 \times 35 = 840$ and $25 \times 36 = 900$.

What do we get if we expand $(a + 1)(b + 1)$ by taking $(b + 1)$ as the single term first? Try it.

Now let one number go up by $1$ and the other come down by $1$. Does the product change? If $a$ is increased by $1$ and $b$ is decreased by $1$, the product becomes $(a + 1)(b - 1)$.

$(a + 1)(b - 1)$ $= (a + 1)b - (a + 1)1$
$= ab + b - (a + 1)$
$= ab + b - a - 1$ the increase is $b - a - 1$
$(24 + 1)(35 - 1)$ $= (24 + 1)35 - (24 + 1)1$
$= 24 \times 35 + 35 - (24 + 1)$
$= 24 \times 35 + 35 - 24 - 1$

So $25 \times 34$ is $35 - 24 - 1 = 10$ more than $24 \times 35$. Indeed, $25 \times 34 = 850$.

  1. When one number goes up by $1$ and the other comes down by $1$, does the product always go up? Find three pairs of numbers for which it goes down.
  2. Do the same rules work for negative integers? Check them with $a = -6$, $b = 9$ and with $a = -3$, $b = -7$.

Integers obey the distributive property too: if $x$, $y$ and $z$ are any three integers, then $x(y + z) = xy + xz$. So the rules we found for the change in a product still hold when the letters stand for negative integers.

A letter that stands for a number is called a variable. Two algebraic expressions are equal when they give the same value every time their variables are replaced by numbers, whatever numbers we choose. A statement that two algebraic expressions are equal in this way is called an identity. For example,

$a(b + 5) = ab + 5a \qquad (a + 1)(b - 1) = ab + b - a - 1$

Identity

An identity is an equality of two algebraic expressions that is true for every value of the variables in it.
