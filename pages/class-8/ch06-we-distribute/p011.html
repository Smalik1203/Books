distributive property $(4y + 3)^2 = (4y + 3)(4y + 3)$
$= (4y \times 4y) + (3 \times 4y) + (4y \times 3) + 3 \times 3$
$= (4y)^2 + 2(4y \times 3) + 3^2 = 16y^2 + 24y + 9$
Identity 1A $(4y + 3)^2 = (4y)^2 + 2 \times (4y \times 3) + 3^2$
$= 16y^2 + 24y + 9$

If an identity is hard to remember, multiply out with the distributive property instead. It always gives the right result. The identity is only a shorter way to the same answer. Expand $(5p + 2q)^2$ both ways and compare.

Can we use $50^2 = 2500$ and $3^2 = 9$ to find $(50 - 3)^2$, that is, $47^2$? Draw a square of side $47$ in one corner of a square of side $50$, as in Fig. 6.5.

Start from the square of side $50$ and take away the two strips with sides $50$ and $3$: that is $50^2 - (50 \times 3) - (3 \times 50)$. But the two strips overlap in the small square of side $3$, so this takes that small square away twice. Adding its area back once puts it right.

$(50 - 3)^2$ $= 50^2 - (50 \times 3) - (3 \times 50) + 3^2$
$= 2500 - 150 - 150 + 9 = 2209$

The area of the square of side $47$ is $2209$ square units.

What is the expansion of $(a - b)^2$? Using the distributive property,

$(a - b)^2$ $= (a - b) \times (a - b)$
$= a^2 - ba - ab + b^2$
$= a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
