Using What You Know

The chapter left you with a short list. Every term of one bracket multiplies every term of the other. Three products come up so often that they have names: the square of a sum, the square of a difference, and the product of a sum and a difference. And an identity is true for numbers as well as for letters, so it can do arithmetic.

No question below says which of these to use, and some of them look as though they need information that has not been given. Try each one before reading what follows it.

Two numbers add to $10$ and multiply to $21$. Without finding either of them, work out $a^2 + b^2$ and $(a - b)^2$.

You are given the sum and the product, and the square of a sum contains both of them: $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. Rearranged, it says that $a^2 + b^2$ is the square of the sum less twice the product.

$a^2 + b^2$ $= (a + b)^2 - 2ab = 100 - 42 = 58$
$(a - b)^2$ $= a^2 + b^2 - 2ab = 58 - 42 = 16$

So $a - b$ is $4$ or $-4$, and with a sum of $10$ the numbers are $7$ and $3$. Finding them was the last thing that happened, and it was not asked for. Both answers came straight from the two numbers that were given.

Find $49 \times 51$ in your head. Then find $4.9 \times 5.1$, and say what changed and what did not.

$49$ and $51$ are each $1$ away from $50$, so their product is a difference of two squares with $a = 50$ and $b = 1$.

$49 \times 51$ $= (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 2500 - 1 = 2499$
$4.9 \times 5.1$ $= (5 - 0.1)(5 + 0.1) = 25 - 0.01 = 24.99$

Only the place of the decimal point changed. Each factor was divided by ten, so the product was divided by a hundred, and $2499$ became $24.99$. The shape of the working did not change: the identity holds whether or not $a$ and $b$ are whole numbers.
