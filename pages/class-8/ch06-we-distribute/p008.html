Describe a general rule for multiplying a number with any number of digits by $11$ and writing the product in one line.

Use it to find (i) $94 \times 11$, (ii) $495 \times 11$, (iii) $3279 \times 11$ and (iv) $4791256 \times 11$.

Can we find a similar rule for multiplying a number by $101$? Try it on $3874 \times 101$. Again take a four-digit number $dcba$. Since $101 = 100 + 1$,

$dcba \times 101 = dcba \times (100 + 1) = dcba \times 100 + dcba$

$\begin{array}{ccccccc} & d & c & b & a & 0 & 0 \\ + & & & d & c & b & a \\ \hline & d & c & (b + d) & (a + c) & b & a \end{array}$

Use this to multiply $3874 \times 101$ in one line. What could a general rule be for multiplying a number by $101$ and writing the product in one line? Extend the rule to multiplying by $1001$, $10001$, and so on.

Methods like these, which use the distributive property to multiply two numbers easily, were discussed at length in the works of Brahmagupta (628 CE), Sridharacharya (750 CE) and Bhaskaracharya (Lilavati, 1150 CE). In Verse 12.56 of the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta, Brahmagupta calls such quick methods of multiplication ista-gunana.
