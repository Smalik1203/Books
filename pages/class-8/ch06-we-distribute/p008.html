Is there a similar rule for multiplying by $101$? Try it on $4786 \times 101$. Again take a four-digit number $dcba$. Since $101 = 100 + 1$,

$dcba \times 101 = dcba \times (100 + 1) = dcba \times 100 + dcba$

$\begin{array}{ccccccc} & d & c & b & a & 0 & 0 \\ + & & & d & c & b & a \\ \hline & d & c & (b + d) & (a + c) & b & a \end{array}$

Now each digit is added to the digit two places to its right. Use this to write $4786 \times 101$ in one line. What would the rule be for multiplying by $1001$, or by $10001$?

Quick methods like these were written about at length by Brahmagupta (628 CE), Sridharacharya (750 CE) and Bhaskaracharya (Lilavati, 1150 CE). Brahmagupta called such methods ista-gunana, in Verse 12.56 of the Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta.
