Multiplying by 11 and 101
  • $n \times 11 = n \times 10 + n$: each digit is added to the digit on its right.
  • $n \times 101 = n \times 100 + n$: each digit is added to the digit two places to its right.

6.2Special Cases of the Distributive Property

6.2.1 The square of a sum or a difference

A square of side $40$ units has an area of $40^2 = 1600$ square units, and a square of side $3$ units has an area of $3^2 = 9$ square units. Can we use these two to find the area of a square of side $43$ units?

Cut a square of side $43$ where the $40$ ends, as in Fig. 6.3. It falls into four parts: a square of side $40$, a square of side $3$, and two rectangles with sides $40$ and $3$. The area of the whole square is the sum of the areas of the four parts.

Adding the four areas gives

$43^2$ $= (40 + 3)^2 = 40^2 + 3^2 + 2 \times (40 \times 3)$
$= 1600 + 9 + 240 = 1849$ square units

Now multiply $(40 + 3) \times (40 + 3)$ using the distributive property:

$(40 + 3) \times (40 + 3)$ $= 40 \times 40 + 3 \times 40 + 40 \times 3 + 3 \times 3$
$= 40^2 + 2 \times (40 \times 3) + 3^2$

The four products are the areas of the four parts of the square. What happens if we write $43^2$ as $(20 + 23)^2$, or as $(33 + 10)^2$, instead? Draw the figures and check the area you get.
