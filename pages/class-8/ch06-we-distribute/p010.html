Using the distributive property, $(a + b)^2$ expands as

$(a + b) \times (a + b)$ $= a \times a + a \times b + b \times a + b \times b$
$= a^2 + 2ab + b^2$

which is what we found in Example 2.

Identity 1A: the square of a sum

$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$. The square of the sum of two numbers is the sum of their squares plus twice their product.

Here is $(6x + 5)^2$ expanded both ways. On the first three rows the distributive property does the work; on the last two the identity does it, with $a = 6x$ and $b = 5$.

distributive property $(6x + 5)^2 = (6x + 5)(6x + 5)$
$= (6x \times 6x) + (5 \times 6x) + (6x \times 5) + 5 \times 5$
$= (6x)^2 + 2(6x \times 5) + 5^2 = 36x^2 + 60x + 25$
Identity 1A $(6x + 5)^2 = (6x)^2 + 5^2 + 2 \times (6x \times 5)$
$= 36x^2 + 25 + 60x$
