We Distribute, Yet Things Multiply

With letters, we can write a rule about numbers once and use it for every number. This chapter is about one such rule, which joins multiplication and addition. It is called distributivity. We use it to see how a product changes, to multiply quickly, and to explain number patterns and areas.

6.1Some Properties of Multiplication

6.1.1 How a product changes

Take the product of two numbers, say $24 \times 35$.

?
Think and Reflect
  1. By how much does the product go up if $24$ is increased by $1$?
  2. By how much does it go up if $35$ is increased by $1$?
  3. By how much does it go up if both numbers are increased by $1$?

Is there a rule that gives the answer for any two numbers?

Start with the simplest case: $35$ goes up by $1$ and $24$ stays. The product $24 \times 36$ is $36$ groups of $24$, which is one group more than $24 \times 35$. So the product goes up by $24$. The same answer comes from a property of multiplication that you have used many times. For any three numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$,

$$a(b + c) = ab + ac$$

Multiplying $a$ by the sum $b + c$ gives the same result as multiplying $a$ by $b$ and by $c$ and then adding the two products. A picture of dots shows why.
