The product of two numbers is $391$. When each number is increased by $1$, the product becomes $432$. What is the sum of the two numbers?

This is the question of Section 6.1.1 turned round. There the numbers were known and the increase was asked for; here the increase is known. Increasing both numbers by $1$ adds $a + b + 1$ to the product.

the increase $(a + 1)(b + 1) - ab = a + b + 1$
here $a + b + 1 = 432 - 391 = 41$
so $a + b = 40$

The two numbers are in fact $17$ and $23$, and $18 \times 24 = 432$ agrees. The sum came out without either number being found.

Simplify $(2x + 3)^2 - (2x - 3)^2$ without expanding either square.

Two squares are being subtracted, so take $a = 2x + 3$ and $b = 2x - 3$ and use $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)$, which is Identity 1C.

$a + b$ $= (2x + 3) + (2x - 3) = 4x$
$a - b$ $= (2x + 3) - (2x - 3) = 6$
product $= 4x \times 6 = 24x$

Expanding both squares gives $4x^2 + 12x + 9 - (4x^2 - 12x + 9)$, which also comes to $24x$, after six terms have been written and four of them have cancelled. The identity did the cancelling in advance.

Two numbers $a$ and $b$ have $a^2 + b^2 = 20$ and $ab = 8$. Find $a^4 + b^4$, again without finding $a$ or $b$.

The expression asked for has fourth powers, and the one given has squares, so square what you are given. Since $a^4 = (a^2)^2$, Identity 1A applies with $a^2$ and $b^2$ in the places of $a$ and $b$, and its middle term needs only the product.

square it $(a^2 + b^2)^2 = a^4 + 2a^2b^2 + b^4 = 400$
middle term $2a^2b^2 = 2(ab)^2 = 2 \times 64 = 128$
so $a^4 + b^4 = 400 - 128 = 272$
