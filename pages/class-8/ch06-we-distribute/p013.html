$6 \times 6 - 2 \times 2 = 8 \times 4$

$9 \times 9 - 5 \times 5 = 14 \times 4$

$11 \times 11 - 3 \times 3 = 14 \times 8$

$7 \times 7 - 4 \times 4 = 11 \times 3$

In each line, the numbers on the right are the sum and the difference of the numbers on the left. So the pattern seems to be $a^2 - b^2 = (a + b) \times (a - b)$. Is this an identity? Using the distributive property,

$(a + b) \times (a - b) = a^2 - ab + ba - b^2$

The like terms $-ab$ and $ba$ add to $0$, so the pattern always holds.

Identity 1C: a sum times a difference

$(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$. The sum of two numbers times their difference is the difference of their squares.

Use Identity 1C to work out $97 \times 103$ and $36 \times 44$. Each pair of numbers is the same distance either side of a round number.
