Identity 1B: the square of a difference

$(a - b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab$. The square of the difference of two numbers is the sum of their squares minus twice their product.

6.2.2 Investigating patterns

Pattern 1. Look at the following pattern.

$2(2^2 + 1^2) = 3^2 + 1^2$

$2(3^2 + 1^2) = 4^2 + 2^2$

$2(6^2 + 5^2) = 11^2 + 1^2$

$2(5^2 + 3^2) = 8^2 + 2^2$

Take a pair of natural numbers and work out the sum of their squares. Can you write twice this sum as a sum of two squares? Try it with other pairs. Have you found the pattern? Notice that $2(5^2 + 6^2) = (6 + 5)^2 + (6 - 5)^2$.

Do the two identities below help to explain the pattern?

$(a + b)^2$ $= a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
$(a - b)^2$ $= a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
$(a + b)^2 + (a - b)^2$ $= (a^2 + 2ab + b^2) + (a^2 - 2ab + b^2)$

Add the like terms: $a^2 + a^2 = 2a^2$, $b^2 + b^2 = 2b^2$, and $2ab - 2ab = 0$. We get

$2(a^2 + b^2) = (a + b)^2 + (a - b)^2$

Pattern 2. Here is a related pattern. Describe it with algebra, and use the algebra to find out whether the pattern always holds.

$9 \times 9 - 1 \times 1 = 10 \times 8$

$8 \times 8 - 6 \times 6 = 14 \times 2$

$7 \times 7 - 2 \times 2 = 9 \times 5$

$10 \times 10 - 4 \times 4 = 14 \times 6$
