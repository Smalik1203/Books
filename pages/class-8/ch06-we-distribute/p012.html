Identity 1B: the square of a difference

$(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$. The square of the difference of two numbers is the sum of their squares minus twice their product.

6.2.2 Investigating patterns

Pattern 1. Look at these four lines.

$2(3^2 + 1^2) = 4^2 + 2^2$

$2(4^2 + 3^2) = 7^2 + 1^2$

$2(7^2 + 2^2) = 9^2 + 5^2$

$2(6^2 + 4^2) = 10^2 + 2^2$

Take two natural numbers and add their squares. Can you write twice this sum as a sum of two squares? Try it with other pairs. Have you found the pattern? Notice, for example, that $2(8^2 + 3^2) = (8 + 3)^2 + (8 - 3)^2$, since $146 = 121 + 25$.

Do Identities 1A and 1B explain the pattern? Add them:

$(a + b)^2 + (a - b)^2$ $= (a^2 + 2ab + b^2) + (a^2 - 2ab + b^2)$
$= 2a^2 + 2b^2$ $2ab - 2ab = 0$

So $2(a^2 + b^2) = (a + b)^2 + (a - b)^2$ for all numbers $a$ and $b$, which is the pattern.
