Summary
  1. The distributive property says that $a(b + c) = ab + ac$ for all numbers $a$, $b$ and $c$. A block of dots split into two parts shows why: nothing is added and nothing is lost.
  2. When one number of a product $ab$ goes up by $1$, the product goes up by the other number. When both go up by $1$, it goes up by $a + b + 1$. For example, $25 \times 36$ is $24 + 35 + 1 = 60$ more than $24 \times 35$.
  3. An identity is an equality of two expressions that is true for every value of its variables. Only like terms, with the same variables raised to the same powers, can be added into one term.
  4. To multiply two brackets, multiply every term of one by every term of the other and add the products: $(a + m)(b + n) = ab + mb + an + mn$. This is Identity 1. A subtraction is the addition of a negative number, so it covers decreases too.
  5. The distributive property gives quick ways of multiplying by $11$, $101$, $1001$, $99$ and $999$. For example, $4786 \times 11 = 47860 + 4786 = 52646$.
  6. Three special cases are used often: $(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$, $\;(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$ and $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$. Each can be shown with the areas of squares and rectangles.
  7. The identities make some calculations easy: $47^2 = (50 - 3)^2 = 2209$ and $97 \times 103 = 100^2 - 3^2 = 9991$, with no long multiplication.
  8. Putting a small number in place of each letter is a quick test of an expansion. If the two sides differ, there is a mistake.
  9. A pattern or an area can often be counted in several ways. The expressions may look different, but when they are simplified correctly they are equal.
