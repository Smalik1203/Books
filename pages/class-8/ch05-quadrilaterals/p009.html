That is a friendlier formula than the interior one, and most questions about regular polygons are quicker through it. A regular hexagon turns $60^\circ$ at each corner, so its interior angles are $180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$ — the same answer as before, reached by dividing $360$ rather than by dividing $720$.

Example 3

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $24^\circ$. How many sides has it, and what is each interior angle?

the turns $24n = 360$,   so $n = 15$
interior $180^\circ - 24^\circ = 156^\circ$

Checking the long way: $(15-2) \times 180 = 2340$, and $2340 \div 15 = 156$. The same answer, three times the work.

The formula also settles which polygons cannot exist, and it settles them quickly, because $n$ has to be a whole number.

Example 4

Is there a regular polygon whose exterior angle is $25^\circ$?

If there were, then $25n = 360$, so $n = 14.4$ — and a polygon cannot have $14.4$ sides. There is no such figure.

The exterior angle of a regular polygon must therefore divide $360^\circ$ exactly. That is a real restriction: of the ninety whole numbers from $1^\circ$ to $90^\circ$, only twenty-one of them do.
