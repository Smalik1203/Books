13 $2 + 3 + 3 + 4 = 12$ parts share $360^\circ$, so one part is $30^\circ$ and the smallest angle is $2 \times 30 = 60$. (b) is one part, not two; (d) is the largest angle.
15 $7 \times 360 = 2520$, and $2520 \div 180 = 14$, so $n - 2 = 14$ and $n = 16$. (a) stops at $n - 2$.
16 $360 \div 70$ is not a whole number, so A is false; R is the rule that shows it. So the answer is (d).
17 Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, so A is true. Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$ and are equal only in a rectangle, so R is false. So the answer is (c).

Two sums, and knowing which to reach for is most of the skill. The interior sum grows with the number of sides and answers questions about totals. The exterior sum is $360^\circ$ for every polygon ever drawn, and answers questions about a single angle — and about whether a figure can exist, since $360$ divided by a whole number has to come out whole.

The other thing to carry is the diagonal. It proved the angle sum, it proved every property of the parallelogram, and it is what a builder nails across a gate. Three lengths settle a triangle and four do not settle a quadrilateral, and almost everything in this chapter is that one difference, looked at from a different side each time.
