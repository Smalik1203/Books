5.6What a Parallelogram Guarantees

A parallelogram is defined by one thing only: both pairs of opposite sides are parallel. Nothing is said about lengths, nothing about angles. And yet three other properties follow, and they follow from the same diagonal that gave the angle sum.

Draw parallelogram $ABCD$ with the diagonal $AC$. Because $AB$ is parallel to $DC$, the diagonal crosses two parallel lines and the alternate angles it makes are equal; the same is true again because $AD$ is parallel to $BC$.

So triangles $ABC$ and $CDA$ have two pairs of equal angles and the side $AC$ between them in common. That makes them congruent — the same triangle, drawn twice the other way up — and everything in one matches something in the other.

In every parallelogram

Opposite sides are equal. Opposite angles are equal. Any two angles next to each other add to $180^\circ$. And the diagonals cut each other in half.

Take the four results one at a time, because each is worth a sentence of its own.

Opposite sides are equal. In the congruent triangles, $AB$ matches $CD$ and $BC$ matches $DA$. Notice how much this gets for nothing: the definition said only parallel, and equal lengths came out of it.

Opposite angles are equal. $\angle B$ and $\angle D$ are whole angles of the two triangles and match directly. $\angle A$ and $\angle C$ are each in two pieces, and the pieces match in pairs, so the wholes are equal too.
