Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$. $AD$ and $BC$ are parallel and $AB$ crosses them, so $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are interior angles on the same side and add to a straight angle. It follows from the other results as well: if opposite angles are equal then the four angles are $x, y, x, y$, and since they add to $360^\circ$, $x + y$ must be $180^\circ$.

The diagonals bisect each other. Draw both diagonals and let them meet at $O$. In triangles $AOB$ and $COD$ the sides $AB$ and $CD$ are equal — that is the first result — and the alternate angles at $A$ and $C$, and at $B$ and $D$, are equal because $AB$ is parallel to $CD$. So those two triangles are congruent, which makes $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$.

Example 5

In parallelogram $PQRS$, $\angle P = 65^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle R = \angle P = 65^\circ$ opposite angles
Step 2 $\angle Q = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$ adjacent angles
Step 3 $\angle S = \angle Q = 115^\circ$ opposite again
Step 4 $65 + 115 + 65 + 115 = 360$ check
Answer $\angle R = 65^\circ$, $\angle Q = 115^\circ$, $\angle S = 115^\circ$

One angle of a parallelogram fixes all four, which is worth remembering — it is why a parallelogram question rarely gives you more than one.

Questions about sides work the same way, and the algebra of the last chapter does the rest.

It is worth naming the pattern, because every one of these questions has the same shape. A property of the figure gives an equation; the equation gives the unknown; and the check at the end is another property of the same figure, used a second time. Where a question gives you two properties, use one to solve and keep the other to check.
