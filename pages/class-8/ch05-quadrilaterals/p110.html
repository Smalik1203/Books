Why the other options are wrong

A1 $(6-2) \times 180 = 720$. Option (a) is the pentagon's total and (d) the octagon's; (b) comes from multiplying by the number of sides rather than by two fewer.
A3 The exterior angles share $360^\circ$ between ten vertices. Option (b) divides $300$ by $10$; (d) is the interior angle of a regular pentagon, which belongs to another question.
A5 A kite's equal sides are adjacent, not opposite, so its opposite sides need not be parallel. The other three are all parallelograms with something added.
A6 $540 \div 5 = 108$. Option (c) is the exterior angle, and taking one for the other is the commonest slip in this set.
A10 The exterior angles of every polygon add to $360^\circ$, whatever the number of sides. Options (a) and (c) are interior sums, offered because the number $15$ invites a calculation that is not needed.
B1 An interior angle of $144^\circ$ leaves an exterior angle of $36^\circ$, and $360 \div 36 = 10$. Option (b) comes from dividing $144$ into $1080$, which is the octagon's total and not this polygon's.
B3 $\dfrac{n(n-3)}{2} = 27$ gives $n(n-3) = 54$, and $9 \times 6$ is the only pair three apart that makes $54$. Option (d) forgets the halving.
B4 The half-diagonals are $3$ and $4$, so each side is $5$ and the perimeter is $20$. Option (c) adds the two diagonals instead; (d) takes the diagonals themselves for sides.
B5 Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$, so $3\angle B + \angle B = 180$ and $\angle B = 45$. Option (a) answers with $\angle B$ rather than with the angle asked for.
