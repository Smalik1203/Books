Example 17

In rhombus $ABCD$ the diagonals meet at $O$, and $\angle ABC = 70^\circ$. Find $\angle BAC$ and $\angle ABO$.

Solution.

Step 1 $AB = BC$ all sides equal
Step 2 $\angle BAC = (180 - 70) \div 2 = 55$ isosceles triangle $ABC$
Step 3 $\angle AOB = 90^\circ$ diagonals cross at right angles
Step 4 $\angle ABO = 180 - 90 - 55 = 35$ triangle $AOB$
Answer $\angle BAC = 55^\circ$ and $\angle ABO = 35^\circ$

So the diagonal $BD$ cuts $\angle ABC$ exactly in half.

Example 18

A quadrilateral frame $ABCD$ has $AB = 5$ cm, $BC = 6$ cm, $CD = 4$ cm and $DA = 7$ cm. Could a brace along $BD$ be (a) $9$ cm (b) $11$ cm (c) $2$ cm long?

Solution.

Step 1 $BD < 5 + 7 = 12$ and $7 < 5 + BD$, so $BD > 2$ triangle $ABD$
Step 2 $BD < 6 + 4 = 10$ and $6 < 4 + BD$, so $BD > 2$ triangle $BCD$
Step 3 $BD$ is more than $2$ cm and less than $10$ cm both at once
Answer Only (a), $9$ cm. An $11$ cm brace is too long for triangle $BCD$, and with $2$ cm triangle $ABD$ lies flat
Example 19

A frame of five rods is pinned into a pentagon. Braces are nailed in along diagonals, all from one corner, until the frame cannot move. How many braces are needed, and how many triangles do they make?

Solution.

Step 1 one corner reaches $5 - 3 = 2$ others by a diagonal
Step 2 two diagonals cut the pentagon into $5 - 2 = 3$ triangles the fan of § 5.3.2
Step 3 each triangle is rigid, so the frame is too
Answer 2 braces, making 3 triangles

With one brace fewer, part of the frame is still a quadrilateral, and that part can lean. An $n$-sided frame needs $n - 3$ braces this way.
