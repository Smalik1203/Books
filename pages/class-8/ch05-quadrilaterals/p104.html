The diagonal that still works

A concave quadrilateral has one vertex whose angle is more than $180^\circ$. The diagonal joining the other two vertices falls outside the figure, and the proof cannot use it. But a quadrilateral has two diagonals, and the other one — the one from the reflex vertex to the vertex opposite — lies inside.

Use that one. It cuts the figure into two triangles exactly as before, the reflex angle is divided into two pieces by it, and the angles of the two triangles are once again precisely the four angles of the quadrilateral.

two triangles $180^\circ + 180^\circ = 360^\circ$

Nothing else changes. What the concave case shows is that the proof needed a diagonal inside the figure, not any particular one — and every quadrilateral, concave or not, has at least one.

Bisecting diagonals, read backwards

Let the diagonals of $ABCD$ meet at $O$ with $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$. Compare triangles $AOB$ and $COD$.

sides $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$, given
angle between $\angle AOB = \angle COD$, vertically opposite
so the triangles are congruent

That gives $\angle OAB = \angle OCD$ — alternate angles equal, so $AB$ is parallel to $CD$. The same argument on triangles $BOC$ and $DOA$ makes $BC$ parallel to $AD$, and the figure is a parallelogram.
