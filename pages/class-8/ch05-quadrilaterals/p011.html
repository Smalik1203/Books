Think and Reflect

Which of these are true, and which are true only sometimes? A rhombus is a kite. A kite is a rhombus. A rectangle is a square. A square is a rhombus.

For each one that is only sometimes true, draw the figure that shows it failing.

The point of an exercise like that is not the six answers. It is that sometimes true and always true are different claims about a figure, and that settling which one you have needs a single counter-example in one direction and a proof in the other.

Exercise Set 5.4
  1. Say whether each statement is always, sometimes or never true.
    1. A square is a rectangle.
    2. A rectangle is a square.
    3. A parallelogram is a trapezium.
    4. A kite is a parallelogram.
  1. Name the quadrilateral described.
    1. All four sides equal, all four angles right.
    2. Both pairs of opposite sides parallel, no angle right.
    3. Exactly one pair of sides parallel.
    4. Two pairs of adjacent sides equal, opposite sides not parallel.
  1. A quadrilateral has all four sides equal. Must it be a square? Draw a figure to support your answer.
  1. A quadrilateral has all four angles equal. Must it be a square? Draw a figure to support your answer.
  1. What is the smallest number of conditions you must add to quadrilateral to reach square? List them.
  1. Every rhombus is a kite, but not every kite is a rhombus. Say in one sentence what a kite has to gain to become one.
  1. In trapezium $ABCD$ the side $AB$ is parallel to $DC$. Explain why $\angle A$ and $\angle D$ must add to $180^\circ$, and find $\angle D$ when $\angle A = 72^\circ$.
  1. Somebody defines a trapezium as a quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides. Under that definition, is a parallelogram still a trapezium? Say which fact in Fig. 5.6 that definition would change.
