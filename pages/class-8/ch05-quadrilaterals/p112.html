Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): A regular polygon can have an exterior angle of $70^\circ$.

    Reason (R): The exterior angle of a regular polygon must divide $360^\circ$ exactly.

  1. Assertion (A): In parallelogram $PQRS$, if $\angle P = 100^\circ$ then $\angle R = 100^\circ$.

    Reason (R): Adjacent angles of a parallelogram are equal.

  1. Assertion (A): The interior angles of a pentagon add to $540^\circ$.

    Reason (R): A pentagon has five diagonals.

  1. Assertion (A): Rods of $2$, $3$, $4$ and $9$ cm cannot be pinned into a quadrilateral.

    Reason (R): In a quadrilateral, each side is shorter than the other three sides together.

Very short answer
  1. How many diagonals can be drawn from one vertex of a $15$-gon, and into how many triangles do they cut it?
  1. Find the sum of the interior angles of a $22$-gon.
  1. In rhombus $ABCD$, $\angle A = 64^\circ$. Find $\angle B$ and $\angle C$.
Short answer
  1. The interior angles of a polygon add to $3060^\circ$. How many sides has it?
  1. Is there a regular polygon each of whose interior angles is $171^\circ$? If there is, how many sides has it?
  1. In parallelogram $ABCD$, $AB = (2x + 3)$ cm and $BC = (x + 4)$ cm, and the perimeter is $38$ cm. Find $x$ and the lengths of $AB$ and $BC$.
  1. The diagonals of rectangle $PQRS$ meet at $O$. $PR = (3y - 2)$ cm and $QS = (2y + 5)$ cm. Find $y$ and the length of $OQ$.
Long answer
  1. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is $160^\circ$ more than each exterior angle. Find both angles, the number of sides, and the sum of the interior angles.
  1. The diagonals of rhombus $ABCD$ are $AC = 30$ cm and $BD = 16$ cm, and they meet at $O$. Find $AO$, $BO$, the length of a side and the perimeter. Explain why the four triangles round $O$ are all the same.
