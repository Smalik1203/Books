Which shapes fill a floor

Copies meeting at a point fill it when a whole number of interior angles come to $360^\circ$ — so the interior angle must divide $360^\circ$ exactly. Run through the regular polygons and read off the angles.

triangle $60^\circ$, six of them
square $90^\circ$, four of them
pentagon $108^\circ$ — does not divide $360$
hexagon $120^\circ$, three of them

Past the hexagon the interior angle is bigger than $120^\circ$, so only two copies would fit and two are never enough to close a point. The list therefore stops at three: triangle, square, hexagon. That is why floor tiles and honeycombs look the way they do.

For a mixed pair, the angles need only add to $360^\circ$ between them. A regular octagon has $135^\circ$, and two octagons with one square give $135 + 135 + 90 = 360$ — the pattern on a great many bathroom floors.

When the angle is a whole number

Each interior angle is $180^\circ - \dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. The $180$ is already whole, so the whole question is when $\dfrac{360}{n}$ is — that is, when $n$ divides $360$.

$360$ $= 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$
divisors $4 \times 3 \times 2 = 24$ of them
drop $n = 1, 2$ $22$ values

The count of divisors comes from Chapter 2: each prime can appear from none of it up to all of it, which is $3+1$ choices for the $2$s, $2+1$ for the $3$s and $1+1$ for the $5$. Only $n = 1$ and $n = 2$ have to go, since no polygon has fewer than three sides.
