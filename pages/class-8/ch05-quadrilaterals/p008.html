5.4Turning the Corners

Extend one side of a polygon past a vertex. The angle between that extension and the next side is an exterior angle at that vertex. It is what is left of a straight angle after the interior angle has taken its share, so the two always add to $180^\circ$.

$$\text{interior} + \text{exterior} = 180^\circ \text{ at every vertex}$$

Now the surprise. Walk round the outside of the polygon, keeping to the sides. At each corner you turn, and the amount you turn by is exactly the exterior angle there. When you arrive back where you started you are facing the way you first faced, so the turns must add up to one whole revolution.

$$\text{the exterior angles of any polygon add to } 360^\circ$$

Read that once more, because it is easy to let it past. The interior sum grows without limit — a decagon's is $1440^\circ$ and a hundred-gon's is $17640^\circ$ — while the exterior sum is $360^\circ$ for every polygon there has ever been. Adding a side adds a corner to turn at, but it also makes each turn smaller, and the two changes cancel exactly.

In a regular polygon all the turns are equal, so each one is a fair share of the revolution.

$$\text{each exterior angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}$$
