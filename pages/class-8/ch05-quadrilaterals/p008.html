$$\text{the exterior angles of any polygon add to } 360^\circ$$

Read that once more. The interior sum grows without limit — a decagon's is $1440^\circ$ — while the exterior sum is $360^\circ$ for every polygon there has ever been. Adding a side adds a corner to turn at and makes each turn smaller, and the two changes cancel exactly.

In a regular polygon all the turns are equal, so each one is a fair share of the revolution.

$$\text{each exterior angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}$$

That is a friendlier formula than the interior one, and most questions about regular polygons are quicker through it. A regular hexagon turns $60^\circ$ at each corner, so its interior angles are $180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ$ — the same answer as before, reached by dividing $360$ rather than by dividing $720$.

Example 3

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is $24^\circ$. How many sides has it, and what is each interior angle?

Solution.

Step 1 $24n = 360$ the turns make a revolution
Step 2 $n = 15$ divide by 24
Step 3 $180^\circ - 24^\circ = 156^\circ$ interior plus exterior
Answer $15$ sides; each interior angle is $156^\circ$

Checking the long way: $(15-2) \times 180 = 2340$, and $2340 \div 15 = 156$. The same answer, three times the work.

The formula also settles which polygons cannot exist, and it settles them quickly, because $n$ has to be a whole number.
