1. A parallelogram has an angle of $90^\circ$. Show that the other three must be right angles too, and say what the figure is called.

5.7Three Special Parallelograms

A rhombus, a rectangle and a square are parallelograms, so all four results of the last section hold in each of them without further argument. What is worth knowing is what each gains on top, and in every case the extra property is about the diagonals.

In a rhombus the diagonals meet at right angles. All four sides are equal, so each diagonal has, on either side of it, two sides of the same length — and a point equidistant from the two ends of a segment lies on its perpendicular bisector. Each diagonal therefore bisects the other at $90^\circ$.

In a rectangle the diagonals are equal. Triangles $ABC$ and $BAD$ have $AB$ in common, $BC = AD$ because opposite sides are equal, and right angles at $B$ and at $A$. They are congruent, so $AC = BD$.

In a square the diagonals are equal and meet at right angles, because a square is a rhombus and a rectangle at once and takes both inheritances. It is also true — and follows at once — that the diagonals of a square cut its corner angles in half, turning it into four small right-angled triangles that are all the same.

Here is the whole of it in one place. Read it downwards: nothing in a lower row cancels anything above it.
