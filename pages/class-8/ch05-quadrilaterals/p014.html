Adjacent angles add to $180^\circ$. $AD$ and $BC$ are parallel and $AB$ crosses them, so $\angle A$ and $\angle B$ are co-interior and add to a straight angle. It follows from the other results as well: if opposite angles are equal then the four angles are $x, y, x, y$, and since they add to $360^\circ$, $x + y$ must be $180^\circ$.

The diagonals bisect each other. Draw both diagonals and let them meet at $O$. In triangles $AOB$ and $COD$ the sides $AB$ and $CD$ are equal — that is the first result — and the alternate angles at $A$ and $C$, and at $B$ and $D$, are equal because $AB$ is parallel to $CD$. So those two triangles are congruent, which makes $AO = OC$ and $BO = OD$.

Example 5

In parallelogram $PQRS$, $\angle P = 65^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

opposite $\angle R = \angle P = 65^\circ$
adjacent $\angle Q = 180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ$
opposite again $\angle S = \angle Q = 115^\circ$

Check: $65 + 115 + 65 + 115 = 360$. One angle of a parallelogram fixes all four, which is worth remembering — it is why a parallelogram question rarely gives you more than one.

Questions about sides work the same way, and the algebra of the last chapter does the rest.
