Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

Four facts came out of this chapter and nothing below needs a fifth. The interior angles of an $n$-gon add to $(n-2) \times 180^\circ$. The exterior angles add to $360^\circ$ whatever $n$ is. A diagonal turns a quadrilateral into two triangles. And the quadrilateral names are conditions stacked on one another.

What is different here is that several of the questions ask whether something is possible rather than what its value is, and a question of that kind is never answered by a measurement. Try each one before turning the page.

Find the interior angle sum of a $20$-gon using only the fact that the exterior angles add to $360^\circ$ — that is, without the $(n-2) \times 180^\circ$ formula.

Is there a polygon whose interior angles add to exactly the same total as its exterior angles? If so, which one; if not, say why not.

You have four rods, of lengths $3$, $4$, $5$ and $20$ centimetres. Show that they cannot be pinned into a quadrilateral, and state the rule that four lengths must satisfy before they can.

A quadrilateral has three right angles. Must the fourth be one too? A pentagon has four right angles. Must the fifth be one?

The angle-sum proof used a diagonal that lay inside the figure. In a concave quadrilateral one diagonal falls outside. Show that the sum is still $360^\circ$.
