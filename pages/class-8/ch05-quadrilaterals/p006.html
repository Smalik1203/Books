How many triangles? One for each side of the polygon except the two that meet at the chosen vertex, so $n - 2$ of them. Each carries $180^\circ$.

$$\text{angle sum of an } n\text{-gon} = (n-2) \times 180^\circ$$

Check it against what you already know. A triangle gives $(3-2) \times 180^\circ = 180^\circ$, which is the fact we started from; a quadrilateral gives $2 \times 180^\circ = 360^\circ$, which is the last section.

triangle $180^\circ$heptagon $900^\circ$
quadrilateral $360^\circ$octagon $1080^\circ$
pentagon $540^\circ$nonagon $1260^\circ$
hexagon $720^\circ$decagon $1440^\circ$

Every entry is $180^\circ$ more than the one above it, which is the formula seen from another side: adding a side adds a triangle.

Example 1

Three angles of a quadrilateral are $85^\circ$, $110^\circ$ and $47^\circ$. Find the fourth.

the four add to $360^\circ$
three of them $85 + 110 + 47 = 242$
fourth $360^\circ - 242^\circ = 118^\circ$

The formula runs backwards just as readily. If you are told the angle sum, you can find the number of sides, because $(n-2) \times 180^\circ$ has only one $n$ that fits.

Example 2

The interior angles of a polygon add to $1440^\circ$. How many sides has it?

set up $(n-2) \times 180 = 1440$
divide $n - 2 = 8$
so $n = 10$, a decagon

The division must come out exactly. An angle sum of $1500^\circ$ would give $n - 2 = 8\tfrac13$, and no polygon has $10\tfrac13$ sides — so a figure with that angle sum does not exist, and saying so is the whole answer.
