1.2Square Numbers

The name is older than the notation, and it comes from where you would expect. A square whose side is $5$ units holds $5 \times 5 = 25$ unit squares, so its area is $25$ square units — and $25$ is called a square because a square of side $5$ is what it measures.

Writing $5 \times 5$ every time is slow, so we shorten it. The small raised $2$ says how many copies of the number are multiplied together:

$5 \times 5 = 5^2 = 25$

It is read five squared, and in general $n \times n = n^2$ for any number $n$. The squares of the natural numbers, $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \ldots$, are called the perfect squares. Here are the first twenty. Learn them by sight, as you know the multiplication tables.
