One more thing can be read off the same corridor. A locker touched exactly twice has exactly two factors — itself and $1$ — and a number with exactly two factors is a prime. So the lockers touched twice are $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \ldots$, and you can pick them out of the hundred without testing one of them.

1.2Square Numbers

The name is older than the notation and comes from exactly where it looks like it comes from. A square whose side is $5$ units holds $5 \times 5 = 25$ unit squares, so its area is $25$ square units — and $25$ is called a square because a square of side $5$ is what it measures.

Writing $5 \times 5$ every time is tiresome, so it is shortened. The small raised $2$ says how many copies of the number are multiplied together:

$5 \times 5 = 5^2 = 25$

It is read five squared, and in general $n \times n = n^2$ for any number $n$ at all. The squares of the natural numbers — $1, 4, 9, 16, 25, \ldots$ — are called the perfect squares, and they are the numbers this section is about.
