Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): $16\,000$ is not a perfect cube.

    Reason (R): A perfect cube ends in a multiple of three zeros.

  1. Assertion (A): $2^6 \times 3^3$ is a perfect cube.

    Reason (R): In a perfect cube, every prime appears a multiple of three times.

  1. Assertion (A): $7744$ could be a perfect square.

    Reason (R): Every number that ends in $4$ is a perfect square.

  1. Assertion (A): $(-4)^2 = -16$.

    Reason (R): The square of a negative number is positive.

Very short answer
  1. Write down the $12$th odd number, and the sum of the first $12$ odd numbers.
  1. Find $\sqrt[3]{-343}$.
  1. Is $7 \times 7 \times 7 \times 2 \times 2$ a perfect cube? Give the reason in one line.
Short answer
  1. Find the smallest number by which $1250$ must be multiplied to give a perfect cube, and the cube root of the product.
  1. Given that $5929$ is a perfect square, find $\sqrt{5929}$ using squares you know and the last-digit rule.
  1. A square courtyard has an area of $2025$ m². How many metres of fencing go round it?
  1. Show that $2^2 \times 3^4 \times 7^2$ is a perfect square, and find its square root without multiplying it out.
Long answer
  1. Two consecutive triangular numbers add up to $400$. Find them, and say which triangular numbers they are.
  1. A wooden cube of edge $5$ cm is painted on the outside and then cut into $1$ cm cubes. How many small cubes are there? How many have no paint on them, and how many have paint on exactly one face?
  1. A rectangular field is $32$ m long and $18$ m wide. A square field has the same area. Find the side of the square field, and how much less fencing it needs than the rectangle.
