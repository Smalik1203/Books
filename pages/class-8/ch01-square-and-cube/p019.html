The next two taxicab numbers after $1729$ are $4104$ and $13\,832$. Find the two ways of writing each as a sum of two cubes.

There is less searching to do than you might expect: start from the largest cube that will fit and work down. For $4104$ the table on the previous page is enough; for $13\,832$ you will have to run it on a few terms.

The claim Ramanujan made is stronger than it sounds, because it is about every number below $1729$ as well as about $1729$ itself. Checking it is not beyond you: for each candidate, run through the cubes that will fit inside it and see whether the remainder is a cube too. Most numbers give no such pair at all, a few give exactly one, and until $1729$ none gives two. What Ramanujan had was not a faster search but a memory in which the answer was already sitting.

1.4.2 Cubes are sums of odd numbers too

Squares turned out to be the running totals of the odd numbers. Cubes are made of the odd numbers as well, but cut into blocks rather than added one at a time.

Write the odd numbers out in one long row and then chop the row into blocks — one number, then two, then three, then four, and so on. Add up each block.

$1 = 1 = 1^3$

$3 + 5 = 8 = 2^3$

$7 + 9 + 11 = 27 = 3^3$

$13 + 15 + 17 + 19 = 64 = 4^3$

$21 + 23 + 25 + 27 + 29 = 125 = 5^3$

Every odd number is used, none is used twice, and the blocks come out as the cubes in order. It is not hard to see why once you look at the middle of each block. The third block is $7, 9, 11$ and its middle number is $9 = 3^2$; the fourth averages $16 = 4^2$; the fifth has middle $25 = 5^2$. A block of $n$ numbers spaced evenly about $n^2$ adds up to $n \times n^2$, which is $n^3$.

The middle number is the key, and it is worth checking it yourself on the fourth block. Its numbers are 13, 15, 17, 19, which average to 16 — and 16 is $4^2$. Four numbers averaging $4^2$ total $4 \times 4^2 = 4^3$, and the block does indeed come to 64. Nothing about the arrangement is accidental: the blocks were cut to lengths 1, 2, 3, ... precisely so that each would land centred on the next square.
