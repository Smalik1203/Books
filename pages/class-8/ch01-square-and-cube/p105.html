The others are more ordinary, but each has one idea that does the work. The first turns on counting factors rather than listing them. The second is a subtraction of two squares in disguise. The fourth asks what a number must look like in its factorised form, which is the chapter's main move; the fifth traps two numbers between squares, and the seventh compares the two ends of a range. And the last is a search, so the question is not whether you can do it but whether you can organise it so that it ends.

One more remark, since it applies to half the list. Several of these questions are about what a number looks like once it has been taken apart into primes, and none of them can be answered by staring at the digits. If you find yourself squinting at a number hoping for inspiration, stop and factorise it instead. That is not a last resort; it is the first move.

That is the end of the eight questions. Read back over the eight and notice how few of them needed a calculation of any size. Three were settled by comparing sizes, two by counting primes, and one by choosing where to stop searching. The arithmetic in this chapter is easy. Deciding which of the chapter's few facts a question is really about is the part worth practising, and the examples and practice that follow are arranged to give you that practice rather than more sums.

Type 1 · Factors and square numbers

Example 1

In the corridor of lockers, which of the lockers numbered 81, 45, 100 and 72 end open?

Solution.

Step 1 a locker ends open when its number has an odd count of factors only squares do
Step 2 $81 = 9 \times 9$ and $100 = 10 \times 10$ are squares
Step 3 81: 1, 3, 9, 27, 81   100: 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, 50, 100 5 and 9, both odd
Step 4 45: 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45   72: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 72 6 and 12, both even
Answer Lockers 81 and 100 end open; 45 and 72 end shut
Example 2

Without listing them, say whether 196 has an odd or an even number of factors. Then list them to check.

Solution.

Step 1 $196 = 14 \times 14$ a square
Step 2 14 is its own partner, so the count is odd
Step 3 1 and 196, 2 and 98, 4 and 49, 7 and 28, then 14 alone
Answer Odd: 196 has 9 factors
