This is why $\sqrt{x^2}$ is not simply $x$. If $x = -5$ then $x^2 = 25$ and $\sqrt{25} = 5$, which is $-x$, not $x$. The square root symbol never returns a negative number.

That is what a root is. Given a number such as $576$, two questions remain: is it a perfect square at all, and if it is, what is its root? We have met two methods already and they both work.

Listing. Square $20, 21, 22, \ldots$ until you reach $576$ or pass it: $400, 441, 484, 529, 576$. It works, and it is honest, but for a six-digit number it is hopeless.

Subtracting the odd numbers. From § 1.2.2 we know $576$ is a square exactly when subtracting $1, 3, 5, \ldots$ lands on zero. For $576$ that is twenty-four subtractions. Also honest, also slow.

The third method is the one to keep, and it comes from asking what a square looks like on the inside.

Take any number and break it into primes. Now square it: every prime that appeared once now appears twice, every prime that appeared three times now appears six. Squaring doubles how many times each prime appears — so the prime factorisation of a perfect square has every prime an even number of times, and the primes can be dealt out into two identical piles.

It is worth seeing that this is a rule about the primes and not about the number's appearance. Nothing on the outside of $1024$ suggests a square: it does not look like one, and no digit of it helps. But $1024 = 2^{10}$, ten $2$s, which deal into two piles of five, so it is $32^2$, and it could not have been anything else. Meanwhile $1000$ looks tidy and is not a square at all: $2^3 \times 5^3$ leaves an odd count of each. Once you can see inside a number, its appearance stops mattering.

Example 4

Is $324$ a perfect square? If so, what is its square root?

Solution.

Step 1 $324 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3 \times 3$ divide repeatedly
Step 2 $(2 \times 2)$ and $(3 \times 3)$ and $(3 \times 3)$ every prime pairs
Step 3 $324 = (2 \times 3 \times 3) \times (2 \times 3 \times 3) = 18^2$ two identical piles
Answer Yes. $\sqrt{324} = 18$

Notice that the method hands over the root as part of the answer. Take one member from each pair and multiply: $2 \times 3 \times 3 = 18$.
