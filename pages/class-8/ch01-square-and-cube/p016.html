1.4Cubic Numbers

Everything so far has been flat. A square number counts the unit squares in a square; now stack that square into the third dimension and count the unit cubes in a cube.

A cube of edge $2$ is built from $8$ unit cubes — two layers of $2 \times 2$. A cube of edge $3$ takes $27$ — three layers of $3 \times 3$. A cube of edge $4$ takes four layers of $16$, which is $64$.

Nine to a layer and three layers is $3 \times 3 \times 3$, and that is the pattern for every edge: a cube of edge $n$ has $n$ layers of $n \times n$, which is $n \times n \times n$ unit cubes. As with squares, the writing is shortened, and the raised number again counts how many copies are multiplied:

$n \times n \times n = n^3$

It is read $n$ cubed. The cubes of the natural numbers — $1, 8, 27, 64, \ldots$ — are the perfect cubes.
