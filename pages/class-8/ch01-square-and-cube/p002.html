Now, factors normally come in pairs. If $3$ divides $12$ then so does $4$, because $3 \times 4 = 12$; each factor has a partner, and the two multiply to give the number. Drawn, a factor pair is a rectangle:

Pair the factors of $12$ off like that and none is left over: $1$ with $12$, $2$ with $6$, $3$ with $4$. Six factors, three pairs, an even count — and locker $12$ ends shut.

The only way to break the pairing is for a factor to be its own partner. That happens when the two sides of the rectangle are equal, which is to say when the rectangle is a square. Sixteen has the pair $4 \times 4$, so its factors are $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$: five of them, an odd number, and locker $16$ ends open.

So the lockers left open are exactly those whose numbers can be written as a number multiplied by itself:

$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 49,\ 64,\ 81,\ 100$

Ten lockers out of a hundred, and not a single one of them was opened and shut by chance. Which is the point: a question about a hundred lockers turned into a question about factors, and a question about factors turned into a question about rectangles.

Squares, and why they are odd out

A number multiplied by itself is a square number. The squares are the only numbers with an odd count of factors, because every factor has a partner and only a square has a factor that is its own partner.
