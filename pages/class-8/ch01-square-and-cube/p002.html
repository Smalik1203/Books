Pair the factors of $12$ off like that and none is left over: $1$ with $12$, $2$ with $6$, $3$ with $4$. Six factors, three pairs, an even count. So light $12$ ends off.

The only way to break the pairing is for a factor to be its own partner. That happens when the two sides of the rectangle are equal, which is to say when the rectangle is a square. Sixteen has the pair $4 \times 4$, so its factors are $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$: five of them, an odd number, and light $16$ ends on.

So the lights left on are exactly those whose numbers can be written as a number multiplied by itself. There are twelve of them:

$1,\ 4,\ 9,\ 16,\ 25,\ 36,\ 49,\ 64,\ 81,\ 100,\ 121,\ 144$

Twelve lights out of $150$, and not one of them is on by chance. A question about $150$ switches turned into a question about factors, and a question about factors turned into a question about rectangles.

Squares, and why they are different

A number multiplied by itself is a square number. The squares are the only numbers with an odd count of factors, because every factor has a partner and only a square has a factor that is its own partner.

One more thing can be read off the same board. A switch pressed exactly twice has exactly two factors — itself and $1$ — and a number with exactly two factors is a prime. So the switches pressed twice are $2, 3, 5, 7, 11, \ldots$, the primes.
