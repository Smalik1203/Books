1.4.2 Cubes are sums of odd numbers too

Squares turned out to be the running totals of the odd numbers. Cubes are made of the odd numbers as well, but cut into blocks rather than added one at a time.

Write the odd numbers out in one long row and then chop the row into blocks — one number, then two, then three, then four, and so on. Add up each block.

$1 = 1 = 1^3$

$3 + 5 = 8 = 2^3$

$7 + 9 + 11 = 27 = 3^3$

$13 + 15 + 17 + 19 = 64 = 4^3$

$21 + 23 + 25 + 27 + 29 = 125 = 5^3$

Every odd number is used, none is used twice, and the blocks come out as the cubes in order. To see why, look at the middle of each block. The third block is $7, 9, 11$ and its middle number is $9 = 3^2$. The fourth averages $16 = 4^2$. The fifth has middle $25 = 5^2$. A block of $n$ numbers spread evenly around $n^2$ adds up to $n \times n^2$, which is $n^3$.

Example 10

Without adding, find $73 + 75 + 77 + 79 + 81 + 83 + 85 + 87 + 89$.

Solution.

Step 1 nine odd numbers in a row, with $81 = 9^2$ in the middle
Step 2 four below $81$ and four above, so this is the ninth block
Step 3 sum $= 9 \times 81 = 9^3$
Answer $729$
