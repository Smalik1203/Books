The middle number is the key, and it is worth checking it yourself on the fourth block. Its numbers are 13, 15, 17, 19, which average to 16 — and 16 is $4^2$. Four numbers averaging $4^2$ total $4 \times 4^2 = 4^3$, and the block does indeed come to 64. Nothing about the arrangement is accidental: the blocks were cut to lengths 1, 2, 3, ... precisely so that each would land centred on the next square.

Example 10

Without adding, find the value of

$91 + 93 + 95 + 97 + 99 + 101 + 103 + 105 + 107 + 109$

Solution.

Step 1 ten consecutive odd numbers: a block of ten
Step 2 the tenth block is centred on $10^2 = 100$
Step 3 $91$ to $109$: five below $100$ and five above it is the tenth block
Step 4 sum $= 10 \times 100$
Answer $10^3 = 1000$

Step 3 is the check that these are the tenth block, and not ten odd numbers picked from somewhere else.

Put the two facts about odd numbers side by side, because together they say something neither says alone. The odd numbers added one at a time give the squares; the same odd numbers taken in blocks of $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ give the cubes. One list, read two ways, produces both of the families this chapter is about — and a coincidence like that usually turns out not to be a coincidence at all. The reason is visible in the block: a block of $n$ odd numbers centred on $n^2$ has total $n \times n^2$, and $n \times n^2$ is what a cube is.

1.5Cube Roots

If $y = x^3$ then $x$ is the cube root of $y$, written $\sqrt[3]{y}$. Since $2^3 = 8$ we have $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$, and since $10^3 = 1000$, $\sqrt[3]{1000} = 10$.

There is no $\pm$ to worry about this time. A number has exactly one cube root, because a positive number has a positive cube and a negative number has a negative one — so $\sqrt[3]{-27} = -3$, and there is no second candidate.

The test for a cube is the test for a square with one word changed. Cubing a number uses each of its primes three times, so the prime factorisation of a perfect cube has every prime a multiple of three times, and the primes can be dealt into three identical piles instead of two.
