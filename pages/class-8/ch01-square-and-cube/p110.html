Why the other options are wrong

A1 (a) is $17 \times 11$; (c) is $17 \times 2$, which is doubling rather than squaring; (d) is $17^3$.
A4 $3087$ ends in $7$, and no square does. The other three end in $4$, $6$ and $6$, which are all allowed — and all three really are squares, at $32^2$, $46^2$ and $74^2$.
A5 $29 = 2 \times 15 - 1$, so this is the first fifteen odd numbers and the total is $15^2$. (a) is $14^2$ and (c) is $16^2$ — both are what you get by miscounting the terms by one. (d) is the sum of the first $29$ natural numbers, which is a different question.
A6 $36$ is a square, so its factor count must be odd, which rules out (a), (b) and (d) before any listing. The nine are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36$.
A7 (a) is $0.3$ doubled; (c) and (d) have the decimal point in the wrong place. Two decimal places in, two decimal places out.
A10 $9 \times 9 = 81$, so a number ending in $9$ has a square ending in $1$. (d) is the trap: the last digit of the square is not the last digit of the number.
B1 $45 = 3^2 \times 5$, so the only stray is the $5$. (a) and (c) supply a $3$ that was already paired, giving $135$ and $405$ — neither a square. (d) is $3 \times 5$, which works but is not the smallest.
B2 The gap from $n^2$ to $(n+1)^2$ is $2n + 1 = 41$, but the two ends are not counted, so the answer is $2n = 40$. (c) counts one end and (d) counts both — this is the fence-post error, and it is the whole point of the question.
B4 The LCM is $120 = 2^3 \times 3 \times 5$, and making every exponent even takes a further $2 \times 3 \times 5 = 30$, giving $3600 = 60^2$. (a) is not divisible by $8$; (b) is not divisible by $12$ or $15$; (d) is a square divisible by all three, but it is $3600 \times 4$ and so not the smallest.
B5 $1024 = 2^{10}$. Ten is even, so it is a square — $32^2$. Ten is not a multiple of three, so it is not a cube. Reading the exponent settles both halves at once, with no root taken.
B6 Consecutive squares differ by the odd number $2n + 1$, here $2 \times 49 + 1 = 99$. (a) is the difference of the roots and (d) is $49^2$ itself.
