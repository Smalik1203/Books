Type 6 · Cubes and cube roots by prime factorisation

Example 14

Find the smallest number by which 2560 must be multiplied to give a perfect cube, and the cube root of the product.

Solution.

Step 1 $2560 = 2^9 \times 5$
Step 2 nine 2s make three triples; the 5 needs two more multiply by $5^2$
Step 3 $2560 \times 25 = 2^9 \times 5^3 = (2^3 \times 5)^3$
Answer Multiply by 25. $64\,000 = 40^3$, so the cube root is 40
Example 15

Find the smallest number by which 4375 must be divided to leave a perfect cube.

Solution.

Step 1 $4375 = 5^4 \times 7$
Step 2 one 5 is left over from a triple, and the 7 is alone divide both out
Step 3 $4375 \div (5 \times 7) = 4375 \div 35 = 125 = 5^3$
Answer 35
Example 16

Find $\sqrt[3]{-1331}$ and $(-0.2)^3$.

Solution.

Step 1 $11^3 = 1331$ from the table
Step 2 $(-11)^3 = -1331$ odd number of minus signs
Step 3 $(-0.2)^3 = -0.2 \times -0.2 \times -0.2 = -0.008$ three decimal places
Answer $\sqrt[3]{-1331} = -11$, $\ (-0.2)^3 = -0.008$
