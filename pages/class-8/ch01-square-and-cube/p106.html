How long a two-digit number's cube can be

The two-digit numbers run from $10$ to $99$, and cubing preserves order — a bigger number has a bigger cube — so the shortest and longest answers come from the two ends.

smallest $10^3 = 1000$ 4 digits
largest $99^3 = 970\,299$ 6 digits

So every answer has $4$, $5$ or $6$ digits, and all three lengths occur: $10^3 = 1000$ has four, $22^3 = 10\,648$ has five, $99^3 = 970\,299$ has six.

There is a shortcut hiding here that is worth naming, because § 1.5.1 leaned on it without saying so. Cubing a number multiplies its digit count by about three, so stripping the last three digits of a cube is the same as asking for the cube of the tens part. That is exactly why the digit method for cube roots works, and why it stops working above six digits — beyond that the root has three digits, and there are three digits to find rather than two.

The smallest number that is a sum of two squares twice over

The answer is $50$, and the way to find it is to organise the search so that it finishes rather than wanders.

$50$ $= 1^2 + 7^2 = 1 + 49$
$= 5^2 + 5^2 = 25 + 25$

To be sure nothing smaller works, go up through the numbers in order. For each one, run the smaller square from $1^2$ upwards, stopping when it passes half the number — beyond that point you would only be finding the same pair the other way round. Ask whether what remains is a square. Most numbers give no pair at all; $25$, $34$ and $45$ each give exactly one; $50$ is the first to give two.

That stopping rule is the whole art of it. Without it the search never ends; with it, testing all fifty numbers takes a few minutes on paper. A search you can prove has finished is worth far more than one you merely got tired of.

That is the end of the worked answers.
