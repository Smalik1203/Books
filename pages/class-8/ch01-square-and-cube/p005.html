Example 1

Which of $2032$, $2048$, $1027$ and $1089$ are certainly not perfect squares?

Read the last digit of each and check it against the six that are possible.

Solution.

Step 1 $2032$ ends in $2$ impossible
Step 2 $2048$ ends in $8$ impossible
Step 3 $1027$ ends in $7$ impossible
Step 4 $1089$ ends in $9$ possible, so look further
Answer $2032$, $2048$ and $1027$

Three are settled without a calculation. The fourth is not settled at all; as it happens $1089 = 33^2$, but the last digit could never have told us that.

Two more habits of squares are worth knowing. Both come from the same place, and both save work later.

Zeros come in pairs. Squaring $10$ gives $100$: one zero becomes two. Squaring $200$ gives $40\,000$: two zeros become four.

$10^2$$= 100$
$200^2$$= 40\,000$
$900^2$$= 810\,000$

Each trailing zero of $n$ is a factor of $10$, and squaring uses it twice — so the square has exactly twice as many. A number with three zeros at the end has a square with six. Read backwards: a perfect square can only end in an even number of zeros, so $2000$ is not a square and neither is any number ending in an odd run of them.

Squaring keeps a number odd or even. An even number is $2k$, and $(2k)^2 = 4k^2$, which is even. An odd number is $2k+1$, and its square is $4k^2 + 4k + 1$, which is odd. So a square is even exactly when the number it came from is even — $6^2 = 36$, $7^2 = 49$ — and there are no exceptions.
