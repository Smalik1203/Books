Exercise Set 1.6
  1. Say for each number whether it is a perfect square, a perfect cube, both, or neither. Give a reason rather than a calculation wherever one will do.
    1. $64$
    2. $216$
    3. $1000$
    4. $4225$
  1. A cubical box holds $1728$ unit cubes. What is the length of its edge, and what is the area of one of its faces?
  1. Find the cube roots, using the last digit and the first three digits only.
    1. $\sqrt[3]{2744}$
    2. $\sqrt[3]{9261}$
    3. $\sqrt[3]{54872}$
  1. Add $1 + 3 + 5 + \cdots + 199$ in one line, and say what makes it possible.
  1. How many perfect squares are there between $100$ and $200$? How many perfect cubes have exactly three digits? Name them all.
  1. $1024$ is $2^{10}$. Is it a perfect square? Is it a perfect cube? Answer from the ten $2$s alone, without working out a root.
  1. Find the smallest number by which $7290$ must be divided to leave a perfect cube.
  1. Which is larger, the number of perfect squares below $10\,000$ or ten times the number of perfect cubes below $10\,000$? Work both out.
  1. A square lawn of side $30$ m is to be replaced by a square lawn of twice the area. Show that its side cannot be a whole number of metres, and give the nearest whole number.
  1. Explain, in a sentence each, why the last digit of a number rules out far more squares than cubes, and why the last digit of a cube gives away more than the last digit of a square.
  1. $196$ and $2744$ have the same root — one as a square, the other as a cube. Find it, and then find another pair of numbers related in the same way.
  1. A square of side $n$ has an L of $2n + 1$ tiles added along two of its sides. Write down the area of the new square in terms of $n$, and check your answer against $n = 7$.
  1. Which of these is impossible? Give the reason in each case.
    1. A perfect square with exactly five zeros at the end.
    2. A perfect cube with exactly five zeros at the end.
    3. A number that is both a perfect square and a perfect cube.
    4. An odd perfect square whose root is even.
  1. Take differences of the list $2, 9, 28, 65, 126, 217$ until they go flat. How many rounds does it take, and what does that tell you about how the numbers were built?
