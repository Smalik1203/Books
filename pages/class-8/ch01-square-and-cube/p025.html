That plant metaphor then travelled. Sanskrit mathematics reached the Arabic world in the eighth and ninth centuries, and the translators did not invent a new word: they used jidhr, which also means the root of a plant. When Arabic mathematics reached Latin Europe some centuries later, the translators did the same again with radix. From radix come the English words root and radical, and the modern sign $\sqrt{\phantom{n}}$ is often explained as a stylised letter r for it.

Three languages and something like fifteen hundred years, and each set of translators looked at the operation and reached for the same image — the thing underground that the visible thing grew out of. When you write $\sqrt{49} = 7$ you are saying that $7$ is what $49$ grew from.
