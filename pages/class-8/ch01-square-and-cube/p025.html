Summary
  1. Factors come in pairs that multiply to give the number. Only a square number has a factor that is its own partner, so the squares are the only numbers with an odd count of factors. For example, $16$ has the five factors $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$.
  2. A number multiplied by itself is written $n^2$ and read $n$ squared. The squares of the natural numbers, $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots$, are the perfect squares.
  3. A perfect square ends in $0, 1, 4, 5, 6$ or $9$, and never in $2, 3, 7$ or $8$. It ends in an even number of zeros. An odd number has an odd square, and an even number has an even square.
  4. The first $n$ odd numbers add up to $n^2$: $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 16 = 4^2$. So there are $2n$ numbers strictly between $n^2$ and $(n+1)^2$. Two consecutive triangular numbers add up to a square: $10 + 15 = 25$.
  5. If $y = x^2$, then $x$ is a square root of $y$. The sign $\sqrt{\phantom{n}}$ always means the positive root: $\sqrt{81} = 9$, not $-9$.
  6. In the prime factorisation of a perfect square, every prime appears an even number of times. The square root takes one prime from each pair. A root that is not a whole number can be trapped between two squares you know.
  7. A number multiplied by itself three times is written $n^3$. The perfect cubes are $1, 8, 27, 64, \ldots$ A cube can end in any digit, and its last digit gives the last digit of its root. The cube of a negative number is negative.
  8. If $y = x^3$, then $x$ is the cube root of $y$, written $\sqrt[3]{y}$. In a perfect cube, every prime appears a multiple of three times: $5832 = 2^3 \times 3^6 = 18^3$.
  9. The odd numbers cut into blocks of $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ add up to the cubes: $7 + 9 + 11 = 27$. $1729 = 1^3 + 12^3 = 9^3 + 10^3$ is the smallest taxicab number.
  10. Taking differences of the squares twice gives a constant, $2$. Taking differences of the cubes three times gives a constant, $6$.
