Check both: $2n = 144 = 12^2$ and $3n = 216 = 6^3$. Notice how the answer came: not by testing numbers one at a time, but by asking what the two conditions demanded of each prime separately. Two conditions on one number are often easiest to read one prime at a time.

Both of these last two solutions did their real work before any arithmetic started. One turned a question about squares into a question about where a number sits between two others. The other turned a question about two properties into a question about how often each prime appears. In each case the arithmetic that followed was easy, which is usually a sign that the new way of looking at it was the right one.

A square sheet of cardboard must have an area of at least $2000$ cm². What is the smallest whole number of centimetres its side can be? How many whole-number sides give an area of at least $2000$ cm² but less than $3000$ cm²?

Neither part needs a root worked out, and neither could have one: $2000$ and $3000$ each end in three zeros, an odd run, so neither is a square. Trap each between two squares instead, starting from $40^2 = 1600$, $50^2 = 2500$ and $60^2 = 3600$.

$2000$ $44^2 = 1936$ is too small; $45^2 = 2025$ is enough
$3000$ $54^2 = 2916$ is below it; $55^2 = 3025$ is not
the sides $45, 46, \ldots, 54$, which is $54 - 45 + 1 = 10$ of them

The smallest side is $45$ cm, and ten whole-number sides fit the range. The question was about the whole numbers on either side of two roots, and the squares you know settled it without a single root being found.

Square five odd numbers of your choosing and divide each answer by $8$. The remainder is always $1$. Explain why it must be.

Check first, then explain. $1, 9, 25, 49, 81$ divided by $8$ leave $1, 1, 1, 1, 1$. It is not a coincidence and the reason is one line of algebra.

Every odd number can be written $2k + 1$. Square it:

$(2k+1)^2$ $= 4k^2 + 4k + 1 = 4k(k+1) + 1$

Now $k$ and $k+1$ are consecutive, so one of them is even, so $k(k+1)$ is even. Write it as $2m$. Then the square is $8m + 1$ — a multiple of $8$, plus one.
