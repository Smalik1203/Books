Example 9

A square of cloth has an area of $125$ cm². Can a square handkerchief of side $15$ cm be cut from it? What is the largest square handkerchief with a whole-number side that can be?

Solution.

Step 1 a side of $15$ cm needs $15^2 = 225$ cm², and there is only $125$ so no
Step 2 either side of $\sqrt{125}$: $11^2 = 121$ fits, $4$ cm² to spare
Step 3 $12^2 = 144$ too big
Answer No. The largest whole-number side is $11$ cm

Notice that the first part was answered by squaring rather than by taking a root.

Sometimes only the nearest whole number is wanted. To estimate $\sqrt{250}$: it lies between $\sqrt{225} = 15$ and $\sqrt{256} = 16$, and since $250$ is much closer to $256$ than to $225$, the root is a little under $16$. That is a good enough answer to a question that has no exact one — $250$ is not a perfect square, and no whole number will ever be its root.
