Exercise Set 1.6
  1. Say whether each number is a perfect square, a perfect cube, both, or neither. Give a reason rather than a calculation wherever one will do.
    1. $121$
    2. $343$
    3. $729$
    4. $1500$
  1. A cubical box holds $2197$ unit cubes. Find the length of its edge and the area of one of its faces.
  1. Find the cube roots, using the last digit and the leading digits only.
    1. $\sqrt[3]{13\,824}$
    2. $\sqrt[3]{39\,304}$
    3. $\sqrt[3]{103\,823}$
  1. How many perfect squares lie between $200$ and $300$? How many perfect cubes have exactly four digits?
  1. $4096 = 2^{12}$. Is it a perfect square? Is it a perfect cube? Answer from the twelve $2$s alone, without working out a root.
  1. Find the smallest number by which $3456$ must be divided to leave a perfect cube, and give the cube root of the result.
  1. A square garden of side $20$ m is to be replaced by a square garden with three times the area. Show that its side cannot be a whole number of metres, and give the nearest whole number.
  1. Which of these are impossible? Give the reason in each case.
    1. A perfect square with exactly seven zeros at the end.
    2. A perfect cube that ends in $8$.
    3. A perfect cube with exactly four zeros at the end.
    4. An even perfect square whose square root is odd.
  1. Take differences of the list $0, 7, 26, 63, 124, 215$ until they go flat. How many rounds does it take? What does that tell you about how the numbers were made?
  1. A cubical tank with an open top holds $343$ m³. Find the length of an edge, the area of the base, and the total area of the five faces to be painted.
