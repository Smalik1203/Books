Cut a square array of dots along its diagonal. The half that keeps the diagonal is a triangle of $1 + 2 + \cdots + n$ dots, and the other half is a triangle one row shorter. Together they hold every dot, so two consecutive triangular numbers always add to a square. Counting one set of dots in two ways is a move mathematicians use often.
