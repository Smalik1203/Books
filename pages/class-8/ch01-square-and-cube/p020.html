Example 10

Without adding, find the value of

$91 + 93 + 95 + 97 + 99 + 101 + 103 + 105 + 107 + 109$

Count them: there are ten numbers, consecutive odd, so this is a block of ten. If it is the tenth block of the pattern above, the sum is $10^3 = 1000$.

Check that it really is the tenth block rather than ten odd numbers picked from somewhere else. A block of ten is centred on $10^2 = 100$, and these ten run from $91$ to $109$ — five below $100$ and five above. It is.

sum $= 10 \times 100$ $1000$

Put the two facts about odd numbers side by side, because together they say something neither says alone. The odd numbers added one at a time give the squares; the same odd numbers taken in blocks of $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ give the cubes. One list, read two ways, produces both of the families this chapter is about — and that is the sort of coincidence that usually turns out not to be one. The reason is visible in the block: a block of $n$ odd numbers centred on $n^2$ has total $n \times n^2$, and $n \times n^2$ is what a cube is.

1.5Cube Roots

If $y = x^3$ then $x$ is the cube root of $y$, written $\sqrt[3]{y}$. Since $2^3 = 8$ we have $\sqrt[3]{8} = 2$, and since $10^3 = 1000$, $\sqrt[3]{1000} = 10$.

There is no $\pm$ to worry about this time. A number has exactly one cube root, because a positive number has a positive cube and a negative number has a negative one — so $\sqrt[3]{-27} = -3$, and there is no second candidate.

The test for a cube is the test for a square with one word changed. Cubing a number uses each of its primes three times, so the prime factorisation of a perfect cube has every prime a multiple of three times, and the primes can be dealt into three identical piles instead of two.

Both statements are the same statement with a different number in it, and it is worth pausing on why the number is the one it is. Squaring uses each prime twice, so the exponents double and every one comes out even. Cubing uses each prime three times, so the exponents treble and every one comes out a multiple of three. The test is simply that arithmetic read backwards, which is why it needs no remembering once you have seen it once.
