7.4Same Base, Same Height

Draw a straight line and mark a segment $PQ$ on it. Draw a second line parallel to the first. Choose any point on the second line and join it to $P$ and to $Q$. This makes a triangle. The three triangles in Fig. 7.3 were all made in this way.

The three triangles have different shapes, but they have the same area. The reason is the height. The height of a triangle is the distance from its top corner straight down to the base, meeting the base at a right angle. Two parallel lines are the same distance apart everywhere. So all three triangles have the same base and the same height, and that is all the area depends on. For example, if $PQ$ is $8$ cm and the lines are $5$ cm apart, each of the three triangles has an area of $\tfrac12 \times 8 \times 5 = 20$ cm2, however far it leans.
