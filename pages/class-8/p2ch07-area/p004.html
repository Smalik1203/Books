7.4Same Base, Same Height

Draw a line, mark a segment $DC$ on it, and draw a second line parallel to the first. Now pick any point on that second line and join it to $D$ and to $C$. The three triangles below were all made that way, and they have the same area.

Two parallels fix the height

Triangles on the same base, with their apexes on one line parallel to that base, all have the same area. The apex may be slid anywhere along the line — even past the end of the base — and nothing changes, because the distance between two parallel lines is the same everywhere.

That is worth having on its own, but it also settles what the area actually is. Slide the apex until it sits directly above one end of the base, and the triangle becomes half of a rectangle — which section 7.2 has already counted.

The general triangle does it in two halves at once. Draw the rectangle that just encloses the triangle, and drop the perpendicular from the apex to the base.
