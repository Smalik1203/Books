Take $BC$ as the base. The height is $6$, so the area is $\tfrac12 \times 10 \times 6 = 30$ square units. Now take $AC$ as the base. The area is still $30$, and this time it is written using $BY$.

the other way $\tfrac12 \times 7.5 \times BY = 3.75 \, BY$
so $3.75 \, BY = 30$
giving $BY = 8$ units

We have found a length without measuring it. The area was worked out from one pair, and then used with the other pair. Whenever one quantity can be written in two ways, the two expressions are equal. An unknown in one of them can then be found from the other.

Example 1

A triangle has an area of $90$ cm2. One of its sides is $20$ cm and another is $15$ cm. Find the height to each of these sides.

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac12 \times 20 \times h = 90$, so $h = 9$ cm on the 20 cm side
Step 2 $\tfrac12 \times 15 \times h = 90$, so $h = 12$ cm on the 15 cm side
Answer 9 cm to the 20 cm side, and 12 cm to the 15 cm side

The longer side has the shorter height. It must be so: base times height is fixed at twice the area, so when one goes up the other comes down. The longest side of a triangle always has the smallest height.

Equal bases and equal heights can answer questions without any measuring. Draw both diagonals of a rectangle $ABCD$. They cut it into four triangles that are not all the same shape. Are they the same size?
