7.6Cutting and Moving

A parallelogram has no unit squares neatly inside it and no diagonal that halves a known shape. What it has is a spare corner at one end and a matching notch at the other, and the whole of its area formula comes from noticing that the one fits the other.

Drop a perpendicular $AX$ from $A$ onto $DC$. That cuts a right-angled triangle $AXD$ off the left-hand end. Carry it across to the right, and the parallelogram becomes a rectangle.

That the piece fits is not something to be taken on the eye's word, and three lines settle it. $BY$ equals $AX$, because $ABYX$ is a rectangle and those are its two ends. Both triangles have a right angle at $X$ and at $Y$. And $BC$ equals $AD$, because they are opposite sides of a parallelogram. A right angle, the hypotenuse and one other side — that is the RHS test, so $\triangle AXD$ and $\triangle BYC$ are congruent, and the one lies exactly over the other.
