the two triangles $\tfrac12 \times BD \times AO \;+\; \tfrac12 \times BD \times CO$
common factor $\tfrac12 \times BD \times (AO + CO)$
and $AO + CO = AC$, giving $\tfrac12 \times BD \times AC$

Notice that the halving of the diagonals was never needed — only that $AO$ and $CO$ add up to $AC$. What was needed is the right angle at $O$, because without it $AO$ would not be the height of anything. The formula therefore holds for any quadrilateral whose diagonals cross at right angles: a kite, or a square, as well as a rhombus.

Example 3

A rhombus has diagonals $20$ cm and $15$ cm. Find its area, and then find its height if its side is $12.5$ cm.

area $\tfrac12 \times 20 \times 15 = 150$ cm2
as a parallelogram $12.5 \times h = 150$
so $h = 12$ cm

Two formulas for one shape, and the second question needed both: the diagonals gave the area, and the area then gave a height that nothing in the question had measured. The $12.5$ cm side is no accident either — a rhombus with diagonals $20$ and $15$ has half-diagonals $10$ and $7.5$, and Chapter 2's theorem makes the side $\sqrt{100 + 56.25} = 12.5$.
