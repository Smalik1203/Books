Example 2

A parallelogram $ABCD$ has $AB = 15$ cm, and the height from $D$ to $AB$ is $8$ cm. Find its area. If $AD = 10$ cm, find the height from $B$ to $AD$.

Solution.

Step 1 $15 \times 8 = 120$ cm2 on base AB
Step 2 $10 \times h = 120$ same area, on base AD
Step 3 $h = 12$ cm divide by 10
Answer Area 120 cm2; the height from $B$ to $AD$ is 12 cm

As in Example 1, the shorter side has the longer height, because the two must multiply to the same area.

This also compares a rectangle with a parallelogram. Build both on the same base, with the other pair of sides the same length in both. Their perimeters are equal. The rectangle's height is its side. The parallelogram's height is less than its side, because a sloping side is longer than the straight-up distance it covers. So, of two figures with the same four side lengths, the rectangle always has the larger area. For example, a rectangle $15$ cm by $10$ cm has an area of $150$ cm2, but the parallelogram of Example 2, with the same sides, has only $120$ cm2.

From parallelogram to rectangle

A parallelogram can be cut and moved into a rectangle with the same base and the same height. So its area is base times height, for either pair of sides.
