Summary
  1. The area of a region is the number of unit squares that fit inside it. A rectangle $6$ m by $5$ m holds $6 \times 5 = 30$ unit squares, so its area is $30$ m2.
  2. Cutting a shape into pieces and joining them in a new way changes the shape but not the area. This is called dissection, and every formula in the chapter comes from it.
  3. Perimeter and area measure different things. Rectangles with the same perimeter can have different areas, and for a fixed perimeter the square has the largest area.
  4. The area of a triangle is $\tfrac12 \times$ base $\times$ height, where the height is measured at a right angle to the base. Triangles on the same base, with their top corners on one line parallel to it, have equal areas.
  5. Any side of a triangle can be the base. Since the area is fixed, a known area gives each height: a triangle of area $90$ cm2 has a height of $9$ cm to a $20$ cm side.
  6. A parallelogram becomes a rectangle when a right-angled triangle is cut off one end and moved to the other. Its area is base $\times$ height.
  7. The area of a rhombus, or of any quadrilateral whose diagonals cross at right angles, is half the product of its diagonals.
  8. The area of a trapezium with parallel sides $a$ and $b$, a distance $h$ apart, is $\tfrac12 h(a + b)$. One cut halfway up and a half-turn make it a parallelogram.
  9. The diagonals from one corner cut a polygon with $n$ sides into $n - 2$ triangles, so its area is a sum of triangle areas.
  10. To change a unit of area, square the factor for length: $1$ m2 is $10{,}000$ cm2, $1$ in2 is $6.4516$ cm2, and $1$ ft2 is $144$ in2.
