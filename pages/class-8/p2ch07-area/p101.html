Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter had one method and several formulas. The method was to cut a shape up and lay the pieces down somewhere better, and the formulas were what that came to when it was written down. The questions below mostly ask for the method, because the formula alone will not reach them.

Two of them are not about area at all until you notice that they are. Try each before turning the page.

A triangle and a parallelogram stand on the same base, with their far sides on one line parallel to it. What is the ratio of their areas, and why does it not depend on how far the triangle leans?

A rhombus has area $240$ cm2 and one diagonal of $30$ cm. Find the other diagonal, the side, and the perpendicular height.

Gopal must fetch water. He walks from his house to a straight stretch of river, fills his pot anywhere along the bank, and carries it to a tank — house and tank both on the same side of the river. Where should he meet the bank to make the whole walk as short as possible?

A field is a quadrilateral. One of its diagonals is $24$ m, and the perpendicular distances to that diagonal from the other two corners are $13$ m and $7$ m. Find its area.

A square of side $12$ cm and a rectangle $16$ cm by $9$ cm have the same area. Which has the longer perimeter, and by how much?
