The same eight questions, worked — and the reasoning that finds them.

The eight are answered below in the order they were asked. Two of them are settled by a single sentence from section 7.4, and one of them is not about area until it has been turned round.

One base, two heights that are the same

Both figures stand on the base $b$, and both have their far sides on a line parallel to it, so both have the same perpendicular height $h$ — that is exactly what parallel lines guarantee.

the triangle $\tfrac12 \, b \, h$
the parallelogram $b \, h$

So the ratio is $1 : 2$, whatever $b$ and $h$ happen to be, and leaning the triangle over changes neither. It is the same reason both figures were indifferent to leaning in the chapter: a lean moves the apex along the parallel and the parallel is where the height is fixed.
