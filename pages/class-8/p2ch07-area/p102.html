Gopal must fetch water. He walks from his house to a straight stretch of river, fills his pot anywhere along the bank, and carries it to a tank — house and tank both on the same side of the river. Where should he meet the bank to make the whole walk as short as possible?

This is not an area question, and section 7.4 answers it anyway. Reflect the tank across the river to a point $T'$ on the far side. For any point $P$ on the bank, the stretch $PT$ and the stretch $PT'$ have exactly the same length, because reflecting a point does not change its distance from anything on the mirror line.

So the walk house $\to P \to$ tank is always the same length as house $\to P \to T'$ — and that second walk is shortest when it is straight. Draw the straight line from the house to $T'$; where it crosses the bank is where Gopal should fill his pot.

The trick was to replace a quantity by another that is always equal to it and easier to think about, which is what the whole chapter did with areas. Here it is done with a length.

A field is a quadrilateral. One of its diagonals is $24$ m, and the perpendicular distances to that diagonal from the other two corners are $13$ m and $7$ m. Find its area.

The diagonal splits the quadrilateral into two triangles, and both stand on it, so both use it as their base.

the two triangles $\tfrac12 \times 24 \times 13 \;+\; \tfrac12 \times 24 \times 7$
common factor $\tfrac12 \times 24 \times (13 + 7) = 240$ m2

The two perpendiculars were added before anything was multiplied, which is the only labour-saving in the question and the reason for taking the common factor out. Notice also what was not needed: none of the four sides, and no angle.

A square of side $12$ cm and a rectangle $16$ cm by $9$ cm have the same area. Which has the longer perimeter, and by how much?

Both hold $144$ cm2, and the perimeters are worked out separately.

the square $4 \times 12 = 48$ cm
the rectangle $2(16 + 9) = 50$ cm

The rectangle needs $2$ cm more edge to hold the same amount, which is section 7.3 again from the other side: of all rectangles with a given area, the square has the shortest way round. Stretch the shape further — $36$ by $4$, still $144$ cm2 — and the perimeter climbs to $80$ cm.
