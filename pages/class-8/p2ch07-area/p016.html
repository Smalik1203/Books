The trapezium, in one cut

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. One way to find its area is to take two copies, turn one over, and fit the pair together into a parallelogram. That needs a second trapezium. The half-turn of Fig. 7.11 does the job with only one.

Let the parallel sides be $a$ at the top and $b$ at the bottom, with height $h$ between them. Join the midpoints of the two sloping sides and cut along this line. Give the upper piece a half-turn about one end of the cut, just as the top of the triangle was turned about $N$. The side of length $a$ swings round and lies in line with the side of length $b$. The result is a parallelogram with base $a + b$ and height $\tfrac{h}{2}$, since the cut was halfway up.

$$\text{area of a trapezium} = \tfrac12 \, h \, (a + b).$$

Now look back at Fig. 7.11. A triangle is like a trapezium whose top side has shrunk to nothing, so $a = 0$. The formula then gives $\tfrac12 h(0 + b)$, which is $\tfrac12 bh$. The two arguments are really one argument: cut halfway up, and turn the top piece about a midpoint.

Example 5

A field is a trapezium. Its parallel sides are $30$ m and $20$ m, and they are $12$ m apart. Find its area. A triangular field on a base of $25$ m has the same area. Find its height.

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac12 \times 12 \times (30 + 20) = 300$ m2 the trapezium
Step 2 $\tfrac12 \times 25 \times h = 300$, so $h = 24$ m the triangle
Answer Area 300 m2; the triangle's height is 24 m

The trapezium has the same area as a rectangle $25$ m by $12$ m, because $25$ m is the average of the two parallel sides. This is what the formula says: take the average of the parallel sides, and multiply by the height.

Everything ends up a rectangle

Each area formula in this chapter is a set of instructions for cutting a shape into a rectangle. The rectangle is where the chain stops, because its area can be read straight from its two sides.
