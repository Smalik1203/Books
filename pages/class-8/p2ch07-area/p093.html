Assertion and reason

Choose (a) if A and R are both true and R is the correct explanation of A; (b) if A and R are both true but R is not the correct explanation of A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): A triangle with a base of $10$ cm and a height of $6$ cm has an area equal to $30$ cm2.

    Reason (R): The area of a triangle is $\tfrac12 \times$ base $\times$ height.

  1. Assertion (A): A rhombus with diagonals of $8$ cm and $6$ cm has an area of $24$ cm2.

    Reason (R): All four sides of a rhombus are equal.

  1. Assertion (A): $1$ m2 is equal to $100$ cm2.

    Reason (R): $1$ m is equal to $100$ cm, so a length in metres is multiplied by $100$ to give it in centimetres.

  1. Assertion (A): A rectangle $6$ cm by $2$ cm and a square of side $4$ cm have the same perimeter, $16$ cm.

    Reason (R): Two shapes with the same perimeter always have the same area, whatever their shapes are.

  1. Assertion (A): When each side of a square is doubled, its area becomes four times as large as before.

    Reason (R): The area of a square is its side multiplied by itself.
