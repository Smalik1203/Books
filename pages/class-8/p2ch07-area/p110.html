Example 9

The floor of a school hall is a trapezium. Its parallel sides are $18$ m and $12$ m long, and they are $10$ m apart. The floor is to be covered with square mats of side $50$ cm, cut where needed, with no gaps and no overlaps. The mats are sold only in packs of $25$, and a pack costs ₹$1000$. The mats are laid in rows parallel to the longer side, and the figure shows the floor.

  1. The area of the floor is
    1. 300 m2
    2. 150 m2
    3. 216 m2
    4. 180 m2
  2. Numerical answer. How many mats are needed?
  3. Numerical answer. What do the mats cost, in rupees?

Solution.

Step 1$\tfrac12 \times 10 \times (18 + 12) = 150$ m2.
Step 2One mat covers $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$ m2, so $150 \div 0.25 = 600$ mats.
Step 3$600 \div 25 = 24$ packs. There is no mat left over, because $24 \times 25 = 600$.
Step 4$24 \times 1000 = 24000$ rupees.
CheckFour mats make $1$ m2, and $150 \times 4 = 600$. Also, $150$ m2 is the area of a rectangle $15$ m by $10$ m, and $15$ is the average of the two parallel sides, $18$ and $12$.
Answer(i) (b) 150 m2; (ii) 600; (iii) 24000
