7.5One Triangle, Three Ways

A triangle has three sides, and any of them may be called the base. Each choice brings its own perpendicular height, and all three pairs must give the same answer — because the triangle has only one area. That sounds like a fact with nothing in it, and it is one of the most useful in the chapter.

Take $BC$ as the base. The perpendicular height is $3$, so the area is $\tfrac12 \times 5 \times 3 = 7.5$ square units, and that is now a fixed number. Take $AC$ as the base instead. The same area must come out, and this time it is written with $BY$ in it.

the other way $\tfrac12 \times 4 \times BY = 2 \, BY$
so $2 \, BY = 7.5$
giving $BY = 3.75$ units

A length has been found without measuring it, and without any construction beyond drawing the perpendicular. The area acted as the go-between: it was worked out from one pair and then spent on the other.

The move is worth naming because it is used again and again. Whenever a quantity can be written two ways, the two expressions may be set equal, and whatever is unknown in one of them is then found from what is known in the other. Here the quantity was an area; in the last chapter it was the top of a pyramid; in Chapter 3 it was a share of a whole. The unknown does not have to be the thing the question seems to be about.

What makes it work here is that a triangle has three base-and-height pairs and only one area. Three ways of writing one quantity give two equations for free, and a question that names five of the six numbers involved has already told you the sixth.
