Type 6 · The trapezium

Example 11

A trapezium has parallel sides of $14$ cm and $10$ cm, $6$ cm apart. Find its area, and the base and height of the parallelogram that the one-cut method turns it into.

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac12 \times 6 \times (14 + 10) = 72$ cm2 half the height times the sum
Step 2 base $14 + 10 = 24$ cm, height $6 \div 2 = 3$ cm cut halfway up, turn
Step 3 $24 \times 3 = 72$ cm2 the same area
Answer 72 cm2; a parallelogram 24 cm by 3 cm
Example 12

A field shaped like a trapezium has an area of $91$ m2. Its parallel sides are $16$ m and $10$ m. How far apart are they?

Solution.

Step 1 $\tfrac12 \times h \times (16 + 10) = 91$ the formula, $h$ unknown
Step 2 $13 \times h = 91$ half of 26
Step 3 $h = 91 \div 13 = 7$ m
Answer 7 m

Type 7 · A polygon in pieces

Example 13

A garden is a pentagon: a rectangle $14$ m by $9$ m with a triangle on top, whose base is one $14$ m side and whose apex is $6$ m above that side. Find the garden's area. Into how many triangles would diagonals from one corner cut it?

Solution.

Step 1 $14 \times 9 = 126$ m2 the rectangle
Step 2 $\tfrac12 \times 14 \times 6 = 42$ m2 the triangle
Step 3 $126 + 42 = 168$ m2 add the pieces
Step 4 $5 - 2 = 3$ $n - 2$ triangles
Answer 168 m2; three triangles

Any cut into pieces whose areas you can find will do. The cut from one corner is the one that always works.
