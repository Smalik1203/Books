Cutting a figure into pieces and rearranging them into a different figure of the same area is called dissection, and it is the method the rest of this chapter runs on.

Now read the rectangle's own measurements off the parallelogram. Its height is $AX$, which is the perpendicular height of the parallelogram. Its width is $XY$, and $XY$ is the same length as $DC$ — because $DX$ and $CY$ are the two congruent triangles' short sides and are therefore equal, so adding the stretch $XC$ to each of them gives $DC$ on one side and $XY$ on the other.

$$\text{area of a parallelogram} = \text{base} \times \text{height}.$$

Nothing in the argument singled out $DC$. Take $AD$ as the base instead, drop the perpendicular onto it from $C$, and the same cut-and-carry turns the parallelogram into a different rectangle — taller and narrower, and of the same area. Either side will do, so long as the height used is the one measured square to the side chosen.

Example 2

A parallelogram $PQRS$ has $SR = 12$ cm and $PQ = 12$ cm, and the perpendicular from $P$ onto $SR$ is $6$ cm. Find its area, and then find the perpendicular from $Q$ onto $PS$ if $PS = 7.6$ cm.

Solution.

Step 1 $12 \times 6 = 72$ cm2 on base SR
Step 2 $7.6 \times h = 72$ same area, on base PS
Step 3 $h = 72 \div 7.6 \approx 9.47$ cm divide by 7.6
Answer Area 72 cm2; the perpendicular from $Q$ is about 9.47 cm

The same trade as in Example 1: the shorter side takes the longer height, because the two must multiply to the same area. A parallelogram leaning far over has a long side and very little height on it, and that is why a lean costs area even though it costs no perimeter at all.

Which answers a question the last section raised. Build a rectangle and a parallelogram on the same base, with the other pair of sides the same length in both. The perimeters are equal. The rectangle's height is its side; the parallelogram's height is less, because a slanting side is longer than the perpendicular it leans away from. So of two figures with the same four side lengths, the rectangle is always the larger.
