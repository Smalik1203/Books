A parallelogram has sides of $13$ cm and $20$ cm, and the perpendicular height on the $20$ cm side is $12$ cm. Find the height on the $13$ cm side. Then say why your answer had to come out at less than $20$ cm.

Section 7.6 said either side may be the base, so long as the height used is the one belonging to it. Take the $20$ cm side first, where both numbers are given.

the area $20 \times 12 = 240$ cm2
the other way $13 \times h = 240$, so $h = \tfrac{240}{13} \approx 18.5$ cm

And it had to be under $20$ cm. The height on the $13$ cm side is the distance between the two $13$ cm sides, and the $20$ cm sides run from one of those to the other — so the $20$ cm side is at least as long as that distance, and longer unless the parallelogram happens to be a rectangle. A height can exceed the side it stands on, as this one does, but never the side it is measured along.

A photograph $6$ cm by $4$ cm is enlarged so that each of its sides is three times as long. How many times as much paper does the enlargement need? If the original print cost ₹$12$ and printing is charged by area, what does the enlargement cost?

The enlargement is $18$ cm by $12$ cm, so the comparison can be done twice over — by counting, or by noticing that each of the two dimensions was tripled and so the product was tripled twice.

before and after $6 \times 4 = 24$ and $18 \times 12 = 216$
the ratio $216 \div 24 = 9$, which is $3^2$
the cost $9 \times 12 = 108$, so ₹$108$

This is the same arithmetic as the square inch of section 7.10 and catches the same people. Anybody expecting to pay three times as much has multiplied the price by the scale factor rather than by its square.

Of all rectangles with an area of $36$ cm2 and whole-centimetre sides, which has the shortest perimeter and which the longest? Is there a longest at all if the sides need not be whole numbers?

Whole-number sides of area $36$ mean factor pairs, and there are five of them.

the pairs $36 \times 1$, $18 \times 2$, $12 \times 3$, $9 \times 4$, $6 \times 6$
their perimeters $74$, $40$, $30$, $26$, $24$
