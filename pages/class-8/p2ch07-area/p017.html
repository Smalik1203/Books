What this chapter established
  1. Area is a count of unit squares, and the count need not be whole. For a rectangle it is length times width, without any counting.
  2. Area does not depend on shape. Cut a region up and lay the pieces down elsewhere and the area is unchanged, because nothing was added and nothing thrown away.
  3. Perimeter is no guide to area. Two rectangles can share a perimeter and differ in area, and a rectangle with the longer perimeter can hold less.
  4. Triangles on the same base, with their apexes on a line parallel to it, all have the same area — however far the apex is slid, and even past the end of the base.
  5. A triangle is half of the rectangle that encloses it, so its area is $\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$. Where the perpendicular falls outside the base, the same formula is recovered by subtracting one right triangle from another.
  6. A triangle has three base-and-height pairs and one area, so a height that cannot be measured can be found from a pair that can.
  7. Cutting a shape up and rearranging it into another is dissection. A parallelogram becomes a rectangle by carrying a triangle from one end to the other, so its area is base times height.
  8. A rhombus fills exactly half of the rectangle built on its diagonals, so its area is $\tfrac12$ the product of the diagonals. Only the right angle between the diagonals is needed, so a kite obeys it too.
  9. The Śulbasūtras ask for shapes to be exchanged without changing area, and every shape here reaches a rectangle: a triangle becomes a parallelogram by a half-turn about a midpoint, and a parallelogram becomes a rectangle.
  10. Any polygon of $n$ sides cuts into $n - 2$ triangles from one corner, so every polygon has a findable area. And a conversion factor for lengths must be squared before it is used on areas.

The formulas in this chapter are worth remembering, but they are not what it was about. Each of them is a set of cutting instructions written down as arithmetic, and a reader who has forgotten one can get it back with a pair of scissors.
