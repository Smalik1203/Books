7.9Every Polygon Is Triangles

A real field is seldom a rectangle and almost never a rhombus. It often has five or six corners and no special name. There is no formula for such a shape, but none is needed. The shape can be cut into triangles, and we already know how to find the area of each triangle.

Section 7.5 can find the area of every triangle, once a base and its height are measured. So the area of every polygon is a sum of triangle areas. That is the whole method, and it is why this chapter spent so long on the triangle: it is the piece that all other polygons are made of. For example, if the three triangles of the pentagon in Fig. 7.12 have areas of $20$, $26$ and $18$ square units, the pentagon has an area of $20 + 26 + 18 = 64$ square units.

Polygons from triangles
  • The diagonals from one corner cut a polygon with $n$ sides into $n - 2$ triangles.
  • The area of the polygon is the sum of the areas of these triangles, each found as half of base times height.
