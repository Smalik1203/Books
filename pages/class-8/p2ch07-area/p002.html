Area is a count of unit squares

The area of a region is the number of unit squares that fit inside it, and that number need not be a whole one — a region may hold three squares and half of a fourth. For a rectangle the count comes out as a product without any counting at all:

$$\text{area of a rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}.$$

One consequence arrives immediately. A diagonal cuts a rectangle into two triangles that fit exactly on top of each other, so each of them holds half the squares. The $7$ cm by $4$ cm patch, cut corner to corner, gives two triangles of $\tfrac12 \times 7 \times 4 = 14$ cm2 apiece.

7.3Why Perimeter Is Not Area

Counting squares is slow work, and a shortcut suggests itself. The perimeter — the length all the way round the edge — can be measured with a piece of string, and a bigger region surely has a longer boundary. Why not use that?

Because it is not true. Here are three rectangles, all with whole-centimetre sides.

RectanglePerimeter (cm)Area (cm2)
$8$ by $1$$18$$8$
$5$ by $4$$18$$20$
$3$ by $3$$12$$9$

The first two settle the first question: equal perimeters, and one holds two and a half times as much as the other. The first and third settle a stronger one. The $8$ by $1$ rectangle has the longer boundary of the two — $18$ cm against $12$ — and the smaller area. So a longer edge does not even guarantee more inside it.
