So the first patch takes $28$ unit squares of powder and the second $24$, and the longer, narrower patch is the smaller of the two. Written down, the counts are $28$ cm2 and $24$ cm2 — square centimetres, because what is being counted is squares.

Area is a count of unit squares

The area of a region is the number of unit squares that fit inside it, and that number need not be a whole one — a region may hold three squares and half of a fourth. For a rectangle the count comes out as a product without any counting at all:

$$\text{area of a rectangle} = \text{length} \times \text{width}.$$

One consequence arrives immediately. A diagonal cuts a rectangle into two triangles that fit exactly on top of each other, so each of them holds half the squares. The $7$ cm by $4$ cm patch, cut corner to corner, gives two triangles of $\tfrac12 \times 7 \times 4 = 14$ cm2 apiece.
