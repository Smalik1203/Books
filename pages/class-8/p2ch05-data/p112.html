What to carry forward

An average is a total in disguise. Multiply the mean by the count and the sum comes back, and once the sum is on the page every question about a missing value, a mistaken entry or a newcomer becomes an equation with one unknown. That single move answered most of this division, and it is the reason a mean given in a question is a piece of data and not merely a conclusion.

In a table, multiply before you add. A frequency table looks like a list of six numbers and is a list of thirty answers. Each value has to be counted as often as it occurs, and the divisor is the total of the frequencies and not the number of rows. The same table then gives the median for nothing, by a running total that says what stands in each position.

Ask how many values have to absorb the change. An error repeated in every reading arrives at the mean whole; an error in one reading out of seven arrives divided by seven; a newcomer shifts the mean by its own distance from the mean divided by the new count. Knowing which of the three you are looking at is most of getting these right, and it explains why some corrections are large and some are almost invisible.

Say not settled when it is not settled. A graph offers what was measured. Between two readings it offers a line, and a line is the simplest guess rather than a record. The hardest habit in the second half of this chapter is being willing to look at a confident-looking picture and say that it does not answer the question asked of it.

The chapter put two things side by side that look unrelated — a fulcrum under a row of dots, and a line drawn through six temperatures. What they share is that each is a summary, and each is useful precisely because it has dropped something. A mean drops which value was which; a median drops how far away they were; a line graph drops everything that happened between the readings. Knowing what each has dropped is the difference between using a summary and being used by one.

Which is why so many of the questions here had answers of the form it rises, and I cannot say by how much. That is not a failure to finish the sum. It is the honest end of the reasoning, and a reader who insists on a number where the data supports only a direction has stopped reading the data and started decorating it.
