5.4The Middle Value

The median is a second kind of average. Put the values in order, from smallest to largest. The median is the value in the middle, with as many values below it as above it. When the number of values is even, there are two middle values. Then the median is the mean of those two.

Take $4$, $7$, $9$, $12$ and $15$. There are five values, and the third is $9$. Two values lie below it and two above it, so the median is $9$. Now include $13$. In order, the values are $4$, $7$, $9$, $12$, $13$ and $15$. The two middle values are $9$ and $12$, so the median is $\frac{9 + 12}{2} = 10.5$.

The median went up, and it had to. The new value, $13$, is above the old median. It adds one more value above the middle, so the middle moves up. If we had included $2$ instead, the values in order would be $2$, $4$, $7$, $9$, $12$, $15$, and the median would have gone down to $8$, the mean of $7$ and $9$.

Notice that $10.5$ is not one of the values. When the number of values is even, the median is halfway between two values, and it is one of the values only when those two are equal. Like the mean, the median need not be a member of the set.

For a long list it helps to know where the middle is. With $n$ values in order, when $n$ is odd, the median is the value in place $\frac{n + 1}{2}$: for $25$ values, the 13th. When $n$ is even, the median is the mean of the values in places $\frac{n}{2}$ and $\frac{n}{2} + 1$: for $30$ values, the 15th and the 16th.
