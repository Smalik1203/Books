The direction of that change was forced. A value greater than the median arrives on the upper side and unbalances the count — there are now more values above the old median than below — so the middle must shift upwards to even the numbers again. Including $2$ instead would have pushed it the other way, to $7$.

Notice that $8.5$ is not one of the values, and that it never was going to be: with an even count the median is the average of two neighbours and lands between them unless they happen to be equal. A median need not be a member of the collection, and the mean need not be either.

The median counts values; the mean weighs them

Both move up when a large value is added and down when a small one is. The difference is by how much. The mean asks each value how far away it is; the median asks only which side it is on. A value a long way out therefore drags the mean and barely touches the median.

Section 5.2 had exactly such a set. The values $10$, $10$, $11$, $17$ have a mean of $12$ and a median of $10.5$, the average of the two middle values. Drag the $17$ out to $37$ and the mean climbs all the way to $17$ — but the sorted order is unchanged, the two middle values are still $10$ and $11$, and the median stays at $10.5$ exactly.

Neither is the right answer and neither is wrong. They are answers to two questions, and which one a reader wants depends on whether the far-out value is the point of the data or a distraction from it.

5.6An Average with a Piece Missing

Everything so far has run forwards: here is the data, find the average. Run it backwards and the average becomes given information, with an unknown inside it — an equation.

Example 4

Coach Balwan wrote down the weights, in kilograms, of his ten kushti players: $42$, $40$, $39$, $33$, $48$, $38$, $42$, $35$, $32$ and one more that has since been smudged. He also wrote that the mean was $39.2$ kg. What was the missing weight?

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{42 + 40 + 39 + 33 + 48 + 38 + 42 + 35 + 32 + w}{10} = 39.2$ $w$ is missing
Step 2 the nine add to $349$, so $349 + w = 392$ multiply both sides by 10
Step 3 $w = 392 - 349 = 43$ kg
Answer The missing weight was $43$ kg

The mean was used in reverse: the known mean was multiplied by ten to recover the total, and the total is what the missing value was hiding in.
