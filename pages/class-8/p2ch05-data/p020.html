Summary
  1. The mean is the sum of the values divided by the number of values. It is also the balance point of the values on a number line: the distances of the values below it add up to the distances of the values above it. For $6$, $6$, $7$, $13$ the mean is $8$, and $2 + 2 + 1 = 5$ balances $5$.
  2. Including a value above the mean raises the mean, and including a value below it lowers it. A value equal to the mean leaves it unchanged. Removing a value has the opposite effect.
  3. Adding $c$ to every value adds $c$ to the mean. Multiplying every value by $k$ multiplies the mean by $k$.
  4. The median is the middle value when the values are in order. For an even number of values, it is the mean of the two middle values. A value far from the rest moves the mean a lot and the median very little.
  5. The mean multiplied by the number of values gives the total. This lets us find a missing value, or correct a mean when one value was wrong.
  6. The number of times a value occurs is its frequency. In a frequency table, the mean is the sum of each value times its frequency, divided by the total frequency. The median is found with a running total of the frequencies.
  7. A line graph joins readings of a quantity that changes over time. A value read between two readings is an estimate.
  8. Read a graph in two steps: first how it is made (its axes, scale and where they begin), then what it shows. A bar graph should start its axis at zero. Some claims follow from a graph, some go against it, and some are not settled by it.
  9. A picture of data without axes, such as a strip of coloured boxes, is read through its key.
