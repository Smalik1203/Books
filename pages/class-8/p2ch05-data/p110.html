Why the other options are wrong

Set A, 6. Option (c), both, is the answer a reader gives who trusts the names: $140$ does appear in both $135$–$140$ and $140$–$145$. That is exactly why a convention is needed. A class takes its lower end and not its upper, so every value falls in one class and one only.

Set B, 1. Option (b), $6$, counts the numbers written along the axis rather than the gaps between them. Six edges make five classes, in the same way that six fence posts make five panels.

Set B, 7. Option (a), $\tfrac85$, is $\tfrac58$ turned upside down, and no probability can exceed $1$. The opposite of an event is found by subtracting from $1$, never by inverting: $1 - \tfrac58 = \tfrac38$.

Set B, 9. Options (b) and (d) are direct proportions and (a) is the recipe of Chapter 3. The taxi is the odd one because of its fixed charge: its graph is straight but misses the origin, so doubling the distance does not double the fare.

Set B, 11. Options (a) and (d) have the bars going the wrong way. Wider classes collect more readings each, so each new bar is the sum of two old ones and can only be taller.
