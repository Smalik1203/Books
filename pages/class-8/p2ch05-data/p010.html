Exercise Set 5.1
  1. Find the mean of the whole numbers from $31$ to $49$. Use it to find, without adding, the mean of the even numbers from $62$ to $98$. Explain your method.
  1. The mean of $12$, $7$, $y$, $15$, $9$ and $11$ is $11$. Find $y$, and then find the median of the six values.
  1. The mean of a set of values is $35$. The values $29$ and $41$ are added to it. Find the new mean, and explain how you know without being told the other values.
  1. In a class, $15$ boys have a mean height of $142$ cm and $10$ girls have a mean height of $137$ cm. Find the mean height of all $25$ students.
  1. Find the median of $5$, $9$, $12$, $18$, $22$, $30$, $30$, $34$, $40$, $47$, $51$, $60$.
    1. Which single values could be included without changing the median?
    2. Which single values could be removed without changing it?
    3. What is it about this set that makes your two answers so wide?
  1. Say whether each statement is always true, sometimes true or never true. Give an example for each.
    1. Including a value greater than the mean raises the mean.
    2. Including a value greater than the median raises the median.
    3. Multiplying every value by $2$ doubles the median.
    4. The median of a set is one of its values.
  1. The values $14$, $22$, $9$, $31$, $17$, $26$, $12$, $28$, $20$ and $q$ have a median of $19$. Which of these could $q$ be: $12$, $17$, $18$, $19$, $20$, $25$?
  1. A cricket team's scores in six matches have a mean of $180$ runs and a median of $172$ runs. In the seventh match it scores $257$, its highest score so far. Without knowing the six scores, say what happens to the mean and what happens to the median. Which of the two can you find exactly? Give a reason for each answer.
