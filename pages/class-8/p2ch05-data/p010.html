5.6A Line for Something That Changes

Both graphs so far have counted things. A different kind of question asks what a single quantity was doing over a stretch of time: the temperature through a day, the water level in a well through a year, a patient's fever through a night. Here the horizontal axis is time, which runs without stopping, so there is a value at every moment and not only at the moments somebody wrote down.

That is what allows the points to be joined. A line graph plots the readings and joins each to the next, and the joining line is a claim: between two readings the quantity moved from one to the other.

Bars would be wrong here, and it is worth seeing why. Six bars would say that the temperature was $22^\circ$ for three hours, then $26^\circ$ for three hours, then jumped again — six flat stretches and five leaps, which is not what a morning does. Every reading would be carried correctly, and the picture would describe a day nobody has ever lived through.
