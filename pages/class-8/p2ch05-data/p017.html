Step two needs care too. Some statements follow from a graph, some go against it, and some are not settled by it either way. Look again at Fig. 5.4 and three claims about it.

the day warmed until 3 pm not settled: nothing was measured between the readings
it was warmer at noon than at 6 pm true: read straight off the graph
it was never above 33° not settled: no reading was above $33^\circ$, which is a different claim

Only the middle claim follows from the graph. The other two are guesses. Both are probably right, but a graph does not give us probably. It gives us what was measured, joined by lines that say only that the quantity got from one reading to the next somehow.

Not settled is a correct answer, and it is often the right one. It is also the answer people least like to give, because a graph looks so complete. To give it, you need to have done step one and noticed that there were only six readings.
