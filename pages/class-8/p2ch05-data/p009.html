5.7When the Data Comes in a Table

A teacher asks each of her thirty students how many people live in their house, and writes the answers down in the order they are called out.

$$5 \quad 4 \quad 6 \quad 5 \quad 3 \quad 7 \quad 4 \quad 5 \quad 8 \quad 4 \quad 6 \quad 5 \quad 3 \quad 4 \quad 5$$
$$6 \quad 7 \quad 4 \quad 5 \quad 3 \quad 8 \quad 5 \quad 4 \quad 6 \quad 5 \quad 3 \quad 7 \quad 4 \quad 6 \quad 5$$

Only six different answers appear, from $3$ to $8$, so the list repeats itself a great deal. Counting how often each one turns up is done in a single pass: read the list from the start and put a stroke against the right row, laying every fifth stroke across the four before it so the marks arrive in bundles that can be counted in fives.
