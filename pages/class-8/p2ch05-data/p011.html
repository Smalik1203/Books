Because the line is continuous, it can be read at places where nothing was measured. At half past ten the line stands a little over $28^\circ$, and that is a reasonable estimate of the temperature then. It is an estimate and not a measurement: the real temperature wandered about between the readings, and the straight line between two dots is the simplest guess, not the truth.

When the line comes out straight

Some quantities do not wander. An autorickshaw that charges ₹$15$ a kilometre charges ₹$15$ for every kilometre, the fourth as much as the first, so the cost rises at a fixed rate and the plotted points fall exactly on a straight line. Such a picture is a linear graph, and reading between the points is no longer an estimate — every point on the line is a fare somebody could actually be charged.
