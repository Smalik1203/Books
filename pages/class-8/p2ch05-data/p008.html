5.5An Average with a Piece Missing

So far we have been given the values and asked for the average. Now we go the other way. The average is given, one value is unknown, and finding it means solving an equation.

Example 5

Tenzin measures the heights, in centimetres, of $8$ plants in the school garden. Seven of them are $24$, $31$, $28$, $35$, $26$, $30$ and $33$. The eighth has been smudged, but the mean of all eight is written as $29.5$ cm. Find the missing height.

Solution. Let the missing height be $h$ cm.

Step 1 $\dfrac{24 + 31 + 28 + 35 + 26 + 30 + 33 + h}{8} = 29.5$
Step 2 The seven add to $207$, so $207 + h = 8 \times 29.5 = 236$
Step 3 $h = 236 - 207 = 29$
Answer The missing height is $29$ cm

The mean was used backwards. Multiplying the mean by the number of values gave the total, and the missing value was the part of the total we could not see.

In the next example, the values themselves are never given.

Example 6

Anjali counts the mangoes on each of $12$ trees in an orchard and finds a mean of $46.5$ mangoes per tree. A second count shows that she wrote down one tree's number as $6$ more than it really was. What is the correct mean?

Solution.

Step 1 $46.5 \times 12 = 558$ mangoes the total she wrote
Step 2 $558 - 6 = 552$ the true total
Step 3 $552 \div 12 = 46$
Answer The correct mean is $46$ mangoes per tree

We never needed the twelve counts. The total was wrong by $6$, and $6$ shared among $12$ trees is $0.5$, which is exactly how much the mean came down. In Example 3 the error was in every value, and the mean moved by the whole error. Here it was in one value out of $12$, so the mean moved by one twelfth of it.
