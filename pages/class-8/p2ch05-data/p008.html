The next one is harder in an interesting way, because the individual values are never given at all.

Example 5

Venkayya records the coconut harvest from each of his $15$ trees and works out an average of $25.6$ per tree. His son checks the counts and finds that one tree's figure was written down as $3$ more than it should have been. What is the correct average?

the recorded total $25.6 \times 15 = 384$ coconuts
the true total $384 - 3 = 381$
the true average $381 \div 15 = 25.4$

Fifteen numbers were never needed. The error was in the total by exactly $3$, and $3$ shared among $15$ trees is $0.2$ — which is precisely how much the average came down. Compare Example 3, where the error was in every value. Here it was in one, so the effect on the mean is smaller by a factor of the number of values.
