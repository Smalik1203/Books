How wide should a class be? Wide classes lose too much: put all forty students in two classes and the table says nothing. Narrow classes lose the point of grouping. Between five and ten classes is the usual aim, so divide the range by about six and round to something convenient.

range $154 - 131 = 23$ cm
so about $23 \div 6 \approx 3.8$, and $5$ is the convenient size near it
giving five classes, from $130$ to $155$

One question is left, and it is the one that catches people. A student of exactly $140$ cm — does that go in $135$–$140$ or in $140$–$145$? Both names contain the number, so the names alone do not decide it, and if the rule is not fixed the same data gives two different tables.

The convention is that a class takes its lower end and not its upper: $135$–$140$ means $135$ cm or more but under $140$ cm, so a student of $140$ cm is counted in the next class up. Written out, the classes are $130 \le h < 135$, then $135 \le h < 140$, and so on. Every height belongs to exactly one class, which is the whole requirement.

One more thing is asked of the classes, and it is easy to overlook because it is usually satisfied without effort: they should all be the same width. A table with classes of $5$ cm and one of $15$ cm can still be counted, but the counts are no longer comparable — the wide class has three times the room to collect students in, so a large number in it means nothing on its own. Even as a table this is more informative than the twenty-four-row version would have been. The fullest class is the middle one, the counts fall away on both sides of it, and nobody in the class is under $130$ cm or over $155$ cm. That is a description of forty students in three short clauses, and it came from choosing the classes well.
