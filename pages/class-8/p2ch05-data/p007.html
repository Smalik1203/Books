The median counts; the mean weighs

A large new value raises both averages. The mean asks each value how far away it is, but the median asks only which side of the middle it is on. So a value far from the rest pulls the mean a long way and hardly moves the median.

Go back to $6$, $6$, $7$ and $13$ from Section 5.1. Their mean is $8$. Their median is $\frac{6 + 7}{2} = 6.5$. Move the $13$ out to $33$. The mean goes up to $13$. But the order of the values has not changed, the two middle values are still $6$ and $7$, and the median is still $6.5$.

Neither average is the right one in every case. They answer two different questions. If one value is far from the rest because of a mistake or an unusual day, the median describes the other values better. If that value matters, as a large bill matters to a family's spending, the mean includes it and the median does not.

Example 4

A tea stall earns ₹780, ₹820, ₹760, ₹850, ₹2200, ₹800 and ₹790 on the seven days of one week. The ₹2200 was on a festival day. Find the mean and the median. Which of the two better describes an ordinary day?

Solution.

Step 1 The seven amounts add to $7000$, so the mean is $7000 \div 7 = 1000$ rupees
Step 2 In order: $760, 780, 790, 800, 820, 850, 2200$. The 4th value is $800$
Answer Mean ₹1000, median ₹800. The median describes an ordinary day better

The stall earned less than ₹1000 on six of the seven days. The festival day pulled the mean up. The median did not notice how large that day was, only that it was above the middle.
