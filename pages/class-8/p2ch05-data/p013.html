And the peak is a peak among the six readings only. Nobody looked between $12$ and $3$ pm, or between $3$ and $6$; the true maximum lies somewhere in that stretch and could be at any moment in it. A graph of six readings can say where the largest reading was, and no more.

Readings taken closer together give a line with more in it. Take a reading every quarter of an hour instead of every three hours, and the graph stops looking like segments joined at corners and starts looking like a curve. The corners have become too small and too many to see. Nothing about the method changed. Only the number of dots did.

5.9Reading a Graph Honestly

A graph is quicker to look at than a table and slower to read properly, and the gap between those two is where mistakes live. It is worth doing deliberately, in two steps that are easy to run together and should not be.

First what is given, then what follows

Step one: see how the graph is organised. What is on each axis, in what unit, at what scale. Where the axis begins. Which points were actually measured and which were joined. Step two: and only then, say what the shape shows — where it rises, where it is steepest, which lines cross. Anything said in step two is only as good as what was noticed in step one.
