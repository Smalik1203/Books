Exercise Set 5.2
  1. Twenty-four students were asked how many pets they have at home. The answers were $1, 0, 2, 1, 3, 1, 0, 4, 1, 2, 1, 0, 3, 2, 1, 1, 0, 2, 4, 1, 3, 2, 0, 1$. Make a frequency table, and find the mean and the median.
  1. In a skipping contest, each student tried until they could skip $50$ times without stopping. The table shows how many tries they needed.
    Tries12345678
    Students258107431
    Describe the data by its smallest value, its largest value, its mean and its median.
  1. Twenty students were asked how many times they ate at a restaurant last month. Three said never, seven said once, five said twice, four said three times and one said four times.
    1. Find the mean and the median.
    2. This month, every student eats out exactly once more than last month. Write the new mean and median without working them out again.
    3. Is the statement every student ate out at least once last month true? Give a reason for your answer.
  1. In a frequency table, the values $1$, $2$, $3$ and $4$ occur $3$, $5$, $f$ and $2$ times. The mean is $2.5$. Find $f$.
  1. In the library data of Section 5.6, how many students visited more often than the mean, and how many less often? Explain why the two numbers are not equal, even though the mean is the balance point.
  1. A frequency table has the value $15$ six times and the value $x$ four times. Its mean is $17$. Find $x$.
  1. Can ten whole numbers, each from $1$ to $4$, have a mean of $3$ and a median of $2$? Either give a frequency table that works, or explain why none exists.
