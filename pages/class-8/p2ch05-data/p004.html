How far does the mean move when one value is added? Usually less than we expect. Eight friends have a mean of ₹60 of pocket money, so together they have $8 \times 60 = 480$ rupees. A ninth friend with ₹96 joins them. The new total is $480 + 96 = 576$ rupees, and $576 \div 9 = 64$. The new value is $36$ above the old mean, and those $36$ rupees are shared among all nine friends: $36 \div 9 = 4$. So the mean goes up by only ₹4. None of the eight friends has more money than before. The mean went up does not mean that any of the values went up.

Adding values without moving the mean
  • A new value above the mean raises it; a new value below the mean lowers it.
  • New values leave the mean unchanged exactly when their own mean is the same.

5.3Changing Every Value

Now change every value at once. The five numbers $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$ have a mean of $6$. Add $10$ to each of them, and they become $12$, $14$, $16$, $18$ and $20$. Their mean is $16$. We can see this without adding the new numbers.
