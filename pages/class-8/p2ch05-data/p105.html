One wrong entry, and how much it cost

Only one figure was wrong, so only the total needs mending — and the total is what the average was hiding.

the recorded total $7 \times 4{,}000 = 28{,}000$ rupees
the shortfall $4{,}500 - 2{,}400 = 2{,}100$ rupees
the true average $30{,}100 \div 7 = 4{,}300$ rupees

Compare it with Example 3, where the error was in every reading and came off the mean whole. Here the error was in one reading out of seven, so it arrives at the mean divided by seven: ₹$2{,}100$ shared out is ₹$300$, which is exactly the distance from ₹$4{,}000$ to ₹$4{,}300$.

That last comparison is the thread through all eight. An average is a total in disguise, and every one of these questions was answered by taking the disguise off — multiplying the mean by the count, doing whatever the question described to the total, and dividing back.

The other half of them had a second, shorter road: ask what the change does to the balance and how many values have to absorb it. Where the numbers are ugly that road is much the better one, and it is the one the chapter's picture of a fulcrum was for.
