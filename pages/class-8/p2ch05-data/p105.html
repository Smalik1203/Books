About ten, which is not the same as ten

The student is not right, and the reason is not that the die is unfair. Each face has probability $\tfrac16$, so $60$ throws are expected to give about $\tfrac16 \times 60 = 10$ of each — and about is doing real work in that sentence. Six counts that are all exactly $10$ would be a considerable surprise; counts like $8, 13, 9, 11, 7, 12$ are the ordinary result.

What is right is the statement the chapter ended on. As the number of throws grows, the fraction of throws showing each face settles towards $\tfrac16$ — but the counts themselves drift further from being equal, not closer. Six thousand throws will give counts far from exactly a thousand each, and fractions far closer to a sixth than sixty throws ever manage.
