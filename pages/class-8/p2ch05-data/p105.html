Example 3

Ten values have a mean of $25$. Two of them, $19$ and $35$, are removed. Does the mean go up or down, and to what?

Solution.

Step 1 the two removed have a mean of $27$, above $25$ so the mean falls
Step 2 $10 \times 25 = 250$ and $250 - 19 - 35 = 196$ the sum that is left
Step 3 $196 \div 8 = 24.5$ eight values remain
Answer The mean goes down, to $24.5$

Type 3 · Changing every value

Example 4

A class's marks in a test out of $20$ have a mean of $13.5$. The teacher turns every mark into a mark out of $100$ by multiplying it by $5$, and then adds $2$ bonus marks to every student. What is the new mean?

Solution.

Step 1 $13.5 \times 5 = 67.5$ every value times 5
Step 2 $67.5 + 2 = 69.5$ every value plus 2
Answer The new mean is $69.5$

No single mark was needed. Each change to every value makes the same change to the mean, in the same order.

Type 4 · Finding and moving the median

Example 5

Find the median of $14$, $9$, $21$, $9$, $17$, $30$, $12$, $25$. What is the median after $40$ is included?

Solution.

Step 1 sorted: $9, 9, 12, 14, 17, 21, 25, 30$ always sort first
Step 2 $(14 + 17) \div 2 = 15.5$ eight values: 4th and 5th
Step 3 with $40$, nine values: the 5th is $17$ $40$ goes on the end
Answer The median is $15.5$, and then $17$

The median moved up to the next value in the list. It would have moved to $17$ whether the new value was $40$ or $400$.
