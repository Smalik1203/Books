Example 3

Consider the values $9$, $12$, $13$, $15$ and $21$. Which statements are correct?

  1. Their differences from $14$ add to $0$.
  2. Their mean is $14$.
  3. Their median is $13$.
  4. Their mean is $15$.

Solution.

Step 1$-5 - 2 - 1 + 1 + 7 = 0$, so the mean is $14$
Step 2The middle of the five values in order is $13$
CheckThe sum is $70$, and $70 \div 5 = 14$, so (d) is false
Answer(a), (b), (c)

Statements (a) and (b) say the same thing in two ways. The differences from the mean always add to zero: the mean is the balance point of Section 5.1.

Example 4

Shop A sold $210$ school bags and Shop B sold $240$. A poster shows both as bars on an axis that starts at $200$. Which statements are correct?

  1. Shop B sold $30$ more bags.
  2. Shop B sold $30\%$ more bags.
  3. Shop B's bar looks $4$ times as tall as Shop A's.
  4. Shop B sold $4$ times as many bags.

Solution.

Step 1$240 - 210 = 30$ more, which is $\frac{30}{210}$, about $14\%$, not $30\%$
Step 2Above $200$ the bars are $10$ and $40$ tall, so B's looks $4$ times as tall; the cut axis makes the difference look bigger
CheckB sold $240 \div 210$, about $1.14$ times as many, so (d) is false
Answer(a), (c)

A cut axis does not change any number. It changes only how the bars look, so the numbers must always be read from the axis.
