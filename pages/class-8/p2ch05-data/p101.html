Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

Almost none of the questions below give you a list of numbers to average. They give an average and ask for a number, or give two averages and ask what happened in between — which is the same arithmetic entered from the other end.

The thing to hold on to is that a mean and a count between them carry the sum. Once the sum is written down, most of these become one line. Try each before turning the page.

A collection of eight values has a mean of $15$. The value $22$ is removed from it. What is the new mean?

Five numbers have a mean of $12$. A sixth number is added and the mean becomes $13$. What is the sixth number?

A dot plot has dots at $6$, $7$, $7$ and $9$, and one more dot that has been rubbed out. The mean of all five is $8$. Where was the missing dot?

Eleven values have a median of $20$. Four more values are added, every one of them greater than $20$. What can you say about the new median — and what can you not?

In a frequency table the value $4$ occurs six times and the value $x$ occurs nine times. The mean is $7$. Find $x$.
