Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

Every rule in this chapter was met going forwards: counts into a table, a table into bars, a share into an angle, outcomes into a fraction. Each of them runs backwards just as well, and that is what most of the questions below ask for — they hand you the picture and want the numbers, or hand you the answer and want the data.

Try each one before turning the page.

Fifty readings are grouped into four classes with frequencies $7$, $18$, something, and $9$. What is the missing frequency?

A bar graph is drawn to a scale of $1$ cm $= 250$ people. Two of its bars differ in height by $1.8$ cm. By how many people do the two towns differ?

A family spends ₹$32{,}000$ a month, and on a pie chart of that spending the slice for food is $135^\circ$. How much goes on food?

The water in a well falls in a straight line from $9$ m to $3$ m over five months. What is the level after two and a half months, and how do you know the answer is exact rather than an estimate?

A bar graph's vertical axis begins at $40$ and its scale is $1$ cm $= 5$ units. One bar is $4$ cm tall. What value does it stand for, and how tall would the same bar be on an axis starting at zero?
