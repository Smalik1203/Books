Exercise Set 5.4
  1. A letter is chosen at random from the word $\text{MATHEMATICS}$. Find the probability that it is
    1. an $\text{M}$
    2. a vowel
    3. not a $\text{T}$
  1. A bag holds $4$ red, $6$ white and $10$ blue balls, all the same size. One is drawn without looking. Find $P(\text{white})$, $P(\text{not blue})$ and $P(\text{green})$.
  1. A die is thrown. Which of these events has the greater probability, and by how much: the face is a multiple of $3$, or the face is a factor of $6$?
  1. Using the household table of section 5.2, one of the thirty students is picked at random. Find the probability that the student lives in a house of
    1. four
    2. seven or more
    3. fewer than five
  1. The probability of an event is $\tfrac{5}{8}$. What is the probability that it does not happen? A student answers $\tfrac{8}{5}$; say in one sentence why that answer cannot be right whatever the event is.
  1. A coin is tossed $50$ times and comes down heads $31$ times.
    1. What fraction of the tosses were heads?
    2. What is the probability of a head on a fair coin?
    3. The two are not equal. Give the reason, and say what you would expect after $5{,}000$ tosses.
  1. A spinner has eight equal sectors numbered $1$ to $8$. Two students disagree: one says the probability of an even number is $\tfrac12$, the other says the probability of a number greater than $6$ is also $\tfrac12$ because two of the eight numbers are $7$ and $8$. One of them is wrong. Say which, and find the probability that student was reaching for.
  1. A box holds $15$ cards numbered $1$ to $15$ and one is drawn at random. Find the probability that the number is even, that it is a multiple of $5$, and that it is neither. Check that the three answers you have found are consistent with each other.
  1. A bag holds red and green counters only, and the probability of drawing a red is $\tfrac{2}{5}$. Twenty of the counters are green. How many are red, and how many counters are there altogether?
