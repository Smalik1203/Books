A value that has two jobs to do

Both sums are available, and their difference is the answer.

before $5 \times 12 = 60$
after $6 \times 13 = 78$
the new value $78 - 60 = 18$

Why $18$ and not $13$? Because the newcomer has two jobs. It must bring its own share of $13$, and it must also bring enough to lift the five that were already there by one apiece — another $5$. A newcomer that brought only $13$ would have left the mean at $12$, which is the rule from section 5.3 about adding a value equal to the mean.

Finding a dot by balancing

The sum of five values with a mean of $8$ is $40$, and the four visible dots come to $29$, so the missing one is at $11$. The balance argument reaches it without any adding at all.

below 8 $6$ is $2$ below, and each $7$ is $1$ below — $4$ in all
above 8 $9$ is $1$ above, so $3$ more is needed
so the dot sits $3$ above $8$, at $11$

The second method is the one to reach for when the numbers are awkward, because it never asks you to add up the whole collection — only to look at how far each dot is from a point you already have.

What four large values do to a middle

Eleven values put the median in the sixth position. Fifteen values put it in the eighth. The four new values are all above $20$, so in the sorted list they take the last four places and disturb nothing below them — which means the new eighth value is simply the eighth of the original eleven.

That value is at or above the sixth, which was $20$. So the median rises or stays where it is, and it certainly does not fall. What cannot be said is by how much: the eighth of the original eleven might be $20$ again, or $200$, and the question never said.
