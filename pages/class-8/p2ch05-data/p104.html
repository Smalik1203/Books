That last comparison is the thread through all eight. An average is a total in disguise, and most of these questions were answered by taking the disguise off — multiplying the mean by the count, doing whatever the question described to the total, and dividing back.

The other half of them had a second, shorter road: ask what the change does to the balance and how many values have to absorb it. Where the numbers are ugly that road is much the better one, and it is the one the chapter's picture of a fulcrum was for.

Solved Examples
Example 1 · Single correct

Nine values have a mean of 40. A tenth value, 60, is included. What is the new mean?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 42
  2. 50
  3. 41
  4. 44

Solution.

Step 1The nine values add to $9 \times 40 = 360$; with 60 the total is 420.
Step 2$420 \div 10 = 42$.
Answer(a) 42
Example 2 · Single correct

Ten values have a mean of 25. Two of them, 19 and 35, are removed. What is the mean of the rest?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 25
  2. 24.5
  3. 26
  4. 23

Solution.

Step 1The ten add to 250; removing $19 + 35 = 54$ leaves 196.
Step 2$196 \div 8 = 24.5$.
Answer(b) 24.5
Example 3 · Single correct

Marks out of 20 have mean 13.5. What is their mean as marks out of 100?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 13.5
  2. 33.5
  3. 67.5
  4. 70

Solution.

Step 1Every mark, and so the mean, is multiplied by 5: $13.5 \times 5 = 67.5$.
Answer(c) 67.5
