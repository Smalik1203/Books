A shopkeeper's takings over seven days average ₹$4{,}000$. He then finds that Wednesday's figure was entered as ₹$2{,}400$ when it should have been ₹$4{,}500$. What is the corrected average of the seven days?

Only one figure was wrong, so only the total needs mending — and the total is what the average was hiding.

the recorded total $7 \times 4{,}000 = 28{,}000$ rupees
the shortfall $4{,}500 - 2{,}400 = 2{,}100$ rupees
the true average $30{,}100 \div 7 = 4{,}300$ rupees

Compare it with Example 3 in section 5.3, where the error was in every reading and came off the mean whole. Here the error was in one reading out of seven, so it arrives at the mean divided by seven: ₹$2{,}100$ shared out is ₹$300$, which is exactly the distance from the old ₹$4{,}000$ to ₹$4{,}300$.

That last comparison is the thread through all eight. An average is a total in disguise, and most of these questions were answered by taking the disguise off — multiplying the mean by the count, doing whatever the question described to the total, and dividing back.

The other half of them had a second, shorter road: ask what the change does to the balance and how many values have to absorb it. Where the numbers are ugly that road is much the better one, and it is the one the chapter's picture of a fulcrum was for.

Example 1

Marks out of $20$ have a mean of $13.5$. What is their mean as marks out of $100$?

  1. $13.5$
  2. $33.5$
  3. $67.5$
  4. $70$

Solution.

Step 1A mark out of $20$ becomes a mark out of $100$ when it is multiplied by $5$
Step 2So the mean is multiplied by $5$ too: $13.5 \times 5 = 67.5$
Check$13.5 \div 20 = 0.675$ and $67.5 \div 100 = 0.675$
Answer(c) $67.5$
