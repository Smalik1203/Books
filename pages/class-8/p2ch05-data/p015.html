Think and Reflect

A graph of monthly rainfall is drawn for six Indian cities, three on the west coast and three on the east. The west-coast lines peak between June and August; the east-coast ones peak between October and December.

The graph shows that. It does not explain it. What questions would you want to ask next, and which of them could be answered with more data of the same kind — and which could not?

5.10Pictures Without Axes

Manoj keeps a record of what he does all day. He rules a strip of paper into forty-eight boxes, one for each half hour from midnight to midnight, and colours each box by what he was doing then. There is no axis on it and not a number anywhere, and it is data all the same.

It reads easily once you know the rule. He slept from midnight until half past five, and again from ten at night. He travelled twice, half an hour each way, with a long block of school between. Inside that block is a short stretch of eating, the lunch break, at noon. None of that was written down in words anywhere.

What makes the strip readable is its key: one box is half an hour, and one colour is one activity. Without the key it is a band of colours. A line graph's rule is written along its axes, a shaded map's rule is its colour scale, and a strip's rule is a list of five colours. In every case the reader's first job is to find the rule, which is step one of the last section wearing different clothes.
