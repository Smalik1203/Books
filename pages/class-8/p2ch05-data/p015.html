Example 9

Using Fig. 5.4, find the mean of the six readings. Explain why the highest reading being at $3$ pm does not tell us that the day was hottest at $3$ pm.

Solution.

Step 1 The six readings are $21, 25, 30, 33, 29, 24$ read off the dots
Step 2 They add to $162$, and $162 \div 6 = 27$
Check $27$ lies between the lowest reading, $21$, and the highest, $33$
Answer The mean is $27^\circ$. The highest reading was at $3$ pm, but the hottest moment is not known

Nobody read the thermometer between noon and $3$ pm, or between $3$ pm and $6$ pm. The day's highest temperature was somewhere in that stretch, and it could have been at any moment in it. A graph of six readings can only tell us where the highest reading was. To find the hottest moment, the club would need to read the thermometer much more often, say every ten minutes.

If the readings are taken closer together, the line shows more, and our estimates between readings become better. With a reading every quarter of an hour, the short straight pieces begin to look like a smooth curve. The method is the same; there are only more dots. But more readings never turn the line into a measurement. Between any two readings, the line is still only an estimate of what happened.

Line graphs
  • A line graph joins readings of a quantity that changes over time.
  • A value read between two dots is an estimate, not a measurement.
