What this chapter established
  1. The mean is a fair share, and it is also a balance point: the distances from it to the values below add up to the distances to the values above. There is exactly one such point.
  2. Including a value above the mean raises the mean, one below lowers it, and one equal to it changes nothing. Removing a value does the opposite in each case.
  3. A set of new values leaves the mean untouched exactly when the new values have that same mean themselves.
  4. Add $c$ to every value and the mean rises by $c$; multiply every value by $k$ and the mean is multiplied by $k$. So an error repeated in every reading becomes the same error in the mean, and can be taken off at the end.
  5. The median is the middle value of the sorted data, or the average of the two middle ones. It moves in the same direction as the mean and by less, because it asks which side a value is on and not how far away it is.
  6. A mean and a count between them carry the sum, which is what lets a missing value, or a correction to one, be recovered without knowing the rest.
  7. In a frequency table, multiply each value by its frequency before adding, and divide by the total of the frequencies. A running total of frequencies gives the median without sorting anything.
  8. Where readings come in time order, plot them and join them: a line graph. Its steepness shows a rate, and a value read between two dots is an estimate, not a measurement.
  9. Read a graph in two steps — first what is given, then what follows. A bar's length must be proportional to its value, so a bar graph's axis starts at zero.
  10. A picture is data when a stated rule links what is drawn to what is meant. Axes, a colour scale, a key of five colours: find the rule before reading the picture.

Both halves of the chapter were about the same discipline. A mean, a median and a line graph are all summaries, and each of them is worth having because it throws something away. Knowing what a summary has thrown away is the difference between reading it and being told what to think by it.
