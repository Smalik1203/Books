Example 5

Using the Mayan landmarks 1, 20, 360 and 7200, $1000 = 2 \times 360 + k \times 20 + u$, where $k$ and $u$ are each less than 20. Find $k$.

Solution.

Step 1$1000 - 2 \times 360 = 1000 - 720 = 280$.
Step 2$280 \div 20 = 14$, with nothing left for the ones, so $u = 0$ and the units place is empty.
Step 3In dots and bars: two dots in the $360$ place, two bars and four dots in the $20$ place, and a shell in the units place.
Check$2 \times 360 + 14 \times 20 = 720 + 280 = 1000$, and $14$ is less than $20$.

Example 6

In the Mesopotamian base-sixty system, $4000 = a \times 3600 + b \times 60 + c$, with $a$, $b$ and $c$ each less than 60. Find $b$.

Solution.

Step 1$4000 - 3600 = 400$, so $a = 1$: one $3600$ fits, and two would make $7200$.
Step 2$400 = 6 \times 60 + 40$, so $b = 6$ and $c = 40$.
Step 3The numeral is $1 \mid 6 \mid 40$: one narrow wedge, then six narrow wedges, then four sideways wedges.
Check$1 \times 3600 + 6 \times 60 + 40 = 3600 + 360 + 40 = 4000$.
