Type 4 · Egyptian numerals

Example 6

How many of each Egyptian symbol are needed to write 40 506, and how many symbols is that in all?

Solution.

Step 1 $40\,506 = 4 \times 10^4 + 0 \times 10^3 + 5 \times 10^2 + 0 \times 10 + 6$ count each landmark
Step 2 4 fingers, 5 coils and 6 strokes no lotus, no arch
Step 3 $4 + 5 + 6 = 15$
Answer 4 fingers, 5 coils, 6 strokes: 15 symbols

An Egyptian numeral needs no mark for the missing thousands and tens. It simply draws none of them.

Example 7

An Egyptian numeral has two lotuses, three coils and five strokes. Multiply it by 100 without converting it.

Solution.

Step 1 multiplying by $100$ moves each symbol up two landmarks $100 = 10 \times 10$
Step 2 two lotuses become two tadpoles: $2000 \to 200\,000$
Step 3 three coils become three fingers: $300 \to 30\,000$
Step 4 five strokes become five coils: $5 \to 500$
Answer two tadpoles, three fingers, five coils: $2305 \times 100 = 230\,500$

Type 5 · The landmarks of a base

Example 8

A number system has the landmarks 1, 9, 81 and 729. What is its base? What is its next landmark, and at most how many of any one landmark can a numeral in it hold?

Solution.

Step 1 $9 = 9 \times 1$, $\ 81 = 9 \times 9$, $\ 729 = 9 \times 81$ each nine times the last
Step 2 $729 \times 9 = 6561$ the next power
Step 3 nine of a landmark make the next one
Answer Base 9; the next landmark is 6561; at most 8 of each
