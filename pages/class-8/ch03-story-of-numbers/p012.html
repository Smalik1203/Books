The alternation is a clever fix for the very problem that defeated Mesopotamia. If a place is empty and left blank, the neighbouring digits are still told apart, because one is a set of uprights and the other a set of bars. A blank between two identical-looking runs would be unclear; a blank between uprights and bars is not.

It is a good fix, and it is not the right one. A system that needs its digits to alternate in order to be readable has got its clarity from a rule that has nothing to do with counting. The right fix is smaller and stranger.
