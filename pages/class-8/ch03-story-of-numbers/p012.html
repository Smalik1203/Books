Example 5

Multiply $324$ by $10$ in Egyptian symbols, without converting anything.

Solution.

Step 1 $324$ is three coils, two arches and four strokes read the symbols
Step 2 coils become lotuses: $300 \to 3000$ one landmark up
Step 3 two arches become two coils: $20 \to 200$
Step 4 four strokes become four arches: $4 \to 40$
Answer three lotuses, two coils and four arches

Every symbol simply moves one place up the ladder, and the answer is $3240$ without a single calculation. That is what a base gives you, and it is why you were taught to multiply by ten by adding a zero: the zero is the symbol moving up.

Multiplying by anything else works the same way, using the distributive law (multiply each part, then add): $324 \times 30$ is $324 \times 10$ promoted once more and then taken three times. That gives $324 \times 30 = 9720$ without any table of products at all. No such shortcut exists for the Romans, which is why they needed the abacus.

Notice how much of Chapter 2 has just been used. The landmarks are powers of ten. Multiplying them adds the exponents. So the product is another landmark. Every one of those steps was proved there, about numbers, with no thought of Egypt — and it turns out to be the reason a four-thousand-year-old notation works. That is a common pattern in mathematics, and worth expecting: a result established for one purpose explains something quite unrelated.

It also settles a question the Egyptians could not have asked. Any base gives the shortcut, so there is nothing mathematically special about base ten. It won because we have ten fingers, and for no better reason than that.

That does not mean the choice makes no difference. A base with many factors divides tidily: base $60$ splits into halves, thirds, quarters, fifths, sixths, tenths, twelfths, fifteenths, twentieths and thirtieths without ever leaving a fraction, which is why we still keep time in sixties. Base $10$ manages only halves and fifths, which is why a third of an hour is twenty minutes but a third of a metre is a decimal that never ends. And base $2$, with no factors at all beyond itself, is the worst for dividing and the best for machines, because a wire is either on or off.
