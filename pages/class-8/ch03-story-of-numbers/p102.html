For a number near a million, do not convert — estimate. Chapter 2 gave us $2^{10} \approx 10^3$, so $2^{20} \approx 10^6$: a million needs about twenty binary digits. The true answer is exactly twenty, and we got it without dividing anything.

Those twenty binary digits are worth a moment. A computer stores a number as a row of switches, each on or off, and twenty switches is what it takes to count to a million. Sixty-four of them — the size of a modern machine's word — reach past $10^{19}$, which is the chessboard of grain from the last chapter. That is the whole reason base two is the base of computing: it is the only base whose digits a piece of wire can hold.

A Mesopotamian numeral shows two wedges, then a gap of uncertain width, then three wedges. Give four different numbers it could be.

Two wedges, a gap, three wedges. The wedges are counts; the gap is supposed to say how many places separate them, and it cannot be measured.

adjacent places $2 \times 60 + 3$$123$
one place skipped $2 \times 3600 + 0 \times 60 + 3$$7203$
or the three in the 60s $2 \times 3600 + 3 \times 60$$7380$
two places skipped $2 \times 60^3 + 3$$432\,003$

Four readings, and no reason to prefer any of them — nothing in the numeral settles it. In practice a scribe's reader used context: a tablet about a month's grain is unlikely to be recording four hundred thousand of anything.

That is why a placeholder is not a small extra. Without it, a place value system does not represent numbers at all; it represents families of numbers, and the reader must choose.

Explain why a place value system cannot have a base of $1$.

Put $n = 1$ into everything the chapter established and watch it collapse.

The landmarks are meant to be $n^0, n^1, n^2, \ldots$ — but every power of one is one. So the landmarks are $1, 1, 1, 1, \ldots$, and the second place is worth exactly what the first place is worth. Position has stopped meaning anything.

And the symbol count fails too. A base-$n$ system needs $n$ symbols, $0$ up to $n-1$; a base-$1$ system would need one symbol, which would have to be $0$. Every numeral would then be a row of zeros, worth nothing at all.
