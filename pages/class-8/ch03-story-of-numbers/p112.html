Example 10

In a board game, points are kept with tokens: a shell for $1$ point, a stone for $6$, a coin for $36$ and a ring for $216$. A player never keeps six tokens of one kind. Six of them are always traded for one token of the next kind.

  1. A player holds $2$ rings, no coins, $5$ stones and $3$ shells. Her score is
    1. 2053
    2. 465
    3. 435
    4. 285
  2. Numerical answer. How many tokens does a player with $100$ points hold?
  3. Numerical answer. A player holds $1$ ring, $5$ coins, $5$ stones and $5$ shells, and then wins $1$ point. How many tokens does she hold after trading?

Solution.

Step 1In base 6 places: $2 \times 216 + 0 \times 36 + 5 \times 6 + 3 = 465$.
Step 2$100 = 2 \times 36 + 4 \times 6 + 4$, so $2$ coins, $4$ stones and $4$ shells: $10$ tokens.
Step 3Six shells become a stone, six stones a coin, six coins a ring: she holds $2$ rings and nothing else.
Answer(i) (b) 465; (ii) 10; (iii) 2
