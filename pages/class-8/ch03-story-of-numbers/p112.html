B1 $255 = 256 - 1$, and $256 = 2^8$, so $255$ is eight ones. (b) is $128$, (d) is $256$ itself, and (c) is $254$.
B2 The powers of three are $1, 3, 9, 27, 81, 243, 729, 2187$. The largest that fits inside $1000$ is $729$, so the numeral runs from the $729$ place down: seven places.
B3 In base $n$ the digit-sum rule tests for $n-1$, because every landmark is one more than a multiple of $n-1$. (c) and (d) are the two numbers people reach for by analogy with base ten's $9$, without asking where the $9$ came from.
B5 The Mayan landmarks jump $1, 20, 360$ — and $360$ is not $20 \times 20$. So position is being used, but the landmarks are not the powers of a single number, and the multiplication shortcut of § 3.5.1 is lost.
B7 $\tfrac16$ has a $3$ in the denominator and base $10$ has only the primes $2$ and $5$. The other three have denominators built from $2$s and $5$s alone.
C1 $121$ in base $3$ is $9 + 6 + 1 = 16$; in base $5$ it is $25 + 10 + 1 = 36$. The difference is $20$. (a) and (d) are the two values themselves — a reminder to read the question to the end.
C2 The first digit has three choices ($1$, $2$, $3$) and the other two have four each, so $3 \times 4 \times 4 = 48$. (c) counts $4^3$ and forgets that a numeral may not begin with $0$.
C5 A gap of any width puts the two wedges somewhere above the three, so the two must sit in a higher place — and $203$ would need the two in the units. It is the only reading the numeral cannot bear.
C8 (a) is false — the quotients need not repeat at all. (c) and (d) are true statements that do not answer the question. What makes the process finish is that a whole number cannot be divided by $b$ for ever and stay a whole number above zero.
C9 (a) is often false: $\text{MMCCCLXVII}$ and $2367$ are much the same length. (c) is simply wrong — Egypt does have a base. The advantage is the one this chapter was built to explain, and it is about the alphabet, not the numerals.
C11 The smallest four-place numeral in base $6$ is $1000_{\,6} = 216$ and the largest is $5555_{\,6} = 6^4 - 1 = 1295$. (a) is the three-place range and (d) mistakes the base for the smallest value.
C12 MMM is three marks, $3000$ is four digits, and a tally is three thousand notches. The Roman numeral wins this number because $3000$ happens to be three of a landmark — which is exactly why (a) is tempting and wrong. Try $3888$ and the order changes.
