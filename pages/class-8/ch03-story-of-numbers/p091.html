1. Ishaan packs pencils in boxes of $5$. Five boxes go in a packet and five packets in a carton. He packs $468$ pencils using as many full cartons, then full packets, then full boxes as he can. Find the number of cartons, packets, boxes and loose pencils. Write $468$ in base $5$.
  1. Find $\text{CDXXIV} + \text{CCLXXXVIII}$. Write the answer in Roman numerals, and check it in our numerals.
  1. A base-$8$ numeral has exactly four places. Find the smallest and the largest number it can stand for.
  1. Write $1$ hour $25$ minutes $40$ seconds in seconds. Also write $4750$ seconds in hours, minutes and seconds.
  1. Explain why the product of two landmarks of base $10$ is always a landmark, but the product of two Roman landmarks need not be. Give an example of each.
  1. In base $b$, the numeral $32$ stands for $23$. Find $b$. Also write $23$ in base $4$.
  1. Ananya reads a Mayan numeral with $1$ in the $360$ place, $5$ in the $20$ place and $12$ in the units place. Find the number. If the same counts were read in a true base-$20$ system, would the number be larger or smaller, and by how much?
Long answer
  1. A number is written $23$ in some base $b$ and $32$ in base $5$. Find the number in base $10$. Form an equation in $b$ and find the base. Check your answer, and write the number in base $2$.
  1. A shop sells beads in strings of $12$. Twelve strings make a pack and twelve packs make a box. Kiran buys $2$ boxes, some packs, $7$ strings and $9$ loose beads: $3981$ beads in all. Taking the number of packs as $p$, form an equation and find $p$. Write $3981$ in base $12$. How many more beads would make exactly $3$ boxes?
  1. Show that the largest three-place numeral in base $n$, whose every digit is $n - 1$, stands for $n^3 - 1$. Hence find the largest number a three-place numeral can stand for in base $5$ and in base $8$.
  1. Siddharth's train leaves at $6$ h $48$ min $35$ s. The journey takes $13\,675$ seconds. Write the journey time in hours, minutes and seconds, and find its time of arrival.
  1. An Egyptian scribe records $3$ lotuses, $4$ coils, $1$ arch and $6$ strokes of sacks from one field, and $1$ lotus, $7$ coils, $9$ arches and $8$ strokes from another. Find the total by gathering like symbols and trading ten for one. Write the total as an Egyptian numeral and in our digits, and count its symbols.
