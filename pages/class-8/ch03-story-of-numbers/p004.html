Some peoples grouped by two and went no further. They had a word for one and a word for two, and they built every other number from those two words. Counting in a language of that kind goes like this:

numbersaid aswhat it means
1oneone
2twotwo
3two-onetwo and one
4two-twotwo and two
5two-two-onetwo, two and one
6two-two-twotwo, two and two

Anything beyond six was simply many. The names are built by repeating the word for two, which is the idea of grouping showing up in speech rather than in writing.

There is something strange about this kind of counting. It has been recorded among peoples of the islands north of Australia, of South America and of southern Africa. Three peoples on three continents, with no known contact between them, arrived at the same arrangement. Historians disagree about why. One idea is that the three peoples share a very old common ancestor, and the system travelled with them. Another is simply that counting in twos is an obvious thing to do, and obvious things get invented more than once.
