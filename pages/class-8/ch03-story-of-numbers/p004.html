Something strange attaches to that system. Almost identical ones — the same counting by twos, the same words built by repetition — have been recorded among the Bakairi of South America and the San of southern Africa. Three peoples on three continents, with no known contact between them, arrived at the same arrangement. Historians disagree about why. One suggestion is a common ancestry so old that the system travelled with the migrations; another is simply that counting in twos is an obvious thing to do, and obvious things get invented more than once.
