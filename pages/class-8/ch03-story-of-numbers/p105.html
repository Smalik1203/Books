The others are conversions and rules, and each rewards the same habit: write the number in expanded form, with the powers showing, and look at what the base is doing. Almost every question about bases becomes easy the moment you rewrite $4344_{\,6}$ as $4 \times 6^3 + 3 \times 6^2 + 4 \times 6 + 4$.

If you cannot get started, do it in base $10$ first, where you already know the answer, and then ask which step used the fact that the base was ten. That step is where the general rule lives.

That last piece of advice is worth more than it looks, and it is a general method rather than a hint about bases. A problem may be stated with a letter in it: a base $n$, an exponent $a$, a side of length $x$. The way in is almost always to replace the letter with a number you know your way around, solve the easy version completely, and then reread your own solution asking which lines actually used the number. Those lines are the ones that need rewriting. The rest was general all along.

Keep whatever you wrote for each of the eight. Several of the explanations above take a different route from the obvious one, and the comparison is the point.

Example 2 · Single correct

What number is MMCDXCVI?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 2596
  2. 2496
  3. 2694
  4. 2406

Solution.

Step 1MM is 2000, CD is $500 - 100 = 400$ and XC is $100 - 10 = 90$.
Step 2VI is 6, so the number is $2000 + 400 + 90 + 6 = 2496$.
Answer(b) 2496
