Beyond the Book

The chapter was history, but what it left you is not. A base-$n$ system has landmarks $n^0, n^1, n^2, \ldots$, needs exactly $n$ symbols, and never uses a landmark $n$ times or more. Position decides which landmark a symbol stands for. And an empty place needs a mark of its own, or the whole thing becomes guesswork.

Every question below is answered from those four facts, and none of them is about remembering a civilisation. Try each before reading what follows it.

How many digits does $1000$ need when written in base $6$? How many different symbols does a base-$6$ system need altogether? The two answers are not the same kind of thing — say what each one counts.

Two different counts, and the question was really about telling them apart.

How many digits does $1000$ need in base $6$? Find the largest landmark that fits. The powers of six are $1, 6, 36, 216, 1296$, and $216$ fits while $1296$ does not — so the numeral runs from the $216$ place down to the units, which is four places.

$1000$ $= 4 \times 216 + 3 \times 36 + 4 \times 6 + 4$
so $4344$ in base $6$ 4 digits

How many symbols does the system need? Six — the digits $0$ to $5$. A place can hold anything from none of its landmark up to five of them, and six would become one of the next landmark instead.

So the first number counts places in one numeral and grows as the number grows; the second counts symbols in the alphabet and never changes at all. Confusing them is the most common error with bases, and the Egyptians' problem was that their second number grew too.

Write $2025$ in base $2$. Then, without converting it, say roughly how many binary digits a number near a million would need.

Divide by $2$ repeatedly and keep the remainders, reading them upwards at the end.

$2025$ $= 1024 + 512 + 256 + 128 + 64 + 32 + 8 + 1$
in base 2 $11111101001$ 11 digits
