Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter was history, but what it left you is not. A base-$n$ system has landmarks $n^0, n^1, n^2, \ldots$, needs exactly $n$ symbols, and never uses a landmark $n$ times or more. Position decides which landmark a symbol stands for. And an empty place needs a mark of its own, or the whole thing becomes guesswork.

Every question below is answered from those four facts, and none of them is about remembering a civilisation. Try each before turning the page.

How many digits does $1000$ need when written in base $7$? How many different symbols does a base-$7$ system need altogether? The two answers are not the same kind of thing — say what each one counts.

Write $2025$ in base $2$. Then, without converting it, say roughly how many binary digits a number near a million would need.

A Mesopotamian numeral shows two wedges, then a gap of uncertain width, then three wedges. Give four different numbers it could be.

Explain why a place value system cannot have a base of $1$.

In base $10$ a number is divisible by $9$ exactly when its digits add to a multiple of $9$. Find the matching rule for base $7$ — which number does it test for, and why that one? Check your rule on $48$.
