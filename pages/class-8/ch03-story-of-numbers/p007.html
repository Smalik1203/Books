It is easy to be condescending about this from where we sit, and the condescension would be misplaced. The Roman system did what it was asked to do for the better part of two millennia: it recorded quantities, on stone and in ledgers, briefly and unambiguously. Nobody expected a numeral to help with the arithmetic, because in Rome the arithmetic was a separate craft done on a board by a specialist. What changed later was not that people got cleverer, but that a system arrived in which writing the number down and calculating with it were the same activity.

Hold on to that thought. The advantage of the modern system is not that it is shorter — $\text{MMCCCLXVII}$ and $2367$ are much of a muchness — but that its notation does arithmetic for you.
