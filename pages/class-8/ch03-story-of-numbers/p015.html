The third place is empty, and the $0$ says so out loud. There is no gap to measure, no context to guess from, and no ambiguity at either end: $36$, $360$ and $3600$ are three different numerals and always were.

The system is Indian. Names for the powers of ten — eka, dasha, shata, sahasra, and onwards past $10^{12}$ — appear in the Yajurveda Saṃhitā, so the spoken structure is very old indeed. The written form with ten digits including a zero is about two thousand years old: the earliest known instance is in the Bakhshali manuscript, where the zero is written as a dot.

Two jobs for zero, and the second is the harder

As a placeholder, $0$ marks an empty column so the other digits can be read. As a number, $0$ is a quantity you can add, subtract and multiply with. Mesopotamia and the Maya got the first. India got both.

That distinction is the heart of the matter. Āryabhaṭa, writing in 499 CE, used the ten-digit system to do serious astronomy — computing with zero, not merely leaving room for it. Brahmagupta, in 628 CE, went further and wrote down the rules: what happens when zero is added to a number, subtracted from it, multiplied by it. He did the same for negative numbers in the same work.

Setting out arithmetic for zero and the negatives together was not tidying-up. It produced, for the first time, a collection of numbers you could add, subtract and multiply without ever falling out of it — and much of modern algebra is built on collections of that kind.

Brahmagupta got division by zero wrong, and said so honestly — he could not make it work. Nobody has since, and it is now known to be impossible rather than merely difficult. Being clear about what you have not settled is part of doing mathematics well.

It is worth asking why the placeholder was the hard part, since a mark for nothing sounds like the easiest thing in the world to invent. The difficulty is that it is a symbol for the absence of a quantity, and for most of history a numeral meant a quantity you could point at. Three sheep are three sheep; no sheep are not a kind of sheep. Writing a sign for them requires treating the empty column as a thing in its own right, and several civilisations got all the way to place value and stopped just short of that step.
