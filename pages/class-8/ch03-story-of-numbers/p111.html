Why the other options are wrong

A1 XIV is $10 + (5-1)$. (b) reads the I as adding rather than subtracting; (c) reads the numeral as digits side by side, which is what a place value system does and the Roman system does not.
A2 (a) lists multiples of five, not powers; (d) forgets that the first landmark of every base is $1$; (c) lists the digits rather than the landmarks.
A3 The symbols run $0$ to $7$, which is eight of them. (a) is the tempting answer and comes from forgetting the zero — exactly the omission this chapter is about.
A5 Egypt has a base but not place value: its symbols carry their own value wherever they stand. The other three all use position.
A6 The $0$ in $305$ sits in the tens place. (d) is the loose way of talking that the chapter's whole argument warns against: the zero says none of this landmark, not nothing.
A7 $27 = 25 + 2$, which is one twenty-five, no fives and two units. (b) has the digits reversed; (d) is $5^2 + 2 \times 5 = 35$.
