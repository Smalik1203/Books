Example 6

Write $1206$ in Egyptian, Roman and Hindu numerals, and count the symbols in each.

Solution. Split the number into its landmarks first.

Step 1 $1206 = 1 \times 1000 + 2 \times 100 + 6$
Step 2 Egyptian: 1 lotus, 2 coils and 6 strokes, which is 9 symbols
Step 3 Roman: M, CC and VI make MCCVI, which is 5 symbols
Step 4 Hindu: $1206$, which is 4 symbols, with a $0$ for the empty tens place
Answer 9, 5 and 4 symbols

Set the whole story out and it is five ideas, each fixing what the last one left broken.

It is worth ending on how ordinary each step looks in isolation. Nobody inventing the five-bar gate was trying to found modern science. They were trying to read a tally without counting it. And yet the line from that mark to the calculations that fly aircraft and run banks is unbroken, and every link in it was somebody solving a small, practical problem that was right in front of them.

The next time you write a number down, look at what you are doing. Ten shapes, each meaning something different depending on where you put it, and one of them meaning nothing at all — and between them they can express any quantity in the universe. It took the whole world a very long time to think of that, and now every child learns it in the first years of school.
