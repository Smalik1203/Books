Solved Examples

Type 1 · Counting a carpet

Example 1

A Sierpiński carpet is begun from a square of side $54$ cm. At step $2$, find the side of each remaining square, how many there are, and the area left.

Solution.

Step 1 $54 \div 3 \div 3 = 6$ cm a third each step
Step 2 $R_2 = 8^2 = 64$ eight for each square
Step 3 $64 \times 6 \times 6 = 2304$ cm² count times one square
Step 4 $54 \times 54 \times \tfrac{64}{81} = 2304$ cm² check: eight ninths, twice
Answer 64 squares of side 6 cm, and 2304 cm² left
Example 2

A square is cut into nine equal squares, and the four corner squares are thrown away, leaving a plus sign of five. The same is done to each square kept, step after step. Starting from an area of $1$, find the number of squares kept at step $3$ and the area left. Is it less than a fifth?

Solution.

Step 1 $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125$ squares five for each square
Step 2 each has side $\tfrac{1}{27}$ and area $\tfrac{1}{729}$ a third each step
Step 3 $125 \times \tfrac{1}{729} = \tfrac{125}{729}$ count times one square
Step 4 $\left(\tfrac59\right)^3 = \tfrac{125}{729}$ check: five ninths kept
Step 5 $\tfrac{125}{729} \approx 0.17$, and a fifth is $0.2$
Answer 125 squares and an area of $\tfrac{125}{729}$, which is less than a fifth

The rule is new, but the counting is the carpet's with five in place of eight.
