This is the compounding of Chapter 1, run downwards: a fixed fraction of what is left goes at every step. The area keeps getting smaller. At step $60$ less than a thousandth of the square is left, and at step $120$ less than a millionth. So the finished carpet has no area at all, and yet its pattern can be seen at every step.

Example 1

A Sierpiński carpet starts from a square of side $45$ cm. At step $2$, find the side of each square left, the number of squares, and the area left.

Solution.

Step 1 side: $45 \to 15 \to 5$ cm a third each step
Step 2 $R_2 = 8^2 = 64$ eight for each square
Step 3 $64 \times 25 = 1600$ cm2 each square is 25 cm²
Answer 64 squares, each of side 5 cm, leaving 1600 cm²

The area formula gives the same answer: $2025 \times \left(\tfrac89\right)^2 = 2025 \times \tfrac{64}{81} = 1600$. Counting the small squares and taking eight ninths twice are one calculation done in two ways.

The side of each square follows a rule too. It is divided by $3$ at every step, so at step $n$ each square is $\tfrac{1}{3^{\,n}}$ as wide as the first, and its area is $\tfrac{1}{9^{\,n}}$ of the first. Multiply this by the number of squares, and the area formula appears again:

$$8^{\,n} \times \frac{1}{9^{\,n}} \;=\; \left(\frac89\right)^{\! n}.$$

So the area of the carpet at any step can be found in two ways, and each way checks the other one.

Counting the carpet
  • At step $n$ there are $8^{\,n}$ squares, each $\tfrac{1}{3^{\,n}}$ as wide as the first.
  • The area left at step $n$ is $\left(\tfrac89\right)^{n}$ of the first square.
