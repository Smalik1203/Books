Type 5 · Faces, vertices and edges together

Example 9

A solid has $20$ faces and $30$ edges. How many vertices has it? And can a solid of this chapter's kind have $7$ faces, $10$ vertices and $16$ edges?

Solution.

Step 1 $20 + \text{vertices} - 30 = 2$ faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges
Step 2 vertices $= 2 + 30 - 20 = 12$
Step 3 $7 + 10 - 16 = 1$ not $2$
Answer 12 vertices. No: those three counts cannot all be right

The first solid is the icosahedron, which has twenty triangles for faces.

Type 6 · Nets: the card they use and the solid they make

Example 10

A box with no lid is $10$ cm long, $6$ cm wide and $4$ cm high. How much card does its net use?

Solution.

Step 1 base: $10 \times 6 = 60$ cm² one base, no lid
Step 2 two sides: $2 \times 10 \times 4 = 80$ cm²
Step 3 two ends: $2 \times 6 \times 4 = 48$ cm²
Step 4 $60 + 80 + 48 = 188$ cm² five rectangles
Answer 188 cm²
Example 11

The net of a cube has an area of $150$ cm². How long is each edge of the cube, and what is its volume?

Solution.

Step 1 one square: $150 \div 6 = 25$ cm² six equal squares
Step 2 edge: $\sqrt{25} = 5$ cm
Step 3 volume: $5 \times 5 \times 5 = 125$ cm³
Answer Edges of 5 cm, and a volume of 125 cm³
