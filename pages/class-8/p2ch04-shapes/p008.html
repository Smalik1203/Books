Chapter 2 finishes it. The straight line is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose short sides are $12$ and $3 + 4 = 7$.

the theorem $d^2 = 12^2 + 7^2 = 144 + 49 = 193$
so $d = \sqrt{193} \approx 13.9$ cm

But there was a choice in the unfolding, and other choices give other answers. Go over the top and down the far end and the triangle has sides $12 + 3$ and $4$, giving $\sqrt{241} \approx 15.5$ cm. Go round the end first and it is $12 + 4$ and $3$, giving $\sqrt{265} \approx 16.3$ cm. All three are straight lines on their own nets, and all three are genuine routes across the box.

Why does the first win? Look at the two short sides in each case: $12$ and $7$, then $15$ and $4$, then $16$ and $3$. Every pair adds to $19$, because every unfolding lays out the same three dimensions — what changes is only how they are shared between the two sides. And for a fixed total, the sum of two squares is smallest when the two numbers are closest to equal.

So the shortest route belongs to the most balanced pair, which is what you get by setting the longest dimension against the other two added together. That is worth keeping: the winner can usually be named before any arithmetic is done.

Straight on the net, shortest on the box

A net makes the surface flat, and on a flat surface the straight line is shortest. So every candidate is a straight line on some net, and an impossible search among endless paths becomes a short list to compare.

Here the list has three entries and $\sqrt{193}$ wins: the most balanced triangle, made by setting the longest dimension against the other two added together.

It is worth seeing why the answer is not $12 + 4$, or $12 + 3$, or any of the routes along the edges. Walking along edges is allowed but never wise: the ant is free to cut across a face, and cutting the corner is exactly what the straight line on the net does.

Example 4

A box is $10$ cm by $5$ cm by $2$ cm. An ant crosses from one corner to the opposite one. Which unfolding gives the shortest route?

Solution.

Step 1 $\sqrt{100 + 49} = \sqrt{149} \approx 12.2$ cm sides $10$ and $5+2$
Step 2 $\sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169} = 13$ cm sides $10+2$ and $5$
Step 3 $\sqrt{225 + 4} = \sqrt{229} \approx 15.1$ cm sides $10+5$ and $2$
Answer The first unfolding, about 12.2 cm

The first, at about $12.2$ cm — and the rule named it in advance. The two short sides always add to $17$ here; $10$ and $7$ is the most balanced way of splitting that, so it gives the shortest hypotenuse.
