A prism has two matching polygons as opposite faces, joined by parallelograms all round. A pyramid has one polygon as its base and a point above it, called the apex, joined to every vertex of the base. Both are named after the polygon: a triangular prism, a hexagonal pyramid. A triangular pyramid has a second name, tetrahedron. Its four faces are all triangles, and any one of them can be called the base.

Count the parts of a prism whose ends have $n$ sides. There are $2$ end faces and $n$ side faces. Each end has $n$ vertices, which makes $2n$ in all. There are $n$ edges round each end and $n$ edges joining the two ends. A pyramid is counted in the same way.

$n$-sided prism $n + 2$ faces,   $3n$ edges,   $2n$ vertices
$n$-sided pyramid $n + 1$ faces,   $2n$ edges,   $n + 1$ vertices

So a prism with ten-sided ends has $12$ faces, $30$ edges and $20$ vertices, and nobody has to draw it. Now add the faces and the vertices, and take away the edges. For this prism, $12 + 20 - 30 = 2$. For the pyramid on the same base, $11 + 11 - 20 = 2$ as well. This is not luck. It is true for every solid of this kind, and it is a useful check on any count.

Example 3

A pyramid has $14$ edges. What is its base, and how many faces and vertices does it have?

Solution.

Step 1 $2n = 14$, so $n = 7$ a pyramid has $2n$ edges
Step 2 $8$ faces 7 triangles and the base
Step 3 $8$ vertices 7 on the base and the apex
Answer A seven-sided base, 8 faces and 8 vertices

Check: $8 + 8 - 14 = 2$, as it must be. A count that fails this test has a mistake in it.
