Summary
  1. A fractal is a shape made of smaller copies of itself. It comes from one short rule applied again and again to its own result. Branches, leaf veins, temple towers and rangoli designs show the first few steps of such a rule.
  2. The Sierpiński carpet removes the middle ninth of every square at each step. At step $n$ it has $8^{\,n}$ squares, and $\left(\tfrac89\right)^{n}$ of its area is left. The holes follow the rule $H_{n+1} = H_n + R_n$, which gives a sum, not a single power.
  3. The Sierpiński triangle removes the middle quarter of every triangle. At step $n$ it has $3^{\,n}$ triangles, and $\left(\tfrac34\right)^{n}$ of its area is left.
  4. The Koch snowflake turns every side into four sides, each a third as long. It has $3 \times 4^{\,n}$ sides at step $n$. Its perimeter is multiplied by $\tfrac43$ at every step and grows without limit, but its area stays inside a circle.
  5. A solid with flat surfaces has faces, edges and vertices. A prism on an $n$-sided polygon has $n + 2$ faces, $3n$ edges and $2n$ vertices. A pyramid has $n + 1$ faces, $2n$ edges and $n + 1$ vertices.
  6. For these solids, faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges $= 2$. For a cube, $6 + 8 - 12 = 2$.
  7. A net is a flat shape that folds into a solid. A cube has eleven different nets.
  8. The shortest route over the surface of a box is a straight line on a net. It is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, and the shortest one puts the longest side of the box against the other two added together. For a box $9$ cm by $5$ cm by $3$ cm it is $\sqrt{81 + 64} = \sqrt{145}$ cm.
  9. A solid is shown flat by its front, top and side views, or by an isometric drawing, in which every edge keeps its true length.
