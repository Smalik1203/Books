Engineers and builders draw objects in this way because the views can be measured. In a photograph, far edges look shorter than near ones. In a projection they do not, so a length on the front view is the real length, and a builder can work from it.

Three views

The front, top and side views each look along one direction and lose that one measurement. One view can belong to many solids. The three views together usually fix the solid.

4.10Drawing a Solid Whole

Three views are exact, but they are hard to picture. Can a solid be drawn in one picture that keeps its measurements? The answer comes from a question: is there a direction from which a cube's three directions all look alike?

There is. Stand a cube on one corner and look straight down on it. Every one of its twelve edges now slopes away from you by the same amount. So every edge is shortened by the same amount, and all twelve come out the same length on paper. This is called an isometric view. The word means equal measure.
