Check both with faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges: $12 + 20 - 30 = 2$ and $16 + 16 - 30 = 2$. The pyramid needs a bigger base to reach the same number of edges, because each of its base vertices carries only two.

An ant walks across the surface of a cube of side $5$ cm, from one corner to the opposite one. How far does it go?

The rule from Section 4.8 names the winner before any arithmetic: set the longest dimension against the other two added together. On a cube all three are equal, so the pairing is $5$ against $5 + 5$.

the theorem $d^2 = 5^2 + 10^2 = 25 + 100 = 125$
so $d = \sqrt{125} \approx 11.2$ cm

Compare that with $15$ cm along three edges, and with the $\sqrt{75} \approx 8.7$ cm a fly could manage straight through the middle. The ant is stuck on the surface and pays about two and a half centimetres for it.

A cuboid $3$ cm long, $2$ cm deep and $1$ cm high lies flat on a table with its long side towards you. What are its front, top and side views, and how big is each?

Each view looks along one of the three directions, and that one measurement is lost. So each view is a rectangle made of the other two.

front view length and height: $3$ cm by $1$ cm
top view length and depth: $3$ cm by $2$ cm
side view depth and height: $2$ cm by $1$ cm

Every measurement turns up in two of the three views, and no view shows all three. That is why one view can never give the volume, $3 \times 2 \times 1 = 6$ cm3. For a cuboid, any two of the views together can.

Starting from a triangle of area $1$, what is the area of the Sierpiński triangle at step $5$? Is it more or less than a tenth?

Every step keeps three quarters of the area, so five steps keep $\left(\tfrac34\right)^5$ of it — not five times a quarter off, which is the slip this question is watching for.

area $\left(\tfrac34\right)^5 = \dfrac{243}{1024}$
