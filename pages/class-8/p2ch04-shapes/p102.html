Behind Each Answer
The same eight questions, worked — and the reasoning that finds them.

The eight are answered below in the order they were asked. Notice how few pictures are needed: once a rule has been written down as a formula, a question about step $40$ is no harder than one about step $2$, and that is exactly what a formula is for.

Dividing by three, four times over

Each step cuts every square into nine, so the side is divided by $3$ and the count is multiplied by $8$. Four steps of each.

side $81 \div 3^4 = 81 \div 81 = 1$ cm
how many $8^4 = 4096$

So the area left is $4096$ cm2 out of the $6561$ the square began with — a little under two thirds, after four steps of throwing away a ninth.

Reading a step number out of a count

The formula $S_n = 3 \times 4^{\,n}$ is usually read forwards. Here it is read backwards, which needs nothing new — divide by $3$ and ask which power of $4$ is left.

so $3 \times 4^{\,n} = 192$, giving $4^{\,n} = 64$
and $4^3 = 64$, so $n = 3$

Part I's chapter on powers is what makes that last line quick: recognising $64$ as $4^3$ is the same skill as recognising it as $8^2$ or $2^6$.
