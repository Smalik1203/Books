Exploring Some Geometric Themes

4.1Shapes Inside Shapes

Pick up a fallen branch and hold it at arm's length. A thick stem runs up the middle. Thinner branches grow out of the stem, and still thinner twigs grow out of those. From a little way off, the branch looks like a small tree.

The same thing happens in many places. The veins of a peepal leaf split into smaller veins, and those split again. A river collects water from streams, and each stream collects water from smaller streams. In each case a part, looked at closely, has the same form as the whole, only smaller.

Fractal

A shape that is made of smaller copies of itself is called a fractal. Each part, seen close up, has the same form as the whole shape.

A fractal drawn by a mathematician goes further than any tree. Its rule is followed for ever. So however closely you look at it, you always find more detail of the same kind, and a small piece, made larger, looks just like the whole.

Fractals are useful as well as beautiful. Some mobile phone aerials are cut in a fractal shape, so that one small piece of metal can pick up signals of many different lengths. Doctors describe the branching of blood vessels in the lungs in the same way.

This chapter has two halves. The first half is about three fractals that can be drawn exactly, by following a rule. The second half is about solid shapes, such as boxes and pyramids.

The two halves use one method. A fractal can never be drawn in full, because it never ends. A box can never be drawn in full on paper, because paper is flat. In both cases we take the shape apart into simple pieces, and then read what we need from the pieces.
