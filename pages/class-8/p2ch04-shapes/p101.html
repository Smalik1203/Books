Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

This chapter had two halves and one habit. A fractal is handled by asking what one step does and then doing it $n$ times: multiply the count, divide the size, multiply the area by a fixed fraction. A solid is handled by flattening it — into a net, or into views — and reading the answer off the flat picture.

Several of the questions below run a rule backwards: they give you the count and ask for the step, or give you the edges and ask for the shape. Try each before turning the page.

A Sierpiński carpet is begun from a square of side $81$ cm. At step $4$, how long is the side of each remaining square, and how many are there?

A Koch snowflake has $192$ sides. Which step is it at?

A prism has $30$ edges and a pyramid also has $30$ edges. How many faces has each?

An ant walks across the surface of a cube of side $6$ cm, from one corner to the opposite one. How far does it go?

A solid has a square top view and a triangular front view. Name one such solid, and then a different one.

Starting from a triangle of area $1$, what is the area of the Sierpiński triangle at step $5$? Is it more or less than a tenth?
