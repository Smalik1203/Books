Using What You Know

This chapter had two halves and one habit. A fractal is handled by asking what one step does and then doing it $n$ times: multiply the count, divide the size, multiply the area by a fixed fraction. A solid is handled by flattening it — into a net, or into views — and reading the answer off the flat picture.

Several of the questions below run a rule backwards: they give you the count and ask for the step, or give you the edges and ask for the shape. Try each before reading what follows it.

A Sierpiński carpet is begun from a square of side $243$ cm. At step $5$, how long is the side of each remaining square, and how many are there?

Each step cuts every square into nine, so the side is divided by $3$ and the count is multiplied by $8$. Five steps of each.

side $243 \div 3^5 = 243 \div 243 = 1$ cm
how many $8^5 = 32768$

So the area left is $32768$ cm2 out of the $59049$ the square began with — a little over half, after five steps of throwing away a ninth.

A Koch snowflake has $192$ sides. Which step is it at?

The formula $S_n = 3 \times 4^{\,n}$ is usually read forwards. Here it is read backwards, which needs nothing new — divide by $3$ and ask which power of $4$ is left.

so $3 \times 4^{\,n} = 192$, giving $4^{\,n} = 64$
and $4^3 = 64$, so $n = 3$

Part I's chapter on powers is what makes that last line quick: recognising $64$ as $4^3$ is the same skill as recognising it as $8^2$ or $2^6$.

A prism has $30$ edges and a pyramid also has $30$ edges. How many faces has each?

A prism has $3n$ edges and a pyramid $2n$, so the same edge count means different polygons.
