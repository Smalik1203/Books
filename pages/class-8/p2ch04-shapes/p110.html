What to carry forward

One step, then $n$ steps. Every fractal question here was answered by asking what one step does to a count, a length or an area, and then doing it $n$ times — which is a power. Counts multiply, lengths divide, and areas take a fixed fraction, so a question about step $40$ costs no more work than one about step $2$. The only care needed is to keep the three apart: at step $n$ of the carpet there are $8^n$ squares of side $\tfrac{1}{3^n}$ covering $\left(\tfrac89\right)^n$ of the paper, and mixing up which of those is being asked for is the commonest way to go wrong.

Flatten it. A solid is hard to reason about and a flat picture is easy, so unfold it. A net answers questions about distance across a surface and about area; the three views answer questions about shape; and both are exact, which a perspective drawing is not. When a question about a box looks impossible, the first move is almost always to lay the box out flat and look again.

Check a solid's counts with faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges. It comes to $2$ for every solid in this chapter, and a count that fails the test is a count with a mistake in it. It also finds the missing number when two of the three are given, which is quicker than counting edges on a drawing and a great deal more reliable.

The two halves of this chapter looked unrelated and were not. A fractal is too detailed to draw and a solid is too round to draw, and both were handled by refusing to draw them — by writing down what one step does, or by cutting the surface open and laying it flat. Neither shape was ever seen whole, and neither needed to be.
