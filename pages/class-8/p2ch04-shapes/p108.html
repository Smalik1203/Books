Type 7 · The shortest route across a box

Example 12

A box is $15$ cm by $6$ cm by $3$ cm. An ant crosses its surface from one corner to the opposite one. Work out all three unfoldings and find the shortest route.

Solution.

Step 1 $\sqrt{225 + 81} = \sqrt{306} \approx 17.5$ cm sides $15$ and $6 + 3$
Step 2 $\sqrt{441 + 9} = \sqrt{450} \approx 21.2$ cm sides $15 + 6$ and $3$
Step 3 $\sqrt{324 + 36} = \sqrt{360} \approx 19.0$ cm sides $15 + 3$ and $6$
Answer About 17.5 cm, from the first unfolding

The longest side set against the other two added together wins again.

Example 13

A glass tank is $20$ cm long, $12$ cm wide and $9$ cm high. An ant walks on the outside from a bottom corner to the opposite top corner. How long is its shortest route, and how much shorter is it than walking along three edges?

Solution.

Step 1 $20$ against $12 + 9 = 21$ the most balanced pair
Step 2 $d^2 = 20^2 + 21^2 = 400 + 441 = 841$ the theorem
Step 3 $d = \sqrt{841} = 29$ cm $29 \times 29 = 841$
Step 4 $20 + 12 + 9 = 41$ cm along three edges
Answer 29 cm, which is $41 - 29 = 12$ cm shorter

The other two unfoldings give $\sqrt{1105} \approx 33.2$ cm and $\sqrt{985} \approx 31.4$ cm.

Type 8 · Front, top and side views

Example 14

Three cubes are laid in a row, and a fourth cube is put on top of the middle one. Describe the front, top and side views.

Solution.

Step 1 front: three squares in a row, with one more above the middle 4 squares seen
Step 2 top: three squares in a row the top cube hides one
Step 3 side: two squares, one above the other the row is end-on
Answer Front: an upside-down T of 4 squares. Top: a row of 3. Side: a column of 2
