4.2The Sierpiński Carpet

The Polish mathematician Wacław Sierpiński described this shape in 1916. Start with a square. Cut it into nine equal squares, like a noughts-and-crosses grid, and remove the middle one. Then do the same to each of the eight squares that are left. Then do it again to every square left after that, and go on for ever.

No drawing can show the finished carpet. Every drawing shows one step on the way to it. But we can count what each step contains, and that is where the mathematics is.

Let $R_n$ be the number of squares left at step $n$, and let $H_n$ be the number of holes. Each square that is left becomes eight squares at the next step. So

$$R_{n+1} = 8 R_n, \qquad\text{and since } R_0 = 1, \qquad R_n = 8^{\,n}.$$

Holes need more care. At each step, every square that is left gets one new hole in its middle, and the old holes stay. So $H_{n+1} = H_n + R_n$. The holes add up as a sum, and not as a single power of $8$.

step 0 $R_0 = 1$,   $H_0 = 0$
step 1 $R_1 = 8$,   $H_1 = 1$
step 2 $R_2 = 64$,   $H_2 = 1 + 8 = 9$
step 3 $R_3 = 512$,   $H_3 = 1 + 8 + 64 = 73$

Now look at the area. Take the area of the first square to be $1$. At each step, every square loses one ninth of its area, so eight ninths is left. At the next step, eight ninths of that is left, and so on.

$$\text{area at step } n \;=\; \left(\frac89\right)^{\! n}.$$
