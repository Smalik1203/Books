Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): At step $2$ the Sierpiński triangle has $9$ triangles.

    Reason (R): Each step replaces every triangle by three smaller ones.

  1. Assertion (A): A prism can have $20$ edges.

    Reason (R): The number of edges of a prism is a multiple of $3$.

  1. Assertion (A): The perimeter of the Koch snowflake grows without limit.

    Reason (R): At every step the number of sides is multiplied by $4$.

  1. Assertion (A): In an isometric drawing of a cube, every edge drawn has the same length.

    Reason (R): An isometric drawing shows a cube's right angles as right angles.

Very short answer
  1. How many faces, edges and vertices has a pyramid whose base has seven sides?
  1. At step $5$ of the Sierpiński carpet, each remaining square's side is what fraction of the first square's side?
  1. A net is made of $2$ nine-sided polygons and $9$ rectangles. What solid does it fold into, and how many edges has that solid?
Short answer
  1. A Sierpiński carpet is begun from a square of side $135$ cm. At step $3$, find the side of each remaining square, how many there are, and the area left.
  1. A solid has $10$ faces and $16$ edges. How many vertices has it? Show that it cannot be a prism or a pyramid.
  1. A box with no lid is $8$ cm long, $5$ cm wide and $3$ cm high. How much card does its net use?
  1. An ant crosses the surface of a box $12$ cm by $5$ cm by $4$ cm from one corner to the opposite one. Find the length of its shortest route.
Long answer
  1. A Koch snowflake is begun from a triangle of side $81$ cm. Find the number of sides and the perimeter at steps $1$, $2$ and $3$. Show that at step $3$ the perimeter is more than twice what it was at the start.
  1. A prism has $10$ faces. A pyramid has the same number of edges as the prism. Find the number of sides of the pyramid's base, and its faces and vertices. Check faces $+$ vertices $-$ edges for both solids.
  1. Three cubes are laid in a row from left to right. A fourth cube is put on top of the left-hand cube, and a fifth is put behind the right-hand cube. Draw the front view, the top view and the side view from the right, and say how many squares each shows.
