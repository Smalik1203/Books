4.3The Sierpiński Triangle

The same idea works with a triangle. Take an equilateral triangle. Mark the midpoint of each side, join the three midpoints, and remove the middle triangle that this makes. Three triangles are left, each with sides half as long as the first. Do the same to each of them, and go on for ever.

Why do the midpoints cut the triangle into four matching triangles? Look at a corner piece. Two of its sides are halves of the sides of the big triangle, and the angle between them is the big triangle's angle. So the three corner pieces are copies of one another. The middle piece has the three joining lines as its sides, and each of these is also half a side of the big triangle. So it matches the other three.

We count as we did for the carpet, with three where the carpet had eight. At step $n$ there are $3^{\,n}$ triangles left. One of every four matching triangles is removed at each step, so three quarters of the area is kept.

$$\text{area at step } n \;=\; \left(\frac34\right)^{\! n}.$$
Counting the triangle
  • At step $n$ there are $3^{\,n}$ triangles, with sides $\tfrac{1}{2^{\,n}}$ as long as the first.
  • The area left at step $n$ is $\left(\tfrac34\right)^{n}$ of the first triangle.
