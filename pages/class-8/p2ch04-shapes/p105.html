Type 2 · The Sierpiński triangle and its area

Example 3

A Sierpiński triangle is begun from an equilateral triangle of side $16$ cm. At step $2$, how long is each side, how many triangles are there, and what fraction of the first area is left?

Solution.

Step 1 $16 \div 2 \div 2 = 4$ cm half each step
Step 2 $3 \times 3 = 9$ triangles three for each
Step 3 each is $\tfrac14 \times \tfrac14 = \tfrac{1}{16}$ of the first a quarter, twice
Step 4 $9 \times \tfrac{1}{16} = \tfrac{9}{16} = \left(\tfrac34\right)^2$
Answer Sides of 4 cm, 9 triangles, and $\tfrac{9}{16}$ of the area left
Example 4

A Sierpiński triangle has $2187$ triangles left. Which step is it at, and what fraction of the first triangle's area is left?

Solution.

Step 1 $3^7 = 2187$, so it is step $7$ $3^n$ triangles at step $n$
Step 2 each is $\tfrac{1}{4^7} = \tfrac{1}{16384}$ of the first a quarter each step
Step 3 $\left(\tfrac34\right)^7 = \tfrac{2187}{16384}$ count times one piece
Answer Step 7, with $\tfrac{2187}{16384}$ of the area left, a little over an eighth

The top of the fraction is the number of triangles. The bottom is how many pieces of that size the first triangle would cut into.

Type 3 · The Koch snowflake

Example 5

A Koch snowflake is begun from a triangle of side $54$ cm. How many sides has it at step $3$, and what is its perimeter?

Solution.

Step 1 $3 \times 4^3 = 192$ sides each side becomes four
Step 2 $54 \div 3^3 = 2$ cm a third each step
Step 3 $192 \times 2 = 384$ cm sides times length
Step 4 $162 \times \tfrac{64}{27} = 384$ cm check: four thirds, three times
Answer 192 sides, and a perimeter of 384 cm
