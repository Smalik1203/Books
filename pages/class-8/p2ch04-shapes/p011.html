Why does the first route win? Look at the two shorter sides each time: $9$ and $8$, then $12$ and $5$, then $14$ and $3$. Every pair adds up to $17$, because every unfolding lays out the same three measurements. Only the way they are shared between the two sides changes. When two numbers have a fixed total, the sum of their squares is smallest when the numbers are closest to each other.

So the shortest route comes from the most even pair. We get it by putting the longest measurement against the other two added together. This means we can usually name the winner before doing any arithmetic.

Straight on the net, shortest on the box

A net makes the surface flat, and on a flat surface a straight line is shortest. So the shortest route is a straight line on one of the nets. An endless search among paths becomes a short list of three to compare.

Example 4

A closed box is $6$ cm long, $4$ cm wide and $2$ cm high. An ant walks on its surface from one corner to the opposite corner. Which unfolding gives the shortest route, and how long is it?

Solution.

Step 1 $\sqrt{36 + 36} = \sqrt{72} \approx 8.5$ cm sides $6$ and $4 + 2$
Step 2 $\sqrt{64 + 16} = \sqrt{80} \approx 8.9$ cm sides $6 + 2$ and $4$
Step 3 $\sqrt{100 + 4} = \sqrt{104} \approx 10.2$ cm sides $6 + 4$ and $2$
Answer The first unfolding, about 8.5 cm

The rule named it in advance. Here the two sides always add up to $12$, and $6$ and $6$ is the most even way to share $12$.
