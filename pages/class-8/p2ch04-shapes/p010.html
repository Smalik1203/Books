4.9Three Views

A solid casts a shadow on a wall, and the shadow is flat. Mathematically the same thing is called a projection: what you would see looking at the solid from straight on, with all the depth thrown away.

One projection is never enough, because a great many different solids cast the same shadow — a sphere and a disc both give a circle. So an object is usually drawn from three directions at once: the front view, the top view looking straight down, and the side view from one end.

Engineers draw objects this way because the three views can be measured. A photograph makes distant edges look shorter; a projection does not, so a length on the front view is the real length, and a builder can work from it.
