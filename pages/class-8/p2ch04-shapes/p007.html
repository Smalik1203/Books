$n$-sided prism $n + 2$ faces,   $3n$ edges,   $2n$ vertices
$n$-sided pyramid $n + 1$ faces,   $2n$ edges,   $n + 1$ vertices

A ten-sided prism therefore has $12$ faces, $30$ edges and $20$ vertices without anyone having to draw it. Try adding the faces and vertices in each row and taking the edges away: $12 + 20 - 30 = 2$, and $11 + 11 - 20 = 2$ as well. That is not a coincidence, and it holds for every solid of this kind.

Example 3

A prism has $21$ edges. What are its ends, and how many faces and vertices has it?

edges $3n = 21$, so $n = 7$
so seven-sided ends: $9$ faces and $14$ vertices

And $9 + 14 - 21 = 2$, as it must be. A count that fails that test is a count with a mistake in it.

4.7Unfolding a Solid

Cardboard boxes are not built face by face. They are cut flat, printed flat, stacked flat, and folded only at the end. The flat shape that folds up into a solid is its net, and every solid with flat faces has one — usually several.

Not every arrangement of six squares folds into a cube — some leave a gap and others need two squares in the same place. There are exactly eleven that work, once arrangements that are rotations or reflections of each other are counted as one. Finding them is a good afternoon with squared paper and scissors.
