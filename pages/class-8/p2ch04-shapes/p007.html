4.5Fractals People Built

Sierpiński and von Koch gave these shapes names and formulas. But builders and artists had used the same idea for a very long time before them.

Many temple towers in north and central India are built in this way. A tall central tower has smaller towers of the same shape set against its sides. Those smaller towers carry still smaller ones. Seen from a distance, the outline of the whole temple appears again at three or four sizes.

Rangoli and kolam artists do something similar. A design may have a large lotus in the middle, with smaller lotuses round its edge and tiny ones round theirs. Printed textiles from many parts of the country repeat a motif inside itself in the same way.

None of these is a true fractal. Each one stops after a few steps, because the stone, the colour or the cloth runs out. But the first few steps are all that anybody can ever draw of a fractal anyway.

A rule, repeated

Every fractal in this chapter comes from one short rule applied again and again to its own result. Real buildings and designs show only the first few steps of such a rule.

4.6Solids and Their Flat Parts

Many solids have only flat surfaces. Three words describe such a solid. Its faces are the flat surfaces. Its edges are the line segments where two faces meet. Its vertices are the corners, where edges meet. A cube has $6$ faces, $12$ edges and $8$ vertices, and so does every cuboid.
