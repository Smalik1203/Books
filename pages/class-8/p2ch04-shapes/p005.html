4.4The Koch Snowflake

The Swedish mathematician Helge von Koch described this shape in 1904. It works the other way round: at each step it adds, instead of taking away. Start again with an equilateral triangle. Cut every side into three equal parts. On each middle part, build a small equilateral triangle that points outwards, and then rub out the middle part itself. Each straight side has now become a bump with four sides. Then do the same to every one of the new sides.

Each side becomes four sides, each a third as long. Let $S_n$ be the number of sides at step $n$, and let the first triangle have sides of length $1$. Then

$$S_n = 3 \times 4^{\,n}, \qquad \text{perimeter} = 3 \times 4^{\,n} \times \frac{1}{3^{\,n}} = 3 \left(\frac43\right)^{\! n}.$$
Example 2

A Koch snowflake starts from a triangle of side $18$ cm. How many sides does it have at step $2$, and what is its perimeter?

Solution.

Step 1 $3 \times 4^2 = 48$ sides each side becomes four
Step 2 $18 \div 3 \div 3 = 2$ cm a third, twice
Step 3 $48 \times 2 = 96$ cm sides times length
Answer 48 sides, and a perimeter of 96 cm

The perimeter began at $54$ cm, so after only two steps it is nearly double. The formula agrees: $54 \times \left(\tfrac43\right)^2 = 96$.

Now put the two facts about the snowflake side by side. Together, they tell us something very strange about it.
