A prism has two congruent polygons as opposite faces and parallelograms all round the sides. A pyramid has one polygon as its base and a point above it, joined to every vertex of the base. Both are named after that polygon: a triangular prism, a hexagonal pyramid. A triangular pyramid has another name too — a tetrahedron, four faces of which any one can be called the base.

Count the parts of a prism whose ends are $n$-sided and a pattern appears at once. There are two end faces and $n$ side faces. The $n$ vertices of each end make $2n$ in all. And the edges are the $n$ round each end plus the $n$ joining them.

$n$-sided prism $n + 2$ faces,   $3n$ edges,   $2n$ vertices
$n$-sided pyramid $n + 1$ faces,   $2n$ edges,   $n + 1$ vertices

A ten-sided prism therefore has $12$ faces, $30$ edges and $20$ vertices without anyone having to draw it. Try adding the faces and vertices in each row and taking the edges away: $12 + 20 - 30 = 2$, and $11 + 11 - 20 = 2$ as well. That is not a coincidence, and it holds for every solid of this kind.

Example 3

A prism has $21$ edges. What are its ends, and how many faces and vertices has it?

Solution.

Step 1 $3n = 21$, so $n = 7$ a prism has $3n$ edges
Step 2 $9$ faces 7 sides and 2 ends
Step 3 $14$ vertices 7 at each end
Answer Seven-sided ends, 9 faces and 14 vertices

And $9 + 14 - 21 = 2$, as it must be. A count that fails that test is a count with a mistake in it.
