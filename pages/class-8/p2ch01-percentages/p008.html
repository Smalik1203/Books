NCERT, Figure it Out, §1.4

1.5Going Up, Coming Down

Two years ago a kilogram of onions cost ₹$40$. Today it costs ₹$52$. The rise is ₹$12$. Is that a lot? Twelve rupees is a large rise on a price of ₹$40$, but it would be a small rise on a price of ₹$400$. So a rise is usually given as a percentage of the price it started from.

$$\text{percentage increase} \;=\; \frac{\text{the increase}}{\text{the original amount}} \times 100$$

Here that is $\tfrac{12}{40} \times 100 = 30\%$. We say the price of onions has risen by $30\%$ in two years.

A fall works the same way. A bus route used to carry $250$ passengers a day, and now it carries $200$. The fall is $50$, and

$$\frac{50}{250} \times 100 = 20\%,$$

so the number of passengers has fallen by $20\%$. Notice what went under the line each time: the amount the quantity started from. Not the amount it ended at, and not the larger of the two. The fall of $50$ is $25\%$ of $200$ and $20\%$ of $250$. Only the second answers the question that was asked.
