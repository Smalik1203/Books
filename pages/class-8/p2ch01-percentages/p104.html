What stays the same while the rest evaporates

Boiling drives off water and leaves the salt behind, so the amount of salt is the fixed quantity in the question and the solution is what shrinks. Find the salt first.

salt $15\%$ of $200 = 30$ g, and it does not change
new solution $0.20 \times s = 30$, so $s = 150$ g
boiled off $200 - 150 = 50$ g

Fifty grams of water. The percentage of salt rose from $15$ to $20$ without a grain of salt being added — the whole underneath it got smaller.

Two equal prices, two different costs

The two shawls sold for the same money, so it is tempting to say the gain and the loss cancel. They do not, because the two percentages were taken of two different cost prices, and the one he lost on was the dearer.

the profitable one $1.1 \times c = 990$, so $c = $ ₹$900$
the other $0.9 \times c = 990$, so $c = $ ₹$1{,}100$
altogether cost ₹$2{,}000$, sold ₹$1{,}980$

A loss of ₹$20$, which is $1\%$. Whenever two items go at the same price with equal profit and loss percentages, the result is a loss — and the same arithmetic will tell you it is always the square of the rate: $10\%$ each way loses $1\%$.

Spending is the price times the amount

What has to stay fixed is the product of the two, so if one is multiplied by $1.25$ the other must be multiplied by whatever undoes it.

the condition $1.25 \times m = 1$
so $m = \dfrac{1}{1.25} = 0.8$, a cut of $20\%$

Not $25\%$. A rise of a quarter in the price is undone by a cut of a fifth in the amount, for the same reason a markup of $50\%$ needed a discount of a third.
