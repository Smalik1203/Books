Example 5

A syrup of 400 g is $12\%$ sugar. How much sugar, in grams, must be stirred in to make it $20\%$ sugar?

Solution.

Step 1It has $48$ g of sugar. Adding $s$ g gives $\frac{48 + s}{400 + s} = \frac{20}{100}$.
Step 2Multiply out: $5(48 + s) = 400 + s$, so $240 + 5s = 400 + s$.
Step 3Take $s$ and 240 from both sides: $4s = 160$, so $s = 40$.
CheckThe syrup is now 440 g with 88 g of sugar, and $88 \div 440 = 0.2$.
Answer40
Example 6

A trader wants to allow $10\%$ off the marked price and still make a profit of $17\%$. By what percentage above the cost price must the marked price be?

Solution.

Step 1Selling price $= 1.17 \times$ cost, and it is also $0.9 \times$ marked price.
Step 2So $0.9 \times$ marked price $= 1.17 \times$ cost, and marked price $= \frac{1.17}{0.9} \times$ cost.
Step 3$\frac{1.17}{0.9} = 1.3$, so the marked price is $1.3 \times$ cost: $30\%$ above.
CheckTake a cost of 100. It is marked at 130 and sold at $130 \times 0.9 = 117$, which is a profit of $17\%$ on the cost.
Answer30
