The same idea helps in a shop. Two breakfast cereals stand side by side. One packet holds $200$ g and the other $300$ g, and each label lists what is inside in grams. Which cereal has more nuts in it?

grains Pack A $\ 120$ g $\to 60\%$    Pack B $\ 210$ g $\to 70\%$
sugar Pack A $\ 50$ g $\to 25\%$    Pack B $\ 54$ g $\to 18\%$
nuts Pack A $\ 20$ g $\to 10\%$    Pack B $\ 24$ g $\to 8\%$
the rest Pack A $\ 10$ g $\to 5\%$    Pack B $\ 12$ g $\to 4\%$

Pack B has more grams of nuts, but a smaller share of nuts in each spoonful. The sugar is the same story: B has more grams of it, and less of it in each spoonful. Only the percentages show this. If you want the most nuts in every bowl, Pack A is the better buy.

Check one thing before you trust a table like this. The percentages for one packet must add up to $100$. Sixty, twenty-five, ten and five make a hundred, and seventy, eighteen, eight and four make a hundred. If they do not, something has been left out.

Compare out of a hundred
  • Two amounts out of different wholes are compared by changing both to percentages.
  • The percentages of all the parts of one whole add up to $100\%$.
