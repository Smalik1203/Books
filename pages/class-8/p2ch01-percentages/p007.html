Mix B has twice the badam by weight and half again as much by share, so on this one it wins either way. But look at the sugar: B has more grams of it and less of it per spoonful, and only the percentages say so. Whichever you buy, both are mostly sugar — which is not what the pictures on the front of the packets suggest.

One check before you trust a table like this: the percentages of a single product must add to $100$. Sixty-six and twenty and ten and four is a hundred, and fifty-five and twenty-five and fifteen and five is a hundred. If they are not, a figure has been left out.

1.5Going Up, Coming Down

A kilogram of tomatoes cost ₹$30$ three years ago and costs ₹$42$ today. The rise is ₹$12$ — but twelve rupees is a large rise on a small price and a small one on a large price, which is why the rise is usually reported as a percentage.

$$\text{percentage increase} \;=\; \frac{\text{the increase}}{\text{the original amount}} \times 100$$

Here that is $\tfrac{12}{30} \times 100 = 40\%$, and we say the price of tomatoes has risen by $40\%$ over three years.
