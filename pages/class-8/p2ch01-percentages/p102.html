Behind Each Answer
The same eight questions, worked — and the reasoning that finds them.

The eight are answered below in the order they were asked. Read each one even where your own answer agreed with it, because what is worth taking away is not the number at the end but the sentence near the start that decides which amount the percentage is of. Once that sentence is written down, most of these questions come to a single multiplication.

Where a question names no starting amount, one has been chosen here, and it is usually a hundred — a percentage of a hundred is the percentage itself, which saves a line of arithmetic. The choice never changes the answer: what these questions ask for is a multiplier, and a multiplier is the same whatever it multiplies.

A markup and a discount are multiplied, not added

Sixty per cent on and twenty-five per cent off look as though they should settle at thirty-five per cent on. They do not, because the sixty is taken of the cost price and the twenty-five of the marked price. Take the cost to be $100$ and follow it.

marked $100 \times 1.60 = 160$
sold $160 \times 0.75 = 120$
profit $20$ on a cost of $100$, so $20\%$

A profit of $20\%$, and the two multipliers gave it in one line: $1.6 \times 0.75 = 1.2$. Choosing $100$ for the cost costs nothing — any other starting figure would leave the same multiplier behind.
