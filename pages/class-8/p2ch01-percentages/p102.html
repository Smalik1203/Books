In a class, $60\%$ of the students are girls. Of the girls, $20\%$ wear glasses; of the boys, $30\%$ do. What percentage of the class wears glasses?

The two groups are different sizes, so the two rates cannot simply be averaged. Take a class of $100$: $60$ girls and $40$ boys.

girls $20\%$ of $60$ is $12$
boys $30\%$ of $40$ is $12$
class $24$ out of $100$, so $24\%$

Halfway between $20\%$ and $30\%$ is $25\%$, and the answer is below it — the smaller group is the one with the higher rate, so its rate counts for less.

A bill comes to ₹$560$ after GST has been charged at $12\%$. What did the goods cost before the tax?

The tax was $12\%$ of the price before tax, not of the bill. So ₹$560$ is $112\%$ of what we want.

the equation $1.12 \times \text{price} = 560$
so price $= \dfrac{560}{1.12} = $ ₹$500$

Taking $12\%$ off ₹$560$ instead gives ₹$492.80$, which is the answer to a different question: what is left of the bill once $12\%$ of it is taken off. The two differ by ₹$7.20$ here and would differ by more on a larger bill, so the habit of asking which price the rate was applied to is worth more than the seven rupees suggests.
