Summary
  1. A percentage is a fraction with the denominator $100$. So $p\%$ means $\dfrac{p}{100}$, and $40\% = \dfrac{40}{100} = \dfrac25$, which is two fifths.
  2. To change a fraction or a decimal into a percentage, multiply it by $100$: $\tfrac{7}{20} = 35\%$ and $0.125 = 12.5\%$. To change a percentage into a fraction, write it over $100$.
  3. A percentage is always a percentage of something. $p\%$ of $q$ is $\dfrac{p}{100} \times q$. Given any two of the part, the whole and the percentage, this finds the third.
  4. A ratio becomes a percentage by comparing one part with the whole. Lemon juice and water in the ratio $3 : 11$ make lemon juice $3$ parts in $14$, about $21.4\%$.
  5. Amounts out of different wholes are compared by writing each as a percentage. The percentages of all the parts of one whole add up to $100\%$.
  6. A percentage increase or decrease is the change divided by the original amount, times $100$.
  7. A rise of $r\%$ is multiplying by $1 + \tfrac{r}{100}$, and a fall of $r\%$ is multiplying by $1 - \tfrac{r}{100}$. Changes one after another multiply, so a rise and a fall of the same percentage do not cancel.
  8. Profit and loss are percentages of the cost price. A discount is a percentage of the marked price. GST is added to the price after any discount.
  9. Simple interest is paid on the principal alone, so the amount is $p(1 + rt)$. Compound interest is paid on the interest too, so the amount is $p(1 + r)^{\,t}$. A value that falls by $r$ each year, as in depreciation, is multiplied by $(1 - r)^{\,t}$.
