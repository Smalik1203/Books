1.3Of What?

Pooja and Dev each drink a glass of mango juice. Pooja's juice is $20\%$ fruit pulp and Dev's is $15\%$ fruit pulp. Who had more pulp?

We cannot answer yet. The working is not hard, but half the facts are missing. A percentage is a share of something, and we have not been told of what. If each glass held $100$ ml, Pooja had more pulp: $20$ ml against $15$ ml. But suppose Pooja's glass held $150$ ml and Dev's held $220$ ml.

Pooja $20\%$ of $150 = \dfrac{20}{100} \times 150 = 30$ ml
Dev $15\%$ of $220 = \dfrac{15}{100} \times 220 = 33$ ml

Dev had more pulp, even though his percentage was smaller. The two percentages alone could never tell us that. Change Dev's glass to $180$ ml and he has only $27$ ml, so the answer changes again.

Of means times
  • $p\%$ of a quantity $q$ is $\dfrac{p}{100} \times q$.
  • Know any two of the part, the whole and the percentage, and this line gives the third.
Example 2

Find $12\%$ of $350$, and $130\%$ of $60$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{12}{100} \times 350 = 12 \times 3.5 = 42$ of means times
Step 2 $\dfrac{130}{100} \times 60 = 1.3 \times 60 = 78$ the same, for $60$
Answer $12\%$ of $350$ is $42$; $130\%$ of $60$ is $78$

The second answer is larger than the number it came from, and that is correct. A percentage above $100$ means more than the whole.

The same line, read backwards, answers a different question. If $30$ ml of pulp is $20\%$ of a glass of juice, how much juice is in the glass? Call it $q$ and write down what you know.
