A change is a multiplier

Going up by $r\%$ is multiplying by $\left(1 + \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$; coming down by $r\%$ is multiplying by $\left(1 - \dfrac{r}{100}\right)$. A rise of $8\%$ is $\times 1.08$, a discount of $20\%$ is $\times 0.8$, a fall of $37.5\%$ is $\times 0.625$. Getting into the habit of the multiplier will make the rest of this chapter much shorter than it would otherwise be.

Example 4

A salary of ₹$25{,}000$ a month is raised by $8\%$. What is the new salary? A year later it is cut by $8\%$. What is it then?

after the rise $25{,}000 \times 1.08 = 27{,}000$
after the cut $27{,}000 \times 0.92 = 24{,}840$

Not back to ₹$25{,}000$, but ₹$160$ short of it. The rise was $8\%$ of $25{,}000$ and the cut was $8\%$ of $27{,}000$, and those are different sums of money. Section 1.8 is about how far that can be taken.
