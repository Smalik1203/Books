1.4Putting Two Beside Each Other

Eesha scored $42$ out of $50$ in English and $70$ out of $80$ in Science. She lost only $8$ marks in English against $10$ in Science, so she says English went better. Reema says Science, because $70$ is more than $42$. Vishu says the two cannot be compared at all, because the papers were out of different totals.

Vishu is the one who has seen the difficulty, and percentages are the answer to it. Put both scores out of a hundred and they become comparable, because they are now counted in the same units.

English $\dfrac{42}{50} \times 100 = 84\%$
Science $\dfrac{70}{80} \times 100 = 87.5\%$

Science went better, by three and a half marks in every hundred. Neither of the first two arguments was silly — marks lost and marks gained are both real — but each compared a number from one paper with a number from another, and those numbers were never in the same units to begin with.

The same move settles an argument in a shop. Two badam drink mixes stand side by side, one packet of $150$ g and one of $200$ g, and the labels list the ingredients in grams. Which has more badam in it?

sugar Mix A $\ 99$ g $\to 66\%$    Mix B $\ 110$ g $\to 55\%$
milk solids Mix A $\ 30$ g $\to 20\%$    Mix B $\ 50$ g $\to 25\%$
badam powder Mix A $\ 15$ g $\to 10\%$    Mix B $\ 30$ g $\to 15\%$
the rest Mix A $\ 6$ g $\to 4\%$    Mix B $\ 10$ g $\to 5\%$
