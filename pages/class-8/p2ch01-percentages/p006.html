1.4Putting Two Beside Each Other

Rohan scored $36$ out of $50$ in Hindi and $64$ out of $80$ in Maths. He lost only $14$ marks in Hindi and $16$ in Maths, so he says Hindi went better. His sister Anjali says Maths went better, because $64$ is more than $36$. Their friend Kiran says the two scores cannot be compared at all, because the two papers had different totals. Who is right?

Kiran has seen the real problem, and percentages solve it. Write both scores out of a hundred. Then they are counted in the same units, and they can be compared.

Hindi $\dfrac{36}{50} \times 100 = 72\%$
Maths $\dfrac{64}{80} \times 100 = 80\%$

Maths went better, by eight marks in every hundred. Rohan and Anjali were not being silly. Marks lost and marks scored are both real. But each of them compared a number from one paper with a number from another, and those numbers were not in the same units.
