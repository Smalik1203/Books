Example 5

Nikhil buys pencil boxes at ₹$45$ each and sells them at a profit of $20\%$. He sells water bottles at ₹$90$ each, at a profit of $25\%$. Find the selling price of a pencil box and the cost price of a water bottle.

Solution.

Step 1 selling price $= 45 \times 1.2 = $ ₹$54$ box: cost known
Step 2 $1.25 \times \text{cost} = 90$ bottle: cost unknown
Step 3 cost $= \dfrac{90}{1.25} = $ ₹$72$ divide by the multiplier
Answer A pencil box sells for ₹$54$; a water bottle cost ₹$72$

Taking $25\%$ off ₹$90$ gives ₹$67.50$, which is wrong: a profit of ₹$22.50$ on ₹$67.50$ is a third, not a quarter.

Example 6

Leela bought $20$ kg of dal at ₹$90$ a kilogram. The dal was getting old, so she sold all of it for ₹$1{,}620$. What was her loss per cent?

Solution.

Step 1 $20 \times 90 = $ ₹$1{,}800$ the cost
Step 2 $1800 - 1620 = $ ₹$180$ the loss
Step 3 $\dfrac{180}{1800} \times 100 = 10\%$ loss on the cost
Answer A loss of $10\%$

Tax is the third thing that changes a price, and it pushes the price up. The Goods and Services Tax (GST) is given as a percentage. It is added to the price you would otherwise pay, so it appears on the bill as a separate line. The total is what you pay.

to pay ₹$2{,}520.00$

This is $2400 \times 1.05$, the multiplier again. A bill prints the rate of tax and the amount it is charged on, and the two should agree with the total. Look at a bill at home and check that they do.
