1.7The Same Rise, Again

A bank offers $10\%$ per annum on a fixed deposit. Per annum means for every year, so ₹$6{,}000$ left with the bank for a year earns $10\%$ of $6{,}000 = $ ₹$600$. The ₹$6{,}000$ is the principal, the $10\%$ is the rate, and the ₹$600$ is the interest.

Leave the money for three years and the answer depends on something the rate does not mention: what happens to each year's interest.

If the interest is paid out every year, the principal stays ₹$6{,}000$ and earns ₹$600$ again and again.

three years $6{,}000 + 3 \times 600 = $ ₹$7{,}800$

If the interest is left in the account, it becomes part of the principal, and next year's $10\%$ is taken of a larger amount. Each year is a multiplication by $1.1$.

after year 1 $6{,}000 \times 1.1 = $ ₹$6{,}600$
after year 2 $6{,}600 \times 1.1 = $ ₹$7{,}260$
after year 3 $7{,}260 \times 1.1 = $ ₹$7{,}986$
