Out of Every Hundred

1.1Introduction

Up to 50% off. Hiya scored 83%. About 3% of the water on Earth is fresh. The little symbol turns up on shop windows, report cards and newspaper front pages, and it has one job: it tells you how much of something there is by pretending the whole of it was a hundred.

The symbol is read per cent, from the Latin per centum — by the hundred. So $25\%$ means twenty-five out of every hundred: twenty-five rupees in every hundred rupees, twenty-five marks in every hundred marks, twenty-five people in every hundred people.

Which means a percentage is a fraction, and a very ordinary one — a fraction whose denominator has been fixed at $100$ and then left unwritten.

$$25\% \;=\; \frac{25}{100} \;=\; \frac14$$

Nothing in that is new to you. What is new, and what nearly every mistake in this chapter comes from, is the small word that follows: a percentage is always a percentage of something. Fifty per cent of a hundred rupees and fifty per cent of ten rupees are the same percentage and are not the same money. Worse, the something can change while nobody says so. A price marked up and then discounted is not the price you started with, and a bank account soon stops paying interest on just the amount you first put in. The last two sections of this chapter are about exactly that.

1.2A Hundred Underneath

Surya is mixing paint for a sunset. Three quarters of the mixture is red. What percentage of it is red?

Since a percentage counts out of a hundred, the question is really this: replace $\tfrac34$ by an equal fraction whose denominator is $100$, and read off the top. Equivalent fractions will walk you there.

$$\frac34 \;=\; \frac{6}{8} \;=\; \frac{30}{40} \;=\; \frac{75}{100} \;=\; 75\%$$

Three out of every four is six out of every eight, thirty out of every forty, seventy-five out of every hundred. Nothing changed but the size of the group we counted in.
