Fractions in Disguise

1.1Introduction

Sale: 40% off. Tanvi got 91% in the test. About 70% of the Earth's surface is covered by water. You see the sign % on shop boards, report cards and in the news. It always does the same job. It tells you how much of something there is, as if the whole thing were split into a hundred equal parts.

The sign is read per cent. The words come from the Latin per centum, which means "for each hundred". So $40\%$ means forty out of every hundred: forty rupees in every hundred rupees, forty marks in every hundred marks, forty people in every hundred people.

This means that a percentage is a fraction. It is a fraction whose denominator is always $100$, so the denominator is not written.

$$40\% \;=\; \frac{40}{100} \;=\; \frac25$$

None of this is new to you. What is new is the small word that comes after a percentage. A percentage is always a percentage of something. Fifty per cent of ₹200 and fifty per cent of ₹20 are the same percentage, but not the same money. The something can also change without anyone saying so. A price that is raised and then cut does not always come back to where it started. Money kept in a bank earns interest on its interest. The last two sections of this chapter are about exactly that.

What a percentage is
  • $p\%$ means $p$ out of every $100$, which is the fraction $\dfrac{p}{100}$.
  • A percentage is always a percentage of some whole amount.
