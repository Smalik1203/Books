Notice that the unknown is the whole this time, and that the equation did not care. Chapter 6 of Part I said that anything may be done to an equation provided it is done to both sides; this is that rule earning its keep.

A ratio becomes a percentage the same way. Millet kanji is made by boiling millet and water in the ratio $2 : 7$. What percentage of the mixture is millet?

The trap is to write $\tfrac27$. The ratio compares millet with water, and a percentage compares millet with the whole mixture — which is $2 + 7 = 9$ parts. So millet is $2$ parts in $9$.

$$\frac29 \times 100 = 22.22\ldots \approx 22.2\%$$

Water is the rest, $77.8\%$, and the two come to $100\%$ as they must. Before dividing anything, it is worth a guess: half of $9$ is $4.5$, so $2$ is well under half; a fifth of $9$ is $1.8$, so $2$ is a little over a fifth. The answer had to lie between $20\%$ and $25\%$, and it does.

Estimate before you calculate. Percentages have friendly landmarks — $50\%$ is half, $25\%$ is half of that, $10\%$ moves the point one place and $1\%$ moves it two — and most questions can be bracketed between two of them in your head. An answer that falls outside the bracket is wrong, and you will know it before you have written anything down.
