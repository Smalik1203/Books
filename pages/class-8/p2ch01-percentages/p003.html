1.3Of What?

Madhu and Madhav are comparing two kinds of biscuit. Madhu's are $25\%$ sugar and Madhav's are $35\%$ sugar. Who ate more sugar?

The question cannot be answered. Not because the arithmetic is hard, but because half of it is missing: a percentage is a share, and a share of nothing is nothing. If they each ate $100$ g then Madhav ate more sugar, $35$ g against $25$ g. But suppose Madhu ate $120$ g of biscuits and Madhav ate $95$ g.

Madhu $25\%$ of $120 = \dfrac{25}{100} \times 120 = 30$ g
Madhav $35\%$ of $95 = \dfrac{35}{100} \times 95 = 33.25$ g

Madhav still ate more, but only just, and the two percentages alone never said so. Change Madhu's biscuits to $150$ g and the answer changes with them.

Of means times

$p\%$ of a quantity $q$ is $\dfrac{p}{100} \times q$. Everything in this chapter is that one line, read whichever way the question needs. Given the percentage and the whole, find the part. Given the part and the whole, find the percentage. Given the part and the percentage, find the whole.
