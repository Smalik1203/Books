Why the other options are wrong

1 $\tfrac{7}{20} = \tfrac{35}{100}$. (a) runs the two numbers together; (b) is $0.35$ with the point in the wrong place; (c) is $\tfrac{7}{10}$.
4 (a) is $30\%$ of $42$ — the question read backwards. When a percentage of an unknown is given, the unknown is larger than what you were told, so an answer below $42$ can be rejected without arithmetic. (b) adds $30$ to $42$; (d) is $42 \times 3$.
5, 6 The same two prices give a rise of $33.3\%$ and a fall of $25\%$, and each question offers the other's answer as a wrong option. The difference is ₹$20$ either way; what changes is what it is divided by, and that is the price the change started from. $75\%$ is $\tfrac{60}{80}$, a share and not a change.
10 (a) is the discount itself, not the price paid; (b) takes ₹$15$ off instead of $15\%$; (c) adds the discount on.
11 (a) adds the two rises. The second $20\%$ is of $120$, so it adds $24$ and not $20$: $1.2 \times 1.2 = 1.44$. (d) counts only the second rise, and (c) is neither sum nor product.
12 (a) takes $12\%$ off ₹$22{,}400$, but the $12\%$ was of the old salary, so the old one is $22{,}400 \div 1.12$. (d) adds $12\%$ again.
13 (c) adds $20$ and $10$. The second discount is of the reduced price, so the multipliers give $0.8 \times 0.9 = 0.72$, which is $28\%$ off.
17 R is true, and it shows that A is false: $0.9 \times 0.9 = 0.81$, a single discount of $19\%$. So the answer is (d).

Ask what the percentage is of, before anything else. Nearly every wrong answer above is the right arithmetic applied to the wrong whole. Name the base out loud — the cost price, the marked price, last year's population, the solution before boiling — and the working after it is usually one line.

Work in multipliers. A rise of $r\%$ is $\times (1 + \tfrac{r}{100})$ and a fall is $\times (1 - \tfrac{r}{100})$. Changes one after another multiply, so several steps collapse into one number, and that number tells you at a glance whether the end is above or below the start.
