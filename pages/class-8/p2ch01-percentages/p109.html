Why the other options are wrong

Set A, 5 and 6. The same two prices, ₹$40$ and ₹$50$, give a rise of $25\%$ and a fall of $20\%$, and each wrong option is the other question's answer. The difference is ₹$10$ either way; what changes is what it is divided by, and that is the price the change started from.

Set A, 4. $12.6$ is $30\%$ of $42$ — the question read backwards. When a percentage of an unknown is given, the unknown is larger than what you were told, so an answer smaller than $42$ can be rejected without arithmetic.

Set B, 1. Option (b), $20\%$, adds the two rises. They cannot be added, because the second $10\%$ is taken of a number the first has already enlarged: $1.1 \times 1.1 = 1.21$. The extra $1\%$ is ten per cent of the first ten per cent.

Set B, 3. Option (a) subtracts $20$ from $25$. In fact $1.25 \times 0.8 = 1$ exactly, and the shop breaks even — the one case in this section where a markup and a discount do cancel, and they cancel because $\tfrac54 \times \tfrac45 = 1$, not because $25$ and $20$ are close together.
