She gets back three quarters of what she paid: a loss of $25\%$. If the goods brought her ₹$12{,}000$, then $0.75x = 12{,}000$, so $x = $ ₹$16{,}000$ and she is ₹$4{,}000$ down.

What discount would have brought her out level? Ask for the $d$ that takes $1.5x$ back to $x$:

the condition $1.5x - d \times 1.5x = x$
so $d = \dfrac{0.5x}{1.5x} = \dfrac13 = 33.3\%$

A markup of $50\%$ is undone by a discount of a third, not by a discount of a half. The two percentages describe the same gap and disagree because they are measured against different prices — the markup against what she paid, the discount against what she asked.

The same reversal catches a comparison. Ariba says: the number of marbles I have is $120\%$ of what Arun has. What can Arun say about his?

Not that he has $80\%$ of hers. Suppose Arun has $100$ marbles, so Ariba has $120$. Then Arun's share of Ariba's is $\tfrac{100}{120} = 83.3\%$. Ariba has $20\%$ more than Arun; Arun has $16.7\%$ fewer than Ariba. Both sentences are true and the numbers in them differ, because more than Arun counts out of Arun's marbles and fewer than Ariba counts out of Ariba's.
