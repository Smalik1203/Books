Example 7

A motorbike bought for ₹$60{,}000$ loses $10\%$ of its value every year. What is it worth after one year, and after three?

Solution.

Step 1 $60{,}000 \times 0.9 = $ ₹$54{,}000$ after 1 year
Step 2 $60{,}000 \times 0.9^3 = 60{,}000 \times 0.729 = 43{,}740$ after 3 years
Answer ₹$54{,}000$ after one year; ₹$43{,}740$ after three

This loss of value with age and use is called depreciation. Three years did not take away $30\%$. Each year's $10\%$ was taken of a smaller value than the year before: the value goes from ₹$54{,}000$ to ₹$48{,}600$ and then to ₹$43{,}740$.

So one formula covers both directions. The multiplier carries the direction of the change. Growth at $r$ is $(1+r)^t$ and a fall at $r$ is $(1-r)^t$. A quantity that falls by a fixed percentage never reaches zero, because it only loses a share of what is left. That is why an old motorbike is still worth something.

The interest itself is the amount less the principal. On the deposit of ₹$8{,}000$, the compound interest for three years is $10{,}648 - 8{,}000 = 2{,}648$ rupees, and the simple interest is $3 \times 800 = 2{,}400$ rupees.

Simple and compound interest
  • Simple interest is the same every year: the amount is $p(1 + rt)$.
  • Compound interest is earned on interest too: the amount is $p(1 + r)^{\,t}$.
