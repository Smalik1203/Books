Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

Three things came out of this chapter. A percentage is a fraction of a stated whole. A change of $r\%$ is a multiplication by $1 \pm \tfrac{r}{100}$, and multiplications can be strung together. And the whole a percentage is taken of can move between one sentence and the next, which is where the interest lies.

Every question below turns on the third. None of them says which amount is the base — that is the part you have to decide — and in several of them the obvious answer is the one that forgets the base has moved. Try each before turning the page.

A shopkeeper marks his goods up by $60\%$ and then advertises $25\%$ off. Is he making a profit or a loss on a sale, and of what percentage?

A number is increased by $20\%$, and the result is then decreased by $20\%$. What single percentage change would have taken the first number to the last?

In a class, $60\%$ of the students are girls. Of the girls, $20\%$ wear glasses; of the boys, $30\%$ do. What percentage of the class wears glasses?

A bill comes to ₹$560$ after GST has been charged at $12\%$. What did the goods cost before the tax?

A jar holds $200$ g of salt water, of which $15\%$ is salt. How much water must be boiled off to leave a solution that is $20\%$ salt?
