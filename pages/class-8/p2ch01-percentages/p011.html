1.6Bought, Marked, Sold

An item in a shop has three prices. Telling them apart is most of the work in any question about buying and selling.

cost price what the seller paid for it
marked price the price on the tag, before any discount
selling price what the customer actually pays

Farida runs a shop in Lucknow. She buys school bags at ₹$400$ each and marks them at ₹$640$. She sells one for ₹$560$. Two things happened at once, and they are measured from two different prices.

The profit is $560 - 400 = $ ₹$160$, and the discount is $640 - 560 = $ ₹$80$. If the selling price had been below the cost price, the difference would have been a loss instead.

Both amounts can be written as percentages. But they are percentages of two different wholes, and it matters which whole each one uses.

Two percentages, two wholes
  • Profit and loss are percentages of the cost price. The seller wants to know how well the money paid out came back.
  • A discount is a percentage of the marked price. The buyer is offered a cut in the price shown on the tag.
profit per cent $\dfrac{160}{400} \times 100 = 40\%$
discount per cent $\dfrac{80}{640} \times 100 = 12.5\%$
