Exercise Set 4.5
  1. Expand, using the square of a sum.
    1. $(x + 3)^2$
    2. $(2a + 1)^2$
    3. $(m + 5n)^2$
    4. $(4p + 3q)^2$
  1. Expand, using the square of a difference.
    1. $(y - 6)^2$
    2. $(3t - 2)^2$
    3. $(a - 7b)^2$
    4. $(5x - 4y)^2$
  1. Write down each product in two terms.
    1. $(x + 8)(x - 8)$
    2. $(2m - 3)(2m + 3)$
    3. $(a + 5b)(a - 5b)$
    4. $(7 - k)(7 + k)$
  1. For each, say which of the three identities applies — or that none does — and then expand.
    1. $(3 + z)^2$
    2. $(x + 2)(x + 7)$
    3. $(4a - b)(4a + b)$
    4. $(p - q)(q - p)$
  1. Write each as a square or as a product of two brackets.
    1. $x^2 + 12x + 36$
    2. $y^2 - 100$
    3. $4a^2 - 4a + 1$
    4. $9m^2 - 25n^2$
  1. Simplify $(x + 4)^2 - (x - 4)^2$. Then explain the answer by looking at the difference of two squares with $a = x+4$ and $b = x-4$.
  1. Show that $(a + b)^2 - (a - b)^2 = 4ab$, and use it to find $ab$ when $a + b = 9$ and $a - b = 5$.
  1. Two of these are equal and one is not. Say which, and expand all three to prove it.
    1. $(a - b)^2$
    2. $(b - a)^2$
    3. $-(a - b)^2$
  1. Redraw Fig. 4.3 for the case $a = 5$, $b = 2$, marking each of the four pieces with its area as a number. Check that they add to $49$.
  1. Why can Fig. 4.5 not be drawn when $b$ is larger than $a$? Show that the identity $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$ holds anyway, by taking $a = 3$ and $b = 7$.
