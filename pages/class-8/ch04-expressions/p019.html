That last case is worth stating on its own, since it is easy to miss. Any two numbers the same distance either side of a round number have a product you can do in your head: $48 \times 52$ is $2500 - 4$, $95 \times 105$ is $10000 - 25$, and $17 \times 23$ is $400 - 9$.

Example 10

Find $1002^2 - 998^2$.

Squaring either number is unpleasant, so do not. This is a difference of two squares, with $a = 1002$ and $b = 998$.

Solution.

Step 1 $1002^2 - 998^2 = (1002 + 998)(1002 - 998)$ as a product
Step 2 $(1002 + 998)(1002 - 998) = 2000 \times 4$ work out the brackets
Answer $1002^2 - 998^2 = 8000$

Read right to left, the identity turns a difference into a product; read left to right, it turns a product into a difference. Which direction is useful depends entirely on which side is the awkward one.

4.7.1 A fourth result, from the same picture

Expand $(x + a)(x + b)$ and something useful appears.

four products $x^2 + bx + ax + ab$
gather $x^2 + (a+b)x + ab$

The middle coefficient is the sum of the two numbers and the last term is their product. That is why $(x+3)(x+8)$ can be written down as $x^2 + 11x + 24$ with no working at all, and why the square of a sum is only the special case $a = b$.

4.7.2 Answering without finding the letters

The identities are also the tool for a kind of question that looks impossible. Suppose you are told that $a + b = 7$ and $ab = 10$, and asked for $a^2 + b^2$. There is no need to find $a$ and $b$ at all.

identity $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
rearrange $a^2 + b^2 = (a+b)^2 - 2ab$
substitute $= 7^2 - 2 \times 10 = 49 - 20 = 29$

Here $a$ and $b$ are in fact $2$ and $5$, and finding them would have worked too. But the method above did not need them, and would have gone through just as smoothly had the numbers been ones you could not find — which is the real reason to learn it.
