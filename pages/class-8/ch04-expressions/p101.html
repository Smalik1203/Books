Using What You Know
The chapter's ideas, in questions that do not say which one to use.

The chapter left you with four things: brackets can always be removed, every term of one bracket multiplies every term of the other, three particular products are worth knowing by heart, and an identity holds for numbers as readily as for letters. Nothing below needs anything else.

What is different is that no question says which of the four to reach for, and several of them look at first as though they need information you have not been given. Try each one before turning the page.

Two numbers add to $10$ and multiply to $21$. Without finding either of them, work out $a^2 + b^2$ and $(a - b)^2$.

Find $49 \times 51$ in your head. Then find $4.9 \times 5.1$, and say what changed and what did not.

A square field has its side lengthened by $3$ metres, and its area goes up by $69$ square metres. How long was the side to begin with?

Simplify $(2x + 3)^2 - (2x - 3)^2$ without expanding either square.

A number $x$ satisfies $x + \frac{1}{x} = 4$. Find $x^2 + \frac{1}{x^2}$, again without finding $x$.

Show that $x^2 - 6x + 10$ is greater than zero for every value of $x$, including the negative ones and the fractions.
