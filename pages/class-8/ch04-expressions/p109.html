Why the other options are wrong

A1 $(x+5)^2$ is $x^2 + 2(5)x + 25$. Option (a) is the standing error of the chapter — the two rectangles of Fig. 4.3 left out; (c) doubles nothing and (d) doubles the wrong thing.
A3 $8 - x$ is $8 + (-1)x$. Option (a) drops the sign, which belongs to the term; (d) reads the missing numeral as no coefficient rather than as $1$.
A4 The square is on the whole term: $(2a)^2 = 2a \times 2a$. Option (a) squares only the letter, which is what $2a^2$ means.
A6 Degree adds the powers, $2 + 3$. Option (d) adds the coefficient in as well; (a) and (b) take one power and ignore the other.
A7 Both terms inside are multiplied: $3x \times x$ and $3x \times (-4)$. Options (a) and (c) leave the $x$ off the second product, which is the bracket only half removed.
A10 Every sign in the expression being taken away changes, so the $-5$ becomes $+5$. Option (a) changes the first sign only.
B1 $a^2 + b^2 = (a+b)^2 - 2ab = 36 - 16$. Option (c) stops at $(a+b)^2$ and forgets to remove the two rectangles; (b) removes $ab$ once instead of twice.
B2 A difference of two squares with $a = 3x+2y$ and $b = 3x-2y$: the sum is $6x$, the difference is $4y$, and the product is $24xy$. Option (b) halves the middle term; (c) assumes the two squares are equal, which they are only when $y = 0$.
