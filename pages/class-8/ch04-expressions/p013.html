4.6Three Results Worth Knowing

Everything from here on is the last section applied to three particular cases. They are not new mathematics — you could expand any of them from Fig. 4.2 in half a minute — and they are worth learning by heart anyway, because they turn up so often that recognising one is faster than doing it.

A statement that is true for every value of its letters is called an identity. That is a stronger claim than an equation like $2x = 6$, which is true for one value of $x$ and false for all the others.
