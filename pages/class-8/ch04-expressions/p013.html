Expand $(x + 5)(x + 2)$ and $(x + 2)(x + 5)$. You will get the same answer, and the four products will arrive in a different order.

Where is that fact in Fig. 4.2? Say what turning the picture through a right angle does to the rectangle, and what it does to the four labels.

Exercise Set 4.4
  1. Expand.
    1. $(x + 1)(x + 6)$
    2. $(y + 3)(y + 3)$
    3. $(a + 2)(a - 9)$
    4. $(t - 4)(t - 5)$
  1. Expand.
    1. $(2x + 1)(x + 3)$
    2. $(3m - 2)(2m + 5)$
    3. $(4p - 1)(4p + 1)$
    4. $(a + b)(a - 2b)$
  1. Expand and simplify.
    1. $(x + 2)(x + 3) + (x + 1)(x - 4)$
    2. $(2a - 1)(a + 5) - 2(a^2 + 3)$
    3. $(m + n)(m + n + 1)$
  1. A rectangle is $(x + 3)$ metres by $(x + 8)$ metres. Write its area, and say how much bigger the area is than $x^2$ square metres.
  1. Expand $(x + a)(x + b)$ in letters. Then use your answer to write down $(x + 6)(x + 9)$ without doing any of the four multiplications.
  1. Use the last question to explain, in one sentence, why the expansion of $(x + a)(x + b)$ has a middle coefficient of $a + b$ and a last term of $ab$ — and why $(x + 12)(x + 1)$ and $(x + 3)(x + 4)$ therefore cannot be equal even though $12 \times 1 = 3 \times 4$.
  1. Find the value of $k$ for which $(x + 4)(x + k) = x^2 + 11x + 28$.
  1. Expand $(x + 1)(x + 2)(x + 3)$ by multiplying the first two brackets and then multiplying the result by the third.
