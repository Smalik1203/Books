1. Write down each product without expanding term by term.
    1. $(x + 5)(x + 9)$
    2. $(y - 4)(y - 6)$
    3. $(m + 10)(m - 3)$
    4. $(t - 8)(t + 8)$
  1. If $a + b = 12$ and $ab = 27$, find $a^2 + b^2$ and $(a - b)^2$.
  1. If $x - y = 5$ and $xy = 14$, find $x^2 + y^2$ and $(x + y)^2$.
  1. A square lawn of side $a$ metres has a square flower bed of side $b$ metres cut from one corner. Write the grassed area in two ways, one of them a product, and find it when $a = 25$ and $b = 15$.
  1. Show that the product of any two consecutive odd numbers is one less than a perfect square. Try $11 \times 13$ first, then prove it by writing the two numbers as $n - 1$ and $n + 1$.
  1. The number $9991$ is not prime. Use the difference of two squares to find its factors, and say what it was about $9991$ that suggested the method.
  1. Explain why $x^2 + y^2$ can never be smaller than $2xy$, whatever $x$ and $y$ are. Begin by writing down what $(x - y)^2$ must always be.
  1. Show that $(x + y)^2 - (x - y)^2$ is a multiple of $4$ for every $x$ and $y$.
  1. A rectangle has perimeter $24$ centimetres and area $32$ square centimetres. Without finding the two sides, work out the sum of their squares.
  1. Which is larger, $99 \times 101$ or $100^2$, and by how much? Answer without multiplying anything out.
  1. Show that the difference between the squares of two consecutive whole numbers is always odd, and that it is always the sum of the two numbers.
  1. Write $x^2 + 8x + 20$ in the form $(x + p)^2 + q$. Use your answer to say what the smallest value of the expression is, and which $x$ gives it.
  1. Decide which of these are identities and which are equations, by trying two or three values in each.
    1. $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$
    2. $(x + 1)^2 = x^2 + 1$
    3. $3x - 4 = 11$
