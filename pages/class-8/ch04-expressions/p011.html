4.5Cut Both Ways

One cut across a rectangle gave the distributive law; cut it the other way as well and you have the rest of the chapter. A rectangle $(a+b)$ tall and $(c+d)$ wide, cut once each way, falls into four pieces, and their areas — $ac$, $ad$, $bc$ and $bd$ — are together the whole thing.

$$(a+b)(c+d) = ac + ad + bc + bd$$

Read the picture again as an instruction and it says: every term of the first bracket multiplies every term of the second. Two terms times two terms makes four products; two times three would make six; and if you ever finish an expansion with fewer products than that, you have missed one.

Example 6

Expand $(x + 4)(x + 7)$.

Two terms in each bracket, so four products.

$x \times x$ $= x^2$
$x \times 7$ $= 7x$
$4 \times x$ $= 4x$
$4 \times 7$ $= 28$
gather $x^2 + 11x + 28$

The two middle products were like terms and joined. They usually are, and that is why a product of two binomials so often has three terms rather than four.
