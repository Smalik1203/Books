Expanding it confirms the picture in one line: $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - ab + ab - b^2$, and the two middle products cancel — they do so because one bracket has a plus where the other has a minus, which is the whole reason this product is short and the others are not. Here are the three together, worth reading aloud a few times; a result you can say is a result you will notice.

square of a sum $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$
square of a difference $(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
difference of squares $(a+b)(a-b) = a^2 - b^2$

The letters $a$ and $b$ stand for whole terms. In $(3x + 5y)^2$ the $a$ is $3x$ and the $b$ is $5y$, so $a^2$ is $9x^2$ and $2ab$ is $30xy$. Writing down what $a$ and $b$ are before starting is the difference between using an identity and misremembering one.

Both examples below write that step out in full.

Example 8

Expand $(3x + 5y)^2$.

here $a = 3x$,   $b = 5y$
$a^2$ $= (3x)^2 = 9x^2$
$2ab$ $= 2 \times 3x \times 5y = 30xy$
$b^2$ $= (5y)^2 = 25y^2$
answer $9x^2 + 30xy + 25y^2$

Note $(3x)^2 = 9x^2$ and not $3x^2$: the square is on the whole term, coefficient included.

Nothing changes when the sign does. Take $a = 7p$ and $b = 2q$, so that $a^2 = 49p^2$ and $b^2 = 4q^2$ in every one of the three results, and only the middle term can differ.

$(7p - 2q)^2$ $= 49p^2 - 28pq + 4q^2$
$(7p - 2q)(7p + 2q)$ $= 49p^2 - 4q^2$

The second has no middle term at all, and that is the whole point of it: two brackets differing in one sign give two terms, not three.
