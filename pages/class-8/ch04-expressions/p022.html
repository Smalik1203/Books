4.8More Than One Road

The identities are shortcuts, and a shortcut is not the only way through. It is worth seeing a single question answered several ways, because the habit of looking for a second route is both a pleasure and the cheapest check there is.

Take $98^2$. Three of this chapter's results reach it, and so does ordinary multiplication.

square of a difference $(100 - 2)^2 = 10000 - 400 + 4 = 9604$
expanding once $98 \times (100 - 2) = 9800 - 196 = 9604$
difference of squares $96 \times 100 + 2^2 = 9600 + 4 = 9604$

The third is the least obvious and the quickest. It works because $a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)$ read backwards gives $a^2 = (a-b)(a+b) + b^2$, and with $a = 98$ and $b = 2$ the product on the right is $96 \times 100$ — a multiplication nobody has to write down.

All three agree, as they must. That is the point worth carrying: a problem has one answer and often several roads to it, and choosing between them is a matter of which numbers are in front of you. Finding a second road is not wasted work — it is how you find out that the first one was right.
