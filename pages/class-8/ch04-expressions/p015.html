The commonest error in algebra is to write $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$, and the picture says exactly what that leaves out: the two rectangles, which between them are most of the square. At $a = 3$ and $b = 4$ the true answer is $49$ and the wrong one is $25$; the missing $24$ is the two rectangles.

4.6.2 The square of a difference

Now a square of side $a - b$, where $b$ is the smaller of the two. It is easiest to see as what is left of a square of side $a$ after two strips have been taken off — one across the top and one down the side, each of them $a$ long and $b$ wide.

The whole square is $a^2$. Each strip is $a$ long and $b$ wide, so each is $ab$. Removing both removes the corner square twice over, and the corner is $b^2$, so one $b^2$ has to be returned.

start $a^2$
two strips off $a^2 - ab - ab$
corner back $a^2 - 2ab + b^2$
$$(a-b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$

It is the first identity with the sign of $b$ reversed throughout, which is what you would expect: $a - b$ is $a + (-b)$, and squaring $-b$ gives $+b^2$ while doubling $a \times (-b)$ gives $-2ab$. Either argument will do. The picture is the one to keep, because it says why the middle term is the part that changes sign while the two squares do not.

One consequence is worth having in advance. Since the middle term carries the sign and the two squares do not, $(a-b)^2$ and $(b-a)^2$ come to the same thing — squaring destroys the order, exactly as it does with numbers.
