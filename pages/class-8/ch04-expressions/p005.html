The $xy$ was a common factor and came out; what was left behind was ordinary arithmetic on the coefficients. Nothing of the kind can be done to $3x + 4y$, because there is no common factor to take out, and $3x + 4y$ is therefore already as simple as it will ever be. An expression whose like terms have all been gathered is in simplest form.

$3x + 4y = 7xy$ is the most frequent wrong answer in the whole of Class 8 algebra. Test it: at $x = 1$, $y = 1$ both sides give $7$, so the error survives the easiest check. At $x = 2$, $y = 1$ the left is $10$ and the right is $14$. One value can never prove an answer right, which is why the rule is worth trusting instead.

4.3.1 Adding and subtracting

To add two expressions, write them one after the other and gather the like terms. To subtract one from another, change the sign of every term of the one being taken away, and then add. That second instruction is the whole of the difficulty, and it is worth seeing why it says every term.

$$a - (b + c) = a - b - c.$$

Taking away a bundle of two things takes away both of them. If you owe ₹$b$ and ₹$c$ and clear the debt, both go. So the minus sign in front of a bracket reverses the sign of everything inside it, not just the first thing it meets.

Example 2

Add $3x^2 - 5x + 2$ and $x^2 + 7x - 9$.

Solution.

Step 1 $(3x^2 - 5x + 2) + (x^2 + 7x - 9)$ write one after the other
Step 2 $3x^2 + x^2 = 4x^2$ gather the $x^2$ terms
Step 3 $-5x + 7x = 2x$ gather the $x$ terms
Step 4 $2 - 9 = -7$ gather the numbers
Answer $4x^2 + 2x - 7$

Nothing there needed a rule beyond the one about pencils. Subtraction is the same job with one extra step at the start, and the step is where the marks are lost.
