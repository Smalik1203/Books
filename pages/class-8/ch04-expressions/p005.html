4.3Like Terms

Three pencils and four pencils are seven pencils. Three pencils and four erasers are three pencils and four erasers, and there is nothing more to say. The whole of adding expressions is in that difference.

Two terms are like terms when their letter parts are identical — the same letters, each to the same power. The coefficients may be anything at all; they are the only part allowed to differ.

$5xy$ and $-2xy$ likeboth are a number times $xy$
$3x^2$ and $3x$ unlike$x^2$ is not $x$
$7ab$ and $7ba$ like$ab$ and $ba$ are the same product
$4x^2y$ and $4xy^2$ unlikethe powers are on different letters

Like terms can be gathered into one, and unlike terms cannot. The reason is the distributive law again, read from right to left:

$$5xy - 2xy = (5-2)xy = 3xy.$$

The $xy$ was a common factor and came out; what was left behind was ordinary arithmetic on the coefficients. Nothing of the kind can be done to $3x + 4y$, because there is no common factor to take out, and $3x + 4y$ is therefore already as simple as it will ever be. An expression whose like terms have all been gathered is in simplest form.

$3x + 4y = 7xy$ is the most frequent wrong answer in the whole of Class 8 algebra. Test it: at $x = 1$, $y = 1$ both sides give $7$, so the error survives the easiest check. At $x = 2$, $y = 1$ the left is $10$ and the right is $14$. One value proving nothing is why the rule is worth trusting instead.

4.3.1 Adding and subtracting

To add two expressions, write them one after the other and gather the like terms. To subtract one from another, change the sign of every term of the one being taken away, and then add. That second instruction is the whole of the difficulty, and it is worth seeing why it says every term.

$$a - (b + c) = a - b - c.$$
