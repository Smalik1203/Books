A square field has its side lengthened by $3$ metres, and its area goes up by $69$ square metres. How long was the side to begin with?

Call the original side $a$. The new field has side $a + 3$, and the extra area is one square less the other.

extra area $(a+3)^2 - a^2 = 69$
expand $a^2 + 6a + 9 - a^2 = 69$
so $6a = 60$,   $a = 10$

The difference of two squares gives the same answer with less writing: $(a+3)^2 - a^2 = \big((a+3)+a\big)\big((a+3)-a\big) = 3(2a+3)$, so $2a + 3 = 23$. Either route works. What both rely on is that the $a^2$ terms go, which is why the equation is an easy one rather than a squared one.

Simplify $(2x + 3)^2 - (2x - 3)^2$ without expanding either square.

The instruction not to expand is a hint about which identity to use. Two squares are being subtracted, so take $a = 2x + 3$ and $b = 2x - 3$ and use $a^2 - b^2 = (a+b)(a-b)$.

$a + b$ $= (2x+3) + (2x-3) = 4x$
$a - b$ $= (2x+3) - (2x-3) = 6$
product $= 4x \times 6 = 24x$

Expanding both squares gives $4x^2 + 12x + 9 - (4x^2 - 12x + 9)$, which comes to $24x$ as well, after six terms have been written and four of them cancelled. The identity did the cancelling in advance.

Two numbers $a$ and $b$ have $a^2 + b^2 = 20$ and $ab = 8$. Find $a^4 + b^4$, again without finding $a$ or $b$.

The expression asked for has fourth powers in it and the one given has squares, so square what you are given. Since $a^4 = (a^2)^2$, the identity for $(a+b)^2$ applies with $a^2$ and $b^2$ in the places of $a$ and $b$, and its middle term needs only the product.

square it $(a^2 + b^2)^2 = a^4 + 2a^2b^2 + b^4 = 400$
middle term $2a^2b^2 = 2(ab)^2 = 2 \times 64 = 128$
so $a^4 + b^4 = 400 - 128 = 272$

The middle term turning into a plain number is what makes these questions work, and it happens because $a^2b^2$ is $(ab)^2$, and $ab$ was given. The numbers are in fact $2$ and $4$, and $16 + 256 = 272$ agrees. The same step run with the other square gives $(a^2 - b^2)^2 = 272 - 128 = 144$.
