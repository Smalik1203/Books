Four in a row, and one more

The arithmetic first: $3 \times 4 \times 5 \times 6 = 360$, and $361 = 19^2$. Then $7 \times 8 \times 9 \times 10 = 5040$, and $5041 = 71^2$. Twice is not a proof, and the proof is in the choice of which pair to multiply first.

Call the numbers $n$, $n+1$, $n+2$, $n+3$. Pair the outer two and the inner two.

outer pair $n(n+3) = n^2 + 3n$
inner pair $(n+1)(n+2) = n^2 + 3n + 2$

The two differ by exactly $2$. Writing $m$ for $n^2 + 3n$, the product is $m(m+2)$, and adding one gives $m^2 + 2m + 1$, which is $(m+1)^2$.

the square $(n^2 + 3n + 1)^2$

Check it at $n = 3$: $9 + 9 + 1 = 19$, and $19^2 = 361$. The pairing was the whole idea — pair them the other way and the two brackets differ by $3$, and nothing tidy happens.
