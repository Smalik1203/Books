4.6.1 The square of a sum

Take a square of side $a + b$ and cut it once each way, at the point where $a$ ends. The four pieces are a square $a$ by $a$, two rectangles $a$ by $b$, and a square $b$ by $b$.

$$(a+b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$$

The commonest error in algebra is to write $(a+b)^2 = a^2 + b^2$, and the picture says exactly what that leaves out: the two rectangles, which between them are most of the square. At $a = 3$ and $b = 4$ the true answer is $49$ and the wrong one is $25$; the missing $24$ is the two rectangles.

4.6.2 The square of a difference

Now a square of side $a - b$, where $b$ is the smaller of the two. It is easiest to see as what is left of a square of side $a$ after two strips have been taken off — one across the top and one down the side, each of them $a$ long and $b$ wide.
