4.2The Parts of an Expression

Before anything can be done to an expression it has to be read, and reading one means seeing where it comes apart. Take

$$5x^2y - 7xy + 4y - 9.$$

Break it at every $+$ and $-$ sign. The four pieces — $5x^2y$, $-7xy$, $4y$ and $-9$ — are its terms. A term carries its own sign with it: the second term is $-7xy$, not $7xy$, and forgetting that is the single commonest slip in this chapter.

Each term is a product. The things multiplied together to make it are its factors, and the numerical factor is the term's coefficient. So $5x^2y$ has factors $5$, $x$, $x$ and $y$, and coefficient $5$. A term with no letters at all — here, $-9$ — is a constant term: it is the same number whatever $x$ and $y$ turn out to be.

Two conventions save ink and are worth stating once. A coefficient of $1$ is never written, so $xy$ means $1xy$; and a coefficient of $-1$ is written as a minus sign alone, so $-xy$ means $-1 \times xy$.

Example 1

Write down the terms of $3ab - b^2 + 7$, and give the coefficient of each.

terms $3ab$,   $-b^2$,   $7$
coefficients $3$,   $-1$,   $7$

The middle coefficient is $-1$ and not $1$: the sign in front of a term belongs to it.

4.2.1 Naming expressions by their terms

Expressions are named by how many terms they have, and the names come straight from Greek and Latin — mono one, bi two, tri three, poly many, and nomos a part.

monomial one term$7x^3$
binomial two terms$2a - 5b$
trinomial three terms$x^2 + 3x - 4$
polynomial any number of termsall of the above
