Check: $4 : 10$ is $2 : 5$, and $10 : 25$ is $2 : 5$ too. The number $10$ sits between $4$ and $25$ in a special way: $4$ is multiplied by $\frac{5}{2}$ to give $10$, and $10$ is multiplied by $\frac{5}{2}$ again to give $25$. ($-10$ also has $-10 \times -10 = 100$, but the terms of a ratio of amounts are never negative.)

Jug P holds $250$ mL of sugar syrup, with sugar and water in the ratio $1 : 4$. Jug Q holds $500$ mL of syrup in the same ratio. Jug R holds $250$ mL with sugar and water in the ratio $2 : 3$. What is the ratio of sugar to water when P and Q are poured together? When P and R are?

A ratio cannot be added to another ratio. Amounts can, so find the amounts first, dividing each jug in its own ratio.

jug P $250 \div 5 = 50$: sugar $50$ mL, water $200$ mL
jug Q $500 \div 5 = 100$: sugar $100$ mL, water $400$ mL
jug R $250 \div 5 = 50$: sugar $2 \times 50 = 100$ mL, water $3 \times 50 = 150$ mL
P and Q $150 : 600$, which is $1 : 4$
P and R $150 : 350$, which is $3 : 7$

Two syrups in the same ratio, poured together, keep that ratio, however much of each there is. Two syrups in different ratios give a new ratio. Here $3 : 7$ is sweeter than $1 : 4$ and less sweet than $2 : 3$: for every $10$ mL of water there are about $4.3$ mL of sugar, against $2.5$ mL and about $6.7$ mL.

A clock loses $4$ minutes every $3$ days. It is set to the right time at noon on 1 September. What time does it show at noon on 1 October?

September has $30$ days. The minutes lost and the days that pass are in proportion, since the clock loses time at a steady rate.

as a proportion $3 : 4 :: 30 : x$ days to minutes
cross multiply $3x = 4 \times 30 = 120$, so $x = 40$

The clock has lost $40$ minutes, so at noon it shows $11{:}20$ in the morning. The last step is not arithmetic but reading: a clock that loses time is behind, so the $40$ minutes are taken away, not added.
