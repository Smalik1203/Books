Example 2

Kesang made $6$ glasses of lemonade for a celebration and added $10$ spoons of sugar. Her father expected more people to come, so he asked her to make $18$ glasses instead. How many spoons of sugar should she add so that the lemonade is just as sweet?

For the same sweetness, the ratio of glasses of lemonade to spoons of sugar must stay proportional. Each term must change by the same factor.

Solution.

Step 1 $6 : 10 :: 18 : \,?$ glasses to spoons
Step 2 $18 \div 6 = 3$ the factor of change
Step 3 $10 \times 3 = 30$ the same factor
Answer $30$ spoons of sugar, since $6 : 10 :: 18 : 30$

Example 3

Nitin and Hari were building a wall around their house. Nitin built the longer side, $60$ ft long, and Hari built the shorter side, $40$ ft long. Nitin used $3$ bags of cement and Hari used only $2$. Nitin was worried that Hari's wall would not be as strong as his, because she used less cement. Is Nitin right?

Compare the ratio of the length of wall to the bags of cement for each of them.

Solution.

Step 1 Nitin: $60 : 3$, which is $20 : 1$ divide by $3$
Step 2 Hari: $40 : 2$, which is $20 : 1$ divide by $2$
Answer No. The ratios are proportional, $20$ ft of wall to each bag, so the walls are equally strong

Step 1 $5 : 170$ is $1 : 34$ divide by the HCF, $5$
Step 2 write your school's ratio of teachers to students
Step 3 reduce it to its simplest form
Answer It is proportional only if it is also $1 : 34$, one teacher for every $34$ students
