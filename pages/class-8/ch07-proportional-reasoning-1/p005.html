Example 2

Lakshmi makes kheer for $4$ people with $6$ spoons of sugar. Today she has to make it for $12$ people. How many spoons of sugar should she use so that the kheer is just as sweet?

For the same sweetness, the ratio of people to spoons of sugar must stay proportional. Each term must change by the same factor.

Solution.

Step 1 $4 : 6 :: 12 : \,?$ people to spoons
Step 2 $12 \div 4 = 3$ the factor of change
Step 3 $6 \times 3 = 18$ the same factor
Answer $18$ spoons of sugar, since $4 : 6 :: 12 : 18$

Example 3

Two groups of students paint the walls of their school. Group A covers $36$ square metres with $4$ litres of paint. Group B covers $27$ square metres with $3$ litres. Kiran says that Group B's coat will be thinner, because it used less paint. Is Kiran right?

Compare the ratio of area covered to litres of paint for each group.

Solution.

Step 1 Group A: $36 : 4$, which is $9 : 1$ divide by $4$
Step 2 Group B: $27 : 3$, which is $9 : 1$ divide by $3$
Answer No. Both groups used $1$ litre for every $9$ square metres, so the coats are equally thick
Example 4

School X has $6$ teachers and $210$ students. School Y has $8$ teachers and $280$ students. Is the ratio of teachers to students the same in both schools?

Solution.

Step 1 School X: $6 : 210$ is $1 : 35$ divide by the HCF, $6$
Step 2 School Y: $8 : 280$ is $1 : 35$ divide by the HCF, $8$
Answer Yes. Both schools have one teacher for every $35$ students
