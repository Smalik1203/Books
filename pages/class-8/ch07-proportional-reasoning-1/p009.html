Example 8

For the mid-day meal in a school of $120$ students, the cook usually makes $15$ kg of rice. On a rainy day only $80$ students came to school. How many kilograms of rice should the cook make so that no food is wasted?

The ratio of the number of students to the amount of rice has to stay proportional.

Solution.

Step 1 $120 : 15 :: 80 : \,?$ students to kilograms
Step 2 $\dfrac{80}{120} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ the factor of change
Step 3 $15 \times \frac{2}{3} = 10$ the same factor
Answer $10$ kg of rice

Example 8 is a typical problem for proportional reasoning. Four quantities are linked in proportion. Three of them are known, and we must find the fourth.

To solve such problems, write the two proportional ratios with letters, as $a : b :: c : d$. For the ratios to be proportional, the term $c$ must be the term $a$ multiplied by some factor, say $f$, and the term $d$ must be the term $b$ multiplied by the same factor $f$:

(1) $c = fa$
(2) $d = fb$
so $f = \dfrac{c}{a}$ and $f = \dfrac{d}{b}$, which means $\dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{d}{b}$
then $ab \times \dfrac{c}{a} = ab \times \dfrac{d}{b}$ multiply both sides by $ab$
so $bc = ad$

So when $a : b :: c : d$, we have $ad = bc$. Working out these two products is called cross multiplication. In $a : b :: c : d$, the outer terms $a$ and $d$ are called the extremes, and the inner terms $b$ and $c$ are called the means. Since $ad = bc$, the fourth term is

$d = \dfrac{bc}{a}$

Cross multiplication

If $a : b :: c : d$, then $a \times d = b \times c$: the product of the extremes equals the product of the means. Two ratios are proportional when their terms give equal products on cross multiplication. An unknown fourth term can be found by cross multiplication: $d = \dfrac{bc}{a}$.
