Trairāśika — the rule of three

In Examples 2 and 7, three terms of a proportion were known, and the fourth was found from the factor of change. Problems like this come up all the time. A recipe for four people is changed for nine. The cost of three metres of cloth gives the cost of seven. The time for one journey gives the time for a longer one at the same speed. Such problems have a name of their own, and a rule that finds the fourth term directly. Here is one more before we meet the rule.

Example 8

A hostel kitchen uses $18$ kg of atta to make rotis for $240$ students. During the examinations there are $320$ students in the hostel. How much atta does the kitchen need for the same number of rotis each?

The ratio of the number of students to the amount of atta has to stay proportional.

Solution.

Step 1 $240 : 18 :: 320 : \,?$ students to kilograms
Step 2 $\dfrac{320}{240} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ the factor of change
Step 3 $18 \times \frac{4}{3} = 24$ the same factor
Answer $24$ kg of atta

In Example 8, four quantities are linked in proportion. Three of them are known, and we must find the fourth.

To solve such problems, write the two proportional ratios with letters, as $a : b :: c : d$. For the ratios to be proportional, the term $c$ must be the term $a$ multiplied by some factor, say $f$, and the term $d$ must be the term $b$ multiplied by the same factor $f$:

(1) $c = fa$
(2) $d = fb$
so $f = \dfrac{c}{a}$ and $f = \dfrac{d}{b}$, which means $\dfrac{c}{a} = \dfrac{d}{b}$
then $ab \times \dfrac{c}{a} = ab \times \dfrac{d}{b}$ multiply both sides by $ab$
so $bc = ad$
