Summary
  1. A ratio $a : b$ says that for every $a$ units of the first quantity there are $b$ units of the second. The numbers $a$ and $b$ are the terms of the ratio.
  2. A ratio is in its simplest form when its terms have no common factor but $1$. To reduce a ratio, divide both terms by their HCF.
  3. Two ratios $a : b$ and $c : d$ are proportional, written $a : b :: c : d$, if their terms change by the same factor, that is, if $ad = bc$. Adding the same number to both terms does not keep a ratio proportional.
  4. If $x$ is divided into two parts in the ratio $m : n$, the first part is $m \times \frac{x}{m + n}$ and the second part is $n \times \frac{x}{m + n}$.
  5. Both terms of a ratio must be in the same unit before two ratios are compared.
