Compare picture P with picture Q. The width of Q is half the width of P, and the height of Q is also half the height of P. Both measurements have been multiplied by the same number, $\frac{1}{2}$. We say that they have changed by the same factor. So the two pictures look alike.

Now compare picture P with picture R. The width of R is $20$ mm less than the width of P, and the height of R is also $20$ mm less than the height of P. The same amount was taken from both, and yet R looks wrong. So look at the factors instead. The width has been multiplied by $\frac{60}{80} = \frac{3}{4}$, but the height has been multiplied by $\frac{40}{60} = \frac{2}{3}$. The two factors are not the same, so the shape has changed.

Check picture S against picture P in the same way. The width has been multiplied by $\frac{100}{80} = \frac{5}{4}$, and the height by $\frac{75}{60} = \frac{5}{4}$. The factors are the same, so S looks like P.

Pictures P, Q and S look alike because their widths and heights have changed by the same factor. We say that the changes in their widths and heights are proportional.

Changing in proportion
  • Two measurements change proportionally when both are multiplied by the same factor.
  • Adding the same amount to both, or taking it from both, does not keep the shape.

7.2Ratios

In mathematics we describe this kind of comparison with a ratio. The ratio of the width to the height of picture P is written $80 : 60$, and read 80 to 60. The numbers $80$ and $60$ are called the terms of the ratio.

In the same way, the ratio of width to height is $40 : 30$ for picture Q and $100 : 75$ for picture S. So in picture P, for every $80$ mm of width there are $60$ mm of height.
