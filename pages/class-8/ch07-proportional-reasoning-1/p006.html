Count the teachers and the students in your own school, and write their ratio in its simplest form. Is it proportional to $1 : 35$?

A ratio can also be used to copy a shape at a different size.

Example 5

The notice board in a school corridor is $150$ cm wide and $90$ cm high. Draw a rectangle in your notebook that has the same shape.

Solution.

Step 1 width to height is $150 : 90$
Step 2 $150 : 90$ is $5 : 3$ divide by the HCF, $30$
Step 3 $5 \times 2 : 3 \times 2$ is $10 : 6$ any factor will do
Answer A rectangle $10$ cm wide and $6$ cm high, or any rectangle whose width and height are in the ratio $5 : 3$

Your classmates may have drawn rectangles of other sizes. If each has width and height in the ratio $5 : 3$, they all have the same shape as the notice board, and none of them is wrong.

The next example asks what happens to a ratio when the same number is added to both of its terms.

Example 6

When Sanjay was $4$ years old, his grandfather was $64$. What was the ratio of Sanjay's age to his grandfather's age? What will the ratio be when Sanjay is $12$? Does it stay the same?

Solution.

Step 1 $4 : 64$ is $1 : 16$ divide by $4$
Step 2 $8$ years later: $12 : 72$ each age grows by $8$
Step 3 $12 : 72$ is $1 : 6$ divide by $12$
Answer $1 : 16$ at first, and $1 : 6$ when Sanjay is $12$. The ratio changes

When the same number is added to both terms of a ratio, or taken away from both, the ratio usually changes. Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same number is what keeps a ratio proportional.

A missing term can be found from the factor of change, as in Example 2. The factor need not be a whole number.
