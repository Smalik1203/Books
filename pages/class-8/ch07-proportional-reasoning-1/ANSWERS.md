## 7.1 Observing Similarity in Change

### In the running text

- **Image D against image A.** Width $90 \div 60 = 1.5$ and height
  $60 \div 40 = 1.5$: both change by the factor $\frac{3}{2}$, so the two factors
  are the same and D looks like A.

## 7.2 Ratios

### In the running text

- **The factor from $60 : 40$ to $90 : 60$** is $\frac{3}{2}$:
  $60 \times \frac{3}{2} = 90$ and $40 \times \frac{3}{2} = 60$.

## 7.4 Problem Solving with Proportional Reasoning

### Example 4 (teachers and students)

Answers will vary. Worked instance: a school with $24$ teachers and $816$
students has the ratio $24 : 816$, which is $1 : 34$ (dividing by the HCF,
24), so it **is** proportional to $5 : 170$. A school with $30$ teachers and
$900$ students has $1 : 30$, which is **not**.

### Example 5 (the blackboard)

Answers will vary. The page works the instance $360 : 120 = 3 : 1$. Another:
a board $240$ cm by $120$ cm gives $2 : 1$, and a notebook rectangle $8$ cm by
$4$ cm has the same ratio. Classmates' rectangles differ in size but have the
same shape when the ratio is the same; none is wrong.

### Filter coffee

- **Why $20 : 30$ is stronger.** Each cup holds $50$ mL. The strong cup has
  $20$ mL of decoction against $15$ mL in the regular cup, so more of each
  mouthful is decoction. Per millilitre of milk: $\frac{20}{30} \approx 0.67$ mL of
  decoction against $\frac{15}{35} \approx 0.43$ mL.
- **Why $10 : 40$ is lighter.** Only $10$ mL of the $50$ is decoction;
  $\frac{10}{40} = 0.25$ mL per millilitre of milk.
- **Table 7.5.** Compare each ratio with the regular $15 : 35 = 3 : 7$
  (decoction $\div$ milk $= 0.43$):

  | decoction | milk | simplest form | decoction ÷ milk | coffee |
  |---:|---:|---|---:|---|
  | 300 | 600 | $1 : 2$ | 0.5 | **stronger** |
  | 150 | 500 | $3 : 10$ | 0.3 | **lighter** |
  | 200 | 400 | $1 : 2$ | 0.5 | **stronger** |
  | 24 | 56 | $3 : 7$ | 0.43 | **regular** |
  | 100 | 300 | $1 : 3$ | 0.33 | **lighter** |

### Exercise Set 7.1

1. **(i), (iv) and (vi)** are true. Cross products:
   - (i) $4 \times 21 = 84 = 7 \times 12$ — true.
   - (ii) $8 \times 6 = 48$, $3 \times 24 = 72$ — false.
   - (iii) $7 \times 7 = 49$, $12 \times 12 = 144$ — false.
   - (iv) $21 \times 10 = 210 = 6 \times 35$ — true.
   - (v) $12 \times 12 = 144$, $18 \times 28 = 504$ — false.
   - (vi) $24 \times 3 = 72 = 8 \times 9$ — true.

2. Answers will vary. Multiply both terms by the same number:
   **$8 : 18$, $12 : 27$, $16 : 36$**.

3. $18 : 24$ is $3 : 4$, so the second term is $\frac{4}{3}$ of the first:
   **$3 : 4$, $12 : 16$, $20 : \frac{80}{3}$ (that is $26\frac{2}{3}$), $27 : 36$**.

4. Measured on Fig. 7.6 (drawn to the millimetre): A is $8$ mm by $24$ mm,
   B $18$ mm by $12$ mm, C $25$ mm by $10$ mm, D $30$ mm by $10$ mm, E $15$ mm by
   $6$ mm. Shorter to longer side: A $1 : 3$, B $2 : 3$, C $2 : 5$, D $1 : 3$,
   E $2 : 5$. So **A and D are similar**, **C and E are similar**, and B is
   similar to none of them. Turning a rectangle does not change its shape.

5. Fig. 7.7 is $4$ cm by $3$ cm, a ratio of $4 : 3$. Answers will vary: for
   example **$2$ cm by $1.5$ cm** and **$8$ cm by $6$ cm**. Classmates'
   rectangles may be of other sizes; if the ratio of width to height is $4 : 3$
   they have the same shape and are not wrong.

6. Count one repeat of each pattern.
   - (a) Every $5$ bricks along, the three rows hold $3$, $2$ and $1$ dark bricks:
     $6$ dark and $15 - 6 = 9$ light. Light to dark $9 : 6 = $ **$3 : 2$**.
   - (b) Every $4$ bricks along, the seven rows hold $1, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1$ dark
     bricks: $12$ dark and $28 - 12 = 16$ light. Light to dark
     $16 : 12 = $ **$4 : 3$**.

7. Answers will vary. Worked instance: head $22$ cm, torso $60$ cm, arms
   $66$ cm, legs $90$ cm. Then head : torso $= 22 : 60 = 11 : 30$,
   torso : arms $= 60 : 66 = 10 : 11$, torso : legs $= 60 : 90 = 2 : 3$. A
   drawing at one tenth of the size has head $2.2$ cm, torso $6$ cm, arms
   $6.6$ cm and legs $9$ cm. A drawing whose ratios match looks more lifelike,
   because a real body's parts keep roughly these ratios whatever its size.

### Think and Reflect (Puneeth's father)

**Less time.** The distance is $50 \times 2 = 100$ km. At $75$ km/h it takes
$100 \div 75 = 1\frac{1}{3}$ hours, that is **1 hour 20 minutes**. It cannot be
written as $50 : 2 :: 75 :$ ___, which would give $3$ hours — more time at a
higher speed. Speed and time here are in inverse proportion (Proportional
Reasoning-2).

### Activity 2 (shampoo)

$6 : 180 = 1 : 30$ but $2 : 154 = 1 : 77$, so the prices are **not**
proportional to the volumes. Price per millilitre from Table 7.10: sachet
about ₹0.33, small bottle about ₹0.86, medium about ₹0.81, large ₹0.54. Reasons
will vary: packing, transport and shop costs are not proportional to volume,
and companies set prices to sell particular sizes. Answers on waste will vary:
larger bottles and refills use less plastic for each millilitre.

## 7.6 Unit Conversions

### Exercise Set 7.4

1. $600 : 900 = $ **$2 : 3$** (HCF 300).
2. One bus holds $162 \div 3 = 54$. $204 \div 54 \approx 3.78$, so **4 buses**. They
   seat $4 \times 54 = 216$, so **not all full**: $216 - 204 = 12$ seats are empty.
3. People per sq km: Delhi $30\,000\,000 \div 1484 \approx 20\,216$; Mumbai
   $20\,000\,000 \div 550 \approx 36\,364$. **Mumbai** is more crowded: it has more
   people on each square kilometre, though fewer people in all.
4. The neck is $\frac{4}{4 + 6} = \frac{4}{10}$ of the height. For the crane:
   $\frac{4}{10} \times 155 = 62$ cm. Answers will vary for the reader: a
   student $150$ cm tall would have a neck of $\frac{4}{10} \times 150 =$ **$60$
   cm**.
5. $2\frac{1}{2} : \frac{3}{7} :: x : 9$ (*palas* to *niṣkas*), so
   $\frac{3}{7} \times x = \frac{5}{2} \times 9$ and
   $x = \frac{45}{2} \times \frac{7}{3} = \frac{105}{2} =$ **$52\frac{1}{2}$ palas**.
6. After $x$ years: $(1 + x) : (5 + x) = 1 : 2$, so $2 + 2x = 5 + x$ and $x = 3$.
   Harmain will be **$4$ years old** (her brother $8$).
7. $37 : 2 :: m : 1$, so $2m = 37$ and $m =$ **$18.5$ kg**.
8. The plot is $200 \times 500 = 100\,000$ sq ft, that is
   $100\,000 \div 43\,560 \approx 2.296$ acres. Manure:
   $10 \times 2.296 \approx$ **$23$ tonnes** ($22.96$ tonnes).
9. $10$ litres is $10\,000$ mL, which is $20$ mugs of $500$ mL:
   $20 \times 15 = 300$ s, that is **$5$ minutes**.
10. $43\,560 : 1\,500\,000 :: 2400 : x$, so
    $x = \frac{1\,500\,000 \times 2400}{43\,560} \approx 82\,644.63$: **about
    ₹82,645**.
11. Oxen: $20 \times 6 =$ **$120$ hours**. The tractor is $4$ times as fast, so
    $120 \div 4 =$ **$30$ hours** ($1.5$ hours an acre).
12. Copper is $\frac{3}{4} \times 7.74 = 5.805$ g and nickel
    $\frac{1}{4} \times 7.74 = 1.935$ g. Copper costs
    $5.805 \times \frac{906}{1000} \approx 5.26$ rupees and nickel
    $1.935 \times \frac{1341}{1000} \approx 2.59$ rupees. The metal in one coin
    costs **about ₹7.85**.
