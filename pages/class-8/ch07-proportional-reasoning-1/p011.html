To decide which tea costs more, we compared the prices of the same weight, $1$ kg, in both places. Working out the value of one unit first, and then of as many units as are needed, is often called the unitary method. Why might one tea be more expensive than the other? Talk about it in class.

Puneeth's father rode his motorcycle from Lucknow to Kanpur in $2$ hours, at a speed of $50$ km/h. If he rides at $75$ km/h, how long will the journey take? Can we write this problem as the proportion $50 : 2 :: 75 :$ ?

Will the journey take Puneeth's father more time or less time? Think about it before reading on.

This problem looks like the earlier ones, but it cannot be solved with the rule of three. When the speed goes up, the time for the journey goes down, not up. The two quantities do not change by the same factor in the same direction, so the problem cannot be written as $50 : 2 :: 75 :$ . You will meet quantities that change this way in Proportional Reasoning-2.
