Using What You Know

The chapter left you with a short list. A ratio records how two quantities compare, and dividing both terms by their HCF gives its simplest form. Two ratios are proportional when their simplest forms match, or, what comes to the same thing, when their cross products are equal. A whole is divided in the ratio $m : n$ by cutting it into $m + n$ equal groups. And before any of this, both terms must be in the same unit.

Every question below can be answered from that list. None of them says which item to use. Try each before reading what follows it.

A photograph $15$ cm wide and $10$ cm high is enlarged, without being stretched, until it is $24$ cm wide. Will it exactly fill a frame $24$ cm wide and $18$ cm high?

Enlarging without stretching means the width and the height change by the same factor, so the ratio of width to height stays the same. Find that ratio, then the new height.

the photograph $15 : 10$ is $3 : 2$
the new height $3 : 2 :: 24 : h$, so $3h = 48$ and $h = 16$
the frame $24 : 18$ is $4 : 3$ a different shape

The enlarged photograph is $16$ cm high, so $2$ cm of the frame is left empty. To fill the frame it would have to be stretched upwards, and it would then look like Image E in Fig. 7.1. Matching one side is not enough; the two shapes match only when their ratios do.

In a class, the ratio of boys to girls is $5 : 4$. Six more girls join, and now there are as many girls as boys. How many students were in the class at first?

Think of the ratio as groups of the same size: the boys make $5$ groups and the girls make $4$. For the girls to equal the boys they need one more group, and the six new girls fill it. So one group is $6$ students.

boys $5 \times 6 = 30$
girls at first $4 \times 6 = 24$
the class $30 + 24 = 54$ $54$ students
