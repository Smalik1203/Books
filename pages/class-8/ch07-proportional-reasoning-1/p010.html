Mathematicians in India worked with such problems long ago. Āryabhaṭa, who wrote in 499 CE, and others after him called problems of proportion rule of three problems, trairāśika in Sanskrit. Three numbers are given: the pramāṇa (the measure, our $a$), the phala (the fruit, our $b$) and the ichchhā (the requisition, our $c$). The number to be found is the ichchhāphala (the fruit of the requisition, our $d$). Āryabhaṭa's rule is: multiply the phala by the ichchhā, and divide the product by the pramāṇa.

In our words, pramāṇa : phala :: ichchhā : ichchhāphala, and so

pramāṇa $\times$ ichchhāphala $=$ phala $\times$ ichchhā

ichchhāphala $=$ (phala $\times$ ichchhā) $\div$ pramāṇa

Example 9

A car travels $90$ km in $150$ minutes. If it goes on at the same speed, how far will it travel in $4$ hours?

At the same speed, the ratio of time taken to distance covered stays proportional. It is tempting to write $150 : 90 :: 4 : \,?$, but that is not right: $150$ is in minutes and $4$ is in hours. Both ratios must use the same unit of time.

Solution.

Step 1 $4$ hours $= 4 \times 60 = 240$ minutes one unit of time
Step 2 $150 : 90 :: 240 : x$ minutes to kilometres
Step 3 $150 \times x = 240 \times 90$ cross multiply
Step 4 $x = \dfrac{240 \times 90}{150} = 144$
Answer $144$ km in $4$ hours

Talk with your classmates about other ways to get $144$. For example, the car covers $90 \div 5 = 18$ km in each $30$ minutes, and $4$ hours holds eight lots of $30$ minutes: $8 \times 18 = 144$.

Example 10

A small farmer in Himachal Pradesh sells tea in $200$ g packets for ₹$200$ each. A large estate in Meghalaya sells tea in $1$ kg packets for ₹$800$ each. Are the ratios of weight to price in the two places proportional? Which tea is more expensive?

Solution.

Step 1 Himachal: $200 : 200$, which is $1 : 1$ grams to rupees
Step 2 Meghalaya: $1000 : 800$, which is $5 : 4$, not $1 : 1$ in grams, not $1 : 800$
Step 3 Himachal, $1$ kg for ₹$x$: $\frac{1}{5} \times x = 200$, so $x = 200 \times 5 = 1000$ $200$ g is $\frac{1}{5}$ kg
Answer Not proportional. Himachal tea costs more: ₹$1{,}000$ a kg against Meghalaya's ₹$800$
