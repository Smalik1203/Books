Will the journey take more time or less time? Decide before reading on.

This problem looks like the earlier ones, but the rule of three does not fit it. When the speed goes up, the time for the journey goes down. The two quantities do not change by the same factor, so the problem cannot be written as $40 : 3 :: 60 :$ . That proportion would give $4\frac{1}{2}$ hours, more time at a higher speed. The distance is $40 \times 3 = 120$ km, so at $60$ km/h the journey takes $2$ hours. You will meet such quantities in Proportional Reasoning-2.

Activity 2. Visit a shop and note the prices of different sizes of one product, such as toothpaste. Make a table like Table 7.6, and check whether the amount is proportional to the price.

Table 7.6 Sample prices for one toothpaste in four sizes
PackAmountPrice
Travel tube20 g₹12
Small tube100 g₹55
Large tube200 g₹100
Family pack400 g₹180

In this sample table, the ratio of the amount in the travel tube to the amount in the small tube is $20 : 100$, which is $1 : 5$. The ratio of their prices is $12 : 55$. These ratios are not proportional: the small tube gives more toothpaste for each rupee. Check the other sizes in the same way. Why might a company price its packs like this? Which pack would you buy, and what happens to the packaging when many small packs are thrown away?

The unitary method makes the comparison easier. Find the price of the same amount, $100$ g, in each pack.

travel tube $12 \times 5 = 60$₹$60$ for $100$ g
small tube already $100$ g₹$55$ for $100$ g
large tube $100 \div 2 = 50$₹$50$ for $100$ g
family pack $180 \div 4 = 45$₹$45$ for $100$ g

For each $100$ g, the family pack costs ₹$15$ less than the travel tube. A bigger pack saves money only if all of it is used. It also saves packaging: one family pack holds as much as twenty travel tubes.
