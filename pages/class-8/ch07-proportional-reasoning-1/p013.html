7.5Sharing, but Not Equally!

Activity 3. Work in pairs. Collect $20$ counters: buttons, beans or small stones will do. Share them between the two of you in different ways.

If you share them equally, each of you gets $10$. The ratio of your partner's counters to yours is $10 : 10$, which is $1 : 1$ in its simplest form. If your partner takes $8$, you get $12$, and the ratio is $8 : 12$, which is $2 : 3$.

Now suppose the $20$ counters are to be shared in the ratio $3 : 1$. One way is to take them in rounds. In each round your partner takes $3$ counters and you take $1$, so each round uses $4$.

Round 1 your partner has $3$ and you have $1$; $16$ are left
Round 2 your partner has $6$ and you have $2$; $12$ are left
Round 3 your partner has $9$ and you have $3$; $8$ are left
Round 4 your partner has $12$ and you have $4$; $4$ are left
Round 5 your partner has $15$ and you have $5$; none are left

So $20$ counters shared in the ratio $3 : 1$ give $15$ to one person and $5$ to the other. There were $5$ rounds, because $20 \div 4 = 5$, and each share is $5$ times its term in the ratio. Rounds work for any ratio, but they are slow when there are many counters.

Now suppose $63$ counters are to be shared in the ratio $5 : 4$. Taking them round by round would take a long time. There is a quicker way.

Put the $63$ counters into equal groups, so that your partner gets $5$ groups and you get $4$ groups. There are $5 + 4 = 9$ groups in all, so each group holds $63 \div 9 = 7$ counters. Your partner gets $5 \times 7 = 35$ counters and you get $4 \times 7 = 28$ counters.
