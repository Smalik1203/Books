Example 12

A carton holds 100 shirts: 88 are good, 8 have minor defects and 4 have major defects. Jimmy, a trader, will accept only the good shirts. Sujatha, another trader, will reject only the shirts with major defects. One shirt is drawn at random from the carton. What is the probability that it is acceptable (i) to Jimmy? (ii) to Sujatha?

One shirt is drawn at random from 100 shirts, so there are 100 equally likely outcomes.

(i) 88 outcomes are acceptable to Jimmy (Why?), so

$P(\text{acceptable to Jimmy}) = \dfrac{88}{100} = 0.88$

(ii) ${88 + 8 = 96}$ outcomes are acceptable to Sujatha (Why?), so

$P(\text{acceptable to Sujatha}) = \dfrac{96}{100} = 0.96$

Example 13

Two dice, one blue and one grey, are thrown at the same time. Write down all the possible outcomes. What is the probability that the sum of the two numbers on top is (i) 8? (ii) 13? (iii) less than or equal to 12?

When the blue die shows 1, the grey die can show any of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. The same is true when the blue die shows 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6. Table 14.1 lists all the outcomes. In each ordered pair, the first number is on the blue die and the second is on the grey die.
