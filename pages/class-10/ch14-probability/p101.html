Using What You Know

The chapter gave you one rule: count the favourable outcomes, and divide by the number of all the equally likely outcomes. What changes now is the counting. The complement may be much easier to count, two descriptions may share outcomes, or the number of objects may be the thing you have to find. None of the questions below needs anything beyond the chapter's one rule.

Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the product of the two numbers is even?

A product is odd only when both numbers are odd. Each die has 3 odd numbers, so ${3 \times 3 = 9}$ of the 36 outcomes give an odd product, and ${P(\text{odd product}) = \frac{9}{36} = \frac{1}{4}}$. So ${P(\text{even product}) = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}}$. Counting the 27 even products one by one would take far longer; the complement had only one simple condition. The same idea works whenever a question says at least one.

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. What is the probability that it is red or a king?

There are 26 red cards and 4 kings. But the king of hearts and the king of diamonds are red, and they are already among the 26 red cards. So only the 2 black kings are new, and ${26 + 2 = 28}$ cards are favourable. The probability is ${\frac{28}{52} = \frac{7}{13}}$. Adding ${26 + 4 = 30}$ would count two cards twice.

Three coins are tossed together. What is the probability of getting exactly two heads?

List the outcomes in order, so that none is missed: HHH, HHT, HTH, THH, HTT, THT, TTH and TTT. There are 8 equally likely outcomes, and 3 of them have exactly two heads, so the probability is $\frac{3}{8}$. Each coin doubles the number of outcomes: one coin gives 2, two coins give 4, and three coins give 8.

A bag contains 6 red balls and some blue balls. The probability of drawing a blue ball is 3 times the probability of drawing a red ball. How many blue balls are in the bag?

Both probabilities have the same denominator, the total number of balls. So if P(blue) is 3 times P(red), the number of blue balls is 3 times the number of red balls, that is, ${3 \times 6 = 18}$. You did not need to know the total at all.
