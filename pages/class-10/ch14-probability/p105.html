Example 8

Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the product of the two numbers is at most 6.

Solution.

Step 1 first die 1: all six numbers work, (1, 1) to (1, 6)
Step 2 first die 2: (2, 1), (2, 2), (2, 3); first die 3: (3, 1), (3, 2) $2 \times 3 = 6$, $3 \times 2 = 6$
Step 3 first die 4, 5 or 6: only (4, 1), (5, 1), (6, 1)
Step 4 ${6 + 3 + 2 + 3 = 14}$ favourable outcomes, so $P = \dfrac{14}{36} = \dfrac{7}{18}$
Answer $\dfrac{7}{18}$

Example 9

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. The probability that it is neither a king nor a heart is

  1. $\dfrac{9}{13}$
  2. $\dfrac{35}{52}$
  3. $\dfrac{3}{4}$
  4. $\dfrac{12}{13}$

Solution.

Step 1 13 hearts and 3 other kings: ${13 + 3 = 16}$ cards are kings or hearts king of hearts once
Step 2 $P(\text{neither}) = \dfrac{52 - 16}{52} = \dfrac{36}{52} = \dfrac{9}{13}$ complementary events
Answer (a)

Option (b) counts the king of hearts twice, (c) removes only the hearts, and (d) only the kings.

Example 10

All the black face cards are removed from a deck of 52 cards. The rest are shuffled well and one card is drawn. Find the probability that it is (i) a red card (ii) a face card (iii) a king.

Solution.

Step 1 6 black face cards go, leaving ${52 - 6 = 46}$ cards K, Q, J of two suits
Step 2 (i) all 26 red cards remain: $P(\text{red}) = \dfrac{26}{46} = \dfrac{13}{23}$
Step 3 (ii) only the 6 red face cards remain: $P(\text{face card}) = \dfrac{6}{46} = \dfrac{3}{23}$
Step 4 (iii) only the 2 red kings remain: $P(\text{king}) = \dfrac{2}{46} = \dfrac{1}{23}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{13}{23}$   (ii) $\dfrac{3}{23}$   (iii) $\dfrac{1}{23}$
