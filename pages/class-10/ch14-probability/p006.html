Example 6

Savita and Hamida are friends. Ignoring leap years, what is the probability that they have (i) different birthdays? (ii) the same birthday?

Savita's birthday can be any day of the year. Hamida's birthday can also be any of the 365 days of the year, and we assume that these 365 outcomes are equally likely.

(i) if Hamida's birthday is not Savita's, there are ${365 - 1 = 364}$ favourable outcomes, so $P(\text{different birthdays}) = \dfrac{364}{365}$

(ii) $P(\text{same birthday}) = 1 - P(\text{different birthdays}) = 1 - \dfrac{364}{365} = \dfrac{1}{365}$

Example 7

Class 10 of a school has 40 students: 25 girls and 15 boys. The class teacher has to choose one student as class representative. She writes the name of each student on a separate card, all the cards being alike, puts the cards in a bag and mixes them well. Then she draws one card. What is the probability that the name on the card is (i) a girl's? (ii) a boy's?

There are 40 students, and one name card is drawn, so there are 40 possible outcomes.

(i) 25 outcomes are favourable to a girl's name (Why?), so $P(\text{girl}) = \dfrac{25}{40} = \dfrac{5}{8}$

(ii) 15 outcomes are favourable to a boy's name (Why?), so $P(\text{boy}) = \dfrac{15}{40} = \dfrac{3}{8}$

Note. You could also find P(boy) as ${1 - P(\text{not boy}) = 1 - P(\text{girl}) = 1 - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}}$.

Example 8

A box contains 3 blue, 2 white and 4 red marbles. A marble is drawn at random from the box. What is the probability that it is (i) white? (ii) blue? (iii) red?

Drawn at random is a short way of saying that every marble is equally likely to be drawn. So the number of possible outcomes is ${3 + 2 + 4 = 9}$. (Why?) Let W, B and R be the events the marble is white, the marble is blue and the marble is red.

(i) 2 outcomes are favourable to W, so $P(W) = \dfrac{2}{9}$

(ii) $P(B) = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$   and   (iii) $P(R) = \dfrac{4}{9}$
