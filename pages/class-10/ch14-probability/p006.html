Example 7

Class 10 of a school has 40 students: 25 girls and 15 boys. The class teacher has to choose one student as class representative. She writes the name of each student on a separate card, all the cards being alike, puts the cards in a bag and mixes them well. Then she draws one card. What is the probability that the name on the card is (i) a girl's? (ii) a boy's?

Solution.

Step 1 there are 40 students and one card is drawn: 40 possible outcomes one card a student
Step 2 (i) 25 outcomes are favourable to a girl's name, so $P(\text{girl}) = \dfrac{25}{40} = \dfrac{5}{8}$
Step 3 (ii) 15 outcomes are favourable to a boy's name, so $P(\text{boy}) = \dfrac{15}{40} = \dfrac{3}{8}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{5}{8}$   (ii) $\dfrac{3}{8}$

Note. You could also find P(boy) as ${1 - P(\text{not boy}) = 1 - P(\text{girl}) = 1 - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}}$.

Example 8

A box contains 3 blue, 2 white and 4 red marbles. A marble is drawn at random from the box. What is the probability that it is (i) white? (ii) blue? (iii) red?

Solution.

Step 1 every marble is equally likely to be drawn that is what at random means
Step 2 the number of possible outcomes is ${3 + 2 + 4 = 9}$ one for each marble
Step 3 let W, B and R be the events the marble is white, the marble is blue and the marble is red
Step 4 (i) 2 outcomes are favourable to W, so $P(W) = \dfrac{2}{9}$
Step 5 (ii) $P(B) = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$   and   (iii) $P(R) = \dfrac{4}{9}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{2}{9}$   (ii) $\dfrac{1}{3}$   (iii) $\dfrac{4}{9}$

Check that ${P(W) + P(B) + P(R) = 1}$.
