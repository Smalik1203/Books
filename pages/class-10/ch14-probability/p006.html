Note. You could also find P(boy) as ${1 - P(\text{not boy}) = 1 - P(\text{girl}) = 1 - \frac{5}{8} = \frac{3}{8}}$.

Example 8

A box contains 3 blue, 2 white and 4 red marbles. A marble is drawn at random from the box. What is the probability that it is (i) white? (ii) blue? (iii) red?

Solution.

Step 1 every marble is equally likely to be drawn that is what at random means
Step 2 the number of possible outcomes is ${3 + 2 + 4 = 9}$ one for each marble
Step 3 let W, B and R be the events the marble is white, the marble is blue and the marble is red
Step 4 (i) 2 outcomes are favourable to W, so $P(W) = \dfrac{2}{9}$
Step 5 (ii) $P(B) = \dfrac{3}{9} = \dfrac{1}{3}$   and   (iii) $P(R) = \dfrac{4}{9}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{2}{9}$   (ii) $\dfrac{1}{3}$   (iii) $\dfrac{4}{9}$

Check that ${P(W) + P(B) + P(R) = 1}$.

Example 9

Harpreet tosses two different coins at the same time, say a ₹1 coin and a ₹2 coin. What is the probability that she gets at least one head?

Solution.

Step 1 H is head and T is tail; (H, H), (H, T), (T, H), (T, T) are equally likely
Step 2 (H, H) means a head on the first coin (the ₹1 coin) and on the second (the ₹2 coin); (H, T) is a head on the first and a tail on the second
Step 3 for E, at least one head, (H, H), (H, T), (T, H) are favourable each has a head
Answer $P(E) = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so the probability of at least one head is $\frac{3}{4}$

Note. You can also find P(E) from its complement. The event $\overline{E}$ is no head, which has one outcome, (T, T). So ${P(E) = 1 - P(\overline{E}) = 1 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{3}{4}}$.
