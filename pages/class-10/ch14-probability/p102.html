Both probabilities have the same denominator, the total number of balls. So if P(blue) is 3 times P(red), the number of blue balls is 3 times the number of red balls, that is, ${3 \times 6 = 18}$. You did not need to know the total at all.

Ravi and Zoya each choose a whole number from 1 to 3, without telling each other. What is the probability that they choose the same number?

Assume each choice is equally likely. Ravi's number and Zoya's number make an ordered pair, as the two dice did in Table 14.1, so there are ${3 \times 3 = 9}$ outcomes. Three of them, (1, 1), (2, 2) and (3, 3), are favourable, and the probability is ${\frac{3}{9} = \frac{1}{3}}$. This is Example 6 with a year of only 3 days.

Example 1

A die is thrown once. The probability of getting a number that is not a multiple of 3 is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{3}$
  2. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
  3. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  4. $\dfrac{5}{6}$

Solution.

Step 1 the outcomes are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, all equally likely
Step 2 the multiples of 3 are 3 and 6, so $P(\text{multiple of } 3) = \dfrac{2}{6}$
Step 3 $P(\text{not a multiple of } 3) = 1 - \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{4}{6} = \dfrac{2}{3}$ complementary events
Answer (b)

Option (a) is the chance of a multiple of 3, (c) counts the odd numbers, and (d) misses 3.
