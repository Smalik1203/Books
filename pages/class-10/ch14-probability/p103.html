Problem 2

Two coins are tossed together. The probability of getting at most one head is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{4}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  3. $\dfrac{3}{4}$
  4. $\dfrac{2}{3}$
Solution

at most one head: (H, T), (T, H), (T, T), so ${P = \frac{3}{4}}$

The answer is (c); (d) takes the outcomes as 0, 1 or 2 heads, which are not equally likely, (b) is exactly one head, and (a) is no head.

Problem 3

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. The probability that it is neither a king nor a heart is

  1. $\dfrac{9}{13}$
  2. $\dfrac{35}{52}$
  3. $\dfrac{3}{4}$
  4. $\dfrac{12}{13}$
Solution

kings or hearts: ${13 + 3 = 16}$ cards, so ${P = \frac{52 - 16}{52} = \frac{36}{52} = \frac{9}{13}}$

The answer is (a); (b) counts the king of hearts twice, (c) removes only the hearts, and (d) only the kings.

Problem 4

Two dice are thrown. The probability that the sum of the two numbers is a prime number is

  1. $\dfrac{5}{11}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  3. $\dfrac{7}{18}$
  4. $\dfrac{5}{12}$
Solution

sums 2, 3, 5, 7, 11 have ${1 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 2 = 15}$ outcomes, so ${P = \frac{15}{36} = \frac{5}{12}}$

The answer is (d); (a) treats the 11 sums as equally likely, (c) misses the sum 2, and (b) guesses half.
