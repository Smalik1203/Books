Example 2

A letter is chosen at random from the letters of the word PROBABILITY. The probability that it is B is

  1. $\dfrac{2}{9}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{11}$
  3. $\dfrac{1}{9}$
  4. $\dfrac{2}{11}$

Solution.

Step 1 PROBABILITY has 11 letters, so there are 11 outcomes each letter equally likely
Step 2 B appears twice, so 2 outcomes are favourable: $P(B) = \dfrac{2}{11}$
Answer (d)

Option (a) counts only the 9 different letters, and (b) counts B once.

Type 2 · Using the complement

Example 3

A number is chosen at random from 1 to 50. Find the probability that it is not a multiple of 4.

Solution.

Step 1 of the 50 equally likely outcomes, the multiples of 4 are 4, 8, …, 48, and $48 \div 4 = 12$ easier to count
Step 2 $P(\text{not a multiple of } 4) = 1 - \dfrac{12}{50} = \dfrac{38}{50} = \dfrac{19}{25}$ complementary events
Answer $\dfrac{19}{25}$

Type 3 · Coins, and a coin with a die

Example 4

Two coins are tossed together. The probability of getting at most one head is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{4}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  3. $\dfrac{3}{4}$
  4. $\dfrac{2}{3}$

Solution.

Step 1 the outcomes are HH, HT, TH and TT, all equally likely
Step 2 at most one head is HT, TH or TT: $P(\text{at most one head}) = \dfrac{3}{4}$ no head, or one
Answer (c)

Option (d) takes the outcomes as 0, 1 or 2 heads, which are not equally likely; (b) is exactly one head, and (a) is no head.
