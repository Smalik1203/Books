Example 5

A coin is tossed and a die is thrown at the same time. Find the probability of getting a head and an even number.

Solution.

Step 1 H1, H2, …, H6 and T1, T2, …, T6: ${2 \times 6 = 12}$ outcomes each face with each side
Step 2 H2, H4 and H6 are favourable: $P(\text{head and even}) = \dfrac{3}{12} = \dfrac{1}{4}$
Answer $\dfrac{1}{4}$

Example 6

Two dice are thrown. The probability of getting the same number on both is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{36}$
  3. $\dfrac{1}{12}$
  4. $\dfrac{1}{3}$

Solution.

Step 1 there are ${6 \times 6 = 36}$ equally likely outcomes
Step 2 the 6 doubles (1, 1) to (6, 6) are favourable: $P(\text{same number}) = \dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
Answer (a)

Option (b) counts only one of the six doubles, and (d) is $\frac{12}{36}$, which counts each double twice.

Example 7

Two dice are thrown. The probability that the two numbers differ by 2 is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{9}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
  3. $\dfrac{5}{18}$
  4. $\dfrac{2}{9}$

Solution.

Step 1 (1, 3), (2, 4), (3, 5), (4, 6) and (3, 1), (4, 2), (5, 3), (6, 4) differ by 2 both orders count
Step 2 $P(\text{difference } 2) = \dfrac{8}{36} = \dfrac{2}{9}$
Answer (d)

Option (a) counts each pair in one order only.
