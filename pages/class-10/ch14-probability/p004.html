Before going further, try to answer these two questions.

  1. What is the probability of getting 8 in a single throw of a die?
  2. What is the probability of getting a number less than 7 in a single throw of a die?

For (i): a single throw of a die has only six possible outcomes, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6. No face of the die is marked 8, so no outcome is favourable to getting 8, and ${P(\text{getting } 8) = \frac{0}{6} = 0}$: getting 8 is impossible.

So the probability of an event that cannot happen is 0. Such an event is called an impossible event.

For (ii): every face of a die is marked with a number less than 7, so a single throw is sure to give a number less than 7. The number of favourable outcomes is the number of all possible outcomes, 6. So ${P(\text{number less than } 7) = \frac{6}{6} = 1}$.

So the probability of an event that is sure to happen is 1. Such an event is called a sure event or a certain event.

Note. In the definition of P(E), the numerator, the number of outcomes favourable to E, is never more than the denominator, the number of all possible outcomes. So

$$0 \leq P(E) \leq 1$$

A probability is never negative and never more than 1. If you get an answer such as $\frac{7}{6}$ or $-0.2$, check how you counted the outcomes.

Now let us take an example with playing cards. A deck of playing cards has 52 cards in 4 suits of 13 cards each: spades (♠), hearts (♥), diamonds (♦) and clubs (♣). Clubs and spades are black, and hearts and diamonds are red. The cards of each suit are the ace, king, queen, jack, 10, 9, 8, 7, 6, 5, 4, 3 and 2. Kings, queens and jacks are called face cards.

Example 4

One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability that the card (i) is an ace, (ii) is not an ace.

Solution.

Step 1 the number of possible outcomes is 52 shuffling makes them equally likely
Step 2 (i) let E be the card is an ace; there are 4 aces in a deck, so 4 outcomes are favourable
Step 3 (ii) let F be the card is not an ace; ${52 - 4 = 48}$ of the 52 outcomes are favourable every card but the aces
Answer (i) ${P(E) = \frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}}$   (ii) ${P(F) = \frac{48}{52} = \frac{12}{13}}$

Remark. F is the same event as $\overline{E}$. So P(F) can also be found as $P(F) = P(\overline{E}) = 1 - P(E) = 1 - \frac{1}{13} = \frac{12}{13}$.
