Why the other options are wrong

A8 PROBABILITY has 11 letters, and B appears twice: ${\frac{2}{11}}$, (d). Option (a) counts only the 9 different letters.
B1 ${\frac{x}{12 + x} = \frac{1}{3}}$ gives ${3x = 12 + x}$, so ${x = 6}$: (b). Option (a) leaves the blue balls out of the total.
B3 The primes up to 30 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and 29: ${\frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3}}$, (a). Option (b) counts 1 as a prime.
B4 The red face cards are the king, queen and jack of hearts and of diamonds: ${\frac{6}{52} = \frac{3}{26}}$, (d). Option (b) is red or a face card.
B5 HHH, HHT, HTH and THH: ${\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}}$, (b). Option (a) is exactly two heads, and (d) is at least one head.
C1 (4, 6), (5, 5), (6, 4), (5, 6), (6, 5) and (6, 6): ${\frac{6}{36} = \frac{1}{6}}$, (c). Option (b) leaves out (6, 6).
C2 7 perfect squares and 5 multiples of 10, with none in both: ${\frac{12}{50} = \frac{6}{25}}$, (b). Option (a) counts only the squares.
C5 Eight ordered pairs differ by 2, from (1, 3) and (3, 1) to (4, 6) and (6, 4): ${\frac{8}{36} = \frac{2}{9}}$, (d). Option (a) counts each pair in one order only.

Three things are worth keeping. A probability counts equally likely outcomes, so first check that the outcomes you listed are equally likely: HH, HT, TH and TT, not two heads, one of each, two tails. When not E is easier to count, count it and subtract from 1. And count every outcome once only: the king of hearts is both a king and a heart.
