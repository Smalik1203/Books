What this chapter showed
  1. The theoretical, or classical, probability P(E) of an event E is the number of outcomes favourable to E divided by the number of all possible outcomes of the experiment, where the outcomes are assumed to be equally likely.
  2. The probability of a sure, or certain, event is 1.
  3. The probability of an impossible event is 0.
  4. For every event E, ${0 \leq P(E) \leq 1}$.
  5. An event with only one outcome is called an elementary event. The probabilities of all the elementary events of an experiment add up to 1.
  6. For any event E, ${P(E) + P(\overline{E}) = 1}$, where $\overline{E}$ is the event not E. E and $\overline{E}$ are called complementary events.
  7. Experimental probability rests on what has actually happened, and theoretical probability predicts what will happen from some assumptions. As the number of trials grows, the two can be expected to come close to each other.
