Example 5

Sangeeta and Reshma play a tennis match. The probability that Sangeeta wins the match is 0.62. What is the probability that Reshma wins the match?

Solution.

Step 1 let S and R be the events Sangeeta wins the match and Reshma wins the match
Step 2 $P(S) = 0.62$ given
Step 3 $P(R) = 1 - P(S) = 1 - 0.62 = 0.38$ R and S are complementary
Answer the probability that Reshma wins is 0.38
Example 6

Savita and Hamida are friends. Ignoring leap years, what is the probability that they have (i) different birthdays? (ii) the same birthday?

Solution.

Step 1 Savita's birthday can be any day; Hamida's can be any of the 365 days of the year
Step 2 these 365 outcomes are equally likely an assumption
Step 3 (i) if Hamida's birthday is not Savita's, there are ${365 - 1 = 364}$ favourable outcomes
Step 4 $P(\text{different birthdays}) = \dfrac{364}{365}$
Step 5 (ii) $P(\text{same birthday}) = 1 - P(\text{different birthdays}) = 1 - \dfrac{364}{365} = \dfrac{1}{365}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{364}{365}$   (ii) $\dfrac{1}{365}$
Example 7

Class 10 of a school has 40 students: 25 girls and 15 boys. The class teacher has to choose one student as class representative. She writes the name of each student on a separate card, all the cards being alike, puts the cards in a bag and mixes them well. Then she draws one card. What is the probability that the name on the card is (i) a girl's? (ii) a boy's?

Solution.

Step 1 there are 40 students and one card is drawn: 40 possible outcomes one card a student
Step 2 (i) 25 outcomes are favourable to a girl's name, so $P(\text{girl}) = \dfrac{25}{40} = \dfrac{5}{8}$
Step 3 (ii) 15 outcomes are favourable to a boy's name, so $P(\text{boy}) = \dfrac{15}{40} = \dfrac{3}{8}$
Answer (i) $\dfrac{5}{8}$   (ii) $\dfrac{3}{8}$
