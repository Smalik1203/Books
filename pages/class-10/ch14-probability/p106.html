Type 6 · Sure events, impossible events and a total of 1

Example 11

A bag holds 18 balls, and each is red, black or white. A ball is drawn at random. The probability that it is red is $\frac{1}{6}$, and the probability that it is black is $\frac{1}{2}$. Find (i) the probability that it is white (ii) the number of white balls (iii) the probability that it is green, and the probability that it is red, black or white.

Solution.

Step 1 $P(\text{red}) + P(\text{black}) + P(\text{white}) = 1$ each ball has one colour
Step 2 (i) $P(\text{white}) = 1 - \dfrac{1}{6} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{3}$
Step 3 (ii) white balls $= \dfrac{1}{3} \times 18 = 6$ favourable out of 18
Step 4 (iii) no ball is green, so $P(\text{green}) = \dfrac{0}{18} = 0$ an impossible event
Step 5 every ball is red, black or white, so this probability is $\dfrac{18}{18} = 1$ a sure event
Answer (i) $\dfrac{1}{3}$   (ii) 6   (iii) 0 and 1

Type 7 · Numbered cards

Example 12

Cards numbered 1 to 50 are put in a box, and one card is drawn at random. The probability that its number is a perfect square or a multiple of 10 is

  1. $\dfrac{7}{50}$
  2. $\dfrac{6}{25}$
  3. $\dfrac{1}{10}$
  4. $\dfrac{13}{50}$

Solution.

Step 1 perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, which is 7 cards
Step 2 multiples of 10: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, which is 5 cards
Step 3 none is in both, so ${7 + 5 = 12}$ and $P = \dfrac{12}{50} = \dfrac{6}{25}$ nothing counted twice
Answer (b)

Option (a) counts only the squares, (c) only the multiples of 10, and (d) counts one card twice, as if a square could also be a multiple of 10 here.
