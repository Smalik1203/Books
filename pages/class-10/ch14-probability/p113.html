2 EXAMINATION has 11 letters, and E, A, I, A, I, O are vowels: $\frac{6}{11}$. Option (a) is the probability of a consonant, and (c) counts each different vowel once.
3 The primes up to 30 are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19, 23 and 29: $\frac{10}{30} = \frac{1}{3}$. Option (a) counts 1 as a prime, and (b) leaves out 2.
5 2 black aces and 2 red kings: $\frac{4}{52} = \frac{1}{13}$. Option (a) counts only one of the two kinds, and (b) counts all 8 aces and kings.
8 6 of the 36 outcomes are doubles, so $1 - \frac{6}{36} = \frac{5}{6}$. Option (a) is the probability of a double, and (d) forgets the doubles.
10 A multiple of both 2 and 3 is a multiple of 6: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30 and 36, which is 6 of the 40 cards, so $\frac{6}{40} = \frac{3}{20}$. Option (a) counts only the multiples of 2, (b) only the multiples of 3, and (c) every card that is a multiple of 2 or of 3.
11 The multiples of 3 are 3 and 6, and the numbers that are not factors of 6 are 4 and 5. Each is 2 of 6 outcomes, so both are right: (c).
12 No two numbers from 1 to 6 have product 13, so A is true; R is true too, but it does not say why this probability is 0: (b).
13 Two of the seven days are favourable, so the probability is $\frac{2}{7}$ and A is false, while R is true: (d).
15 There are 9 equally likely balls and 4 are red, so A is true. The colours are not equally likely, since there are more green balls, so R is false: (c).

Three things are worth keeping. A probability counts equally likely outcomes, so first check that the outcomes you listed are equally likely: from a bag of 4 red and 5 green balls, red and green are not. When not E is easier to count, count it and subtract from 1. And count every outcome once only: the king of hearts is both a king and a heart.
