Example 5

Aftab tells his daughter, “Seven years ago, I was seven times as old as you were then. Also, three years from now, I shall be three times as old as you will be.” Write this situation as a pair of linear equations, and find their ages by the substitution method.

Solution.

Step 1 let Aftab’s age be $s$ years and his daughter’s age be $t$ years
Step 2 $s - 7 = 7(t - 7)$, that is, $s - 7t + 42 = 0$   (1) seven years ago
Step 3 $s + 3 = 3(t + 3)$, that is, $s - 3t = 6$   (2) three years from now
Step 4 $s = 3t + 6$ from (2)
Step 5 $(3t + 6) - 7t + 42 = 0$, that is, $4t = 48$, so $t = 12$ putting this in (1)
Step 6 $s = 3 \times 12 + 6 = 42$ putting $t = 12$ in (2)
Answer Aftab is 42 years old and his daughter is 12

Check that these ages fit both of his statements.

Example 6

In a shop, 2 pencils and 3 erasers cost ₹9, and 4 pencils and 6 erasers cost ₹18. Find the cost of each pencil and each eraser.

Solution.

Step 1 let a pencil cost ₹$x$ and an eraser cost ₹$y$
Step 2 $2x + 3y = 9$   (1)   and   $4x + 6y = 18$   (2)
Step 3 $x = \frac{9 - 3y}{2}$ from (1)
Step 4 putting this in (2), $\frac{4(9 - 3y)}{2} + 6y = 18, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 18 - 6y + 6y = 18, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 18 = 18$
Step 5 this statement is true for every value of $y$ (2) is (1) multiplied by 2
Answer the pair has infinitely many solutions, so there is no one cost for a pencil and one for an eraser

The statement in Step 4 gives no particular value of $y$, and so no particular value of $x$. This has happened because the two equations are really the same. Many pairs of costs fit the information.
