1. Form a pair of linear equations for each problem, and find the solution, if there is one, by the elimination method.
    1. If we add 1 to the numerator of a fraction and subtract 1 from its denominator, it becomes 1. If we only add 1 to the denominator, it becomes $\frac{1}{2}$. What is the fraction?
    2. Five years ago, Nuri was three times as old as Sonu. Ten years from now, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are Nuri and Sonu?
    1. The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 9. Nine times the number is twice the number made by reversing its digits. Find the number.
    2. Meena went to a bank to withdraw ₹2000. She asked the cashier for ₹50 and ₹100 notes only, and she got 25 notes in all. How many ₹50 notes and how many ₹100 notes did she get?
    1. A lending library has a fixed charge for the first three days and an extra charge for each day after that. Saritha paid ₹27 for a book she kept for seven days, and Susy paid ₹21 for a book she kept for five days. Find the fixed charge and the charge for each extra day.
What this chapter showed
  1. A pair of linear equations in two variables can be solved by the graphical method or by an algebraic method.
  2. The graph of a pair of linear equations is two lines. If the lines intersect, their point of intersection gives the unique solution, and the pair is consistent. If the lines coincide, every point on the line is a solution, and the pair is dependent (and consistent). If the lines are parallel, there is no solution, and the pair is inconsistent.
  3. The two algebraic methods are the substitution method and the elimination method.
  4. For the pair $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$: if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \frac{b_1}{b_2}$, it has a unique solution; if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \frac{c_1}{c_2}$, it is inconsistent; if $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = \frac{b_1}{b_2} = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$, it is dependent and consistent.

Compare $\frac{a_1}{a_2}$ and $\frac{b_1}{b_2}$ first. If they are equal, the pair has either no solution or infinitely many, and no amount of working will give you a single answer.
