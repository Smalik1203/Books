Two straight lines in a plane can meet in only three ways, so a pair of linear equations behaves in one of three ways.

(i) The lines intersect at a single point. Then the pair has a unique solution, and it is consistent.

(ii) The lines are parallel. Then the pair has no solution, and it is inconsistent.

(iii) The lines coincide. Then the pair has infinitely many solutions, and it is dependent (and so consistent).

Take the following three pairs of equations.

(i) $x - 2y = 0$ and $3x + 4y - 20 = 0$   (the lines intersect)

(ii) $2x + 3y - 9 = 0$ and $4x + 6y - 18 = 0$   (the lines coincide)

(iii) $x + 2y - 4 = 0$ and $2x + 4y - 12 = 0$   (the lines are parallel)

Let us write down the ratios $\frac{a_1}{a_2}$, $\frac{b_1}{b_2}$ and $\frac{c_1}{c_2}$ for each pair, and compare them. Table 3.1 does this.

From the table, you can see that the lines of the pair $a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0$ and $a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$ follow a rule.

Reading a pair from its coefficients

(i) If the lines intersect, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$.

(ii) If the lines coincide, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$.

(iii) If the lines are parallel, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$.

The converse is also true: from the ratios, we can tell how the lines lie.
