Why the other options are wrong

A3 $\frac{3}{6} = \frac{-5}{-10} = \frac{1}{2}$ but $\frac{7}{3} \neq \frac{1}{2}$, so the lines are parallel: (b). Two different lines never meet in exactly two points, so (d) cannot happen.
A4 $3 \times 2 + k \times 1 = 10$ gives $k = 4$: (c). Option (b) forgets the term $3 \times 2$, (a) also divides by 2, and (d) subtracts the wrong way round.
A6 Subtracting $2y$ from both sides gives $x = 7 - 2y$: (c). Options (a) and (b) get a sign wrong, and (d) divides by 2 as if $2x$ were in the equation.
A7 Multiplying by 2 and 3 gives $4x + 6y = 16$ and $15x - 6y = 3$; adding removes $y$: (d). Option (a) makes the coefficients of $x$ equal instead, and (b) and (c) give $9y$ and $-4y$, or $6y$ and $-10y$.
B1 We need $\frac{2}{4} = \frac{k}{6} = \frac{5}{10}$, so $k = 3$: (a). Option (b) makes $\frac{b_1}{b_2} = 1$, not $\frac{1}{2}$.
B3 $2x + 2y = 6$ and $3x - 2y = 4$ add to $5x = 10$, so $x = 2$, $y = 1$ and $2x + 3y = 7$: (c). Option (a) is $2x + y$, and (b) is $3x + 2y$.
B5 $4$ more km cost ₹40 more, so the charge is ₹10 a km and the fixed charge is $110 - 80 = 30$; 20 km cost $30 + 200 = 230$: (d). Option (c) forgets the fixed charge, (a) doubles the cost of 8 km, and (b) charges ₹12.50 for every km, as if ₹150 had no fixed part.
C1 $9x + 6y = 5550$ and $10x + 6y = 5900$ give $x = 350$, and then $y = 400$: (b). Option (a) is the cost of a chair.
C2 Putting $y = 0$ gives $x = 2$, and putting $x = 0$ gives $y = -4$: (a). Options (b) and (d) put the numbers on the wrong axis or with the wrong sign, and (c) loses the sign of $-y$.
C3 A unique solution needs $\frac{p}{3} \neq \frac{2}{1}$, that is, $p \neq 6$: (c). Option (b) is the one value that makes the lines parallel.
C4 $x + y = 180$ and $x - y = 30$ give $x = 105$ and $y = 75$: (d). Option (a) differs by $60^\circ$, (b) by $10^\circ$, and (c) adds up to $170^\circ$.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Adding or subtracting a pair of equations can give a useful expression, such as $x + y$, straight away. The ratios of the coefficients tell you how many solutions to expect before you start, and one solution that works does not show that it is the only one. And an answer must be checked against the question as well as the equations.
