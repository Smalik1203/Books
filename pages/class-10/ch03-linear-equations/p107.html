Example 15 · Matching

Match each pair of lines with how they lie. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) $2x + 3y = 5$, $4x + 6y = 10$(1) intersect at $(2, 2)$
(Q) $2x + 3y = 5$, $4x + 6y = 9$(2) coincident
(R) $2x + 3y = 5$, $3x - 2y = 1$(3) parallel
(S) $x - y = 0$, $2x + y = 6$(4) intersect at $(1, 1)$

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–1, Q–3, R–4, S–2
  2. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  3. P–2, Q–4, R–3, S–1
  4. P–2, Q–3, R–4, S–1

Solution.

Step 1Equal ratios throughout give one line; equal $x$ and $y$ ratios alone give parallel lines.
Step 2The last two pairs have unequal ratios and meet: at $(1, 1)$ and at $(2, 2)$.
Answer(d) P–2, Q–3, R–4, S–1
