Let us solve two more examples with this method. In the first, the working ends in a statement with no variable in it, and that statement tells us about the pair. In the second, the question allows two possibilities, and each one gives its own pair of equations to solve.

Example 9

Use the elimination method to find all the solutions of the pair of linear equations $2x + 3y = 8$ and $4x + 6y = 7$.

Solution.

Step 1 $2x + 3y = 8$   (1)   and   $4x + 6y = 7$   (2) name the equations
Step 2 $4x + 6y = 16$   (3)   and   $4x + 6y = 7$   (4) (1) by 2, (2) by 1
Step 3 $(4x - 4x) + (6y - 6y) = 16 - 7$, that is, $0 = 9$ subtract (4) from (3)
Answer $0 = 9$ is a false statement, so the pair of equations has no solution
Example 10

The sum of a two-digit number and the number made by reversing its digits is 66. The digits of the number differ by 2. Find the number. How many such numbers are there?

Solution.

Step 1 the number is $10x + y$, as $56 = 10(5) + 6$ tens digit $x$, units digit $y$
Step 2 reversed, it is $10y + x$, and the sum of the two is 66
Step 3 $(10x + y) + (10y + x) = 66, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 11(x + y) = 66, \quad \text{that is,} \quad x + y = 6 \quad (1)$
Step 4 either $x - y = 2$   (2)   or $y - x = 2$   (3) the digits differ by 2
Step 5 (1) and (2) give $x = 4$ and $y = 2$: the number is 42 by elimination
Step 6 (1) and (3) give $x = 2$ and $y = 4$: the number is 24 by elimination
Answer there are two such numbers, 42 and 24

Check: $42 + 24 = 66$ and $4 - 2 = 2$.
