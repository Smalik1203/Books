Reading a pair from its coefficients

(i) If the lines intersect, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} \neq \dfrac{b_1}{b_2}$.

(ii) If the lines coincide, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$.

(iii) If the lines are parallel, then $\dfrac{a_1}{a_2} = \dfrac{b_1}{b_2} \neq \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$.

The converse is also true: from the ratios, we can tell how the lines lie.

You can check this rule on more pairs of your own. Now let us look at some more examples.

Example 1

Check graphically whether the pair of equations $x + 3y = 6$ and $2x - 3y = 12$ is consistent. If it is, solve it graphically.
