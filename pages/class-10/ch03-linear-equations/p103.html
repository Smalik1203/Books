Example 2

The lines $2x + y = 10$ and $x - y = 2$ form a triangle with the $y$-axis. Its vertices are

  1. $(5, 0)$, $(2, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$
  2. $(0, 10)$, $(0, 2)$ and $(4, 2)$
  3. $(0, 10)$, $(0, -2)$ and $(4, 2)$
  4. $(10, 0)$, $(-2, 0)$ and $(4, 2)$

Solution.

Step 1 adding the equations, $3x = 12$, so $x = 4$ and $y = 2$ where the lines meet
Step 2 on the $y$-axis $x = 0$, so the first line meets it at $(0, 10)$ and the second at $(0, -2)$
Answer (c)

Option (a) uses the points where the lines meet the $x$-axis, (b) gets a sign wrong, and (d) swaps the coordinates.

Type 2 · Reading a pair from its ratios

Example 3

The pair $kx + 3y = k - 3$ and $12x + ky = k$ has no solution when $k$ is

  1. $6$
  2. $-6$
  3. $6$ or $-6$
  4. $12$

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{k}{12} = \dfrac{3}{k}$, so $k^2 = 36$ no solution needs this
Step 2 $k = 6$ or $k = -6$
Step 3 $k = 6$:   $\dfrac{c_1}{c_2} = \dfrac{-3}{-6} = \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{3}{6}$, so the lines coincide infinitely many
Step 4 $k = -6$:   $\dfrac{c_1}{c_2} = \dfrac{9}{6} = \dfrac{3}{2} \neq \dfrac{3}{-6}$, so the lines are parallel no solution
Answer (b)

Option (c) stops at $k^2 = 36$ and forgets to check $\frac{c_1}{c_2}$; (a) is the value that gives infinitely many solutions; (d) makes $\frac{a_1}{a_2} = 1$ but not $\frac{b_1}{b_2}$.
