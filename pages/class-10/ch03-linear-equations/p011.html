Example 9

Use the elimination method to find all the solutions of the pair of linear equations $2x + 3y = 8$ and $4x + 6y = 7$.

Call the equations (1) and (2). Multiplying (1) by 2 and (2) by 1 makes the coefficients of $x$ equal: $4x + 6y = 16$ (3) and $4x + 6y = 7$ (4). Subtracting (4) from (3) gives $(4x - 4x) + (6y - 6y) = 16 - 7$, that is, $0 = 9$. This is a false statement, so the pair of equations has no solution.

Example 10

The sum of a two-digit number and the number made by reversing its digits is 66. The digits of the number differ by 2. Find the number. How many such numbers are there?

Let the tens digit of the first number be $x$ and its units digit be $y$. Then the number is $10x + y$; for example, $56 = 10(5) + 6$. When the digits are reversed, $x$ becomes the units digit and $y$ the tens digit, so the new number is $10y + x$. The sum of the two numbers is 66, so

$$(10x + y) + (10y + x) = 66, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 11(x + y) = 66, \quad \text{that is,} \quad x + y = 6 \quad (1)$$

The digits differ by 2, so either $x - y = 2$ (2) or $y - x = 2$ (3). If $x - y = 2$, solving (1) and (2) by elimination gives $x = 4$ and $y = 2$, and the number is 42. If $y - x = 2$, solving (1) and (3) gives $x = 2$ and $y = 4$, and the number is 24. So there are two such numbers, 42 and 24. Check: $42 + 24 = 66$ and $4 - 2 = 2$.
