From (2), $s = 3t + 6$. Putting this in (1) gives $(3t + 6) - 7t + 42 = 0$, that is, $4t = 48$, so $t = 12$. Putting $t = 12$ in (2) gives $s = 3 \times 12 + 6 = 42$. So Aftab is 42 years old and his daughter is 12. Check that these ages fit both of his statements.

Example 6

In a shop, 2 pencils and 3 erasers cost ₹9, and 4 pencils and 6 erasers cost ₹18. Find the cost of each pencil and each eraser.

Let a pencil cost ₹$x$ and an eraser cost ₹$y$. Then $2x + 3y = 9$ (1) and $4x + 6y = 18$ (2). From (1), $x = \frac{9 - 3y}{2}$. Putting this in (2),

$$\frac{4(9 - 3y)}{2} + 6y = 18, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 18 - 6y + 6y = 18, \quad \text{that is,} \quad 18 = 18$$

This statement is true for every value of $y$, so it gives no particular value of $y$, and so no particular value of $x$. This has happened because the two equations are really the same: (2) is just (1) multiplied by 2. So the pair has infinitely many solutions. We cannot find one cost for a pencil and one cost for an eraser, because many pairs of costs fit the information.

Example 7

Two rails are represented by the equations $x + 2y - 4 = 0$ and $2x + 4y - 12 = 0$. Will the rails cross each other?

From the first equation, $x = 4 - 2y$. Putting this in the second equation gives $2(4 - 2y) + 4y - 12 = 0$, that is, $8 - 12 = 0$, that is, $-4 = 0$. This is a false statement. So the two equations have no common solution, and the rails will not cross each other.
