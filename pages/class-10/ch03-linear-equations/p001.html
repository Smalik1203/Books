Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables

3.1Introduction

You may have met a situation like this one. Akhila went to a fair in her village. She wanted to go on the Giant Wheel and to play Hoopla, a game in which you throw a ring over the things kept on a stall and win anything the ring covers completely. She played Hoopla half as many times as she rode the Giant Wheel. Each ride cost ₹3 and each game of Hoopla cost ₹4, and she spent ₹20 in all. How would you find out how many rides she had, and how many games of Hoopla she played?

You might try different cases. Is it possible that she had one ride? Two rides? And so on. Or you might use what you learnt in Class IX, and write the situation as linear equations in two variables. Let us try this.

Let the number of rides Akhila had be $x$, and the number of games of Hoopla she played be $y$. Then the situation gives two equations.

$y = \dfrac{1}{2}x$   (1)

$3x + 4y = 20$   (2)

Can we find the solutions of this pair of equations? There are several ways of doing it, and we study them in this chapter.

3.2Graphical Method of Solution of a Pair of Linear Equations

Any pair of linear equations in two variables $x$ and $y$ can be written in the general form

$$a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0$$

where $a_1$, $b_1$, $c_1$, $a_2$, $b_2$ and $c_2$ are real numbers, $a_1$ and $b_1$ are not both zero, and $a_2$ and $b_2$ are not both zero. In Class IX you saw that the graph of each such equation is a straight line. So a pair of linear equations is drawn as two straight lines, and a solution of the pair is a point that lies on both lines.
