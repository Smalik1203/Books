Look at Column 4 of Table 13.4. Every value in it is a multiple of 15, which is the class size. Dividing Column 4 by 15 gives even smaller numbers to multiply by $f_i$. So let

$$u_i = \dfrac{x_i - a}{h}$$

where $a$ is the assumed mean and $h$ is the class size. Work out each $u_i$, then each $f_iu_i$, and then $\sum f_iu_i$, as before. With ${h = 15}$ this gives Table 13.5.

Let ${\bar{u} = \frac{\sum f_iu_i}{\sum f_i}}$. Again, let us find how $\bar{u}$ is related to $\bar{x}$.

$\bar{u} = \dfrac{\sum f_i \left(\frac{x_i - a}{h}\right)}{\sum f_i} = \dfrac{1}{h}\left(\dfrac{\sum f_ix_i - a\sum f_i}{\sum f_i}\right) = \dfrac{1}{h}\left(\dfrac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i} - a\right) = \dfrac{1}{h}(\bar{x} - a)$

So ${h\bar{u} = \bar{x} - a}$, which gives

$$\bar{x} = a + h\bar{u} = a + h\left(\dfrac{\sum f_iu_i}{\sum f_i}\right)$$

With the values of $a$, $h$, $\sum f_iu_i$ and $\sum f_i$ from Table 13.5,

$$\bar{x} = 47.5 + 15 \times \dfrac{29}{30} = 47.5 + 14.5 = 62$$

So the mean mark is 62, as before. This way of finding the mean is called the step-deviation method. It gives the same answer as the direct method, with much smaller numbers to multiply.
