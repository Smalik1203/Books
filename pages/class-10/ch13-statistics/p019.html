But the mean is affected by extreme values. If one class has frequency 2 and five others have frequencies 20, 25, 20, 21 and 18, the mean does not show how the data behaves, and it is not a good representative of the data.

When single observations do not matter and you want a typical observation, the median is more suitable. Examples are the typical output of workers, or the average wage in a country. Such data may have extreme values, so the median is the better measure.

When you want the most frequent value or the most popular item, the mode is the best choice. Examples are the most-watched TV programme, the product in greatest demand, or the colour of vehicle that most people use.

Remark 1. The three measures are related, roughly, by the empirical relationship $3\,\text{Median} = \text{Mode} + 2\,\text{Mean}$. It was found from data, not proved.

Remark 2. The median of grouped data with classes of different sizes can also be found, but this book does not discuss it.
