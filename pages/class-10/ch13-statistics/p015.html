Use the table to answer these questions. How many students got less than 10 marks? Clearly, 5.

How many students got less than 20 marks? They are the students with marks in the class 0 – 10 together with those in the class 10 – 20. So ${5 + 3 = 8}$ students got less than 20 marks. We say that the cumulative frequency of the class 10 – 20 is 8.

In the same way you can find the cumulative frequencies of the other classes, that is, the number of students with marks less than 30, less than 40, …, less than 100. They are given in Table 13.13.

This is called a cumulative frequency distribution of the less than type. The numbers 10, 20, 30, …, 100 are the upper limits of the classes.

You can make a similar table of the number of students with marks greater than or equal to 0, greater than or equal to 10, greater than or equal to 20, and so on. From Table 13.12, all 53 students got 0 or more marks. There are 5 students in the class 0 – 10, so ${53 - 5 = 48}$ students got 10 or more marks. In the same way, ${48 - 3 = 45}$ students got 20 or more, ${45 - 4 = 41}$ got 30 or more, and so on (Table 13.14).
