Example 6

Table 13.9 gives the marks of 30 students in a mathematics examination. Find the mode of the data. Then compare the mode with the mean, and say what each of them tells you.

Table 13.9 (repeated from Section 13.2, for Example 6)

Number of students ($f_i$)23766630
Class mark ($x_i$)17.532.547.562.577.592.5
$f_ix_i$35.097.5332.5375.0465.0555.01860.0

Solution.

Step 1 in Table 13.9 the largest number of students, 7, got marks in the class 40 – 55, so the modal class is 40 – 55
Step 2 $l = 40$,   $h = 15$,   $f_1 = 7$,   $f_0 = 3$,   $f_2 = 6$ from the modal class
Step 3 $\text{Mode} = 40 + \left(\dfrac{7 - 3}{14 - 3 - 6}\right) \times 15 = 40 + \dfrac{4}{5} \times 15 = 52$ the formula
Step 4 the mean of the same marks is 62 found in Section 13.2
Answer The mode is 52 marks and the mean is 62. More students got about 52 marks than any other mark, while the average mark of a student is 62

Remark 1. In Example 6 the mode is less than the mean. In other data the mode may be equal to the mean, or greater than it.

Remark 2. Which measure you need depends on the question being asked. If you want the average mark of the students, find the mean. If you want the mark that most students got, find the mode.

Activity 3. Stay in the groups of Activity 2, with the same data. Each group finds the mode of its data, compares it with the mean, and explains what each of the two tells about the data.

Remark 3. The mode of grouped data with classes of different sizes can also be found, but this book does not discuss it.
