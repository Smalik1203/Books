13.1Introduction

In Class 9 you sorted data into ungrouped and grouped frequency distributions. You also showed data in pictures: bar graphs, histograms (some with bars of different widths) and frequency polygons. And you met three numbers that each describe the centre of a set of ungrouped data: the mean, the median and the mode. They are called measures of central tendency.

In this chapter you will find the same three measures for grouped data. On the way you will meet cumulative frequency and the cumulative frequency distribution.

13.2Mean of Grouped Data

The mean, or average, of a set of observations is the sum of all the observations divided by the number of observations. Recall from Class 9 what it means to say that the observations $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_n$ have the frequencies $f_1, f_2, \ldots, f_n$: the value $x_1$ occurs $f_1$ times, the value $x_2$ occurs $f_2$ times, and so on.

So the sum of all the observations is ${f_1x_1 + f_2x_2 + \cdots + f_nx_n}$, and the number of observations is ${f_1 + f_2 + \cdots + f_n}$. The mean $\bar{x}$ of the data is

$$\bar{x} = \dfrac{f_1x_1 + f_2x_2 + \cdots + f_nx_n}{f_1 + f_2 + \cdots + f_n}$$

You can write this more briefly with the Greek letter $\Sigma$ (capital sigma), which stands for the sum of:

$$\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_ix_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i}, \qquad \text{or simply} \qquad \bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i}$$
