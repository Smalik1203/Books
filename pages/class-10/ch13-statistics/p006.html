Mean of grouped data

Take the class mark of each class as its $x_i$. The mean can then be found in three ways, and all three give the same value.

direct method:   $\bar{x} = \dfrac{\sum f_ix_i}{\sum f_i}$

assumed mean method:   $\bar{x} = a + \dfrac{\sum f_id_i}{\sum f_i}$,   where $d_i = x_i - a$

step-deviation method:   $\bar{x} = a + h\left(\dfrac{\sum f_iu_i}{\sum f_i}\right)$,   where $u_i = \dfrac{x_i - a}{h}$

The step-deviation method is the easiest to use when all the $d_i$ have a common factor. The formula ${\bar{x} = a + h\bar{u}}$ still holds when $a$ and $h$ are any numbers, with $h$ not zero, as long as ${u_i = \frac{x_i - a}{h}}$. In Table 13.4 every $d_i$ had the common factor 15, the class size, and that is why ${h = 15}$ was chosen.

The assumed mean method and the step-deviation method are shorter forms of the direct method, not new kinds of mean. That is why the three methods always agree. Use whichever keeps the arithmetic shortest.
