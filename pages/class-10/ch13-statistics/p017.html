In grouped data you usually cannot find the middle observation by looking at the cumulative frequencies, because the middle observation is some value inside a class. So you need the value inside a class that divides the whole distribution into two halves. But which class is it?

To find this class, work out the cumulative frequencies of all the classes, and $\frac{n}{2}$. Then find the class whose cumulative frequency is greater than, and nearest to, $\frac{n}{2}$. This class is called the median class. In the distribution above, ${n = 53}$, so ${\frac{n}{2} = 26.5}$. The class 60 – 70 has cumulative frequency 29, which is greater than and nearest to 26.5. So 60 – 70 is the median class.

Once you have the median class, the median is given by the formula below.

Median of grouped data
$$\text{Median} = l + \left(\dfrac{\frac{n}{2} - cf}{f}\right) \times h$$

Here $l$ is the lower limit of the median class, $n$ is the number of observations, $cf$ is the cumulative frequency of the class before the median class, $f$ is the frequency of the median class, and $h$ is the class size, all the classes being of the same size.

Here ${\frac{n}{2} = 26.5}$, ${l = 60}$, ${cf = 22}$, ${f = 7}$ and ${h = 10}$. So

$$\text{Median} = 60 + \left(\dfrac{26.5 - 22}{7}\right) \times 10 = 60 + \dfrac{45}{7} = 66.4$$

So about half the students got less than 66.4 marks, and the other half got more than 66.4 marks.
