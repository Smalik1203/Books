Why the other options are wrong

1 The class mark is $\dfrac{35 + 50}{2} = 42.5$. Option (a) is the class size, (c) is the sum of the two limits, and (d) is not the middle of the class.
4 $\sum f_ix_i = 2 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 25 = 36$ and $\sum f_i = 10$, so the mean is 3.6. Option (b) ignores the frequencies, and (d) divides by 5.
7 With $a = 25$ and $h = 10$, $\sum f_iu_i = -10 - k + 6 + 6 = 2 - k$. The mean is $a$ itself, so $\sum f_iu_i = 0$ and $k = 2$.
8 $3\,\text{Median} = 21 + 2 \times 24 = 69$, so the median is 23. Option (c) is the average of the mean and the mode, and (d) swaps the mean and the mode.
10 $\sum f_ix_i = 4 + 36 + 80 + 56 = 176$ and $176 \div 20 = 8.8$. Option (d) divides by the 4 classes, and (c) uses the upper limits.
11 The cumulative frequencies are 2, 8, 16 and 20, so the median class is 8 – 12 and the median is $8 + \dfrac{10 - 8}{8} \times 4 = 9$. Arjun took $h = 2$.
12 Mean $\dfrac{1110}{50} = 22.2$, median $20 + \dfrac{25 - 20}{20} \times 10 = 22.5$, mode $20 + \dfrac{8}{22} \times 10 = 23.64$.
14 Mode $30 + \dfrac{6}{10} \times 10 = 36$ and median $30 + \dfrac{20 - 12}{15} \times 10 = 35.33$. Option (b) swaps them, and (c) and (d) use the class mark 35.
16 $10 + \dfrac{9 - 4}{18 - 4 - 4} \times 10 = 15$, so A is true. R is false: in Example 9 the mode is 15, not the class mark 10. The answer is (c).
18 Doubling every $f_i$ doubles both $\sum f_ix_i$ and $\sum f_i$, so the mean does not change. A is false and R is true: the answer is (d).

Three things are worth keeping. Every grouped formula rests on one assumption, that each class is centred at its class mark, so a grouped mean, mode or median is an estimate. The assumed mean and the class size only shift and scale the numbers, so every choice of $a$ and $h$ gives the same mean. And one extreme value pulls the mean but not the median, which is why a typical value is often the median.
