The $n$th term is the sum of the first $n$ terms minus the sum of the first $n - 1$ terms. Since $3(n - 1)^2 + 5(n - 1) = 3n^2 - n - 2$, this gives ${a_n = (3n^2 + 5n) - (3n^2 - n - 2) = 6n + 2}$. Then $a_{n+1} - a_n = 6$ for every $n$, so the list is an AP with first term 8 and common difference 6. As a check, $S_1 = 3 + 5 = 8$ and $S_2 = 12 + 10 = 22$, which is ${8 + 14}$.

Can the sum of the first few odd numbers, $1 + 3 + 5 + \cdots$, ever be 200?

The odd numbers form an AP with $a = 1$ and $d = 2$, so the sum of the first $n$ of them is $\frac{n}{2}[2 + (n - 1) \times 2] = n^2$. The sum is always a perfect square: 1, 4, 9, 16, and so on. Since $14^2 = 196$ and $15^2 = 225$, no whole number $n$ gives $n^2 = 200$. So the answer is no, and we did not need to add up a single list.

The three angles of a triangle are in AP. Show that one of them must be $60^\circ$.

Call the angles $a - d$, $a$ and $a + d$, as in the first question. They add up to $180^\circ$, so $3a = 180^\circ$ and $a = 60^\circ$, whatever $d$ is. We never needed to know the other two angles.

Problem 1

The 25th term of the AP $-5, -\frac{5}{2}, 0, \frac{5}{2}, \ldots$ is

  1. 55
  2. 60
  3. 57.5
  4. 62.5
Solution

$a = -5$ and $d = -\frac{5}{2} - (-5) = \frac{5}{2}$

$a_{25} = -5 + (25 - 1) \times \frac{5}{2} = -5 + 60 = 55$

The answer is (a). Option (b) is $24 \times \frac{5}{2}$, leaving out the first term. Option (c) uses 25 in place of $n - 1 = 24$, and (d) is $25 \times \frac{5}{2}$, which makes both mistakes.
