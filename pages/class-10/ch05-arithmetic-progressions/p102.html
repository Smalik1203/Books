The even numbers form an AP with $a = 2$ and $d = 2$, so the sum of the first $n$ of them is $\frac{n}{2}[4 + (n - 1) \times 2] = n(n + 1)$. The sum is always the product of two consecutive whole numbers: 2, 6, 12, 20, and so on. Since $13 \times 14 = 182$ and $14 \times 15 = 210$, no whole number $n$ gives $n(n + 1) = 200$. So the answer is no, and we did not need to add up a single list.

The three angles of a triangle are in AP. Show that one of them must be $60^\circ$.

Call the angles $a - d$, $a$ and $a + d$, as in the first question. They add up to $180^\circ$, so $3a = 180^\circ$ and $a = 60^\circ$, whatever $d$ is. We never needed to know the other two angles.

Type 1 · Recognising an AP and its common difference

Example 1

The $n$th term of an AP is $7 - 4n$. Its common difference is

  1. 4
  2. $-4$
  3. 3
  4. 7

Solution.

Step 1 $a_1 = 7 - 4 = 3$ and $a_2 = 7 - 8 = -1$
Step 2 $a_{n+1} - a_n = [7 - 4(n + 1)] - [7 - 4n] = -4$ the same for every $n$
Answer (b)

Option (a) subtracts the wrong way round, (c) is the first term, and (d) is the fixed number 7 in the formula.

Example 2

Show that $(a - b)^2$, $a^2 + b^2$ and $(a + b)^2$ are in AP.

Solution.

Step 1 $(a^2 + b^2) - (a - b)^2 = a^2 + b^2 - a^2 + 2ab - b^2 = 2ab$ second minus first
Step 2 $(a + b)^2 - (a^2 + b^2) = a^2 + 2ab + b^2 - a^2 - b^2 = 2ab$ third minus second
Step 3 the two differences are equal the meaning of an AP
Answer The three expressions are in AP, with common difference $2ab$
