Example 8

Find the 11th term from the last term of the AP $10, 7, 4, \ldots, -62$, counting back towards the first term.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 10$, $d = 7 - 10 = -3$ and the last term is $l = -62$, where $l = a + (n - 1)d$
Step 2 $-62 = 10 + (n - 1)(-3)$ first, how many terms
Step 3 $-72 = (n - 1)(-3)$, so $n - 1 = 24$ and $n = 25$
Step 4 the AP has 25 terms, so the 11th term from the last is the 15th term not the 14th. Why?
Step 5 $a_{15} = 10 + (15 - 1)(-3) = 10 - 42 = -32$
Answer The 11th term from the last is $-32$

Another way. Write the AP in reverse order. Then its first term is $-62$ and its common difference is 3. (Why?) The question now asks for the 11th term of this AP, which is $-62 + (11 - 1) \times 3 = -62 + 30 = -32$, the same answer as before.

Example 9

A sum of ₹1000 is invested at 8% simple interest per year. Find the interest at the end of each year. Do these interests form an AP? If they do, use this to find the interest at the end of 30 years.

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{Simple interest} = \dfrac{P \times R \times T}{100}$ the formula
Step 2 at the end of the 1st year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 1}{100} = 80$ in rupees
Step 3 at the end of the 2nd year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 2}{100} = 160$
Step 4 at the end of the 3rd year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 3}{100} = 240$
Step 5 in the same way for the 4th year, the 5th year, and so on: the interests at the end of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, … years are $80, 160, 240, \ldots$
Step 6 the difference of consecutive terms is always 80, so the list is an AP with $d = 80$, and also $a = 80$
Step 7 the interest at the end of 30 years is the 30th term: $a_{30} = a + (30 - 1)d = 80 + 29 \times 80 = 2400$
Answer The interests ₹80, ₹160, ₹240, … form an AP; the interest at the end of 30 years will be ₹2400
