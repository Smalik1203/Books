Example 6

Check whether 301 is a term of the list of numbers $5, 11, 17, 23, \ldots$

Here $a_2 - a_1 = 11 - 5 = 6$, $a_3 - a_2 = 17 - 11 = 6$ and $a_4 - a_3 = 23 - 17 = 6$. Since $a_{k+1} - a_k$ is the same for $k = 1, 2, 3$ and so on, the list is an AP with $a = 5$ and $d = 6$. Suppose 301 is a term of this AP, say its $n$th term. Then

$301 = 5 + (n - 1) \times 6 = 6n - 1$

$6n = 302$, so $n = \dfrac{302}{6} = \dfrac{151}{3}$

But $n$ must be a positive integer. (Why?) So 301 is not a term of the list.

Example 7

How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 3?

The two-digit numbers divisible by 3 are $12, 15, 18, \ldots, 99$. Is this list an AP? Yes, it is. Here $a = 12$, $d = 3$ and $a_n = 99$. Since ${a_n = a + (n - 1)d}$,

$99 = 12 + (n - 1) \times 3$

$87 = (n - 1) \times 3$, so $n - 1 = \dfrac{87}{3} = 29$ and $n = 30$

So there are 30 two-digit numbers that are divisible by 3.

Example 8

Find the 11th term from the last term of the AP $10, 7, 4, \ldots, -62$, counting back towards the first term.

Here $a = 10$, $d = 7 - 10 = -3$ and the last term is $l = -62$, where $l = a + (n - 1)d$. To find the 11th term from the last, we first find how many terms the AP has.

$-62 = 10 + (n - 1)(-3)$

$-72 = (n - 1)(-3)$, so $n - 1 = 24$ and $n = 25$

So the AP has 25 terms, and the 11th term from the last is the 15th term. (It is not the 14th term. Why?) So the term we want is $a_{15} = 10 + (15 - 1)(-3) = 10 - 42 = -32$.
