5.3nth Term of an AP

Go back to Reena from Section 5.1. Her starting salary is ₹8000 a month, and it rises by ₹500 every year. What will her monthly salary be in the fifth year?

First find her salary in the second year. It is ₹8000 + ₹500 = ₹8500. In the same way, adding ₹500 to one year’s salary gives the next year’s. So her monthly salaries, in rupees, are

3rd year:   $8500 + 500 = 8000 + 2 \times 500 = 8000 + (3 - 1) \times 500 = 9000$

4th year:   $9000 + 500 = 8000 + 3 \times 500 = 8000 + (4 - 1) \times 500 = 9500$

5th year:   $9500 + 500 = 8000 + 4 \times 500 = 8000 + (5 - 1) \times 500 = 10000$

This gives the list of salaries $8000, 8500, 9000, 9500, 10000, \ldots$ These numbers are in AP. (Why?)

Can you now find her monthly salary in the 6th year? In the 15th year? If she is still in the job, what about the 25th year? You could keep adding ₹500 to each year’s salary, but there is a shorter way. Look again at the pattern in the salaries above.

15th year:   $8000 + 14 \times 500 = 8000 + (15 - 1) \times 500 = 15000$

25th year:   $8000 + 24 \times 500 = 8000 + (25 - 1) \times 500 = 20000$

In words, the salary in a year is the first salary plus one less than the number of the year, times the yearly rise. This gives you an idea of how to write the 15th term, the 25th term and, in general, the $n$th term of an AP.
