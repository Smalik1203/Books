Example 2

Which of these lists of numbers form an AP? For each one that does, write the next two terms.

(i) $4, 10, 16, 22, \ldots$     (ii) $1, -1, -3, -5, \ldots$

(iii) $-2, 2, -2, 2, -2, \ldots$     (iv) $1, 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, \ldots$

(i) Here $a_2 - a_1 = 10 - 4 = 6$, $a_3 - a_2 = 16 - 10 = 6$ and $a_4 - a_3 = 22 - 16 = 6$. The difference $a_{k+1} - a_k$ is the same every time, so the list is an AP with common difference 6. Its next two terms are $22 + 6 = 28$ and $28 + 6 = 34$.

(ii) Here $a_2 - a_1 = -1 - 1 = -2$, $a_3 - a_2 = -3 - (-1) = -2$ and ${a_4 - a_3 = -5 - (-3) = -2}$. So the list is an AP with common difference $-2$. Its next two terms are $-5 + (-2) = -7$ and $-7 + (-2) = -9$.

(iii) Here $a_2 - a_1 = 2 - (-2) = 4$, but $a_3 - a_2 = -2 - 2 = -4$. Since $a_2 - a_1 \neq a_3 - a_2$, the list does not form an AP.

(iv) Here $a_2 - a_1 = 1 - 1 = 0$ and $a_3 - a_2 = 1 - 1 = 0$, but $a_4 - a_3 = 2 - 1 = 1$. So the list does not form an AP.
