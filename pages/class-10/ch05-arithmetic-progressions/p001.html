Arithmetic Progressions

5.1Introduction

Many things in nature follow a pattern: the petals of a sunflower, the cells of a honeycomb, the grains on a maize cob, and the spirals on a pineapple or a pine cone.

Patterns also come up in everyday life. Here are some examples.

(i) Reena has been offered a job with a starting salary of ₹8000 a month, and her salary will rise by ₹500 every year. Her monthly salary, in rupees, in the first, second, third, … years will be

$$8000,\ 8500,\ 9000,\ \ldots$$

(ii) The rungs of a ladder get shorter by 2 cm from each rung to the one above it, as in Fig. 5.1. The bottom rung is 45 cm long. So the lengths, in cm, of the first to the eighth rung, counted from the bottom, are

$$45,\ 43,\ 41,\ 39,\ 37,\ 35,\ 33,\ 31$$

(iii) In a savings scheme, the amount becomes $\frac{5}{4}$ times itself every 3 years. An investment of ₹8000 grows, after 3, 6, 9 and 12 years, to these amounts in rupees:

$$10000,\ 12500,\ 15625,\ 19531.25$$

(iv) The numbers of unit squares in squares of side 1, 2, 3, … units, as in Fig. 5.2, are

$$1^2,\ 2^2,\ 3^2,\ \ldots$$
