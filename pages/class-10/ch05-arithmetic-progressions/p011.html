Another way. Write the AP in reverse order. Then its first term is $-62$ and its common difference is 3. (Why?) The question now asks for the 11th term of this AP, which is $-62 + (11 - 1) \times 3 = -62 + 30 = -32$, the same answer as before.

Example 9

A sum of ₹1000 is invested at 8% simple interest per year. Find the interest at the end of each year. Do these interests form an AP? If they do, use this to find the interest at the end of 30 years.

Simple interest is given by the formula

$$\text{Simple interest} = \dfrac{P \times R \times T}{100}$$

So the interest, in rupees, is

at the end of the 1st year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 1}{100} = 80$

at the end of the 2nd year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 2}{100} = 160$

at the end of the 3rd year:   $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \times 3}{100} = 240$

In the same way you can find the interest at the end of the 4th year, the 5th year, and so on. So the interests, in rupees, at the end of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, … years are $80, 160, 240, \ldots$ This list is an AP, because the difference of consecutive terms is always 80. So $d = 80$, and also $a = 80$. The interest at the end of 30 years is the 30th term,

$$a_{30} = a + (30 - 1)d = 80 + 29 \times 80 = 2400$$

So the interest at the end of 30 years will be ₹2400.
