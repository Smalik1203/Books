Example 12

The sum of the first 14 terms of an AP is 1050, and its first term is 10. Find its 20th term.

Here $S_{14} = 1050$, $n = 14$ and $a = 10$. Using ${S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]}$,

$1050 = \dfrac{14}{2}[20 + 13d] = 140 + 91d$

$910 = 91d$, so $d = 10$

$a_{20} = 10 + (20 - 1) \times 10 = 200$

So the 20th term of the AP is 200.

Example 13

How many terms of the AP $24, 21, 18, \ldots$ must be taken so that their sum is 78?

Here $a = 24$, $d = 21 - 24 = -3$ and $S_n = 78$. We need to find $n$. Using ${S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]}$,

$78 = \dfrac{n}{2}[48 + (n - 1)(-3)] = \dfrac{n}{2}[51 - 3n]$

$156 = 51n - 3n^2$, that is, $3n^2 - 51n + 156 = 0$

$n^2 - 17n + 52 = 0$, that is, $(n - 4)(n - 13) = 0$

$n = 4$ or $n = 13$

Both values of $n$ are possible. So the number of terms is either 4 or 13.

Two answers make sense here. The sum of the first 4 terms and the sum of the first 13 terms are both 78, because the terms from the 5th to the 13th add up to 0. Since $a$ is positive and $d$ is negative, the later terms are negative, and they cancel the positive ones.
