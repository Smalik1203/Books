The difference of any two consecutive terms is 3 each time. So the list is an AP with first term 6 and common difference 3. For the list $6, 3, 0, -3, \ldots$ we have

$a_2 - a_1 = 3 - 6 = -3$

$a_3 - a_2 = 0 - 3 = -3$

$a_4 - a_3 = -3 - 0 = -3$

So this list is also an AP. Its first term is 6 and its common difference is $-3$. In general, for an AP $a_1, a_2, \ldots, a_n$ we have

$$d = a_{k+1} - a_k$$

where $a_{k+1}$ and $a_k$ are the $(k + 1)$th and $k$th terms. Once you know that a list is an AP, you need to find only one of the differences $a_2 - a_1$, $a_3 - a_2$, $a_4 - a_3$, …

Now look at the list $1, 1, 2, 3, 5, \ldots$ Here $a_2 - a_1 = 0$, $a_3 - a_2 = 1$, $a_4 - a_3 = 1$ and ${a_5 - a_4 = 2}$. These differences are not all the same, so this list is not an AP.

To find $d$ in the AP $6, 3, 0, -3, \ldots$ we took 6 away from 3, and not 3 away from 6. Always subtract the $k$th term from the $(k + 1)$th term, even when the $(k + 1)$th term is the smaller one.

Let us make the idea clearer through some examples.

Example 1

For the AP $\frac{3}{2}, \frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{3}{2}, \ldots$ write the first term $a$ and the common difference $d$.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = \frac{3}{2}$ the first term
Step 2 $d = \frac{1}{2} - \frac{3}{2} = -1$ second term minus first
Answer $a = \frac{3}{2}$ and $d = -1$

Once we know that the numbers are in AP, any two consecutive terms give us $d$.
