(a) The heights, in cm, of some students standing in a line in the morning assembly are 147, 148, 149, …, 157.

(b) The lowest temperatures, in degrees Celsius, recorded on the seven days of a week in January in a city, arranged from lowest to highest, are $-3.1, -3.0, -2.9, -2.8, -2.7, -2.6, -2.5$

(c) A loan of ₹1000 is repaid by paying 5% of it, that is ₹50, every month. The amounts, in rupees, still to be paid after each payment are 950, 900, 850, 800, …, 50.

(d) The cash prizes, in rupees, that a school gives to the toppers of Classes I to XII are 200, 250, 300, 350, …, 750.

(e) If ₹50 is saved every month, the total savings, in rupees, at the end of each of 10 months are 50, 100, 150, 200, 250, 300, 350, 400, 450, 500.

Try to explain for yourself why each of these lists is an AP.

If $a$ is the first term of an AP and $d$ is its common difference, the AP is

$$a,\ \ a + d,\ \ a + 2d,\ \ a + 3d,\ \ \ldots$$

This is called the general form of an AP.

Each of the lists (a) to (e) has only a limited number of terms, and each has a last term. An AP like this is called a finite AP. The lists (i) to (v) at the start of this section go on without end and have no last term. They are called infinite APs.

What is the least you need to know to write down an AP? Is the first term enough? Is the common difference enough? You need both of them, $a$ and $d$: the first term and the common difference.

For example, if $a = 6$ and $d = 3$, the AP is 6, 9, 12, 15 and so on. If $a = 6$ and $d = -3$ instead, the AP is 6, 3, 0, $-3$ and so on. Here are four more.

$a = -7$, $d = -2$:   the AP is $-7, -9, -11, -13, \ldots$

$a = 1.0$, $d = 0.1$:   the AP is $1.0, 1.1, 1.2, 1.3, \ldots$

$a = 0$, $d = 1\frac{1}{2}$:   the AP is $0, 1\frac{1}{2}, 3, 4\frac{1}{2}, 6, \ldots$

$a = 2$, $d = 0$:   the AP is $2, 2, 2, 2, \ldots$

So if you know $a$ and $d$, you can write down as many terms of the AP as you like. Now think of it the other way round. Given a list of numbers, can you tell whether it is an AP, and find $a$ and $d$? The first term $a$ is easy to read. In an AP, each term is got by adding $d$ to the term before it. So when you subtract any term from the term just after it, you must get the same number, $d$, every time.
