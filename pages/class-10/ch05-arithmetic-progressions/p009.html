Example 5

Find the AP whose 3rd term is 5 and whose 7th term is 9.

Solution.

Step 1 $a_3 = a + (3 - 1)d = a + 2d = 5$   (1) using ${a_n = a + (n - 1)d}$
Step 2 $a_7 = a + (7 - 1)d = a + 6d = 9$   (2)
Step 3 $4d = 4$, so $d = 1$ subtracting (1) from (2)
Step 4 $a = 3$ then (1)
Answer The AP is $3, 4, 5, 6, 7, \ldots$
Example 6

Check whether 301 is a term of the list of numbers $5, 11, 17, 23, \ldots$

Solution.

Step 1 $a_2 - a_1 = 11 - 5 = 6$, $a_3 - a_2 = 17 - 11 = 6$ and $a_4 - a_3 = 23 - 17 = 6$
Step 2 $a_{k+1} - a_k$ is the same for $k = 1, 2, 3$ and so on, so the list is an AP with $a = 5$ and $d = 6$
Step 3 $301 = 5 + (n - 1) \times 6 = 6n - 1$ suppose 301 is the $n$th term
Step 4 $6n = 302$, so $n = \dfrac{302}{6} = \dfrac{151}{3}$
Step 5 but $n$ must be a positive integer. (Why?)
Answer No, 301 is not a term of the list
Example 7

How many two-digit numbers are divisible by 3?

Solution.

Step 1 the two-digit numbers divisible by 3 are $12, 15, 18, \ldots, 99$ this list is an AP
Step 2 $a = 12$, $d = 3$ and $a_n = 99$
Step 3 $99 = 12 + (n - 1) \times 3$ since ${a_n = a + (n - 1)d}$
Step 4 $87 = (n - 1) \times 3$, so $n - 1 = \dfrac{87}{3} = 29$ and $n = 30$
Answer There are 30 two-digit numbers that are divisible by 3
