(v) Shakila put ₹100 into her daughter’s money box when the daughter was one year old, and every year after that she put in ₹50 more than the year before. The amounts, in rupees, that she put in on the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, … birthdays were

$$100,\ 150,\ 200,\ 250,\ \ldots$$

(vi) A pair of rabbits is too young to have young ones in its first month. From its second month on, it produces one new pair every month. Each new pair does the same: nothing in its first month, and a new pair every month after that (Fig. 5.3). If no rabbit dies, the numbers of pairs at the start of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, …, 6th months are

$$1,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3,\ 5,\ 8$$

Each list above follows a pattern. In some, each number is got by adding a fixed number to the one before it. In one, each number is got by multiplying the one before it by a fixed number. In another, the numbers are the squares of consecutive numbers, and so on.

In this chapter you will study one of these patterns: lists in which each number is got by adding a fixed number to the one before it. You will learn how to find any term of such a list and the sum of its first $n$ terms, and you will use them to solve everyday problems.
