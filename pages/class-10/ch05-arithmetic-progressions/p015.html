We can also write this as $S = \frac{n}{2}[a + a + (n - 1)d]$, that is,

$$S = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + a_n) \qquad (3)$$

If an AP has only $n$ terms, then $a_n$ is its last term $l$, and (3) becomes

$$S = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l) \qquad (4)$$

This form is useful when you know the first and the last terms of an AP, but not its common difference.

Now we can answer the question we started with. The amounts, in rupees, that Shakila put into the money box on her daughter’s 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th, … birthdays were $100, 150, 200, 250, \ldots$ This is an AP, and we need the sum of its first 21 terms. Here $a = 100$, $d = 50$ and $n = 21$. Using ${S = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]}$,

$S = \dfrac{21}{2}[2 \times 100 + (21 - 1) \times 50] = \dfrac{21}{2}[200 + 1000]$

$\phantom{S} = \dfrac{21}{2} \times 1200 = 12600$

So by her 21st birthday the money box holds ₹12600. Using the formula made the problem much easier to solve.

We also write $S_n$ for the sum of the first $n$ terms of an AP. For example, $S_{20}$ is the sum of the first 20 terms. The formula connects four quantities, $S$, $a$, $d$ and $n$. If you know any three of them, you can find the fourth.

One more fact follows from what a sum is. The $n$th term of an AP is the sum of its first $n$ terms minus the sum of its first $n - 1$ terms, that is, $a_n = S_n - S_{n-1}$. Let us now look at some examples.

Example 11

Find the sum of the first 22 terms of the AP $8, 3, -2, \ldots$

Here $a = 8$, $d = 3 - 8 = -5$ and $n = 22$. Using ${S = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]}$,

$$S = \dfrac{22}{2}[16 + 21 \times (-5)] = 11(16 - 105) = 11 \times (-89) = -979$$

So the sum of the first 22 terms of the AP is $-979$.
