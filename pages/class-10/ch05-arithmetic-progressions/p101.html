Using What You Know

The chapter gave you two formulas: the $n$th term ${a_n = a + (n - 1)d}$, and the sum ${S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d]}$. What changes now is that a question may not tell you $a$ and $d$, or may ask whether something is possible at all. The questions below need nothing more than these two formulas and the meaning of an AP.

Three numbers in AP add up to 24, and their product is 440. Find the numbers.

We could call the numbers $a$, $a + d$ and $a + 2d$, but there is a neater choice: call them $a - d$, $a$ and $a + d$. Their sum is $3a = 24$, so $a = 8$, and $d$ has dropped out. The product now gives $(8 - d) \times 8 \times (8 + d) = 440$, so $64 - d^2 = 55$ and $d^2 = 9$. So $d = 3$ or $d = -3$, and either way the numbers are 5, 8 and 11, in one order or the other. Taking the middle number as $a$ let the sum give it at once. As a check, $5 + 8 + 11 = 24$ and ${5 \times 8 \times 11 = 440}$, so both conditions hold.

For what value of $k$ are $k + 9$, $2k - 1$ and $2k + 7$ consecutive terms of an AP?

In an AP, the second term minus the first is equal to the third term minus the second. So $(2k - 1) - (k + 9) = (2k + 7) - (2k - 1)$, that is, $k - 10 = 8$, and $k = 18$. The three terms are then 27, 35 and 43, which go up by 8 each time. We needed neither the first term of the AP nor the positions of these terms, only the equal differences.

For every $n$, the sum of the first $n$ terms of a list of numbers is $3n^2 + 5n$. Is the list an AP?

The $n$th term is the sum of the first $n$ terms minus the sum of the first $n - 1$ terms. Since $3(n - 1)^2 + 5(n - 1) = 3n^2 - n - 2$, this gives ${a_n = (3n^2 + 5n) - (3n^2 - n - 2) = 6n + 2}$. Then $a_{n+1} - a_n = 6$ for every $n$, so the list is an AP with first term 8 and common difference 6. As a check, $S_1 = 3 + 5 = 8$ and $S_2 = 12 + 10 = 22$, which is ${8 + 14}$.
