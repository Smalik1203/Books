Type 5 · The sum of the first n terms

Example 9

The first term of an AP is 4, and the sum of its first 10 terms is 175. Its 10th term is

  1. 35
  2. 34
  3. 17.5
  4. 31

Solution.

Step 1 $175 = \frac{10}{2}(4 + a_{10})$ from the first and last terms
Step 2 $4 + a_{10} = 35$, so $a_{10} = 31$
Answer (d)

Then $4 + 9d = 31$ gives $d = 3$. Option (a) is $a + a_{10}$, (b) is ${4 + 10 \times 3}$, using $n$ in place of $n - 1$, and (c) is the average of the ten terms.

Example 10

The sum of all the odd two-digit numbers is

  1. 2500
  2. 2475
  3. 4905
  4. 2420

Solution.

Step 1 the numbers are $11, 13, \ldots, 99$, an AP with $d = 2$
Step 2 $99 = 11 + (n - 1) \times 2$ gives $n = 45$
Step 3 $S = \frac{45}{2}(11 + 99) = 45 \times 55 = 2475$
Answer (b)

Option (a) also counts the odd one-digit numbers, (c) is the sum of all the two-digit numbers, and (d) takes 44 terms, forgetting to add 1.

Example 11

How many terms of the AP $54, 51, 48, \ldots$ must be taken so that their sum is 513?

  1. 18 only
  2. 19 only
  3. 18 or 19
  4. 17 or 19

Solution.

Step 1 $513 = \frac{n}{2}[108 + (n - 1)(-3)] = \frac{n}{2}(111 - 3n)$ with $a = 54$, $d = -3$
Step 2 $3n^2 - 111n + 1026 = 0$, so $n^2 - 37n + 342 = 0$
Step 3 $(n - 18)(n - 19) = 0$, so $n = 18$ or $n = 19$
Step 4 the 19th term is $54 + 18 \times (-3) = 0$ so both sums agree
Answer (c)

Options (a) and (b) keep one root each, and (d) comes from a wrong factorisation.
