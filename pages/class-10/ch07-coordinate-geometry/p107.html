Type 7 · Mid-points

Example 13

If ${\left(\frac{a}{3}, 4\right)}$ is the mid-point of the line segment joining $(-6, 5)$ and $(-2, 3)$, then $a$ is

  1. $-4$
  2. 12
  3. $-12$
  4. $-6$

Solution.

Step 1 the mid-point is ${\left(\frac{-6 - 2}{2}, \frac{5 + 3}{2}\right) = (-4, 4)}$ mid-point formula
Step 2 ${\frac{a}{3} = -4}$, so ${a = -12}$
Answer (c)

Option (a) is the $x$-coordinate of the mid-point, which is $\frac{a}{3}$ and not $a$. Option (b) loses the minus sign, and (d) is the $x$-coordinate of one end.

Example 14

The vertices of a triangle are $A(4, 2)$, $B(-2, 6)$ and $C(0, -4)$. D and E are the mid-points of AB and AC. Show that ${DE = \frac{1}{2}BC}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $D = \left(\dfrac{4 - 2}{2},\ \dfrac{2 + 6}{2}\right) = (1, 4)$ mid-point formula
Step 2 $E = \left(\dfrac{4 + 0}{2},\ \dfrac{2 - 4}{2}\right) = (2, -1)$
Step 3 ${DE = \sqrt{1^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{26}}$ distance formula
Step 4 ${BC = \sqrt{2^2 + (-10)^2} = \sqrt{104} = 2\sqrt{26}}$
Answer ${DE = \sqrt{26} = \frac{1}{2}BC}$

This is the mid-point theorem of Class IX, checked here with coordinates.
