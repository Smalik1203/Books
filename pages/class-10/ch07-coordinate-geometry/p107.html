Why the other options are wrong

A3 ${\left(\frac{2 - 6}{2}, \frac{-3 + 7}{2}\right) = (-2, 2)}$: (a). Option (b) halves the differences.
A4 ${\left(\frac{1(9) + 2(0)}{3}, \frac{1(6) + 2(0)}{3}\right) = (3, 2)}$: (b). Option (a) uses the ratio ${2 : 1}$.
A6 B is ${(2 \times 1 + 3, 2 \times 2 - 5) = (5, -1)}$: (c). Option (a) is the mid-point of A and $(1, 2)$, and (b) goes the wrong way from A.
A7 All three sides are 4, since ${\sqrt{2^2 + (2\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{16}}$: (b). An equilateral triangle is also isosceles, but (c) says it is not equilateral.
A8 With ${k : 1}$, ${\frac{6k + 1}{k + 1} = 3}$ gives ${k = \frac{2}{3}}$, so the ratio is ${2 : 3}$: (c).
B1 The mid-point is $(1.5, 2)$, and its distance from $(3, 0)$ is ${\sqrt{2.25 + 4} = 2.5}$: (a). Option (b) is the whole side.
B2 The mid-point of AC, ${\left(\frac{1}{2}, 1\right)}$, is also the mid-point of BD, so ${D = (-2, 1)}$: (b). Option (a) would make the parallelogram ABDC, and (c) ADBC.
B4 ${AB^2 = CD^2 = 10}$ and ${BC^2 = DA^2 = 18}$: opposite sides are equal and neighbouring sides are not, so it is not a rhombus: (b).
B5 B is halfway across from A to C, so on the line it is the mid-point of AC, ${\left(4, \frac{3 - 3}{2}\right)}$, and ${k = 0}$: (c).
B6 ${\frac{7k - 6}{k + 1} = 0}$ gives ${k = \frac{6}{7}}$: (d). Option (a) turns the ratio round.
C1 The radius is ${\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5}$, and ${B = (2 \times 2 + 1, 2 \times 1 + 3) = (5, 5)}$: (a). Option (b) gives the diameter, and (c) the mid-point of A and the centre.
C2 R divides PQ in the ratio ${2 : 1}$, so ${R = \left(\frac{-4 + 2}{3}, \frac{8 + 1}{3}\right)}$: (c). Option (a) uses ${1 : 2}$, and (b) is the mid-point.
C3 All four sides are ${\sqrt{13}}$, but the diagonals are 4 and 6, so it is a rhombus and not a square: (b).
C4 The diagonals are 2 and 6, so the area is ${\frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 6 = 6}$: (d). Option (a) forgets the half, and (b) is the square of a side.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. When you only need to compare lengths, compare their squares and leave out the square roots. A ratio written as ${k : 1}$ leaves one unknown to find. And the diagonals of a parallelogram share a mid-point, which gives a missing vertex without any distances.
