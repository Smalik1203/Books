Example 8

Find the coordinates of the points of trisection of the line segment joining the points $A(2, -2)$ and $B(-7, 4)$. The points of trisection are the points that divide the segment into three equal parts.

Solution.

Step 1 let P and Q be the points of trisection of AB, so that ${AP = PQ = QB}$ (Fig. 7.11)
Step 2 P divides AB internally in the ratio ${1 : 2}$
Step 3 P is $\left(\dfrac{1(-7) + 2(2)}{1 + 2},\ \dfrac{1(4) + 2(-2)}{1 + 2}\right), \text{ that is, } (-1, 0)$ section formula
Step 4 Q divides AB internally in the ratio ${2 : 1}$
Step 5 Q is $\left(\dfrac{2(-7) + 1(2)}{2 + 1},\ \dfrac{2(4) + 1(-2)}{2 + 1}\right), \text{ that is, } (-4, 2)$
Answer The points of trisection are $(-1, 0)$ and $(-4, 2)$

Note. We could also have found Q by seeing that it is the mid-point of PB, and using the mid-point formula.

Example 9

Find the ratio in which the $y$-axis divides the line segment joining the points $(5, -6)$ and $(-1, -4)$. Also find the point of intersection.

Solution.

Step 1 call the points $A(5, -6)$ and $B(-1, -4)$, and let the ratio be ${k : 1}$
Step 2 the point of division is $\left(\dfrac{-k + 5}{k + 1},\ \dfrac{-4k - 6}{k + 1}\right)$ section formula
Step 3 $\dfrac{-k + 5}{k + 1} = 0$, so ${-k + 5 = 0}$ on the $y$-axis the abscissa is 0
Step 4 ${k = 5}$, so the ratio is ${5 : 1}$
Step 5 the $y$-coordinate is ${\frac{-20 - 6}{6} = -\frac{13}{3}}$ putting ${k = 5}$
Answer The ratio is ${5 : 1}$, and the point of intersection is ${\left(0, -\frac{13}{3}\right)}$
