Another way. Find the four sides and one diagonal, say AC, as above. Here ${AD^2 + DC^2 = 34 + 34 = 68 = AC^2}$. So by the converse of the Pythagoras theorem, ${\angle D = 90^\circ}$. A quadrilateral with all four sides equal and one angle of $90^\circ$ is a square. So ABCD is a square.

Example 3

Fig. 7.6 shows how the desks in a classroom are arranged. Ashima, Bharti and Camella are seated at $A(3, 1)$, $B(6, 4)$ and $C(8, 6)$. Do you think they are seated in a line? Give reasons for your answer.

Solution.

Step 1 ${AB = \sqrt{(6 - 3)^2 + (4 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}}$ distance formula
Step 2 ${BC = \sqrt{(8 - 6)^2 + (6 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}}$
Step 3 ${AC = \sqrt{(8 - 3)^2 + (6 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}}$
Step 4 ${AB + BC = 3\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2} = AC}$, so the points A, B and C are collinear the two parts make the whole
Answer Yes. The three girls are seated in a line
Example 4

Find a relation between $x$ and $y$ such that the point $(x, y)$ is equidistant from the points $(7, 1)$ and $(3, 5)$.

Solution.

Step 1 $P(x, y)$ is equidistant from $A(7, 1)$ and $B(3, 5)$: ${AP = BP}$, so ${AP^2 = BP^2}$
Step 2 ${(x - 7)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x - 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2}$ distance formula
Step 3 ${x^2 - 14x + 49 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 - 6x + 9 + y^2 - 10y + 25}$ expanding
Step 4 ${-14x - 2y + 50 = -6x - 10y + 34}$ taking away $x^2 + y^2$ from both sides
Step 5 ${8y - 8x = -16}$, that is, ${x - y = 2}$
Answer ${x - y = 2}$
