Using What You Know

The chapter gave you two tools: the distance formula, and the section formula with the mid-point formula as its special case. What changes now is that a question may give you a condition instead of a number, and choosing the tool is part of the work.

Show that the points $A(2, 3)$, $B(6, 6)$ and $C(-1, 7)$ are the vertices of a right triangle that is also isosceles.

Work with the squares of the distances, so that no square roots appear. ${AB^2 = 4^2 + 3^2 = 25}$, ${AC^2 = (-3)^2 + 4^2 = 25}$ and ${BC^2 = 7^2 + (-1)^2 = 50}$. Since ${AB^2 = AC^2}$, ${AB = AC}$ and the triangle is isosceles. Since ${AB^2 + AC^2 = 50 = BC^2}$, the angle at A is $90^\circ$ by the converse of the Pythagoras theorem. The distances themselves were never needed.

The vertices of a triangle are $O(0, 0)$, $A(6, 0)$ and $B(0, 8)$. Find the point that is equidistant from all three vertices.

Let the point be $P(x, y)$. Equidistant from O and A means ${x^2 + y^2 = (x - 6)^2 + y^2}$, which gives ${12x = 36}$, so ${x = 3}$. Equidistant from O and B means ${x^2 + y^2 = x^2 + (y - 8)^2}$, which gives ${16y = 64}$, so ${y = 4}$. So P is $(3, 4)$, and its distance from each vertex is ${\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5}$. This point is also the mid-point of AB, because ${\left(\frac{6 + 0}{2}, \frac{0 + 8}{2}\right) = (3, 4)}$. In a right triangle, the mid-point of the hypotenuse is at the same distance from all three vertices.

In what ratio does the line ${3x + y = 9}$ divide the line segment joining the points $(1, 3)$ and $(2, 7)$?

Let the ratio be ${k : 1}$. The point of division is ${\left(\frac{2k + 1}{k + 1}, \frac{7k + 3}{k + 1}\right)}$, and it lies on the line, so its coordinates satisfy the equation: ${3(2k + 1) + (7k + 3) = 9(k + 1)}$. This gives ${13k + 6 = 9k + 9}$, so ${k = \frac{3}{4}}$ and the ratio is ${3 : 4}$. Writing the ratio as ${k : 1}$ left only one unknown to find.
