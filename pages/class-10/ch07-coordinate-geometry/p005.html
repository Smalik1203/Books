Example 1

Do the points $(3, 2)$, $(-2, -3)$ and $(2, 3)$ form a triangle? If they do, name the type of triangle they form.

Solution.

Step 1 call the given points $P(3, 2)$, $Q(-2, -3)$ and $R(2, 3)$
Step 2 ${PQ = \sqrt{(3 + 2)^2 + (2 + 3)^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{50} = 7.07}$ (approx.) distance formula
Step 3 ${QR = \sqrt{(-2 - 2)^2 + (-3 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{(-4)^2 + (-6)^2} = \sqrt{52} = 7.21}$ (approx.)
Step 4 ${PR = \sqrt{(3 - 2)^2 + (2 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2} = \sqrt{2} = 1.41}$ (approx.)
Step 5 the sum of any two of these distances is greater than the third, so P, Q and R form a triangle
Step 6 ${PQ^2 + PR^2 = 50 + 2 = 52 = QR^2}$, so ${\angle P = 90^\circ}$ converse of Pythagoras
Answer Yes. PQR is a right triangle

If one distance had been equal to the sum of the other two, the three points would have been on one line.

Example 2

Show that the points $(1, 7)$, $(4, 2)$, $(-1, -1)$ and $(-4, 4)$ are the vertices of a square.

Solution.

Step 1 let $A(1, 7)$, $B(4, 2)$, $C(-1, -1)$ and $D(-4, 4)$ be the given points
Step 2 ${AB = \sqrt{(1 - 4)^2 + (7 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 25} = \sqrt{34}}$ distance formula
Step 3 ${BC = \sqrt{(4 + 1)^2 + (2 + 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 9} = \sqrt{34}}$
Step 4 ${CD = \sqrt{(-1 + 4)^2 + (-1 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 25} = \sqrt{34}}$
Step 5 ${DA = \sqrt{(1 + 4)^2 + (7 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 9} = \sqrt{34}}$
Step 6 ${AC = \sqrt{(1 + 1)^2 + (7 + 1)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 64} = \sqrt{68}}$ the diagonals
Step 7 ${BD = \sqrt{(4 + 4)^2 + (2 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 4} = \sqrt{68}}$
Step 8 ${AB = BC = CD = DA}$ and ${AC = BD}$ four equal sides, equal diagonals
Answer ABCD is a square

To show that ABCD is a square, we showed that all its sides are equal and both its diagonals are equal. Four equal sides alone would not be enough: a rhombus has them too.
