${AB = \sqrt{(6 - 3)^2 + (4 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 9} = \sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}}$

${BC = \sqrt{(8 - 6)^2 + (6 - 4)^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}}$

${AC = \sqrt{(8 - 3)^2 + (6 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}}$

Since ${AB + BC = 3\sqrt{2} + 2\sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2} = AC}$, the points A, B and C are collinear. So the three girls are seated in a line.

Example 4

Find a relation between $x$ and $y$ such that the point $(x, y)$ is equidistant from the points $(7, 1)$ and $(3, 5)$.

Let $P(x, y)$ be equidistant from the points $A(7, 1)$ and $B(3, 5)$. We are given that ${AP = BP}$, so ${AP^2 = BP^2}$. That is,

${(x - 7)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = (x - 3)^2 + (y - 5)^2}$

${x^2 - 14x + 49 + y^2 - 2y + 1 = x^2 - 6x + 9 + y^2 - 10y + 25}$

${-14x - 2y + 50 = -6x - 10y + 34}$   (taking away $x^2 + y^2$ from both sides)

${8y - 8x = -16}$, that is, ${x - y = 2}$

This is the relation we want.

Remark. The graph of the equation ${x - y = 2}$ is a line. From your earlier classes you know that a point which is equidistant from A and B lies on the perpendicular bisector of AB. So the graph of ${x - y = 2}$ is the perpendicular bisector of AB (Fig. 7.7).
