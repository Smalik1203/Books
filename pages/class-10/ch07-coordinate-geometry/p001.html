Coordinate Geometry

7.1Introduction

In Class IX you learnt that to fix the position of a point in a plane, you need a pair of coordinate axes. The distance of a point from the $y$-axis is called its $x$-coordinate, or abscissa. The distance of a point from the $x$-axis is called its $y$-coordinate, or ordinate. A point on the $x$-axis has coordinates of the form $(x, 0)$, and a point on the $y$-axis has coordinates of the form $(0, y)$. For example, the point $(-3, 2)$ is 3 units to the left of the $y$-axis and 2 units above the $x$-axis.

You have also seen that a linear equation in two variables, ${ax + by + c = 0}$ with $a$ and $b$ not both zero, gives a straight line when you draw its graph. In Chapter 2 you saw that the graph of ${y = ax^2 + bx + c}$, with ${a \neq 0}$, is a parabola. Coordinate geometry was developed as an algebraic tool for studying the geometry of figures. It lets us study geometry using algebra, and understand algebra with the help of geometry. This is why coordinate geometry is used in many fields, such as physics, engineering, navigation, seismology and art.

In this chapter you will learn how to find the distance between two points whose coordinates are given. You will also learn how to find the coordinates of the point which divides the line segment joining two given points in a given ratio.
