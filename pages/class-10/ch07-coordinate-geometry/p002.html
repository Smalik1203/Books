7.2Distance Formula

Think about this situation. A town B is 36 km east and 15 km north of a town A. How would you find the distance from town A to town B without actually measuring it? You can show the situation on a graph, as in Fig. 7.1, and then use the Pythagoras theorem to work out the distance.

First, suppose two points lie on the $x$-axis. Can we find the distance between them? Look at the points $A(4, 0)$ and $B(6, 0)$ in Fig. 7.2. Both lie on the $x$-axis. From the figure, ${OA = 4}$ units and ${OB = 6}$ units. So the distance of B from A is ${AB = OB - OA = 6 - 4 = 2}$ units. So if two points lie on the $x$-axis, we can easily find the distance between them.

Now take two points on the $y$-axis. Can you find the distance between them? For the points $C(0, 3)$ and $D(0, 8)$ in Fig. 7.2, we find in the same way that ${CD = 8 - 3 = 5}$ units.

Next, can you find the distance of A from C in Fig. 7.2? Since ${OA = 4}$ units, ${OC = 3}$ units and $\angle AOC$ is a right angle, the distance of A from C is ${AC = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5}$ units. In the same way, you can find that the distance of B from D is ${BD = 10}$ units.
