The mid-point of a line segment divides it in the ratio ${1 : 1}$. So its coordinates come from the section formula with ${m_1 = m_2 = 1}$.

The mid-point formula

The coordinates of the mid-point P of the line segment joining the points $A(x_1, y_1)$ and $B(x_2, y_2)$ are

$$\left(\dfrac{1 \cdot x_1 + 1 \cdot x_2}{1 + 1},\ \dfrac{1 \cdot y_1 + 1 \cdot y_2}{1 + 1}\right) = \left(\dfrac{x_1 + x_2}{2},\ \dfrac{y_1 + y_2}{2}\right)$$

Let us solve a few examples that use the section formula.

Example 6

Find the coordinates of the point which divides the line segment joining the points $(4, -3)$ and $(8, 5)$ internally in the ratio ${3 : 1}$.

Let $P(x, y)$ be the point we want. Using the section formula, we get

$x = \dfrac{3(8) + 1(4)}{3 + 1} = \dfrac{28}{4} = 7$   and   $y = \dfrac{3(5) + 1(-3)}{3 + 1} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$

So $(7, 3)$ is the point we want.

Example 7

In what ratio does the point $(-4, 6)$ divide the line segment joining the points $A(-6, 10)$ and $B(3, -8)$?

Let $(-4, 6)$ divide AB internally in the ratio ${m_1 : m_2}$. Using the section formula, we get

$$(-4, 6) = \left(\dfrac{3m_1 - 6m_2}{m_1 + m_2},\ \dfrac{-8m_1 + 10m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\right) \qquad (1)$$
