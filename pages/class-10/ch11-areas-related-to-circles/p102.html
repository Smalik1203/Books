Let the radius be $r$. The sector is a quarter of the circle, ${\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2}$, and the triangle is a right triangle with two sides $r$, of area ${\frac{1}{2}r^2}$. So the segment is ${\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2}r^2}$. As a fraction of ${\pi r^2}$, this is ${\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{7}{44} = \frac{4}{44} = \frac{1}{11}}$. The radius cancelled, so the answer is the same for every circle.

Two circles have the same centre and radii 7 cm and 14 cm. A sector of angle $60^\circ$ is cut from the ring between them. Find its area. (Use ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

The piece is the sector of the larger circle less the sector of the smaller one, and both have the same angle. So its area is ${\frac{60}{360} \times \pi \times 14^2 - \frac{60}{360} \times \pi \times 7^2 = \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{22}{7} \times (196 - 49)}$, which is ${\frac{1}{6} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 147 = 77}$ cm2. Taking the common factor ${\frac{60}{360} \times \pi}$ out first leaves only ${196 - 49}$ to work out.

A square has side 14 cm. With each corner as centre, a quadrant of radius 7 cm is drawn inside the square. Find the area of the part of the square that is left. (Use ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

Each quadrant has a right angle at its corner, so the four quadrants together have an angle of $360^\circ$ and radius 7 cm. They make up exactly one whole circle, of area ${\frac{22}{7} \times 49 = 154}$ cm2. The square has area 196 cm2, so the part left is ${196 - 154 = 42}$ cm2. Adding the angles of the pieces first saved working out four separate sectors.
