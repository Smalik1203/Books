Length of an arc

length of the arc of a sector of angle $\theta$ $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$

A sector takes the same fraction of the area of its circle as its angle takes of $360^\circ$, and its arc takes that same fraction of the circumference. A sector of $90^\circ$ is a quarter of the circle in both.

Now take the segment APB of a circle with centre O and radius $r$ (Fig. 11.4). You can see that

area of the segment APB $=$ area of the sector OAPB $-$ area of $\triangle OAB$

$\phantom{\text{area of the segment APB}} = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2 -$ area of $\triangle OAB$

Note. From Fig. 11.3 and Fig. 11.4, you can also see that

area of the major sector OAQB $= \pi r^2 -$ area of the minor sector OAPB

area of the major segment AQB $= \pi r^2 -$ area of the minor segment APB

Let us look at some examples.

Example 1

Find the area of the sector of a circle of radius 4 cm with an angle of $30^\circ$. Also find the area of the corresponding major sector. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.) Give both answers correct to two decimal places.

The sector is OAPB (Fig. 11.5).
