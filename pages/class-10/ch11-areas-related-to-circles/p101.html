Using What You Know

The chapter gave you the area of a sector, the length of its arc, and the area of a segment as a sector less a triangle. What changes now is that a question may give you the area and ask for the angle, or hand you a shape that is made of several sectors at once.

A sector of a circle of radius 14 cm has an area of 77 cm2. Find its angle and the length of its arc. (Use ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

The whole circle has area ${\frac{22}{7} \times 196 = 616}$ cm2, and 77 is one eighth of 616. So the sector is one eighth of the circle, and its angle is ${\frac{360^\circ}{8} = 45^\circ}$. Its arc is one eighth of the circumference: ${\frac{1}{8} \times 88 = 11}$ cm. Notice that ${\frac{1}{2} \times 11 \times 14 = 77}$: the area of a sector is half the arc times the radius, just like a triangle whose base is the arc and whose height is the radius. You can check this in general, since ${\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r \times r = \frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2}$.

The radius of a sector is doubled and its angle is halved. What happens to its area, and to the length of its arc?

The area ${\frac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2}$ has the radius squared in it. Halving the angle gives a factor of $\frac{1}{2}$, and doubling the radius gives a factor of ${2^2 = 4}$, so the area is multiplied by ${\frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2}$: it doubles. The arc ${\frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r}$ has the radius only once, so it is multiplied by ${\frac{1}{2} \times 2 = 1}$: it stays the same. The same change affects the area and the arc differently, because the area depends on the square of the radius.

A chord of a circle subtends a right angle at the centre. What fraction of the whole circle is the minor segment? (Use ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

Let the radius be $r$. The sector is a quarter of the circle, ${\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2}$, and the triangle is a right triangle with two sides $r$, of area ${\frac{1}{2}r^2}$. So the segment is ${\frac{1}{4}\pi r^2 - \frac{1}{2}r^2}$. As a fraction of ${\pi r^2}$, this is ${\frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{2\pi} = \frac{1}{4} - \frac{7}{44} = \frac{4}{44} = \frac{1}{11}}$. The radius cancelled, so the answer is the same for every circle.
