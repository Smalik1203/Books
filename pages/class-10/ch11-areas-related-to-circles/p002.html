With these words in place, let us find formulas for the areas of a sector and a segment. Let OAPB be a sector of a circle with centre O and radius $r$ (Fig. 11.3), and let the degree measure of $\angle AOB$ be $\theta$.

You know that the area of a circle, or more exactly of a circular region, is $\pi r^2$. We can think of the whole circular region as a sector whose angle at the centre O is $360^\circ$. Now use the unitary method.

when the angle at the centre is $360^\circ$, the area of the sector is $\pi r^2$

so when the angle at the centre is $1^\circ$, the area of the sector is $\dfrac{\pi r^2}{360}$

so when the angle at the centre is $\theta$, the area of the sector is $\dfrac{\pi r^2}{360} \times \theta = \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$

This gives a formula for the area of a sector.

Area of a sector

area of the sector of angle $\theta$ $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times \pi r^2$

Here $r$ is the radius of the circle and $\theta$ is the angle of the sector in degrees.

Can we also find the length of the arc APB of this sector? Yes. The whole circle has an angle of $360^\circ$ at the centre and a length of $2\pi r$, so the unitary method again gives the length of the arc APB.

Length of an arc

length of the arc of a sector of angle $\theta$ $= \dfrac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$

A sector takes the same fraction of the area of its circle as its angle takes of $360^\circ$, and its arc takes that same fraction of the circumference. A sector of $90^\circ$ is a quarter of the circle in both.
