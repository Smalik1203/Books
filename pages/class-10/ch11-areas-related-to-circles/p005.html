area of the segment AYB $=$ area of the sector OAYB $-$ area of $\triangle OAB$   (1)

area of the sector OAYB $= \dfrac{120}{360} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times 21 \times 21$ cm2 $= 462$ cm2   (2)

To find the area of $\triangle OAB$, draw ${OM \perp AB}$ (Fig. 11.7). Since ${OA = OB}$, the triangles AMO and BMO are congruent, by RHS. So M is the mid-point of AB, and ${\angle AOM = \angle BOM = \frac{1}{2} \times 120^\circ = 60^\circ}$. Let ${OM = x}$ cm.

in $\triangle OMA$,   $\dfrac{OM}{OA} = \cos 60^\circ$,   so   $\dfrac{x}{21} = \dfrac{1}{2}$   and   $OM = x = \dfrac{21}{2}$ cm

also $\dfrac{AM}{OA} = \sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$,   so   $AM = \dfrac{21\sqrt{3}}{2}$ cm   and   ${AB = 2AM = 21\sqrt{3}}$ cm

area of $\triangle OAB = \dfrac{1}{2} \times AB \times OM = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 21\sqrt{3} \times \dfrac{21}{2}$ cm2 $= \dfrac{441\sqrt{3}}{4}$ cm2   (3)

So, from (1), (2) and (3),

area of the segment AYB $= \left(462 - \dfrac{441\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)$ cm2 $= \dfrac{21}{4}(88 - 21\sqrt{3})$ cm2
