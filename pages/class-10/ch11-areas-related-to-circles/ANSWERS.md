## 11.1 Areas of Sector and Segment of a Circle

### The question in the running text

*Can we also find the length of the arc APB of this sector?* Answered in the
text that follows it: yes. By the unitary method the arc of a sector of angle
$\theta$ is $\frac{\theta}{360} \times 2\pi r$ (the key idea *Length of an
arc*).

### Exercise Set 11.1

1. $\frac{60}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 36 = \frac{132}{7}$, that is
   **$18\frac{6}{7}$ cm²**.

2. $2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times r = 22$ gives $r = 3.5$ cm. The quadrant is
   $\frac{1}{4} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 3.5 \times 3.5 = 9.625$, that is
   **$\frac{77}{8}$ cm²**.

3. The minute hand turns $360^\circ$ in 60 minutes, so $30^\circ$ in 5 minutes.
   The area is $\frac{30}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 14 \times 14 = \frac{154}{3}$,

4. With $\pi = 3.14$ and $r = 10$ cm:

     $78.5 - 50 = 28.5$ **cm²**

5. With $r = 21$ cm and $\theta = 60^\circ$:
   - (i) arc $\frac{60}{360} \times 2 \times \frac{22}{7} \times 21 = 22$ **cm**
   - (ii) sector $\frac{60}{360} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 21 \times 21 = 231$ **cm²**
   - (iii) The triangle is equilateral (two sides 21 cm and the angle between
     them $60^\circ$), so its area is $\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4} \times 441 = \frac{441\sqrt{3}}{4}$.
     The segment is **$\left(231 - \frac{441\sqrt{3}}{4}\right)$ cm²**, about
     40.04 cm². The question gives no value for $\sqrt{3}$, so the surd form
     is the answer.

6. With $\pi = 3.14$, $\sqrt{3} = 1.73$ and $r = 15$ cm:
   - sector $\frac{60}{360} \times 3.14 \times 225 = 117.75$
   - the triangle is equilateral: $\frac{1.73}{4} \times 225 = 97.3125$
   - minor segment $117.75 - 97.3125 = 20.4375$ **cm²**
   - major segment $706.5 - 20.4375 = 686.0625$ **cm²**, since the circle is

7. With $\pi = 3.14$, $\sqrt{3} = 1.73$ and $r = 12$ cm:

     chord is $12\sqrt{3}$ cm, so the triangle is
     $\frac{1}{2} \times 12\sqrt{3} \times 6 = 36\sqrt{3} = 62.28$

9. - (i) circle $\frac{22}{7} \times 35 = 110$ mm, five diameters
