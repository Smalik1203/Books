Type 2 · HCF and LCM by prime factorisation

Example 3

The HCF and the LCM of $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$ and $2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7$ are, in that order,

  1. 216, 1260
  2. 36, 7560
  3. 36, 1260
  4. 6, 7560

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{HCF} = 2^2 \times 3^2 = 36$ common primes, smallest powers
Step 2 $\text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 7560$ every prime, greatest powers
Answer (b)

Option (a) swaps the two rules, (c) uses the smallest powers for the LCM too, and (d) takes every power as 1.

Example 4

Find the HCF and LCM of 84, 126 and 210.

Solution.

Step 1 $84 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 7$, $\ 126 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 7$, $\ 210 = 2 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7$
Step 2 $\text{HCF} = 2 \times 3 \times 7 = 42$ in all three, smallest powers
Step 3 $\text{LCM} = 2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 \times 7 = 1260$ in any, greatest powers
Answer HCF 42, LCM 1260

Type 3 · Using HCF × LCM

Example 5

Two numbers have HCF 16. Which of these cannot be their LCM?

  1. 64
  2. 240
  3. 124
  4. 160

Solution.

Step 1 the HCF divides each number, and each number divides the LCM
Step 2 so 16 must divide the LCM
Step 3 $64 = 16 \times 4$, $\ 240 = 16 \times 15$, $\ 160 = 16 \times 10$; 124 is not a multiple of 16
Answer (c)

The other three do happen: 16 and 64, 48 and 80, and 16 and 160 have HCF 16 and LCMs 64, 240 and 160.
