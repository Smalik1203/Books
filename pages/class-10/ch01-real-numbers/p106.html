Example 11

Copy the proof that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational, with 4 in place of 2. Which step fails?

  1. writing $\sqrt{4} = \frac{a}{b}$, with $a$ and $b$ coprime
  2. squaring to get $4b^2 = a^2$
  3. saying that 4 divides $a$, because 4 divides $a^2$
  4. saying that $a$ and $b$ have a common factor

Solution.

Step 1 Theorem 1.2 is about a prime, and 4 is not a prime
Step 2 4 divides $6^2 = 36$, but 4 does not divide 6 so the step fails
Answer (c)

Steps (a) and (b) are correct, since $\sqrt{4} = \frac{2}{1}$ and squaring is always allowed, and (d) only follows from (c).

Example 12

Show that $2 + 5\sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Solution.

Step 1 $2 + 5\sqrt{3} = \frac{a}{b}$, for coprime integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$ assume it is rational
Step 2 $5\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{a}{b} - 2 = \dfrac{a - 2b}{b}$, so $\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{a - 2b}{5b}$ rearranging
Step 3 $\dfrac{a - 2b}{5b}$ is rational, so $\sqrt{3}$ is rational $a$ and $b$ are integers
Step 4 but $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational Example 5 of the chapter
Answer The assumption is false, so $2 + 5\sqrt{3}$ is irrational
