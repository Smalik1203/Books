In general, we write a composite number $x$ as $x = p_1 p_2 \ldots p_n$, where $p_1, p_2, \ldots, p_n$ are primes written in ascending order, that is, $p_1 \leq p_2 \leq \ldots \leq p_n$. When we put equal primes together, we get powers of primes. For example,

$$32760 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5 \times 7 \times 13 = 2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5 \times 7 \times 13$$

Once we agree to write the primes in ascending order, there is exactly one way to write the factorisation of a number. The theorem has many uses, both inside mathematics and in other fields. Let us look at some examples.

Example 1

Take the numbers $4^n$, where $n$ is a natural number. Is there any value of $n$ for which $4^n$ ends with the digit zero?

Solution.

Step 1 suppose $4^n$ ends with the digit zero for some $n$ assume the opposite
Step 2 then it is divisible by 10, and so by 5
Step 3 $4^n = (2^2)^n = 2^{2n}$
Step 4 the only prime in its factorisation is 2, so the prime 5 cannot appear uniqueness of the factorisation
Answer No. There is no natural number $n$ for which $4^n$ ends with the digit zero

In earlier classes you found the HCF and LCM of two positive integers from their prime factorisations. You were using the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic without knowing it! This way of finding the HCF and LCM is called the prime factorisation method. Let us recall it with an example.

Example 2

Find the LCM and HCF of 6 and 20 by the prime factorisation method.

Solution.

Step 1 $6 = 2^1 \times 3^1$ and $20 = 2 \times 2 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 5^1$
Step 2 $\text{HCF}(6, 20) = 2^1 = 2$ the common prime, smaller power
Step 3 $\text{LCM}(6, 20) = 2^2 \times 3^1 \times 5^1 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 60$ every prime, larger power
Answer HCF 2, LCM 60

Look at the powers of the primes in these two answers. The only prime factor that 6 and 20 share is 2. It occurs as $2^1$ in 6 and as $2^2$ in 20, and the HCF takes the smaller of these powers. The LCM takes every prime that occurs in either number, each with its larger power, so it has $2^2$, $3^1$ and $5^1$.
