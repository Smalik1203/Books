Real Numbers

1.1Introduction

In Class IX you met the real numbers, and among them the irrational numbers. This chapter takes that work further. It begins with a basic property of the positive integers, called the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, and then puts it to use.

You already know that every composite number can be written as a product of primes. The theorem adds that there is only one way to do this, if we do not care about the order of the primes. The fact is easy to state and easy to understand. Even so, it has deep uses all through mathematics.

In this chapter we use the theorem in two ways. First, we use it to find the HCF and LCM of positive integers. Second, we use it to prove that some numbers you met in Class IX, such as $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{5}$, are irrational. So let us begin.

1.2The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

In earlier classes you wrote natural numbers as products of their prime factors, as in ${4 = 2 \times 2}$ and $253 = 11 \times 23$. Now look at this the other way round. Can we make natural numbers by multiplying primes together?

Take a few primes, say 2, 3, 7, 11 and 23. Multiply some or all of them, and use any of them as many times as you like. You can make a very large collection of positive integers in this way. In fact, you can make infinitely many. Here are a few of them.

$7 \times 11 \times 23 = 1771$

$3 \times 7 \times 11 \times 23 = 5313$

$2 \times 3 \times 7 \times 11 \times 23 = 10626$

$2^3 \times 3 \times 7^3 = 8232$

$2^2 \times 3 \times 7 \times 11 \times 23 = 21252$
