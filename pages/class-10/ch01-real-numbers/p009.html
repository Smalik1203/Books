What this chapter showed
  1. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every composite number can be written as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.
  2. The HCF of numbers is the product of the smallest power of each common prime factor. The LCM is the product of the greatest power of each prime factor.
  3. For any two positive integers $a$ and $b$, $\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$. This is not true for three numbers.
  4. If $p$ is a prime, $a$ is a positive integer and $p$ divides $a^2$, then $p$ divides $a$.
  5. $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3}$ are irrational. A proof by contradiction shows this, and it also shows that numbers such as $5 - \sqrt{3}$ and $3\sqrt{2}$ are irrational.

A Note to the Reader

You have seen that $\text{HCF}(p, q, r) \times \text{LCM}(p, q, r) \neq p \times q \times r$ for positive integers $p$, $q$ and $r$ (see Example 4). But these two results do hold for any three positive integers.

$$\text{LCM}(p, q, r) = \dfrac{pqr \cdot \text{HCF}(p, q, r)}{\text{HCF}(p, q) \cdot \text{HCF}(q, r) \cdot \text{HCF}(p, r)}$$
$$\text{HCF}(p, q, r) = \dfrac{pqr \cdot \text{LCM}(p, q, r)}{\text{LCM}(p, q) \cdot \text{LCM}(q, r) \cdot \text{LCM}(p, r)}$$

Check both of them with the numbers 6, 72 and 120 from Example 4.
