Example 2

Find the LCM and HCF of 6 and 20 by the prime factorisation method.

We have $6 = 2^1 \times 3^1$ and $20 = 2 \times 2 \times 5 = 2^2 \times 5^1$. As in earlier classes, $\text{HCF}(6, 20) = 2$ and $\text{LCM}(6, 20) = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5 = 60$. Now look at the powers of the primes in these two answers: $\text{HCF}(6, 20) = 2^1$ and $\text{LCM}(6, 20) = 2^2 \times 3^1 \times 5^1$. The only prime factor that 6 and 20 share is 2. It occurs as $2^1$ in 6 and as $2^2$ in 20, and the HCF takes the smaller of these powers. The LCM takes every prime that occurs in either number, each with its larger power, so it has $2^2$, $3^1$ and $5^1$.

HCF and LCM from prime factorisations

The HCF of two or more numbers is the product of the smallest power of each prime factor that is common to all the numbers.

The LCM is the product of the greatest power of each prime factor that occurs in any of the numbers.

From Example 2, you can see that $\text{HCF}(6, 20) \times \text{LCM}(6, 20) = 2 \times 60 = 120$, which is equal to $6 \times 20$. In fact, for any two positive integers $a$ and $b$,

$$\text{HCF}(a, b) \times \text{LCM}(a, b) = a \times b$$

So once we have found the HCF of two positive integers, we can use this result to find their LCM.

Example 3

Find the HCF of 96 and 404 by the prime factorisation method. Then find their LCM.

The prime factorisations are $96 = 2^5 \times 3$ and $404 = 2^2 \times 101$. The only common prime factor is 2, and its smallest power is $2^2$. So the HCF of 96 and 404 is $2^2 = 4$. Then

$$\text{LCM}(96, 404) = \dfrac{96 \times 404}{\text{HCF}(96, 404)} = \dfrac{96 \times 404}{4} = 9696$$
