Assertion and reason

In Questions 16 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): $5 \times 7 \times 11 + 7$ is a composite number.

    Reason (R): A natural number greater than 1 that has a factor other than 1 and itself is composite.

  1. Assertion (A): $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{12}$ is rational.

    Reason (R): The product of two irrational numbers is always irrational.

  1. Assertion (A): For the numbers 4, 6 and 8, $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM} = 4 \times 6 \times 8$.

    Reason (R): For any two positive integers, $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$ is equal to their product.

  1. Assertion (A): $8^n$ never ends with the digit 0, for any natural number $n$.

    Reason (R): 8 is not a prime number.

Very short answer
  1. Write 1001 as a product of primes.
  1. The HCF of two numbers is 23 and their LCM is 1449. One of them is 161. Find the other.
  1. Find the HCF of $2^4 \times 3 \times 5$ and $2^2 \times 3^3$.
Short answer
  1. Find the HCF and LCM of 120, 144 and 204 by the prime factorisation method.
  1. Prove that $\sqrt{11}$ is irrational.
  1. Prove that $2 - 3\sqrt{5}$ is irrational.
  1. Two tanks hold 850 litres and 680 litres of water. Find the capacity of the largest container that can measure the water of either tank a whole number of times.
Long answer
  1. Find the smallest natural number that leaves remainders 8, 14 and 20 when it is divided by 18, 24 and 30 respectively. Then find the next number with the same property.
  1. Prove that $\sqrt{6}$ is irrational. (6 is not a prime, so Theorem 1.2 cannot be used with $p = 6$.)
  1. A shopkeeper has 391 apples and 544 oranges. She packs them in boxes, each box holding the same number of fruits of one kind only, as many as possible. How many fruits go in each box, and how many boxes does she need?
