Why the other options are wrong

A1 $3150 = 2 \times 3^2 \times 5^2 \times 7$: (a). Option (b) is 1050 and (d) is 1260. Option (c) is equal to 3150, but 14 is not a prime.
A2 $135 = 3^3 \times 5$ and $225 = 3^2 \times 5^2$, so the HCF is $3^2 \times 5 = 45$: (b). Option (c) is the LCM, and (a) and (d) are common factors but not the highest.
A4 The other number is $\frac{9 \times 459}{27} = 153$: (b). Option (a) is $\frac{459}{27}$, which leaves out the HCF, and (d) is the LCM itself.
A5 $\sqrt{12} = 2\sqrt{3}$, which is irrational: (c). The others are rational: $\sqrt{49} = 7$, $\frac{\sqrt{9}}{\sqrt{16}} = \frac{3}{4}$ and $0.25 = \frac{1}{4}$.
B1 The number divides $70 - 5 = 65$ and $125 - 8 = 117$, and $\text{HCF}(65, 117) = 13$: (d). Option (a) does not divide 117, (b) ignores the remainders, and (c) is the LCM of 65 and 117.
B2 $\text{LCM}(9, 12, 15) = 180$ minutes, which is 3 hours: (c). Options (a), (b) and (d) use the LCM of only two of the times: 36, 60 and 45 minutes.
B3 $(\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3})^2 = 2 + 2\sqrt{6} + 3 = 5 + 2\sqrt{6}$: (c). Option (a) leaves out the middle term, (b) forgets its 2, and (d) squares $2 + 3$.
B4 The LCM has $3^2$, but $a$ and $b$ have only $3^1$, so $c$ must supply $3^2$: (b). With ${n = 1}$ the LCM would have $3^1$, and with $n = 3$ or 4 it would have a higher power.
C3 The greatest powers are $2^2$, $3^2$, 5 and 7, so the LCM is 1260: (d). Option (a) takes $2^1$, (b) is the HCF, and (c) is the product of the three numbers.
C4 The side must divide both 360 and 450 and be as large as possible, so it is $\text{HCF}(360, 450) = 90$ cm: (b). Options (a) and (d) divide both but are not the largest, and (c) is the LCM.
C5 If $r$ is rational, so is $\frac{4 - r}{3}$, and then $\sqrt{2}$ would be rational: (c). Options (a), (b) and (d) do not contradict anything we know.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. The HCF of two numbers divides their LCM, and their product is $\text{HCF} \times \text{LCM}$, so one fact can be found or ruled out from the others. A question about remainders becomes an HCF or LCM question once the remainders are taken away. And to show that a number is irrational, assume it is rational and rearrange until a number known to be irrational equals a rational one.
