Example 5

Prove that $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Solution.

Step 1 $\sqrt{3} = \frac{a}{b}$, for integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$ assume $\sqrt{3}$ is rational
Step 2 $a$ and $b$ are coprime divide out any common factor other than 1
Step 3 $b\sqrt{3} = a$, so $3b^2 = a^2$ squaring both sides
Step 4 3 divides $a^2$, so 3 divides $a$ Theorem 1.2
Step 5 $a = 3c$ for some integer $c$, so $3b^2 = 9c^2$, that is, $b^2 = 3c^2$
Step 6 3 divides $b^2$, so 3 divides $b$ Theorem 1.2 with $p = 3$
Step 7 3 is a common factor of $a$ and $b$ this contradicts Step 2
Answer The assumption is false, so $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational

In Class IX we stated two facts about irrational numbers. The sum or difference of a rational number and an irrational number is irrational. The product or quotient of a non-zero rational number and an irrational number is irrational. We now prove some particular cases of these facts.

Example 6

Show that $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Solution.

Step 1 $5 - \sqrt{3} = \frac{a}{b}$, for coprime integers $a$ and $b$ with $b \neq 0$ assume it is rational
Step 2 $\sqrt{3} = 5 - \dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{5b - a}{b}$ rearranging
Step 3 $\frac{5b - a}{b}$ is rational, so $\sqrt{3}$ is rational $a$ and $b$ are integers
Step 4 but $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational proved in Example 5
Answer The assumption is false, so $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is irrational
