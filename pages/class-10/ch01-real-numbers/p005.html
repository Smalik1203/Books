1.3Revisiting Irrational Numbers

In Class IX you met irrational numbers and many of their properties. You saw that the rational numbers and the irrational numbers together make up the real numbers, and you even located some irrational numbers on the number line. But we did not prove that these numbers are irrational. In this section we prove that $\sqrt{2}$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $\sqrt{5}$ are irrational. The same argument shows that $\sqrt{p}$ is irrational for every prime $p$. One of the tools we use in the proofs is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic.

Recall that a number $s$ is called irrational if it cannot be written in the form $\frac{p}{q}$, where $p$ and $q$ are integers and $q \neq 0$. Here are some irrational numbers that you already know.
