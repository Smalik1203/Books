Take 5 away from such a number. What is left is divisible by 12, 18 and 30, so it is a common multiple of the three numbers, and so a multiple of their LCM. Since ${12 = 2^2 \times 3}$, $18 = 2 \times 3^2$ and $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$, the LCM is $2^2 \times 3^2 \times 5 = 180$. The number we want is 5 more than a multiple of 180. Now $900 + 5 = 905$ has only three digits, and the next multiple of 180 is 1080. So the answer is $1080 + 5 = 1085$.

Is the product of two irrational numbers always irrational?

Try some numbers. Both $\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$ are irrational, but $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{16} = 4$, which is rational. One example like this is enough to show that the claim is false. The product can also be irrational: $\sqrt{2} \times (1 + \sqrt{2}) = \sqrt{2} + 2$, which is the sum of a rational and an irrational number. So each product has to be worked out; no rule decides it in advance.

Is $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ rational or irrational?

Suppose it is rational, say $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3} = r$. Then $\sqrt{3} = r - \sqrt{2}$. Squaring both sides gives $3 = r^2 - 2r\sqrt{2} + 2$, so $2r\sqrt{2} = r^2 - 1$ and $\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{r^2 - 1}{2r}$. Here $r$ is not 0, because $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is positive. The right-hand side is rational, but $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational. This contradiction shows that $\sqrt{2} + \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Problem 1

The HCF and the LCM of $2^3 \times 3^2 \times 5$ and $2^2 \times 3^3 \times 7$ are, in that order,

primes 2, 3, 5 and 7, greatest powers: $\text{LCM} = 2^3 \times 3^3 \times 5 \times 7 = 7560$
