So 3 is a common factor of $a$ and $b$. But this contradicts the fact that $a$ and $b$ are coprime. The contradiction arose from our assumption that $\sqrt{3}$ is rational. So $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

In Class IX we stated two facts about irrational numbers. The sum or difference of a rational number and an irrational number is irrational. The product or quotient of a non-zero rational number and an irrational number is irrational. We now prove some particular cases of these facts.

Example 6

Show that $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Let us assume the opposite, that $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is rational. Then we can find coprime integers $a$ and $b$, with $b \neq 0$, such that $5 - \sqrt{3} = \frac{a}{b}$. Rearranging this equation, we get

$$\sqrt{3} = 5 - \dfrac{a}{b} = \dfrac{5b - a}{b}$$

Since $a$ and $b$ are integers, $\frac{5b - a}{b}$ is rational, and so $\sqrt{3}$ is rational. But this contradicts the fact that $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational, which we proved in Example 5. The contradiction arose from our assumption that $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is rational. So $5 - \sqrt{3}$ is irrational.

Example 7

Show that $3\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

Let us assume the opposite, that $3\sqrt{2}$ is rational. Then we can find coprime integers $a$ and $b$, with $b \neq 0$, such that $3\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{b}$. Rearranging, we get $\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{3b}$. Since 3, $a$ and $b$ are integers, $\frac{a}{3b}$ is rational, and so $\sqrt{2}$ is rational. But this contradicts the fact that $\sqrt{2}$ is irrational (Theorem 1.3). So $3\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.
