Example 12

Using the identity ${\sec^2\theta = 1 + \tan^2\theta}$, prove that

$$\dfrac{\sin\theta - \cos\theta + 1}{\sin\theta + \cos\theta - 1} = \dfrac{1}{\sec\theta - \tan\theta}$$

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{\sin\theta - \cos\theta + 1}{\sin\theta + \cos\theta - 1} = \dfrac{\tan\theta - 1 + \sec\theta}{\tan\theta + 1 - \sec\theta}$ dividing through by $\cos\theta$
Step 2 $= \dfrac{(\tan\theta + \sec\theta) - 1}{(\tan\theta - \sec\theta) + 1}$ regrouping
Step 3 $= \dfrac{\{(\tan\theta + \sec\theta) - 1\}(\tan\theta - \sec\theta)}{\{(\tan\theta - \sec\theta) + 1\}(\tan\theta - \sec\theta)}$ same factor above and below
Step 4 $= \dfrac{(\tan^2\theta - \sec^2\theta) - (\tan\theta - \sec\theta)}{\{\tan\theta - \sec\theta + 1\}(\tan\theta - \sec\theta)}$ multiplying out the numerator
Step 5 $= \dfrac{-1 - \tan\theta + \sec\theta}{(\tan\theta - \sec\theta + 1)(\tan\theta - \sec\theta)}$ since ${\tan^2\theta - \sec^2\theta = -1}$
Step 6 $= \dfrac{-1}{\tan\theta - \sec\theta} = \dfrac{1}{\sec\theta - \tan\theta}$ cancelling the common factor
Answer This is the RHS of the identity we were asked to prove, so $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$

We are to use the identity involving $\sec\theta$ and $\tan\theta$, so the first step writes the LHS in terms of $\sec\theta$ and $\tan\theta$, by dividing the numerator and the denominator by $\cos\theta$.
