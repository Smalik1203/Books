Type 6 · Proving an identity

Example 11

Prove that ${\sec^4 A - \sec^2 A = \tan^4 A + \tan^2 A}$.

Solution.

Step 1 ${\text{LHS} = \sec^2 A\,(\sec^2 A - 1)}$ taking out $\sec^2 A$
Step 2 ${= (1 + \tan^2 A)\tan^2 A}$ ${\sec^2 A = 1 + \tan^2 A}$
Step 3 ${= \tan^2 A + \tan^4 A = \text{RHS}}$ multiplying out
Answer $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$, so the identity is proved
Example 12

Prove that ${\dfrac{\tan A}{\sec A - 1} = \dfrac{\sec A + 1}{\tan A}}$, where A is an acute angle.

Solution.

Step 1 ${\tan^2 A = \sec^2 A - 1}$ ${1 + \tan^2 A = \sec^2 A}$
Step 2 ${\tan A \times \tan A = (\sec A - 1)(\sec A + 1)}$ difference of two squares
Step 3 $\tan A$ and ${\sec A - 1}$ are not zero $\sec A \gt 1$ for acute A
Step 4 ${\dfrac{\tan A}{\sec A - 1} = \dfrac{\sec A + 1}{\tan A}}$ dividing both sides by ${\tan A\,(\sec A - 1)}$
Answer The identity is proved
Example 13

Prove that ${\dfrac{1}{1 + \sin A} + \dfrac{1}{1 - \sin A} = 2\sec^2 A}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{(1 - \sin A) + (1 + \sin A)}{(1 + \sin A)(1 - \sin A)}$ a common denominator
Step 2 $= \dfrac{2}{1 - \sin^2 A}$ difference of two squares
Step 3 $= \dfrac{2}{\cos^2 A}$ ${\sin^2 A + \cos^2 A = 1}$
Step 4 ${= 2\sec^2 A = \text{RHS}}$
Answer $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$, so the identity is proved
