Example 10

Prove that ${\sec A\,(1 - \sin A)(\sec A + \tan A) = 1}$. Start from the left-hand side, and write every ratio in terms of $\sin A$ and $\cos A$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{LHS} = \sec A\,(1 - \sin A)(\sec A + \tan A)$
Step 2 $= \left(\dfrac{1}{\cos A}\right)(1 - \sin A)\left(\dfrac{1}{\cos A} + \dfrac{\sin A}{\cos A}\right)$ in sines and cosines
Step 3 $= \dfrac{(1 - \sin A)(1 + \sin A)}{\cos^2 A}$ multiplying the fractions
Step 4 $= \dfrac{1 - \sin^2 A}{\cos^2 A}$ difference of two squares
Step 5 $= \dfrac{\cos^2 A}{\cos^2 A} = 1 = \text{RHS}$ since ${\cos^2 A + \sin^2 A = 1}$
Answer $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$, so the identity is proved
Example 11

Prove that ${\dfrac{\cot A - \cos A}{\cot A + \cos A} = \dfrac{\operatorname{cosec} A - 1}{\operatorname{cosec} A + 1}}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $\text{LHS} = \dfrac{\cot A - \cos A}{\cot A + \cos A} = \dfrac{\dfrac{\cos A}{\sin A} - \cos A}{\dfrac{\cos A}{\sin A} + \cos A}$ since $\cot A = \dfrac{\cos A}{\sin A}$
Step 2 $= \dfrac{\cos A\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin A} - 1\right)}{\cos A\left(\dfrac{1}{\sin A} + 1\right)}$ taking out $\cos A$
Step 3 $= \dfrac{\dfrac{1}{\sin A} - 1}{\dfrac{1}{\sin A} + 1}$ cancelling $\cos A$
Step 4 $= \dfrac{\operatorname{cosec} A - 1}{\operatorname{cosec} A + 1} = \text{RHS}$ since $\operatorname{cosec} A = \dfrac{1}{\sin A}$
Answer $\text{LHS} = \text{RHS}$, so the identity is proved
