Now $\triangle ABD$ is a right triangle, right-angled at D, with ${\angle BAD = 30^\circ}$ and ${\angle ABD = 60^\circ}$ (Fig. 8.15). To find the trigonometric ratios, we need to know the lengths of the sides of the triangle. So let ${AB = 2a}$. Then

${BD = \frac{1}{2}BC = a}$

${AD^2 = AB^2 - BD^2 = (2a)^2 - a^2 = 3a^2}$, so ${AD = a\sqrt{3}}$

Now we have

$\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{BD}{AB} = \dfrac{a}{2a} = \dfrac{1}{2}$,   $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{a\sqrt{3}}{2a} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{BD}{AD} = \dfrac{a}{a\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

also $\operatorname{cosec} 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sin 30^\circ} = 2$,   $\sec 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\cos 30^\circ} = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$   and   $\cot 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\tan 30^\circ} = \sqrt{3}$

In the same way,

$\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{a\sqrt{3}}{2a} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$,   $\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$,   $\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}$

$\operatorname{cosec} 60^\circ = \dfrac{2}{\sqrt{3}}$,   $\sec 60^\circ = 2$   and   $\cot 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$

Trigonometric Ratios of 0° and 90°

Let us see what happens to the trigonometric ratios of angle A if we make it smaller and smaller in the right triangle ABC of Fig. 8.16, until it becomes zero. As $\angle A$ gets smaller and smaller, the side BC gets shorter. The point C gets closer to the point B, and when $\angle A$ is very close to $0^\circ$, AC becomes almost the same as AB (Fig. 8.17).
