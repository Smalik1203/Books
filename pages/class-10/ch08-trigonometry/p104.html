Problem 5

If ${5\tan\theta = 4}$, then $\frac{5\sin\theta - 3\cos\theta}{5\sin\theta + 2\cos\theta}$ is

  1. $\dfrac{1}{6}$
  2. $\dfrac{17}{22}$
  3. $6$
  4. $-\dfrac{3}{2}$
Solution

divide the top and the bottom by $\cos\theta$: $\dfrac{5\tan\theta - 3}{5\tan\theta + 2} = \dfrac{4 - 3}{4 + 2} = \dfrac{1}{6}$

The answer is (a). Option (b) takes ${\tan\theta = 4}$ in place of ${5\tan\theta = 4}$, (c) turns the fraction upside down, and (d) cancels ${5\sin\theta}$ from the top and the bottom, which is not allowed in a sum. Dividing by $\cos\theta$ is allowed here: $\tan\theta$ is defined, so $\cos\theta$ is not zero. As a check, take a right triangle with opposite side 4 and adjacent side 5: the answer does not depend on its hypotenuse.
