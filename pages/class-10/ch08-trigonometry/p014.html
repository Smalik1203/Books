Now let us see what happens to the trigonometric ratios of $\angle A$ when we make it larger and larger in $\triangle ABC$, until it becomes $90^\circ$. As $\angle A$ gets larger and larger, $\angle C$ gets smaller and smaller. So, as before, the side AB gets shorter, and the point A gets closer to the point B. When $\angle A$ is very close to $90^\circ$, $\angle C$ is very close to $0^\circ$, and the side AC almost lies along the side BC (Fig. 8.18).

When $\angle C$ is very close to $0^\circ$, $\angle A$ is very close to $90^\circ$, the side AC is nearly the same as the side BC, and so $\sin A$ is very close to 1. Also, when $\angle A$ is very close to $90^\circ$, $\angle C$ is very close to $0^\circ$ and the side AB is nearly zero, so $\cos A$ is very close to 0. So we define ${\sin 90^\circ = 1}$ and ${\cos 90^\circ = 0}$.

Now, why don't you find the other trigonometric ratios of $90^\circ$?
