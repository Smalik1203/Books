Example 6

In $\triangle ABC$, right-angled at B, ${AB = 5}$ cm and ${\angle ACB = 30^\circ}$ (Fig. 8.19). Find the lengths of the sides BC and AC.

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{AB}{BC} = \tan C$ AB opposite, BC adjacent to C
Step 2 $\dfrac{5}{BC} = \tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, which gives ${BC = 5\sqrt{3}}$ cm Table 8.1
Step 3 $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{AC}$ Why this ratio?
Step 4 $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{AC}$, so ${AC = 10}$ cm
Answer ${BC = 5\sqrt{3}}$ cm   and   ${AC = 10}$ cm

To find a side, choose the trigonometric ratio that involves that side and the side you are given. We could also have used the Pythagoras theorem to find the third side here: ${AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + (5\sqrt{3})^2}}$ cm ${= \sqrt{100}}$ cm ${= 10}$ cm.
