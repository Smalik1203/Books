Example 6

In $\triangle ABC$, right-angled at B, ${AB = 5}$ cm and ${\angle ACB = 30^\circ}$ (Fig. 8.19). Find the lengths of the sides BC and AC.

To find the length of the side BC, we choose the trigonometric ratio that involves BC and the given side AB. BC is the side adjacent to angle C, and AB is the side opposite to angle C, so

$\dfrac{AB}{BC} = \tan C$

$\dfrac{5}{BC} = \tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$, which gives ${BC = 5\sqrt{3}}$ cm

To find the length of the side AC, we use (Why?)

$\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{AC}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{AC}$, so ${AC = 10}$ cm

We could also have used the Pythagoras theorem to find the third side here: ${AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{5^2 + (5\sqrt{3})^2}}$ cm ${= \sqrt{100}}$ cm ${= 10}$ cm.
