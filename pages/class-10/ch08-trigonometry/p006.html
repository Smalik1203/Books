now $\cos A = \dfrac{AB}{AC} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}k}{3k} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$

In the same way, you can find the other trigonometric ratios of the angle A.

Remark. The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle, so the value of $\sin A$ or $\cos A$ is always less than 1 when A is an acute angle. For the angles of $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, defined in Section 8.3, one of them is equal to 1.

Let us look at some examples.

Example 1

Given ${\tan A = \frac{4}{3}}$, find the other trigonometric ratios of the angle A.

First draw a right triangle ABC, right-angled at B (Fig. 8.8). We know that ${\tan A = \frac{BC}{AB} = \frac{4}{3}}$. So if ${BC = 4k}$, then ${AB = 3k}$, where $k$ is a positive number.

Now, by the Pythagoras theorem,

${AC^2 = AB^2 + BC^2 = (3k)^2 + (4k)^2 = 25k^2}$, so ${AC = 5k}$

Now we can write all the trigonometric ratios from their definitions.
