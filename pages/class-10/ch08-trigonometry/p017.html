Example 7

In $\triangle PQR$, right-angled at Q (Fig. 8.20), ${PQ = 3}$ cm and ${PR = 6}$ cm. Find $\angle QPR$ and $\angle PRQ$. Use the trigonometric ratio that involves both of the given sides, and then the angle sum of the triangle.

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{PQ}{PR} = \sin R$,   that is,   $\sin R = \dfrac{3}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$ PQ opposite, PR hypotenuse
Step 2 ${\angle PRQ = 30^\circ}$ Table 8.1
Step 3 ${\angle QPR = 60^\circ}$ Why?
Answer ${\angle QPR = 60^\circ}$   and   ${\angle PRQ = 30^\circ}$

You may notice that if one of the sides of a right triangle and any other part, either an acute angle or another side, are known, the remaining sides and angles of the triangle can be found.

Example 8

If ${\sin(A - B) = \frac{1}{2}}$, ${\cos(A + B) = \frac{1}{2}}$, ${0^\circ \lt A + B \leq 90^\circ}$ and ${A \gt B}$, find A and B.

Solution.

Step 1 since ${\sin(A - B) = \frac{1}{2}}$, ${A - B = 30^\circ}$   (1) Why?
Step 2 since ${\cos(A + B) = \frac{1}{2}}$, ${A + B = 60^\circ}$   (2) Why?
Answer ${A = 45^\circ}$ and ${B = 15^\circ}$ solving (1) and (2)
