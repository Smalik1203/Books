Example 3

Take $\triangle ACB$, right-angled at C, in which ${AB = 29}$ units, ${BC = 21}$ units and ${\angle ABC = \theta}$ (Fig. 8.10). Find the values of (i) ${\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta}$ and (ii) ${\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta}$.

Solution.

Step 1 ${AC = \sqrt{AB^2 - BC^2} = \sqrt{29^2 - 21^2} = \sqrt{(29 - 21)(29 + 21)} = \sqrt{(8)(50)} = \sqrt{400} = 20}$ units the Pythagoras theorem
Step 2 $\sin\theta = \dfrac{AC}{AB} = \dfrac{20}{29}$   and   $\cos\theta = \dfrac{BC}{AB} = \dfrac{21}{29}$ the definitions
Step 3 (i) $\cos^2\theta + \sin^2\theta = \left(\dfrac{21}{29}\right)^2 + \left(\dfrac{20}{29}\right)^2 = \dfrac{21^2 + 20^2}{29^2} = \dfrac{441 + 400}{841} = 1$
Step 4 (ii) $\cos^2\theta - \sin^2\theta = \left(\dfrac{21}{29}\right)^2 - \left(\dfrac{20}{29}\right)^2 = \dfrac{(21 + 20)(21 - 20)}{29^2} = \dfrac{41}{841}$ difference of two squares
Answer (i) 1   (ii) $\dfrac{41}{841}$
