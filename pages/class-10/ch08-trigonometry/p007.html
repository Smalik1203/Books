$\sin A = \dfrac{BC}{AC} = \dfrac{4k}{5k} = \dfrac{4}{5}$   and   $\cos A = \dfrac{AB}{AC} = \dfrac{3k}{5k} = \dfrac{3}{5}$

so $\cot A = \dfrac{1}{\tan A} = \dfrac{3}{4}$,   $\operatorname{cosec} A = \dfrac{1}{\sin A} = \dfrac{5}{4}$   and   $\sec A = \dfrac{1}{\cos A} = \dfrac{5}{3}$

Example 2

If $\angle B$ and $\angle Q$ are acute angles such that ${\sin B = \sin Q}$, prove that ${\angle B = \angle Q}$. Use two right triangles, one containing each angle.

Take two right triangles ABC and PQR, right-angled at C and at R, in which ${\sin B = \sin Q}$ (Fig. 8.9). We shall show that their corresponding sides are in the same ratio, and then use a criterion from Chapter 6.

$\sin B = \dfrac{AC}{AB}$   and   $\sin Q = \dfrac{PR}{PQ}$

so $\dfrac{AC}{AB} = \dfrac{PR}{PQ}$, and $\dfrac{AC}{PR} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ} = k$, say   (1)

by the Pythagoras theorem, ${BC = \sqrt{AB^2 - AC^2}}$ and ${QR = \sqrt{PQ^2 - PR^2}}$

so $\dfrac{BC}{QR} = \dfrac{\sqrt{AB^2 - AC^2}}{\sqrt{PQ^2 - PR^2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{k^2PQ^2 - k^2PR^2}}{\sqrt{PQ^2 - PR^2}} = \dfrac{k\sqrt{PQ^2 - PR^2}}{\sqrt{PQ^2 - PR^2}} = k$   (2)

from (1) and (2), $\dfrac{AC}{PR} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR}$

So, by Theorem 6.4, ${\triangle ACB \sim \triangle PRQ}$, and therefore ${\angle B = \angle Q}$. Recall that Theorem 6.4 is the SSS similarity criterion: triangles whose sides are in the same ratio are similar. This shows that an acute angle is fixed by the value of its sine: two different acute angles cannot have the same sine. So once the sine of an acute angle is known, the angle itself is known.
