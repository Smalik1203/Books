The ratios depend only on the angle

The values of the trigonometric ratios of an angle do not change with the lengths of the sides of the triangle, as long as the angle stays the same.

Note. For convenience, we write $\sin^2 A$, $\cos^2 A$, and so on, in place of $(\sin A)^2$, $(\cos A)^2$, and so on. But ${\operatorname{cosec} A = (\sin A)^{-1} \neq \sin^{-1} A}$. The symbol $\sin^{-1} A$, read "sine inverse A", has a different meaning, which you will study in higher classes. The same conventions hold for the other trigonometric ratios. Sometimes the Greek letter $\theta$ (theta) is also used to name an angle.

We have defined six trigonometric ratios of an acute angle. If we know any one of them, can we find the others? Let us see.

Suppose that in a right triangle ABC, ${\sin A = \frac{1}{3}}$. This means ${\frac{BC}{AC} = \frac{1}{3}}$, that is, the lengths of the sides BC and AC of the triangle are in the ratio ${1 : 3}$ (Fig. 8.7). So if ${BC = k}$, then ${AC = 3k}$, where $k$ is any positive number. To find the other trigonometric ratios of the angle A, we need the length of the third side AB. Do you remember the Pythagoras theorem? Let us use it to find AB.

${AB^2 = AC^2 - BC^2 = (3k)^2 - k^2 = 8k^2 = (2\sqrt{2}k)^2}$

so ${AB = \pm 2\sqrt{2}k}$

and ${AB = 2\sqrt{2}k}$   (Why is AB not ${-2\sqrt{2}k}$?)

now $\cos A = \dfrac{AB}{AC} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}k}{3k} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{3}$

In the same way, you can find the other trigonometric ratios of the angle A.

Remark. The hypotenuse is the longest side of a right triangle, so the value of $\sin A$ or $\cos A$ is always less than 1 when A is an acute angle. For the angles of $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, defined in Section 8.3, one of them is equal to 1.
