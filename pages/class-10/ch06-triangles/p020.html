$OA \cdot OB = OC \cdot OD$ (given), so $\dfrac{OA}{OC} = \dfrac{OD}{OB}$   (1)

also $\angle AOD = \angle COB$   (vertically opposite angles)   (2)

so ${\triangle AOD \sim \triangle COB}$   [SAS similarity criterion, from (1) and (2)]

so $\angle A = \angle C$ and $\angle D = \angle B$   (corresponding angles of similar triangles)

Example 7

A girl 90 cm tall is walking away from the base of a lamp-post at a speed of 1.2 m/s. The lamp is 3.6 m above the ground. Take the ground to be level and the lamp-post to be upright, and assume that she walks in a straight line directly away from the foot of the lamp-post. Find the length of her shadow after 4 seconds, in metres, and show each step of your working.

Let AB be the lamp-post and CD the girl after she has walked away from it for 4 seconds (Fig. 6.32). You can see from the figure that DE is the girl’s shadow. Let DE be $x$ metres. In 4 seconds she walks 1.2 m × 4 = 4.8 m, so BD = 4.8 m. In $\triangle ABE$ and $\triangle CDE$,

$\angle B = \angle D$   (each is $90^\circ$, because the lamp-post and the girl both stand upright)

$\angle E = \angle E$   (the same angle)

so ${\triangle ABE \sim \triangle CDE}$   (AA similarity criterion)

so $\dfrac{BE}{DE} = \dfrac{AB}{CD}$, that is, $\dfrac{4.8 + x}{x} = \dfrac{3.6}{0.9}$   (90 cm = 0.9 m)

$4.8 + x = 4x$, so $3x = 4.8$ and $x = 1.6$

So after walking for 4 seconds, the girl’s shadow is 1.6 m long.
