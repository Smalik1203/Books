Example 8

In Fig. 6.33, CM and RN are the medians of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, so M is the mid-point of AB and N is the mid-point of PQ. If ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR}$, prove that

(i) ${\triangle AMC \sim \triangle PNR}$   (ii) ${\frac{CM}{RN} = \frac{AB}{PQ}}$   (iii) ${\triangle CMB \sim \triangle RNQ}$

Solution. (i) Since ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR}$,

Step 1 $\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   (1) sides in the same ratio
Step 2 $\angle A = \angle P$, $\angle B = \angle Q$ and $\angle C = \angle R$   (2) corresponding angles
Step 3 AB = 2AM and PQ = 2PN CM and RN are medians
Step 4 $\dfrac{2AM}{2PN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$, that is, $\dfrac{AM}{PN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   (3) so from (1)
Step 5 $\angle MAC = \angle NPR$   (4) from (2)
Step 6 ${\triangle AMC \sim \triangle PNR}$   (5) SAS similarity, from (3) and (4)

(ii) From (5) and (1),

Step 7 $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   (6) from (5)
Step 8 $\dfrac{CA}{RP} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}$   (7) from (1)
Step 9 $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}$   (8) from (6) and (7)

(iii) Again using (1) and (8),

Step 10 $\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR}$, so $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{BC}{QR}$   (9)
Step 11 $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{2BM}{2QN} = \dfrac{BM}{QN}$   (10) M and N are mid-points
Step 12 $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{BC}{QR} = \dfrac{BM}{QN}$ from (9) and (10)
Answer (i) and (ii) are Steps 6 and 9; (iii) ${\triangle CMB \sim \triangle RNQ}$, by SSS similarity
