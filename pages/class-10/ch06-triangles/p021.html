Example 8

In Fig. 6.33, CM and RN are the medians of $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle PQR$, so M is the mid-point of AB and N is the mid-point of PQ. If ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR}$, prove that

(i) ${\triangle AMC \sim \triangle PNR}$   (ii) ${\frac{CM}{RN} = \frac{AB}{PQ}}$   (iii) ${\triangle CMB \sim \triangle RNQ}$

(i) Since ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR}$,

$\dfrac{AB}{PQ} = \dfrac{BC}{QR} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   (1)

$\angle A = \angle P$, $\angle B = \angle Q$ and $\angle C = \angle R$   (2)

But AB = 2AM and PQ = 2PN, because CM and RN are medians. So from (1),

$\dfrac{2AM}{2PN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$, that is, $\dfrac{AM}{PN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   (3)

$\angle MAC = \angle NPR$   [from (2)]   (4)

so ${\triangle AMC \sim \triangle PNR}$   [SAS similarity, from (3) and (4)]   (5)

(ii) From (5) and (1),

$\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{CA}{RP}$   [from (5)]   (6)

but $\dfrac{CA}{RP} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}$   [from (1)]   (7)

so $\dfrac{CM}{RN} = \dfrac{AB}{PQ}$   [from (6) and (7)]   (8)
