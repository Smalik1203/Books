Triangles whose corresponding angles are equal are called equiangular. The Greek mathematician Thales (640–546 BCE) found that the ratio of any two corresponding sides in two equiangular triangles is always the same. He is believed to have used a result called the Basic Proportionality Theorem, now also known as the Thales Theorem.

To understand the Basic Proportionality Theorem, try this activity.

Activity 2. Draw any angle XAY. On its arm AX, mark five points P, Q, D, R and B such that AP = PQ = QD = DR = RB. Through B, draw any line meeting the arm AY at C. Then through D, draw a line parallel to BC, meeting AC at E (Fig. 6.9).

From your construction, $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{3}{2}$. Now measure AE and EC. What is $\frac{AE}{EC}$? You will find that it is also $\frac{3}{2}$. So in $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$ and ${\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}}$. Is this a coincidence? No. It follows from the theorem below, the Basic Proportionality Theorem.

Theorem 6.1

If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.

Proof. We are given a triangle ABC in which a line parallel to the side BC meets the other two sides AB and AC at D and E (Fig. 6.10). We need to prove that ${\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}}$. Join BE and CD, and draw $DM \perp AC$ and $EN \perp AB$.
