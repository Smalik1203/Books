The area of a triangle is $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$, and, as in Class IX, we write the area of $\triangle ADE$ as ar(ADE). Take AD and DB as bases, with the same height EN, and then AE and EC as bases, with the same height DM. This gives

$\text{ar}(ADE) = \frac{1}{2} AD \times EN$   and   $\text{ar}(BDE) = \frac{1}{2} DB \times EN$

$\text{ar}(ADE) = \frac{1}{2} AE \times DM$   and   $\text{ar}(DEC) = \frac{1}{2} EC \times DM$

Dividing,

$\dfrac{\text{ar}(ADE)}{\text{ar}(BDE)} = \dfrac{\frac{1}{2} AD \times EN}{\frac{1}{2} DB \times EN} = \dfrac{AD}{DB}$   (1)

$\dfrac{\text{ar}(ADE)}{\text{ar}(DEC)} = \dfrac{\frac{1}{2} AE \times DM}{\frac{1}{2} EC \times DM} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$   (2)

Now $\triangle BDE$ and $\triangle DEC$ are on the same base DE and between the same parallels BC and DE. So

$$\text{ar}(BDE) = \text{ar}(DEC) \qquad (3)$$

By (3), the left-hand sides of (1) and (2) are equal. So their right-hand sides are equal too, and $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$ as required.
