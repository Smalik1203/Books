It can be proved by taking two triangles ABC and DEF such that $\angle A = \angle D$, $\angle B = \angle E$ and $\angle C = \angle F$ (Fig. 6.24). Some steps of the proof are left for you to explain, and they are marked (Why?) and (How?). Mark P on DE and Q on DF such that DP = AB and DQ = AC, and join PQ. Then

${\triangle ABC \cong \triangle DPQ}$   (Why?)

this gives $\angle B = \angle P = \angle E$ and $PQ \parallel EF$   (How?)

so $\dfrac{DP}{PE} = \dfrac{DQ}{QF}$   (Why?), that is, $\dfrac{AB}{DE} = \dfrac{AC}{DF}$   (Why?)

In the same way, $\frac{AB}{DE} = \frac{BC}{EF}$, and so the three ratios $\frac{AB}{DE}$, $\frac{BC}{EF}$ and $\frac{AC}{DF}$ are all equal.

Remark. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, then by the angle sum property of a triangle, their third angles are also equal. So the AAA criterion can also be stated in the following way.

The AA similarity criterion

If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar.

You have seen that if the three angles of one triangle are equal to the three angles of another, then their corresponding sides are proportional. What about the converse? If the sides of one triangle are proportional to the sides of another, are their corresponding angles equal? Let us find out with an activity.
