Let us look at some examples that use these theorems.

Example 1

A line meets the sides AB and AC of $\triangle ABC$ at D and E and is parallel to BC (Fig. 6.13). Prove that ${\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}}$.

$DE \parallel BC$   (given)

so $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$   (Theorem 6.1)

or $\dfrac{DB}{AD} = \dfrac{EC}{AE}$   (turning both fractions upside down)

or $\dfrac{DB}{AD} + 1 = \dfrac{EC}{AE} + 1$

or $\dfrac{AB}{AD} = \dfrac{AC}{AE}$   (since $DB + AD = AB$ and $EC + AE = AC$)

so $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$
