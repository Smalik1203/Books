Quadrilateral A′B′C′D′ is an enlargement of quadrilateral ABCD. This is because light travels in straight lines: A′ lies on ray OA, B′ on ray OB, C′ on ray OC and D′ on ray OD. So the two quadrilaterals have the same shape but different sizes, and quadrilateral A′B′C′D′ is similar to quadrilateral ABCD. We can also say that ABCD is similar to A′B′C′D′.

Here vertex A corresponds to vertex A′, B to B′, C to C′ and D to D′. We write these correspondences as $A \leftrightarrow A'$, $B \leftrightarrow B'$, $C \leftrightarrow C'$ and $D \leftrightarrow D'$. By measuring the angles and the sides of the two quadrilaterals, you can check that

(i) $\angle A = \angle A'$, $\angle B = \angle B'$, $\angle C = \angle C'$, $\angle D = \angle D'$ and

(ii) $\dfrac{AB}{A'B'} = \dfrac{BC}{B'C'} = \dfrac{CD}{C'D'} = \dfrac{DA}{D'A'}$

This shows again that two polygons with the same number of sides are similar if all their corresponding angles are equal and all their corresponding sides are in the same ratio. From this you can see that the quadrilaterals ABCD and PQRS in Fig. 6.5 are similar. Check the four angles and the four ratios of sides for yourself.

Remark. You can check that if one polygon is similar to a second polygon, and the second polygon is similar to a third, then the first polygon is similar to the third.

In the square and the rectangle of Fig. 6.6, the corresponding angles are equal, but the corresponding sides are not in the same ratio. So the two quadrilaterals are not similar. Every angle is a right angle, but the ratios 3 : 3.5 and 3 : 3 are not equal.
