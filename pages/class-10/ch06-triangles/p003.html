Similar polygons

Two polygons with the same number of sides are similar if (i) their corresponding angles are equal, and (ii) their corresponding sides are in the same ratio, that is, in proportion.

The common ratio of the corresponding sides is called the scale factor, or the representative fraction, of the two polygons. World maps and the plans, or blueprints, for a building are drawn using a suitable scale factor.

To understand similarity better, try the following activity.

Activity 1. Fix a lighted bulb at a point O on the ceiling of your classroom, with a table directly below it. Cut a quadrilateral ABCD out of flat cardboard and hold it parallel to the ground, between the bulb and the table. A shadow of ABCD falls on the table. Mark the outline of this shadow as A′B′C′D′ (Fig. 6.4).

Quadrilateral A′B′C′D′ is an enlargement of quadrilateral ABCD. This is because light travels in straight lines: A′ lies on ray OA, B′ on ray OB, C′ on ray OC and D′ on ray OD. So the two quadrilaterals have the same shape but different sizes, and quadrilateral A′B′C′D′ is similar to quadrilateral ABCD. We can also say that ABCD is similar to A′B′C′D′.

Here vertex A corresponds to vertex A′, B to B′, C to C′ and D to D′. We write these correspondences as $A \leftrightarrow A'$, $B \leftrightarrow B'$, $C \leftrightarrow C'$ and $D \leftrightarrow D'$. By measuring the angles and the sides of the two quadrilaterals, you can check that

(i) $\angle A = \angle A'$, $\angle B = \angle B'$, $\angle C = \angle C'$, $\angle D = \angle D'$ and

(ii) $\dfrac{AB}{A'B'} = \dfrac{BC}{B'C'} = \dfrac{CD}{C'D'} = \dfrac{DA}{D'A'}$
