Assertion and reason

In Questions 14 to 17, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): If $\angle A = \angle D$ and $\angle B = \angle E$ in $\triangle ABC$ and $\triangle DEF$, then ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF}$.

    Reason (R): The angles of a triangle add up to $180^\circ$, so the third angles are equal too.

  1. Assertion (A): A triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm is similar to a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm.

    Reason (R): All right triangles are similar.

  1. Assertion (A): Any two rectangles are similar.

    Reason (R): Every angle of a rectangle is $90^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$ with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 2 cm and DB = 3 cm, then AE : EC = 2 : 3.

    Reason (R): Two triangles with two pairs of equal angles are similar.

Very short answer
  1. ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle PQR}$, with $\angle A = 45^\circ$ and $\angle Q = 75^\circ$. Find $\angle C$.
  1. In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$ with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 1.8 cm, DB = 5.4 cm and AE = 2.1 cm, find EC.
  1. The sides of a triangle are 5 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm. The shortest side of a similar triangle is 15 cm. Find the perimeter of the second triangle.
  1. A map is drawn with the scale factor 1 : 50000. Two towns are 6 cm apart on the map. How far apart are they, in kilometres?

  1. P and Q are points on the sides AB and AC of $\triangle ABC$, with AP = 3 cm, PB = 6 cm, AQ = 2.5 cm and QC = 5 cm. Show that $PQ \parallel BC$, and find PQ if BC = 12 cm.
  1. Prove that two similar triangles have perimeters in the ratio of their corresponding sides.
  1. A girl 1.2 m tall stands upright 4 m from the foot of an upright lamp-post, on level ground. Her shadow is 1.5 m long. Find the height of the lamp above the ground.
