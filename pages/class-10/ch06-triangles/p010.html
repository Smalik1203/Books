Join AC, and let it meet EF at G (Fig. 6.15).

$AB \parallel DC$ and $EF \parallel AB$ (given), so $EF \parallel DC$   (lines parallel to the same line are parallel to each other)

in $\triangle ADC$, $EG \parallel DC$, so $\dfrac{AE}{ED} = \dfrac{AG}{GC}$   (Theorem 6.1)   (1)

in $\triangle CAB$, $GF \parallel AB$, so $\dfrac{CG}{GA} = \dfrac{CF}{FB}$, that is, $\dfrac{AG}{GC} = \dfrac{BF}{FC}$   (2)

From (1) and (2), the ratios $\frac{AE}{ED}$ and $\frac{BF}{FC}$ are both equal to $\frac{AG}{GC}$, so they are equal, which is what we had to show.
