Using What You Know
Similar triangles in questions that do not say which triangles to compare.

The chapter gave you the Basic Proportionality Theorem, its converse, and the AA, SSS and SAS criteria. What changes now is that a question may not point to the triangles you need: finding a pair of similar triangles is part of the work.

In $\triangle ABC$, D is on AB and E is on AC, with $DE \parallel BC$. If AD = $x$, DB = ${x - 2}$, AE = ${x + 2}$ and EC = ${x - 1}$, find $x$.

Since $DE \parallel BC$, Theorem 6.1 gives $\frac{AD}{DB} = \frac{AE}{EC}$, so $\frac{x}{x - 2} = \frac{x + 2}{x - 1}$. Cross-multiplying, $x(x - 1) = (x + 2)(x - 2)$, that is, $x^2 - x = x^2 - 4$. The $x^2$ terms cancel, so $x = 4$. Then AD = 4, DB = 2, AE = 6 and EC = 3, and both ratios are 2. The equation looked quadratic, but it was linear.

ABC is a right triangle with the right angle at B, and BD is perpendicular to AC, with D on AC. Show that $BD^2 = AD \cdot DC$.

There are no lengths to work with, so look for two similar triangles that both contain BD. In $\triangle ADB$ and $\triangle BDC$, the angles at D are both $90^\circ$. Also $\angle A = 90^\circ - \angle ABD$, from $\triangle ADB$, and $\angle DBC = 90^\circ - \angle ABD$, because $\angle ABD + \angle DBC = 90^\circ$. So $\angle A = \angle DBC$, and by the AA criterion ${\triangle ADB \sim \triangle BDC}$. This gives ${\frac{AD}{BD} = \frac{BD}{DC}}$, and cross-multiplying gives the result.

In $\triangle PQR$, S is on PQ and T is on PR, with PS = 4 cm, SQ = 6 cm, PT = 5 cm and TR = 7.5 cm. Is ST parallel to QR? If QR = 15 cm, find ST.

Here $\frac{PS}{SQ} = \frac{4}{6} = \frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{PT}{TR} = \frac{5}{7.5} = \frac{2}{3}$, so $ST \parallel QR$ by Theorem 6.2. Then $\angle PST = \angle PQR$ (corresponding angles), and $\angle P$ is common, so ${\triangle PST \sim \triangle PQR}$ by the AA criterion. The ratio of their sides is $\frac{PS}{PQ} = \frac{4}{10}$, not $\frac{PS}{SQ}$, so ST = $\frac{4}{10} \times 15$ = 6 cm.
