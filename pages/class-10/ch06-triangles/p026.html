What this chapter showed
  1. Two figures that have the same shape, but not necessarily the same size, are called similar figures.
  2. All congruent figures are similar, but similar figures need not be congruent.
  3. Two polygons with the same number of sides are similar if (i) their corresponding angles are equal and (ii) their corresponding sides are in the same ratio, that is, in proportion.
  4. If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
  5. If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side.
  6. If in two triangles corresponding angles are equal, then their corresponding sides are in the same ratio, and so the two triangles are similar (AAA similarity criterion).
  7. If two angles of one triangle are equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar (AA similarity criterion).
  8. If in two triangles corresponding sides are in the same ratio, then their corresponding angles are equal, and so the triangles are similar (SSS similarity criterion).
  9. If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are in the same ratio, then the triangles are similar (SAS similarity criterion).
