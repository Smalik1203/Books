Are any two isosceles triangles similar? Are any two right-angled isosceles triangles similar?

An isosceles triangle with angles $40^\circ$, $40^\circ$ and $100^\circ$ and one with angles $70^\circ$, $70^\circ$ and $40^\circ$ are not equiangular, so they are not similar. One example is enough to show that the answer to the first question is no. In a right-angled isosceles triangle, the two equal angles add up to $90^\circ$, so each is $45^\circ$. So every such triangle has angles $45^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, and any two of them are similar by the AA criterion.

A girl puts a small mirror flat on level ground, 6 m from the foot of a tree. She stands 1.2 m from the mirror, on the side away from the tree, and sees the top of the tree in it. Her eyes are 1.5 m above the ground. How tall is the tree?

Light from the top of the tree reaches the mirror and is reflected to her eyes, making equal angles with the ground on the two sides of the mirror. So the triangle made by the tree and the mirror, and the triangle made by the girl and the mirror, have equal angles at the mirror. Both also have a right angle at the ground, so they are similar by the AA criterion. So the height of the tree divided by 6 is equal to $\frac{1.5}{1.2}$, and the tree is 7.5 m tall. Nobody climbed the tree: two short distances and one height were enough.

Problem 1

In $\triangle ABC$, $DE \parallel BC$ with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 3 cm, DB = 5 cm and AC = 12 cm, then AE is

  1. 7.2 cm
  2. 20 cm
  3. 4.5 cm
  4. 7.5 cm
Solution

By Example 1, $\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}$, and AB = 3 + 5 = 8 cm, so AE = $\frac{3}{8} \times 12$ = 4.5 cm: (c). Option (d) is EC, and (a) takes AC as if it were EC. Option (b) turns the ratio $\frac{AD}{DB}$ upside down.
