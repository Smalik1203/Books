$\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE'}{E'C}$   (Why?)

so $\dfrac{AE}{EC} = \dfrac{AE'}{E'C}$   (Why?)

Adding 1 to both sides of the second line, you can see that E and E′ must be the same point. (Why?)

Let us look at some examples that use these theorems.

Example 1

A line meets the sides AB and AC of $\triangle ABC$ at D and E and is parallel to BC (Fig. 6.13). Prove that ${\frac{AD}{AB} = \frac{AE}{AC}}$.

Solution.

Step 1 $DE \parallel BC$ given
Step 2 $\dfrac{AD}{DB} = \dfrac{AE}{EC}$ Theorem 6.1
Step 3 $\dfrac{DB}{AD} = \dfrac{EC}{AE}$ both fractions upside down
Step 4 $\dfrac{DB}{AD} + 1 = \dfrac{EC}{AE} + 1$ adding 1 to both sides
Step 5 $\dfrac{AB}{AD} = \dfrac{AC}{AE}$ since $DB + AD = AB$ and $EC + AE = AC$
Answer $\dfrac{AD}{AB} = \dfrac{AE}{AC}$, turning both fractions upside down again
