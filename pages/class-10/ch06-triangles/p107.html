Why the other options are wrong

A2 $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{EC}$ gives EC = 6 cm: (b). Option (d) multiplies 4 by 2.
A4 $\frac{EF}{BC} = \frac{DE}{AB} = 1.5$, so EF = 7.5 cm: (a). Option (b) adds the difference 1.5 to 5, and (c) divides by 1.5 instead of multiplying.
A5 The third angles are $80^\circ$ and $40^\circ$, so both triangles have angles $40^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $80^\circ$: similar by AA, (a). Nothing says the sides are equal, so (c) does not follow.
A7 $\frac{h}{10} = \frac{3}{2}$ gives $h = 15$ m: (b). Option (a) turns the ratio upside down, and (c) adds the difference of 1 m.
A8 $DE \parallel BC$ by Theorem 6.2, so ${\triangle ADE \sim \triangle ABC}$ with ratio $\frac{1}{2}$, and DE = 5 cm: (c). Option (a) doubles instead of halving.
B1 $\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}$, so $ST \parallel QR$, and then $\frac{ST}{QR} = \frac{PS}{PQ} = \frac{2}{5}$: (a). Option (b) uses PS : SQ instead.
B2 All three sides are in the same ratio, so their sums are too: $\frac{DE}{AB} = \frac{36}{24}$, and DE = 9 cm: (c). Option (a) uses $\frac{24}{36}$, and (b) doubles AB.
B4 ${\triangle AOB \sim \triangle COD}$, so $\frac{AO}{OC} = \frac{BO}{OD}$, $\frac{6}{4} = \frac{9}{OD}$ and OD = 6 cm: (b).
B5 $\frac{5}{10} = \frac{6}{12} = \frac{1}{2}$, but $\frac{7}{15}$ is not $\frac{1}{2}$, so the triangles are not similar: (b).
B6 With shadow $x$ m, $\frac{3 + x}{x} = \frac{4.5}{1.5} = 3$, so $x = 1.5$: (c). Option (d) multiplies 3 by 3.
C1 ${\triangle ADC \sim \triangle BAC}$ (AA), so $\frac{CA}{CB} = \frac{CD}{CA}$, $CA^2 = 9 \times 4 = 36$ and CA = 6 cm: (c). Option (a) is $CA^2$.
C2 ${\triangle ADB \sim \triangle BDC}$, so $BD^2 = AD \cdot DC = 36$ and BD = 6 cm: (d). Option (b) is $BD^2$, and (a) is the average of 4 and 9.
C3 ${\triangle ABE \sim \triangle CFB}$, so $\frac{AB}{CF} = \frac{AE}{CB}$, $\frac{8}{CF} = \frac{12}{6}$ and CF = 4 cm: (a).
C4 The equal angles lie between the proportional sides, so SAS gives ${\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF}$, and DF = $\frac{3}{2} \times 8$ = 12 cm: (b). Option (a) uses $\frac{2}{3}$.
C5 A right triangle with angles $45^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ is not similar to one with angles $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, so (c) is false.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Write a similarity with its vertices in matching order before you use it, because the order tells you which sides go together. A pair of similar triangles can give a length that nobody can measure directly. And two equal angles are enough to show that two triangles are similar.
