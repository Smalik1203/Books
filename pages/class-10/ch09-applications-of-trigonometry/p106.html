Type 5 · On the same side, or on opposite sides

Example 9

Two ships are on opposite sides of a lighthouse 100 m high, in a line with its foot. From the top of the lighthouse, their angles of depression are $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. The distance between the ships is

  1. ${100\sqrt{3}}$ m
  2. ${100(\sqrt{3} - 1)}$ m
  3. ${100(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  4. 200 m

Solution.

Step 1 the ship at $45^\circ$ is 100 m from the foot, and the ship at $30^\circ$ is ${100\sqrt{3}}$ m from it distance ${= \frac{100}{\tan\theta}}$
Step 2 ${100\sqrt{3} + 100 = 100(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m opposite sides, so add
Answer (c), about 273.2 m

Option (b) subtracts the distances, which is right only when both ships are on the same side. Option (a) is the distance of one ship only. A rough figure, with the lighthouse between the two ships, shows at once whether to add or to subtract.

Example 10

From an aeroplane flying horizontally, the angles of depression of two kilometre stones on a straight road, on the same side of the aeroplane, are $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. The stones are 1 km apart. Find the height of the aeroplane. (Take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.732}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 let the height be $h$ km; the stone at $60^\circ$ is ${\frac{h}{\sqrt{3}}}$ km from the spot below, and the stone at $45^\circ$ is $h$ km distance ${= \frac{h}{\tan\theta}}$
Step 2 ${h - \frac{h}{\sqrt{3}} = 1}$,   so   ${h(\sqrt{3} - 1) = \sqrt{3}}$ same side, so subtract
Step 3 $h = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = \dfrac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2}$
Step 4 $\dfrac{3 + \sqrt{3}}{2} \approx \dfrac{4.732}{2} = 2.366$
Answer The aeroplane is about 2.366 km high
