Behind Each Answer
Five multiple-choice problems, each solved, with the reason for every option.
Problem 1

A ladder 10 m long just reaches a window when it is inclined at $60^\circ$ to the ground. The height of the window above the ground is

  1. 5 m
  2. ${5\sqrt{3}}$ m
  3. ${10\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. $\dfrac{20}{\sqrt{3}}$ m
Solution

the ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height is the side opposite to the angle of $60^\circ$, so use the sine

height ${= 10\sin 60^\circ = 10 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}}$ m

The answer is (b). Option (a) uses $\cos 60^\circ$, which gives the distance of the foot of the ladder from the wall. Option (c) takes 10 m as the distance along the ground and uses $\tan 60^\circ$. Option (d) divides by $\sin 60^\circ$; it is about 11.5 m, longer than the ladder itself, which cannot be.

Problem 2

From the top of a cliff 60 m high, the angles of depression of the top and the bottom of a tower are $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. The height of the tower is

  1. 40 m
  2. 20 m
  3. ${20\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. 30 m
Solution

from the bottom of the tower: ${\tan 60^\circ = \frac{60}{d}}$, so the distance between them is ${d = \frac{60}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\sqrt{3}}$

from the top of the tower: the cliff is higher by ${d \tan 30^\circ = 20\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 20}$

height of the tower ${= 60 - 20 = 40}$ m

The answer is (a). Option (b) is how much higher the cliff is than the tower, (c) is the distance between them, and (d) is half the height of the cliff. Both angles are angles of depression, so the tower must be shorter than the cliff.
