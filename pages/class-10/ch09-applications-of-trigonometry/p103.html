Example 2

An aeroplane is flying at a height of 300 m. From a point on the ground, the angle of elevation of the aeroplane is $60^\circ$. How far is the point from the spot on the ground straight below the aeroplane, and how far is it from the aeroplane? (Take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.732}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 let the horizontal distance be $d$ m; then ${\tan 60^\circ = \frac{300}{d}}$ opposite over adjacent
Step 2 ${d = \frac{300}{\sqrt{3}} = 100\sqrt{3} \approx 173.2}$ multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{3}$
Step 3 let the line of sight be $L$ m; then ${\sin 60^\circ = \frac{300}{L}}$, so ${L = \frac{600}{\sqrt{3}} = 200\sqrt{3} \approx 346.4}$ opposite over hypotenuse
Answer About 173.2 m from the spot below the aeroplane, and about 346.4 m from the aeroplane

Example 3

Two vertical poles, 5 m and 15 m tall, stand ${10\sqrt{3}}$ m apart on level ground. The angle of elevation of the top of the taller pole from the top of the shorter one is

  1. $60^\circ$
  2. $45^\circ$
  3. $30^\circ$
  4. $15^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1 the horizontal through the top of the shorter pole meets the taller one ${15 - 5 = 10}$ m below its top
Step 2 ${\tan\theta = \frac{10}{10\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}$ rise over horizontal distance
Answer (c), since ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}$

Option (a) divides the distance by the rise, the wrong way up, and gets $\sqrt{3}$. Option (b) would need the poles to be only 10 m apart.

Example 4

The string of a kite is 50 m long, and the kite is ${25\sqrt{3}}$ m above the ground. Taking the string to be straight, find the angle it makes with the ground.

Solution.

Step 1 the string is the hypotenuse, and the height of the kite is the side opposite the angle $\theta$ at the ground
Step 2 ${\sin\theta = \frac{25\sqrt{3}}{50} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$ opposite over hypotenuse
Answer ${\theta = 60^\circ}$, since ${\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}}$
