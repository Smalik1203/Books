9 The building is ${\frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}}$ m high and the top of the flagstaff is 20 m high, so the flagstaff is ${20 - \frac{20}{\sqrt{3}}}$ m. Option (d) is the height of the building, and (a) is the height of the top of the flagstaff.
13 The pole is ${20 \times \frac{3}{4} = 15}$ m high, and the distance to its top is ${\sqrt{20^2 + 15^2} = 25}$ m. Option (b) is the height, and (d) adds the two sides.
14 The cars are ${\frac{30}{\tan 30^\circ} = 30\sqrt{3}}$ m and ${\frac{30}{\tan 60^\circ} = 10\sqrt{3}}$ m from the foot, on the same side, so they are ${20\sqrt{3}}$ m apart. Option (a) adds the two distances, (c) is the nearer car’s distance, and (d) is the height of the tower.
15 A higher Sun makes ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{s}}$ larger, so the shadow $s$ gets shorter, and (c) is false. The other three are true.
16 The ladder is the hypotenuse, so ${\sin\theta = \frac{5}{10} = \frac{1}{2}}$ and the angle is $30^\circ$. R is exactly what gives that angle, so the answer is (a).
18 The height is ${8 \sin 60^\circ = 4\sqrt{3}}$ m, so A is true. R is true, but the height needs the sine, not the cosine: the answer is (b).
19 The shadow is ${\frac{6}{\tan 30^\circ} = 6\sqrt{3}}$ m, not ${2\sqrt{3}}$ m, so A is false while R is true: the answer is (d).

Three things from this section are worth keeping. Draw the figure first, and mark which angles are at the ground and which are at the top. An angle of depression is equal to an angle of elevation, so you can always move it inside the right triangle. And look at the angles before writing equations: an isosceles triangle, or a ratio in which the lengths cancel, can give the answer in one line.
