Example 5

The shadow of a tower standing on level ground is 40 m longer when the Sun's altitude is $30^\circ$ than when it is $60^\circ$. Find the height of the tower.

Solution.

Step 1 in Fig. 9.8, AB is the tower, BC is its shadow when the Sun's altitude is $60^\circ$, and DB is its shadow when the altitude is $30^\circ$ altitude: the elevation from the shadow's tip
Step 2 let ${AB = h}$ m and ${BC = x}$ m; DB is 40 m longer, so ${DB = (40 + x)}$ m
Step 3 in $\triangle ABC$,   $\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BC}$,   so   $\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{h}{x}$   (1) AB is opposite, BC adjacent
Step 4 in $\triangle ABD$,   $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BD}$,   so   $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{h}{x + 40}$   (2)
Step 5 from (1), ${h = x\sqrt{3}}$; putting this value of $h$ in (2), ${x\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = x + 40}$
Step 6 ${3x = x + 40}$   and   ${x = 20}$
Step 7 ${h = 20\sqrt{3}}$ from (1)
Answer The height of the tower is ${20\sqrt{3}}$ m
