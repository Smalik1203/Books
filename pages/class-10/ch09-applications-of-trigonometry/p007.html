Let ${AB = h}$ m and ${BC = x}$ m. The shadow DB is 40 m longer than BC, so ${DB = (40 + x)}$ m. There are two right triangles, ABC and ABD. In each of them, AB is the side opposite to the angle on the ground, and BC or BD is the side adjacent to it, so use the tangent.

in $\triangle ABC$,   $\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BC}$,   so   $\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{h}{x}$   (1)

in $\triangle ABD$,   $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BD}$,   so   $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{h}{x + 40}$   (2)

From (1), ${h = x\sqrt{3}}$. Put this value of $h$ in (2).

${x\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = x + 40}$,   so   ${3x = x + 40}$   and   ${x = 20}$

${h = 20\sqrt{3}}$   [from (1)]

So the height of the tower is ${20\sqrt{3}}$ m.

Example 6

From the top of a multi-storeyed building, the angles of depression of the top and the bottom of a building 8 m tall are $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. Find the height of the multi-storeyed building and the distance between the two buildings.

In Fig. 9.9, PC is the multi-storeyed building and AB is the building 8 m tall. We need the height PC and the distance AC between the two buildings.

Look at the figure carefully. PQ and BD are both horizontal, so they are parallel, and PB is a transversal to them. So $\angle QPB$ and $\angle PBD$ are alternate angles, and they are equal: ${\angle PBD = 30^\circ}$. In the same way, ${\angle PAC = 45^\circ}$.
