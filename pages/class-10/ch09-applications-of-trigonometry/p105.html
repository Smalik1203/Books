Type 4 · One height seen from two points

Example 7

The Sun's altitude falls from $45^\circ$ to $30^\circ$, and the shadow of a vertical pole becomes 5 m longer. The height of the pole is

  1. ${5(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  2. ${\dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  3. ${5\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. ${\dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} - 1)}$ m

Solution.

Step 1 at $45^\circ$ the shadow is $h$; at $30^\circ$ it is ${h\sqrt{3}}$ shadow ${= \frac{h}{\tan\theta}}$
Step 2 ${h\sqrt{3} - h = 5}$,   so   $h = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$
Step 3 $h = \dfrac{5(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{(\sqrt{3} + 1)(\sqrt{3} - 1)} = \dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} + 1)$ remove the root below
Answer (b), about 6.83 m

Option (a) forgets to divide by ${(\sqrt{3})^2 - 1 = 2}$. Option (d) multiplies by ${\sqrt{3} - 1}$ instead of ${\sqrt{3} + 1}$, and (c) is the shadow at $30^\circ$ of a pole 5 m tall. As a check, a pole 6.83 m tall has shadows of 6.83 m and 11.83 m, which differ by 5 m.

Example 8

From a point A on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $30^\circ$. From a point B, 20 m nearer the tower on the line from A to its foot, the angle of elevation is $45^\circ$. Find the height of the tower. (Take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.732}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 let the tower be $h$ m high; from B, ${\tan 45^\circ = 1}$, so B is $h$ m from the foot
Step 2 A is ${(h + 20)}$ m from the foot, so ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{h + 20}}$
Step 3 ${h\sqrt{3} = h + 20}$,   so   $h = \dfrac{20}{\sqrt{3} - 1}$ cross-multiply
Step 4 $h = \dfrac{20(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{2} = 10(\sqrt{3} + 1) \approx 10 \times 2.732 = 27.32$ remove the root below
Answer The tower is about 27.32 m high
