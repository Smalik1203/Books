$\tan 60^\circ = \dfrac{AB}{BC}$,   so   $\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{AB}{15}$,   which gives ${AB = 15\sqrt{3}}$

So the height of the tower is ${15\sqrt{3}}$ m.

Example 2

An electrician has to repair a fault on an electric pole 5 m high. She needs to reach a point 1.3 m below the top of the pole (Fig. 9.5). She will use a ladder inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. How long should the ladder be to reach that point? How far from the foot of the pole should she place the foot of the ladder? (You may take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.73}$.)

In Fig. 9.5, AD is the pole and B is the point the electrician has to reach. So

${BD = AD - AB = 5 - 1.3 = 3.7}$ m

BC is the ladder, and we need its length. BC is the hypotenuse of the right triangle BDC, and we know BD, the side opposite to the angle of $60^\circ$ at C. Which ratio uses these two sides? It is $\sin 60^\circ$.

$\dfrac{BD}{BC} = \sin 60^\circ$,   so   $\dfrac{3.7}{BC} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$

$BC = \dfrac{3.7 \times 2}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{7.4}{1.73} = 4.28$   (approx.)

So the ladder should be about 4.28 m long. For the distance DC, use the cotangent, since DC is the side adjacent to the angle at C.

$\dfrac{DC}{BD} = \cot 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$,   so   $DC = \dfrac{3.7}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3.7}{1.73} = 2.14$   (approx.)

So she should place the foot of the ladder about 2.14 m from the pole.
