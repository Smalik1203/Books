As you walk towards a tower on level ground, the angle of elevation of its top gets larger. Can it ever become $90^\circ$? (Ignore your own height.)

If the tower is $h$ m tall and you are $d$ m from its foot, the angle of elevation $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}}$. As $d$ gets smaller, $\frac{h}{d}$ gets larger, and it can be made as large as you like: at ${d = 1}$ it is $h$, and at ${d = 0.01}$ it is ${100h}$. So $\theta$ gets closer and closer to $90^\circ$. But it never reaches $90^\circ$ while you stand away from the tower, because $\frac{h}{d}$ is always a number, and Chapter 8 showed that $\tan 90^\circ$ is not defined. At the foot of the tower there is no triangle left.

From a point on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $30^\circ$. You walk straight towards the tower until you are only one third as far from its foot as you were. What is the angle of elevation now? Is it three times as large?

Let the tower be $h$ m high and let you start $d$ m from its foot, so ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{d} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}$. At the new point you are $\frac{d}{3}$ m from the foot, so the new angle $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d/3} = 3 \times \frac{h}{d} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}}$, and from Table 8.19, ${\theta = 60^\circ}$. Neither $h$ nor $d$ was needed, only their ratio. Dividing the distance by 3 multiplied the tangent by 3, but the angle only doubled, from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$: it did not become $90^\circ$. An angle and its tangent do not grow in the same proportion.

Example 1 · Single correct

A kite's string is 60 m long and makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the ground. Taking the string as straight, how high is the kite?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 30 m
  2. $30\sqrt{3}$ m
  3. 60 m
  4. $20\sqrt{3}$ m

Solution.

Step 1The string is the hypotenuse and the height is opposite the $30^\circ$ angle: $h = 60 \sin 30^\circ$.
Step 2$h = 60 \times \frac{1}{2} = 30$ m.
Answer(a) 30 m
