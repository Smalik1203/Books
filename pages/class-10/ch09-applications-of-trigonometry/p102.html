From the top of a building 10 m high, the angle of depression of a car on the road is $30^\circ$. What is the angle of elevation of the top of the building, seen from the car? How far is the car from the building?

The horizontal line through the top of the building and the road are parallel, and the line of sight from the top to the car is a transversal to them. So the angle of depression at the top and the angle of elevation at the car are alternate angles, and they are equal: both are $30^\circ$. This is what Example 6 used. If the car is $d$ m from the foot of the building, then ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{10}{d}}$, so ${d = 10\sqrt{3}}$, about 17.32 m. An angle of depression can always be moved to the object, where it becomes an angle of elevation inside the right triangle.

As you walk towards a tower on level ground, the angle of elevation of its top gets larger. Can it ever become $90^\circ$? (Ignore your own height.)

If the tower is $h$ m tall and you are $d$ m from its foot, the angle of elevation $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}}$. As $d$ gets smaller, $\frac{h}{d}$ gets larger, and it can be made as large as you like: at ${d = 1}$ it is $h$, and at ${d = 0.01}$ it is ${100h}$. So $\theta$ gets closer and closer to $90^\circ$. But it never reaches $90^\circ$ while you stand away from the tower, because $\frac{h}{d}$ is always a number, and Chapter 8 showed that $\tan 90^\circ$ is not defined. At the foot of the tower there is no triangle left.

Two points on the ground are 4 m and 9 m from the foot of a tower, on the same side of it and in a line with its foot. The angles of elevation of the top of the tower from these points add up to $90^\circ$. Find the height of the tower.

Let the tower be AB, $h$ m high, and let C and D be the points 4 m and 9 m from its foot B. Let ${\angle ACB = \theta}$; then ${\angle ADB = 90^\circ - \theta}$. In the right triangle ABC, ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{4}}$. In the right triangle ABD, the three angles add up to $180^\circ$, so the angle at A is ${180^\circ - 90^\circ - (90^\circ - \theta) = \theta}$. For this angle, BD is the opposite side and AB the adjacent side, so ${\tan\theta = \frac{9}{h}}$. The two values of $\tan\theta$ are equal, so ${\frac{h}{4} = \frac{9}{h}}$, ${h^2 = 36}$ and ${h = 6}$ m. You never needed $\theta$: the same angle appeared in both triangles, once at the ground and once at the top.
