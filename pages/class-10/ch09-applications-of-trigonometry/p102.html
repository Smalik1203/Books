As you walk towards a tower on level ground, the angle of elevation of its top gets larger. Can it ever become $90^\circ$? (Ignore your own height.)

If the tower is $h$ m tall and you are $d$ m from its foot, the angle of elevation $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}}$. As $d$ gets smaller, $\frac{h}{d}$ gets larger, and it can be made as large as you like: at ${d = 1}$ it is $h$, and at ${d = 0.01}$ it is ${100h}$. So $\theta$ gets closer and closer to $90^\circ$. But it never reaches $90^\circ$ while you stand away from the tower, because $\frac{h}{d}$ is always a number, and Chapter 8 showed that $\tan 90^\circ$ is not defined. At the foot of the tower there is no triangle left.

From a point on level ground, the angle of elevation of the top of a tower is $30^\circ$. You walk straight towards the tower until you are only one third as far from its foot as you were. What is the angle of elevation now? Is it three times as large?

Let the tower be $h$ m high and let you start $d$ m from its foot, so ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{d} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}$. At the new point you are $\frac{d}{3}$ m from the foot, so the new angle $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d/3} = 3 \times \frac{h}{d} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}}$, and from Table 8.1, ${\theta = 60^\circ}$. Neither $h$ nor $d$ was needed, only their ratio. Dividing the distance by 3 multiplied the tangent by 3, but the angle only doubled, from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$: it did not become $90^\circ$. An angle and its tangent do not grow in the same proportion.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · A height or a distance from one right triangle

Example 1

A ladder 10 m long just reaches a window when it is inclined at $60^\circ$ to the ground. The height of the window above the ground is

  1. 5 m
  2. ${5\sqrt{3}}$ m
  3. ${10\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. $\dfrac{20}{\sqrt{3}}$ m

Solution.

Step 1 the ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height is opposite the angle of $60^\circ$ so use the sine
Step 2 height ${= 10\sin 60^\circ = 10 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}}$ m
Answer (b)

Option (a) uses $\cos 60^\circ$, which gives the distance of the foot of the ladder from the wall. Option (c) takes 10 m as the distance along the ground. Option (d) divides by $\sin 60^\circ$; it is about 11.5 m, longer than the ladder itself, which cannot be.
