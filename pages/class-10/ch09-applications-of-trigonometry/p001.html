Some Applications of Trigonometry

9.1Heights and Distances

In Chapter 8 you met the trigonometric ratios of an acute angle. In this chapter you will use them to find heights and distances that are hard to measure directly, such as the height of a tower or the width of a river.

Look again at Fig. 8.1 from the previous chapter, where a student looks at the top of the Qutub Minar. It is drawn again in Fig. 9.1, with its points named.

The student's eye is at A, and the student is looking at the top C of the minar. The line AC, from the eye to the top of the minar, is called the line of sight. AB is the horizontal line through the eye. The angle BAC between the line of sight and the horizontal is called the angle of elevation of the top of the minar, seen from the student's eye.

In general, the line of sight is the line from the eye of an observer to the point of the object that the observer is looking at. When that point is above the horizontal level of the eye, you raise your head to look at it. The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal is then the angle of elevation of the point (Fig. 9.2).

Now look again at Fig. 8.2. The girl on the balcony is looking down at a flower pot on a step of the temple. This time the line of sight is below the horizontal level of her eye. The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal is called the angle of depression of the flower pot, seen from her eye.
