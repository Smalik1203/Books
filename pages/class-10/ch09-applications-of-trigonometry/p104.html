Type 3 · Angles of depression

Example 5

From the top of a cliff 60 m high, the angles of depression of the top and the bottom of a tower are $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. The height of the tower is

  1. 40 m
  2. 20 m
  3. ${20\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. 30 m

Solution.

Step 1 ${\tan 60^\circ = \frac{60}{d}}$, so the distance between them is ${d = \frac{60}{\sqrt{3}} = 20\sqrt{3}}$ from the bottom of the tower
Step 2 the cliff is higher than the tower by ${d \tan 30^\circ = 20\sqrt{3} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = 20}$ from the top of the tower
Step 3 height of the tower ${= 60 - 20 = 40}$ m
Answer (a)

Option (b) is how much higher the cliff is than the tower, (c) is the distance between them, and (d) is half the height of the cliff. Both angles are angles of depression, so the tower must be shorter than the cliff.

Example 6

From a window 18 m above the ground, the angle of depression of a car parked on the road is $60^\circ$. How far is the car from the foot of the building? (Take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.732}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 the angle of elevation of the window from the car is also $60^\circ$ alternate angles
Step 2 ${\tan 60^\circ = \frac{18}{d}}$, so ${d = \frac{18}{\sqrt{3}} = 6\sqrt{3}}$
Step 3 ${6\sqrt{3} \approx 6 \times 1.732 = 10.392}$
Answer About 10.39 m
