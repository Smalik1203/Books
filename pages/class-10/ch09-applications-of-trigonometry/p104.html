Problem 3

The Sun's altitude falls from $45^\circ$ to $30^\circ$, and the shadow of a vertical pole becomes 5 m longer. The height of the pole is

  1. ${5(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  2. ${\dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  3. ${5\sqrt{3}}$ m
  4. ${\dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} - 1)}$ m
Solution

at $45^\circ$ the shadow is $h$; at $30^\circ$ it is ${h\sqrt{3}}$

${h\sqrt{3} - h = 5}$,   so   $h = \dfrac{5}{\sqrt{3} - 1} = \dfrac{5(\sqrt{3} + 1)}{(\sqrt{3} + 1)(\sqrt{3} - 1)} = \dfrac{5}{2}(\sqrt{3} + 1)$

The answer is (b), about 6.83 m. Option (a) forgets to divide by ${(\sqrt{3})^2 - 1 = 2}$ when removing the root from the denominator. Option (d) multiplies by ${\sqrt{3} - 1}$ instead of ${\sqrt{3} + 1}$, and (c) is the shadow at $30^\circ$ of a pole 5 m tall. As a check, a pole 6.83 m tall has shadows of 6.83 m and 11.83 m, which differ by 5 m.

Problem 4

Two ships are on opposite sides of a lighthouse 100 m high, in a line with its foot. From the top of the lighthouse, their angles of depression are $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. The distance between the ships is

  1. ${100\sqrt{3}}$ m
  2. ${100(\sqrt{3} - 1)}$ m
  3. ${100(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m
  4. 200 m
Solution

the ship at $45^\circ$ is 100 m from the foot, and the ship at $30^\circ$ is ${100\sqrt{3}}$ m from it

on opposite sides the distances add: ${100\sqrt{3} + 100 = 100(\sqrt{3} + 1)}$ m

The answer is (c), about 273.2 m. Option (b) subtracts, which is the answer when both ships are on the same side, as in Question 13 of Exercise Set 9.1. Option (a) is the distance of one ship only. A rough figure shows at once whether to add or to subtract. Here the figure has the lighthouse between the two ships.
