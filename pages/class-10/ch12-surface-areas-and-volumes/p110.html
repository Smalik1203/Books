1. Which of these statements is false?
    1. Joining two solids does not change their total volume.
    2. The surface area of a joined solid can be less than the sum of the surface areas of its parts.
    3. Scooping a hemisphere out of a face of a cube increases its surface area.
    4. The total surface area of a toy made of a cone and a hemisphere is the sum of their total surface areas.
Assertion and reason

In Questions 15 to 19, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): The total surface area of a toy made of a cone on a hemisphere is less than the sum of the total surface areas of the cone and the hemisphere.

    Reason (R): The two flat faces that are joined are hidden.

  1. Assertion (A): A solid made of a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 5 cm, with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top, has volume $63\pi$ cm3.

    Reason (R): The volume of a hemisphere of radius $r$ is $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.

  1. Assertion (A): Two cones of radius 3 cm and height 4 cm are joined base to base. The surface area of the new solid is $48\pi$ cm2.

    Reason (R): The curved surface area of a cone of radius $r$ and slant height $l$ is $\pi rl$.

  1. Assertion (A): A solid made of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, with a hemisphere of radius 2 cm on top, has volume $\frac{76}{3}\pi$ cm3.

    Reason (R): The volume of a cone is one third of the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.

  1. Assertion (A): A solid made of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm, with a cone of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm on top, has volume $24\pi$ cm3.

    Reason (R): The volume of a cone of radius $r$ and height $h$ is $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.
