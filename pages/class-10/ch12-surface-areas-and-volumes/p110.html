1. Assertion (A): A solid made of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, with a hemisphere of radius 2 cm on top, has volume $\frac{76}{3}\pi$ cm3.

    Reason (R): The volume of a cone is one third of the volume of a cylinder with the same base and height.

  1. Assertion (A): A solid made of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm, with a cone of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm on top, has volume $24\pi$ cm3.

    Reason (R): The volume of a cone of radius $r$ and height $h$ is $\frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$.

Very short answer
  1. Two hemispheres of radius 3 cm are joined at their flat faces. Find the volume of the solid, in terms of $\pi$.
  1. A cone of radius 2 cm stands, base down, on the top face of a cube of edge 5 cm. How much surface, in terms of $\pi$, is hidden by joining them?
  1. A cone and a cylinder have the same radius and the same height. How many such cones hold as much as the cylinder?
Short answer
  1. A solid is a cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 12 cm, with a hemisphere of radius 5 cm on top. Find its total surface area, base included. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.)
  1. A solid is a cylinder of radius 3.5 cm and height 12 cm, with a hemisphere of the same radius on top. Find its volume, to two decimal places. (Take ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)
  1. A wooden block 12 cm long, 8 cm wide and 6 cm high has a cylindrical hole of radius 1 cm drilled through it from the top face to the bottom face. Find the volume of wood left. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.)
  1. A hemispherical bowl has inner radius 10.5 cm. How many litres of water can it hold, to two decimal places? (Take ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)
Long answer
  1. A tent is a cylinder of radius 2.4 m and height 2 m, with a cone of height 1.8 m on top. The base is not covered. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.)
    1. Find the slant height of the cone.
    2. Find the area of canvas needed, and its cost at ₹$120$ per m2.
    3. Find the volume of air inside the tent, to two decimal places.
  1. A cylinder of radius $r$ and height $2r$ just encloses a sphere of radius $r$. Prove that the volume of the sphere is two thirds of the volume of the cylinder, and that the curved surface area of the cylinder is equal to the surface area of the sphere.
