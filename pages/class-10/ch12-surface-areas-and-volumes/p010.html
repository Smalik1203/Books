Example 7

A solid toy is a hemisphere with a right circular cone on top of it. The cone is 2 cm high, and the diameter of its base is 4 cm. Find the volume of the toy. A right circular cylinder circumscribes the toy, that is, just encloses it. Find the difference between the volumes of the cylinder and the toy. (Take ${\pi = 3.14}$.)

Let BPC be the hemisphere and ABC the cone standing on its base (Fig. 12.14). The radius BO of the hemisphere, which is also the radius of the cone, is ${\frac{1}{2} \times 4 = 2}$ cm.

volume of the toy $= \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 + \dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \left[\dfrac{2}{3} \times 3.14 \times 2^3 + \dfrac{1}{3} \times 3.14 \times 2^2 \times 2\right]$ cm3

$= 25.12$ cm3

Let the right circular cylinder EFGH enclose the toy. The radius of its base is ${HP = BO = 2}$ cm, and its height is ${EH = AO + OP = 2 + 2 = 4}$ cm. So

volume of the cylinder $-$ volume of the toy $= (3.14 \times 2^2 \times 4 - 25.12)$ cm3 $= 25.12$ cm3
