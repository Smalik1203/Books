Example 6

A juice seller served his customers in glasses like the one in Fig. 12.13. The inner diameter of the cylindrical glass was 5 cm, but the bottom of the glass had a raised hemispherical part, which reduced its capacity. If the glass was 10 cm high, find its apparent capacity and its actual capacity. The apparent capacity is what the glass seems to hold, judged from its outside. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 apparent capacity $= \pi r^2 h = 3.14 \times 2.5 \times 2.5 \times 10$ cm3 $= 196.25$ cm3 radius 2.5 cm
Step 2 volume of the hemisphere $= \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 3.14 \times 2.5 \times 2.5 \times 2.5$ cm3 $= 32.71$ cm3 the raised bottom
Step 3 actual capacity $= (196.25 - 32.71)$ cm3 $= 163.54$ cm3
Answer apparent capacity 196.25 cm3, actual capacity 163.54 cm3

The actual capacity is less than the apparent capacity, by the volume of the hemisphere at the bottom of the glass. So each customer got less juice than the glass seemed to hold, although the glass was full to the brim.
