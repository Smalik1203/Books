Surface Areas and Volumes

12.1Introduction

From Class 9 you know several solids, such as the cuboid, the cone, the cylinder and the sphere (Fig. 12.1). You have also learnt how to find their surface areas and volumes. You will need all of those formulas again in this chapter.

In daily life we meet many solids that are made by putting two or more of these basic solids together. A water tank, a capsule, or an ice-cream cone with a scoop of ice cream on top are some examples.

You may have seen a truck with a container fitted on its back, carrying oil or water from one place to another (Fig. 12.2). Is the container one of the four basic solids above? You may guess that it is a cylinder with a hemisphere at each end.

You may also have seen an object like the one in Fig. 12.3. Can you name it? It is a test tube, which you may have used in your science laboratory. A test tube is also a combination of solids: a cylinder and a hemisphere. In the same way, when you travel you may see large buildings and monuments made up of the solids above.
