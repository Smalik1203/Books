TSA of the top $=$ CSA of the hemisphere $+$ CSA of the cone

CSA of the hemisphere $= \dfrac{1}{2}(4\pi r^2) = 2\pi r^2 = \left(2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2}\right)$ cm2

The height of the cone is the height of the top less the height of the hemisphere, which is its radius.

height of the cone $= \left(5 - \dfrac{3.5}{2}\right)$ cm $= 3.25$ cm

slant height of the cone, $l = \sqrt{r^2 + h^2} = \sqrt{\left(\dfrac{3.5}{2}\right)^2 + (3.25)^2}$ cm $= 3.7$ cm   (approx.)

CSA of the cone $= \pi rl = \left(\dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2} \times 3.7\right)$ cm2

So the surface area of the top is

$\left(2 \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2}\right)$ cm2 $+ \left(\dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2} \times 3.7\right)$ cm2

$= \dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{3.5}{2} \times (3.5 + 3.7)$ cm2 $= \dfrac{11}{2} \times 7.2$ cm2 $= 39.6$ cm2   (approx.)

Notice that the total surface area of the top is not the sum of the total surface areas of the cone and the hemisphere.

total surface area of the cube $= 6 \times (\text{edge})^2 = 6 \times 5 \times 5$ cm2 $= 150$ cm2
