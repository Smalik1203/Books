Example 10

A hemisphere of radius 7 cm is scooped out of one end of a solid cylinder of radius 7 cm and height 14 cm. The volume left is (Take ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

  1. 2156 cm3
  2. 1437.33 cm3
  3. 718.67 cm3
  4. 2874.67 cm3

Solution.

Step 1 cylinder ${= \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 14 = 2156}$ cm3
Step 2 hemisphere ${= \frac{2}{3} \times \frac{22}{7} \times 7 \times 7 \times 7 \approx 718.67}$ cm3
Step 3 ${2156 - 718.67 = 1437.33}$ cm3
Answer (b)

Option (a) is the whole cylinder, (c) is the hemisphere, and (d) adds the hemisphere instead of taking it away.

Example 11

A cone is cut out of a solid cylinder. The cone has the same base and the same height as the cylinder. Prove that the volume left is twice the volume of the cone.

Solution.

Step 1 let the radius be $r$ and the height $h$ the same for both
Step 2 volume of the cylinder $= \pi r^2 h$
Step 3 volume of the cone $= \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$
Step 4 volume left $= \pi r^2 h - \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h = \frac{2}{3}\pi r^2 h$ a part cut out is subtracted
Step 5 $\frac{2}{3}\pi r^2 h = 2 \times \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h$ the cone, from Step 3
Answer the volume left is twice the volume of the cone
