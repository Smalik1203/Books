12.3Volume of a Combination of Solids

In the previous section, you found the surface area of solids made by joining two basic solids. Now we find their volumes. To find the surface area, we did not simply add the surface areas of the two parts, because some of the surface is hidden where they are joined. With volume this does not happen: the volume of a solid made by joining two basic solids is the sum of their volumes, as the examples below show. Nothing inside a solid is hidden when two of them are joined.

Volume of a combination

The volume of a solid made by joining two solids is the sum of their volumes. When one solid is cut out of another, subtract its volume.

Example 5

Shanta runs a factory in a shed shaped like a cuboid with a half cylinder on top (Fig. 12.12). The base of the shed is 7 m by 15 m, and the cuboidal part is 8 m high. Find the volume of air the shed can hold. The machinery in the shed takes up 300 m3 in all, and there are 20 workers, each taking up about 0.08 m3 on average. How much air is in the shed then? (Take ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

With no people or machinery, the air in the shed fills the cuboid and the half cylinder together. The cuboid is 15 m long, 7 m wide and 8 m high. The half cylinder has diameter 7 m and length 15 m.
