Volume of a combination

The volume of a solid made by joining two solids is the sum of their volumes. When one solid is cut out of another, subtract its volume.

Example 5

Shanta runs a factory in a shed shaped like a cuboid with a half cylinder on top (Fig. 12.12). The base of the shed is 7 m by 15 m, and the cuboidal part is 8 m high. Find the volume of air the shed can hold. The machinery in the shed takes up 300 m3 in all, and there are 20 workers, each taking up about 0.08 m3 on average. How much air is in the shed then? (Take ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 the cuboid is 15 m long, 7 m wide and 8 m high; the half cylinder has diameter 7 m and length 15 m empty, the air fills both
Step 2 volume of the shed $=$ volume of the cuboid $+ \dfrac{1}{2} \times$ volume of the cylinder
Step 3 $= \left[15 \times 7 \times 8 + \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{7}{2} \times \dfrac{7}{2} \times 15\right]$ m3 $= 1128.75$ m3
Step 4 space taken by the machinery $= 300$ m3;   space taken by the workers ${= 20 \times 0.08 = 1.6}$ m3
Step 5 air in the shed $= 1128.75 - (300 + 1.6) = 827.15$ m3
Answer 1128.75 m3 when empty; 827.15 m3 with the machinery and workers
