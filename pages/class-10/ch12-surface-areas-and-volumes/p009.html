volume of the shed $=$ volume of the cuboid $+ \dfrac{1}{2} \times$ volume of the cylinder

$= \left[15 \times 7 \times 8 + \dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{22}{7} \times \dfrac{7}{2} \times \dfrac{7}{2} \times 15\right]$ m3 $= 1128.75$ m3

space taken by the machinery $= 300$ m3;   space taken by the workers ${= 20 \times 0.08 = 1.6}$ m3

air in the shed $= 1128.75 - (300 + 1.6) = 827.15$ m3

Example 6

A juice seller served his customers in glasses like the one in Fig. 12.13. The inner diameter of the cylindrical glass was 5 cm, but the bottom of the glass had a raised hemispherical part, which reduced its capacity. If the glass was 10 cm high, find its apparent capacity and its actual capacity. The apparent capacity is what the glass seems to hold, judged from its outside. (Use ${\pi = 3.14}$.)

apparent capacity $= \pi r^2 h = 3.14 \times 2.5 \times 2.5 \times 10$ cm3 $= 196.25$ cm3

The actual capacity is less than this, by the volume of the hemisphere at the bottom of the glass. So each customer got less juice than the glass seemed to hold, although the glass was full to the brim.

volume of the hemisphere $= \dfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \dfrac{2}{3} \times 3.14 \times 2.5 \times 2.5 \times 2.5$ cm3 $= 32.71$ cm3

actual capacity $= (196.25 - 32.71)$ cm3 $= 163.54$ cm3
