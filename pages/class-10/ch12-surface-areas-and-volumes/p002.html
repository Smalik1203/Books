12.2Surface Area of a Combination of Solids

Look at the container in Fig. 12.2. How do we find the surface area of such a solid? Whenever we meet a new problem, we first try to break it into smaller problems that we have solved before. This solid is a cylinder with a hemisphere stuck on at each end. When the pieces are put together, they look like Fig. 12.4.

On the surface of the new solid, you can see only the curved surfaces of the two hemispheres and the curved surface of the cylinder. So the total surface area of the new solid is the sum of the curved surface areas of the three parts:

TSA of the new solid $=$ CSA of one hemisphere $+$ CSA of the cylinder $+$ CSA of the other hemisphere

Here TSA and CSA stand for total surface area and curved surface area.

Now take another case. Suppose we make a toy by joining a hemisphere and a cone. First we take a cone and a hemisphere and bring their flat faces together.
