Every area in the working, such as $2\pi rh$ or $4\pi r^2$, is a product of two lengths, so each is multiplied by ${2 \times 2 = 4}$. Every volume, such as $\pi r^2 h$ or $\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, is a product of three lengths, so each is multiplied by ${2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8}$. So the surface area becomes 4 times as large and the volume 8 times as large. This is true for a solid of any shape.

A hemisphere is scooped out of one face of a cube. Does the surface area go up or down?

The volume goes down, but the surface area goes up. The flat circle of area $\pi r^2$ is removed from the face, and the curved inside of the hollow, of area $2\pi r^2$, is added. So the surface area increases by $\pi r^2$. Cutting a solid away can add surface.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · Surface area of solids joined together

Example 1

Two cubes of edge 5 cm are joined end to end. The surface area of the cuboid they form is

  1. 300 cm2
  2. 250 cm2
  3. 150 cm2
  4. 275 cm2

Solution.

Step 1 the cuboid is 10 cm by 5 cm by 5 cm two edges end to end
Step 2 ${2(10 \times 5 + 5 \times 5 + 10 \times 5) = 250}$ cm2
Answer (b)

Option (a) forgets the two hidden faces, (d) takes away only one, and (c) is one cube.

Example 2

A toy is a cone of radius 3 cm and height 4 cm on a hemisphere of radius 3 cm. Its total surface area is

  1. $33\pi$ cm2
  2. $51\pi$ cm2
  3. $24\pi$ cm2
  4. $42\pi$ cm2

Solution.

Step 1 ${l = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5}$ cm slant height of the cone
Step 2 CSA of the cone ${= \pi \times 3 \times 5 = 15\pi}$, of the hemisphere ${= 2\pi \times 9 = 18\pi}$
Step 3 ${15\pi + 18\pi = 33\pi}$ cm2 the flat faces are hidden
Answer (a)

Option (b) adds both total surface areas, (d) counts the flat face of the hemisphere, and (c) is the cone alone.
