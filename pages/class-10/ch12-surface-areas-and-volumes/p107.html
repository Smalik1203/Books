Type 5 · The capacity of a vessel

Example 12

A cylindrical glass has inner radius 3 cm and height 12 cm. Its bottom is raised inside the glass as a cone of radius 3 cm and height 3 cm. The actual capacity of the glass is

  1. $108\pi$ cm3
  2. $99\pi$ cm3
  3. $117\pi$ cm3
  4. $81\pi$ cm3

Solution.

Step 1 the whole cylinder ${= \pi \times 3^2 \times 12 = 108\pi}$ cm3 the apparent capacity
Step 2 the raised cone ${= \frac{1}{3}\pi \times 3^2 \times 3 = 9\pi}$ cm3
Step 3 ${108\pi - 9\pi = 99\pi}$ cm3 the raised part holds nothing
Answer (b)

Option (a) is the apparent capacity, (c) adds the cone instead of taking it away, and (d) takes away a cylinder 3 cm high instead of a cone.

Example 13

An oil can is a cuboid 30 cm long, 20 cm wide and 25 cm high, with a cylindrical spout of inner radius 2 cm and length 7 cm on top. Taking these as inner measurements and ${\pi = \frac{22}{7}}$, the can holds, to two decimal places,

  1. 15.00 litres
  2. 15.35 litres
  3. 150.88 litres
  4. 15.09 litres

Solution.

Step 1 cuboid ${= 30 \times 20 \times 25 = 15000}$ cm3
Step 2 spout ${= \frac{22}{7} \times 2 \times 2 \times 7 = 88}$ cm3 the spout holds oil too
Step 3 ${15000 + 88 = 15088}$ cm3
Step 4 ${15088 \div 1000 = 15.088}$ litres 1000 cm3 make 1 litre
Answer (d)

Option (a) leaves out the spout, (b) takes the diameter of the spout as its radius, and (c) divides by 100 instead of 1000.
