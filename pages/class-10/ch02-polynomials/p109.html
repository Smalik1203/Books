Assertion and reason

In Questions 17 to 20, choose (a) if both A and R are true and R explains A; (b) if both are true but R does not explain A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): $x^2 + 4$ has no zero.

    Reason (R): The graph of $y = x^2 + 4$ lies wholly above the $x$-axis.

  1. Assertion (A): The sum of the zeroes of $2x^2 - 8x + 5$ is 4.

    Reason (R): The product of the zeroes of $ax^2 + bx + c$ is $\frac{c}{a}$.

  1. Assertion (A): $x^3 - x$ has three zeroes.

    Reason (R): Every cubic polynomial has exactly three zeroes.

  1. Assertion (A): The product of the zeroes of $x^2 + 5x - 6$ is 6.

    Reason (R): If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $ax^2 + bx + c$, then $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$.

Very short answer
  1. Find the value of $p(x) = 2x^3 - 3x + 1$ at $x = -2$.
  1. Write a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes have sum $-\frac{3}{2}$ and product $-1$.
  1. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $3x^2 + 2x - 7$, find $\alpha + \beta + \alpha\beta$.
