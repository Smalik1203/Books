Case (i). The graph cuts the $x$-axis at two different points $A$ and $A'$. The $x$-coordinates of $A$ and $A'$ are the two zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ in this case (see Fig. 2.3).

Case (ii). The graph meets the $x$-axis at exactly one point, that is, at two points that coincide. The two points $A$ and $A'$ of Case (i) have come together to make one point $A$ (see Fig. 2.4). The $x$-coordinate of $A$ is the only zero of $ax^2 + bx + c$ in this case.

Case (iii). The graph is either wholly above the $x$-axis or wholly below it, so it does not meet the $x$-axis at any point (see Fig. 2.5). So the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no zero in this case.

So the graphs show that a quadratic polynomial can have two different zeroes, two equal zeroes (that is, one zero), or no zero. This also means that a polynomial of degree 2 has at most two zeroes.
