So the graphs show that a quadratic polynomial can have two different zeroes, two equal zeroes (that is, one zero), or no zero. This also means that a polynomial of degree 2 has at most two zeroes.

What do you expect the zeroes of a cubic polynomial to mean on a graph? Let us find out. Take the cubic polynomial $x^3 - 4x$. To see what the graph of $y = x^3 - 4x$ looks like, Table 2.2 lists a few values of $y$.

Table 2.2 Values of $y = x^3 - 4x$
$x$$-2$$-1$$0$$1$$2$
$y$$0$$3$$0$$-3$$0$

Marking these points on graph paper and drawing the curve through them, we see that the graph of $y = x^3 - 4x$ looks like Fig. 2.6.

From Table 2.2, $-2$, 0 and 2 are zeroes of the cubic polynomial $x^3 - 4x$. They are also the $x$-coordinates of the only points where the graph of $y = x^3 - 4x$ cuts the $x$-axis. Since the curve meets the $x$-axis at only these three points, their $x$-coordinates are the only zeroes of the polynomial.
