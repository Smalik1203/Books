Polynomials

2.1Introduction

In Class IX you studied polynomials in one variable and their degrees. Recall that if $p(x)$ is a polynomial in $x$, the highest power of $x$ in $p(x)$ is called the degree of $p(x)$. For example, $4x + 2$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree 1, and $2y^2 - 3y + 4$ is a polynomial in $y$ of degree 2. Also, $5x^3 - 4x^2 + x - \sqrt{2}$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree 3, and $7u^6 - \frac{3}{2}u^4 + 4u^2 + u - 8$ is a polynomial in $u$ of degree 6. Expressions such as $\dfrac{1}{x - 1}$, $\sqrt{x} + 2$ and $\dfrac{1}{x^2 + 2x + 3}$ are not polynomials.

A polynomial of degree 1 is called a linear polynomial. For example, $2x - 3$, $\sqrt{3}x + 5$, $y + \sqrt{2}$, $x - \frac{2}{11}$, $3z + 4$ and $\frac{2}{3}u + 1$ are all linear polynomials. Polynomials such as $2x + 5 - x^2$ and $x^3 + 1$ are not linear.

A polynomial of degree 2 is called a quadratic polynomial. The name comes from the word quadrate, which means square. Here are some quadratic polynomials whose coefficients are real numbers.

$$2x^2 + 3x - \frac{2}{5}, \quad y^2 - 2, \quad 2 - x^2 + \sqrt{3}x, \quad \frac{u}{3} - 2u^2 + 5, \quad \sqrt{5}v^2 - \frac{2}{3}v, \quad 4z^2 + \frac{1}{7}$$

Every quadratic polynomial in $x$ has the form $ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are real numbers and $a \neq 0$. A polynomial of degree 3 is called a cubic polynomial. Some examples are $2 - x^3$, $x^3$, $\sqrt{2}x^3$, $3 - x^2 + x^3$ and $3x^3 - 2x^2 + x - 1$. The general form of a cubic polynomial is

$$ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d, \quad \text{where } a, b, c, d \text{ are real numbers and } a \neq 0$$

Now take the polynomial $p(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4$. Putting $x = 2$ in it, we get $p(2) = 2^2 - 3 \times 2 - 4 = -6$. The number $-6$ is the value of $x^2 - 3x - 4$ at $x = 2$. In the same way, $p(0) = -4$ is the value of $p(x)$ at $x = 0$. To find the value of a polynomial at a number, we put that number in place of the variable and work out the result.
