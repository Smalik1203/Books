Value of a polynomial

If $p(x)$ is a polynomial in $x$ and $k$ is any real number, the number we get by putting $k$ in place of $x$ in $p(x)$ is called the value of $p(x)$ at $x = k$. It is written $p(k)$.

What is the value of $p(x) = x^2 - 3x - 4$ at $x = -1$? We have

$$p(-1) = (-1)^2 - 3 \times (-1) - 4 = 1 + 3 - 4 = 0$$

Also, $p(4) = 4^2 - 3 \times 4 - 4 = 0$. Since $p(-1) = 0$ and $p(4) = 0$, the numbers $-1$ and 4 are called the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $x^2 - 3x - 4$. In general, a real number $k$ is a zero of a polynomial $p(x)$ if $p(k) = 0$.

In Class IX you found the zeroes of linear polynomials. For example, if $k$ is a zero of $p(x) = 2x + 3$, then $p(k) = 0$ gives $2k + 3 = 0$, that is, $k = -\frac{3}{2}$. In general, if $k$ is a zero of $p(x) = ax + b$, then $p(k) = ak + b = 0$, so $k = -\frac{b}{a}$. So the zero of the linear polynomial $ax + b$ is

$$-\frac{b}{a} = \dfrac{-(\text{constant term})}{\text{coefficient of } x}$$

So the zero of a linear polynomial is tied to its coefficients. Does this happen for other polynomials too? For example, are the zeroes of a quadratic polynomial also tied to its coefficients? In this chapter we try to answer these questions.

2.2Geometrical Meaning of the Zeroes of a Polynomial

You know that a real number $k$ is a zero of the polynomial $p(x)$ if $p(k) = 0$. But why are the zeroes of a polynomial so important? To answer this, we first look at the graphs of linear and quadratic polynomials, and at what their zeroes mean on a graph.

Take first a linear polynomial $ax + b$, with $a \neq 0$. You studied in Class IX that the graph of $y = ax + b$ is a straight line. For example, the graph of $y = 2x + 3$ is the straight line through the points $(-2, -1)$ and $(2, 7)$.
