What this chapter showed
  1. Polynomials of degrees 1, 2 and 3 are called linear, quadratic and cubic polynomials.
  2. A quadratic polynomial in $x$ with real coefficients has the form $ax^2 + bx + c$, where $a$, $b$, $c$ are real numbers and $a \neq 0$.
  3. A real number $k$ is a zero of $p(x)$ if $p(k) = 0$. The zeroes of $p(x)$ are exactly the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph of $y = p(x)$ meets the $x$-axis.
  4. A polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeroes: a quadratic polynomial at most 2, and a cubic polynomial at most 3.
  5. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $ax^2 + bx + c$, then $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$ and $\alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a}$.
  6. If $\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ are the zeroes of $ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d$, then $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = -\frac{b}{a}$, $\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = \frac{c}{a}$ and $\alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{d}{a}$.

A sum and a product are enough to rebuild a quadratic polynomial, as Example 4 showed.

If you know the sum $S$ and the product $P$ of the zeroes, one quadratic polynomial with those zeroes is $x^2 - Sx + P$. Watch the sign: the sum goes in with a minus sign, the product without one.
