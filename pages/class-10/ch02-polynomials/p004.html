If we mark these points on graph paper and join them with a smooth curve, the graph looks like Fig. 2.2.

In fact, for any quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ with $a \neq 0$, the graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ has one of two shapes. If $a \gt 0$ it opens upwards, and if $a \lt 0$ it opens downwards. These curves are called parabolas.

From Table 2.1, $-1$ and 4 are the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial. From Fig. 2.2, $-1$ and 4 are also the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph of $y = x^2 - 3x - 4$ cuts the $x$-axis. So the zeroes of $x^2 - 3x - 4$ are the $x$-coordinates of the points where its graph cuts the $x$-axis. This is true for every quadratic polynomial.

Zeroes on the graph

The zeroes of a quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$, $a \neq 0$, are exactly the $x$-coordinates of the points where the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ meets the $x$-axis.
