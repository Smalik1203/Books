From Table 2.2, $-1$ and 4 are the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial. From Fig. 2.3, $-1$ and 4 are also the $x$-coordinates of the points where the graph of $y = x^2 - 3x - 4$ cuts the $x$-axis. Zeroes on the graph

The zeroes of a quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$, $a \neq 0$, are exactly the $x$-coordinates of the points where the parabola $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ meets the $x$-axis.

Because a parabola opens either upwards or downwards, three cases can happen.

Case (i). The graph cuts the $x$-axis at two different points $A$ and $A'$. The $x$-coordinates of $A$ and $A'$ are the two zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ in this case (see Fig. 2.4).

Case (ii). The graph meets the $x$-axis at exactly one point, that is, at two points that coincide. The two points $A$ and $A'$ of Case (i) have come together to make one point $A$ (see Fig. 2.5). The $x$-coordinate of $A$ is the only zero of $ax^2 + bx + c$ in this case.

Case (iii). The graph is either wholly above the $x$-axis or wholly below it, so it does not meet the $x$-axis at any point (see Fig. 2.6). So the quadratic polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ has no zero in this case.
