We do not need to factorise anything. The sum of the zeroes is $(2 + \sqrt{3}) + (2 - \sqrt{3}) = 4$, and the product is $(2 + \sqrt{3})(2 - \sqrt{3}) = 4 - 3 = 1$. So one such polynomial is ${x^2 - 4x + 1}$.

The zeroes of $x^2 + 3x + 2$ are $\alpha$ and $\beta$. Find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are $2\alpha$ and $2\beta$.

Here $\alpha + \beta = -3$ and $\alpha\beta = 2$. The new zeroes have sum $2\alpha + 2\beta = 2(\alpha + \beta) = -6$ and product $2\alpha \times 2\beta = 4\alpha\beta = 8$. So one such polynomial is $x^2 + 6x + 8$.

The zeroes of $x^2 - kx + 6$ are in the ratio $3 : 2$. The value of $k$ is

  1. 5 only
  2. $-5$ only
  3. 5 or $-5$
  4. 6
Solution

zeroes $3m$ and $2m$: product $6m^2 = 6$, so $m = \pm 1$; sum $5m = k$, so $k = \pm 5$

The answer is (c): $x^2 + 5x + 6$ has zeroes $-3$ and $-2$, also in the ratio $3 : 2$. Options (a) and (b) keep one value of $m$, and (d) is the product.

Problem 2

A quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are $-3$ and 4 is

  1. $x^2 - x - 12$
  2. $x^2 + x - 12$
  3. $x^2 - x + 12$
  4. $x^2 + 7x + 12$
Solution

The sum of the zeroes is $-3 + 4 = 1$ and the product is $-3 \times 4 = -12$, so the polynomial is $x^2 - 1x + (-12) = x^2 - x - 12$: (a). Option (b) puts the sum in without its minus sign, and (c) loses the sign of the product. Option (d) is $(x + 3)(x + 4)$, whose zeroes are $-3$ and $-4$.
