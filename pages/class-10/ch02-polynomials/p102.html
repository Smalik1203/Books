Here $\alpha + \beta = -3$ and $\alpha\beta = 2$. The new zeroes have sum $2\alpha + 2\beta = 2(\alpha + \beta) = -6$ and product $2\alpha \times 2\beta = 4\alpha\beta = 8$. So one such polynomial is $x^2 + 6x + 8$.

Type 1 · Degree, values and zeroes

Example 1

For $p(x) = 2x^3 - 5x^2 + 4$, write the degree, and find $p(2)$ and $p(-1)$. Which of 2 and $-1$ is a zero of $p(x)$? Then find the zero of $3x - 7$.

Solution.

Step 1 the highest power of $x$ is $x^3$, so the degree is 3
Step 2 $p(2) = 2 \times 2^3 - 5 \times 2^2 + 4 = 16 - 20 + 4 = 0$ put 2 for $x$
Step 3 $p(-1) = 2 \times (-1)^3 - 5 \times (-1)^2 + 4 = -2 - 5 + 4 = -3$
Step 4 $3x - 7 = 0$ gives $x = \frac{7}{3}$ the zero is $-\frac{b}{a}$
Answer Degree 3; $p(2) = 0$ and $p(-1) = -3$, so 2 is a zero and $-1$ is not; the zero of $3x - 7$ is $\frac{7}{3}$

Type 2 · Zeroes and graphs

Example 2

The graph of $y = x^2 + 2x + 1$

  1. cuts the $x$-axis at two points
  2. touches the $x$-axis at one point
  3. does not meet the $x$-axis
  4. meets the $x$-axis at three points

Solution.

Step 1 $x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2$ a perfect square
Step 2 the only zero is $-1$, and $(x + 1)^2$ is never negative
Step 3 it meets the $x$-axis only at $(-1, 0)$, and lies above it it touches, not cuts
Answer (b)

Option (a) would need two different zeroes and (c) would need none. A quadratic polynomial never has three zeroes, as (d) would need.
