Example 6

Find a quadratic polynomial with integer coefficients whose zeroes are $\frac{2}{3}$ and $-\frac{1}{2}$.

Solution.

Step 1 sum $= \dfrac{2}{3} - \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1}{6}$
Step 2 product $= \dfrac{2}{3} \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\right) = -\dfrac{1}{3}$
Step 3 $x^2 - \frac{1}{6}x - \frac{1}{3}$ has these zeroes using $x^2 - Sx + P$
Step 4 $6\left(x^2 - \frac{1}{6}x - \frac{1}{3}\right) = 6x^2 - x - 2$ clear the fractions
Answer $6x^2 - x - 2$

Multiplying by 6 does not change the zeroes. Check: $(3x - 2)(2x + 1) = 6x^2 + 3x - 4x - 2 = 6x^2 - x - 2$.

Example 7

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $2x^2 + 5x - 3$, then $\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2$ is

  1. $-\dfrac{15}{4}$
  2. $\dfrac{15}{4}$
  3. $15$
  4. $\dfrac{15}{2}$

Solution.

Step 1 $\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2 = \alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta)$ take out $\alpha\beta$
Step 2 $\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{5}{2}$ and $\alpha\beta = -\dfrac{3}{2}$
Step 3 $\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = \left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\dfrac{5}{2}\right) = \dfrac{15}{4}$
Answer (b)

Option (a) takes the sum as $+\frac{5}{2}$, (c) never divides by $a = 2$, and (d) divides by it only once.
