The only zero of the polynomial $x^3$ is 0, and from Fig. 2.9(a), 0 is the $x$-coordinate of the only point where the graph of $y = x^3$ meets the $x$-axis. Similarly, since $x^3 - x^2 = x^2(x - 1)$, the only zeroes of $x^3 - x^2$ are 0 and 1. From Fig. 2.9(b), these are the $x$-coordinates of the only points where the graph of $y = x^3 - x^2$ meets the $x$-axis.

From these examples we see that a cubic polynomial has at most 3 zeroes. In other words, a polynomial of degree 3 can have at most three zeroes.

At most n zeroes

If $p(x)$ is a polynomial of degree $n$, the graph of $y = p(x)$ meets the $x$-axis at no more than $n$ points. So a polynomial of degree $n$ has at most $n$ zeroes.
