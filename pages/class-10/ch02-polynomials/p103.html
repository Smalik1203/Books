Problem 3

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $2x^2 + 5x - 3$, then $\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2$ is

  1. $-\dfrac{15}{4}$
  2. $\dfrac{15}{4}$
  3. $15$
  4. $\dfrac{15}{2}$
Solution

$\alpha^2\beta + \alpha\beta^2 = \alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta)$

$\alpha + \beta = -\dfrac{5}{2}$ and $\alpha\beta = -\dfrac{3}{2}$, so $\alpha\beta(\alpha + \beta) = \left(-\dfrac{3}{2}\right)\left(-\dfrac{5}{2}\right) = \dfrac{15}{4}$

The answer is (b). Option (a) takes the sum as $+\frac{5}{2}$, (c) never divides by $a = 2$, and (d) divides by it only once.

Problem 4

If $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the zeroes of $x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6$, then $\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha$ is

  1. $-11$
  2. $6$
  3. $11$
  4. $-6$
Solution

Here $a = 1$ and $c = 11$, so $\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = \frac{c}{a} = 11$: (c). The zeroes are 1, 2 and 3, and $2 + 6 + 3 = 11$. Option (a) puts in a minus sign that belongs to the sum and the product, (b) is the sum $-\frac{b}{a}$, and (d) is the constant term.

Problem 5

The graph of $y = x^2 + 2x + 1$

  1. cuts the $x$-axis at two points
  2. touches the $x$-axis at one point
  3. does not meet the $x$-axis
  4. meets the $x$-axis at three points
Solution

Since $x^2 + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)^2$, the only zero is $-1$, and $y$ is never negative. So the graph meets the $x$-axis only at $(-1, 0)$, where it touches the axis: (b). Two points would need two different zeroes, and a quadratic polynomial never has three.
