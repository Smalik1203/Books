$2x^2 - 8x + 6 = 2x^2 - 6x - 2x + 6$
$= 2x(x - 3) - 2(x - 3)$
$= (2x - 2)(x - 3) = 2(x - 1)(x - 3)$

So $p(x) = 2x^2 - 8x + 6$ is zero when $x - 1 = 0$ or $x - 3 = 0$, that is, when $x = 1$ or $x = 3$. The zeroes of $2x^2 - 8x + 6$ are 1 and 3. Now look at their sum and product.

sum of the zeroes $= 1 + 3 = 4 = \dfrac{-(-8)}{2} = \dfrac{-(\text{coefficient of } x)}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}$

product of the zeroes $= 1 \times 3 = 3 = \dfrac{6}{2} = \dfrac{\text{constant term}}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}$

Let us take one more quadratic polynomial, $p(x) = 3x^2 + 5x - 2$. Splitting the middle term,

$$3x^2 + 5x - 2 = 3x^2 + 6x - x - 2 = 3x(x + 2) - 1(x + 2) = (3x - 1)(x + 2)$$

So $3x^2 + 5x - 2$ is zero when $3x - 1 = 0$ or $x + 2 = 0$, that is, when $x = \frac{1}{3}$ or $x = -2$. The zeroes of $3x^2 + 5x - 2$ are $\frac{1}{3}$ and $-2$, and again

sum of the zeroes $= \dfrac{1}{3} + (-2) = -\dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{-(\text{coefficient of } x)}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}$

product of the zeroes $= \dfrac{1}{3} \times (-2) = -\dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{\text{constant term}}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}$

Why does this happen? Suppose $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of the quadratic polynomial $p(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$, $a \neq 0$. (The Greek letters $\alpha$, $\beta$ and, later, $\gamma$ are read alpha, beta and gamma.) Then you know that $x - \alpha$ and $x - \beta$ are factors of $p(x)$, so we can write it with some constant $k$ as follows.

$ax^2 + bx + c = k(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)$
$= k[x^2 - (\alpha + \beta)x + \alpha\beta]$
$= kx^2 - k(\alpha + \beta)x + k\alpha\beta$

Comparing the coefficients of $x^2$ and $x$ and the constant terms on the two sides, we get $a = k$, $b = -k(\alpha + \beta)$ and $c = k\alpha\beta$. Since $k = a$, this gives the result below.

Zeroes and coefficients of a quadratic polynomial

If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the zeroes of $ax^2 + bx + c$, $a \neq 0$, then

$$\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a} = \dfrac{-(\text{coefficient of } x)}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}, \qquad \alpha\beta = \frac{c}{a} = \dfrac{\text{constant term}}{\text{coefficient of } x^2}$$
