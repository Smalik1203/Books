Now let us look at cubic polynomials. Do you think a similar relationship holds between the zeroes of a cubic polynomial and its coefficients? Take $p(x) = 2x^3 - 5x^2 - 14x + 8$. You can check that $p(x) = 0$ for $x = 4$, $-2$ and $\frac{1}{2}$. Since $p(x)$ can have at most three zeroes, these are all its zeroes. Now

sum of the zeroes $= 4 + (-2) + \dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac{-(-5)}{2} = \dfrac{-(\text{coefficient of } x^2)}{\text{coefficient of } x^3}$

product of the zeroes $= 4 \times (-2) \times \dfrac{1}{2} = -4 = \dfrac{-8}{2} = \dfrac{-(\text{constant term})}{\text{coefficient of } x^3}$

There is one more relationship here. Take the sum of the products of the zeroes, two at a time:

$$4 \times (-2) + (-2) \times \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} \times 4 = -8 - 1 + 2 = -7 = \frac{-14}{2} = \dfrac{\text{coefficient of } x}{\text{coefficient of } x^3}$$
Zeroes and coefficients of a cubic polynomial

It can be proved that if $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are the zeroes of $ax^3 + bx^2 + cx + d$, $a \neq 0$, then

$$\alpha + \beta + \gamma = -\frac{b}{a}, \qquad \alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = \frac{c}{a}, \qquad \alpha\beta\gamma = -\frac{d}{a}$$
Example 5

Check that $3$, $-1$ and $-\frac{1}{3}$ are the zeroes of the cubic polynomial $p(x) = 3x^3 - 5x^2 - 11x - 3$. Then check the relationship between the zeroes and the coefficients.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 3$, $b = -5$, $c = -11$ and $d = -3$ comparing coefficients
Step 2 $p(3) = 3 \times 3^3 - 5 \times 3^2 - 11 \times 3 - 3 = 81 - 45 - 33 - 3 = 0$
Step 3 $p(-1) = 3 \times (-1)^3 - 5 \times (-1)^2 - 11 \times (-1) - 3 = -3 - 5 + 11 - 3 = 0$
Step 4 $p\left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = 3 \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{27}\right) - 5 \times \dfrac{1}{9} - 11 \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) - 3$
$= -\dfrac{1}{9} - \dfrac{5}{9} + \dfrac{11}{3} - 3 = -\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{2}{3} = 0$
Step 5 so these are the zeroes; take $\alpha = 3$, $\beta = -1$ and $\gamma = -\frac{1}{3}$
Step 6 $\alpha + \beta + \gamma = 3 + (-1) + \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = 2 - \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{5}{3} = \dfrac{-(-5)}{3} = -\dfrac{b}{a}$
Step 7 $\alpha\beta + \beta\gamma + \gamma\alpha = 3 \times (-1) + (-1) \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) + \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) \times 3$
$= -3 + \dfrac{1}{3} - 1 = -\dfrac{11}{3} = \dfrac{c}{a}$
Step 8 $\alpha\beta\gamma = 3 \times (-1) \times \left(-\dfrac{1}{3}\right) = 1 = \dfrac{-(-3)}{3} = -\dfrac{d}{a}$
Answer 3, $-1$ and $-\frac{1}{3}$ are the zeroes, and all three relations hold
