Example 3

PQ is a chord of length 8 cm of a circle of radius 5 cm. The tangents at P and Q meet at a point T (Fig. 10.10). Find the length TP.

Solution. Join OT, and let it meet PQ at the point R.

Step 1 The triangle TPQ is isosceles, since ${TP = TQ}$, and TO bisects $\angle PTQ$ Theorem 10.2, Remark 2
Step 2 ${OT \perp PQ}$, and ${PR = RQ = 4}$ cm the bisector in an isosceles triangle
Step 3 ${OR = \sqrt{OP^2 - PR^2} = \sqrt{5^2 - 4^2} = 3}$ cm right triangle PRO
Step 4 ${\angle TPR + \angle RPO = 90^\circ}$ Theorem 10.1
Step 5 ${\angle TPR + \angle PTR = 90^\circ}$ acute angles of triangle TRP
Step 6 ${\angle RPO = \angle PTR}$ from Steps 4 and 5
Step 7 the right triangle TRP is similar to the right triangle PRO AA, right angles at R
Step 8 $\dfrac{TP}{PO} = \dfrac{RP}{RO}$,   that is,   $\dfrac{TP}{5} = \dfrac{4}{3}$ corresponding sides
Answer $TP = \dfrac{20}{3}$ cm

The fact used in Step 2 is this: in an isosceles triangle, the bisector of the angle between the equal sides is perpendicular to the base and bisects it. You proved this fact in Class 9, using congruent triangles.

Note. TP can also be found with the Pythagoras theorem. This takes longer, but needs no similar triangles. Let ${TP = x}$ and ${TR = y}$. Then

${x^2 = y^2 + 16}$   (right triangle PRT)   (1)

${x^2 + 5^2 = (y + 3)^2}$   (right triangle OPT)   (2)

subtracting (1) from (2):   ${25 = 6y - 7}$,   so   $y = \dfrac{32}{6} = \dfrac{16}{3}$

$x^2 = \left(\dfrac{16}{3}\right)^2 + 16 = \dfrac{16(16 + 9)}{9} = \dfrac{16 \times 25}{9}$   [from (1)],   so   $x = \dfrac{20}{3}$
