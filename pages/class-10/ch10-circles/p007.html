Example 1

Prove that in two concentric circles, the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.

Solution. We are given two concentric circles C1 and C2 with centre O, and a chord AB of the larger circle C1 that touches the smaller circle C2 at the point P (Fig. 10.8). We need to prove that ${AP = BP}$. Join OP.

Step 1 AB is a tangent to C2 at P, and OP is a radius of C2 given, and the join
Step 2 ${OP \perp AB}$ Theorem 10.1
Step 3 AB is a chord of C1, and ${OP \perp AB}$ from Step 2
Step 4 OP bisects the chord AB perpendicular from the centre
Answer ${AP = BP}$
Example 2

Two tangents TP and TQ are drawn to a circle with centre O from an external point T. Prove that ${\angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ}$.

Solution. We are given a circle with centre O, an external point T, and two tangents TP and TQ with points of contact P and Q (Fig. 10.9). We need to prove that ${\angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ}$.

Step 1 ${\angle PTQ = \theta}$ naming the angle
Step 2 ${TP = TQ}$, so TPQ is an isosceles triangle Theorem 10.2
Step 3 $\angle TPQ = \angle TQP = \dfrac{1}{2}(180^\circ - \theta) = 90^\circ - \dfrac{1}{2}\theta$ angles opposite equal sides
Step 4 ${\angle OPT = 90^\circ}$ Theorem 10.1
Step 5 $\angle OPQ = \angle OPT - \angle TPQ = 90^\circ - \left(90^\circ - \dfrac{1}{2}\theta\right) = \dfrac{1}{2}\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}\angle PTQ$
Answer So ${\angle PTQ = 2\angle OPQ}$
