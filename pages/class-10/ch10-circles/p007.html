Join OP, OQ and OR. By Theorem 10.1, the angles OQP and ORP, between the radii and the tangents, are right angles. So in the right triangles OQP and ORP,

${OQ = OR}$   (radii of the same circle)

${OP = OP}$   (common)

so ${\triangle OQP \cong \triangle ORP}$   (RHS)

and ${PQ = PR}$   (CPCT)

Remark 1. The theorem can also be proved with the Pythagoras theorem:

${PQ^2 = OP^2 - OQ^2 = OP^2 - OR^2 = PR^2}$   (as ${OQ = OR}$),   which gives ${PQ = PR}$

Remark 2. The congruence also gives ${\angle OPQ = \angle OPR}$. So OP bisects $\angle QPR$: the centre lies on the bisector of the angle between the two tangents.

Let us look at some examples.

Example 1

Prove that in two concentric circles, the chord of the larger circle that touches the smaller circle is bisected at the point of contact.

We are given two concentric circles C1 and C2 with centre O, and a chord AB of the larger circle C1 that touches the smaller circle C2 at the point P (Fig. 10.8). We need to prove that ${AP = BP}$.

Join OP. AB is a tangent to C2 at P, and OP is a radius of C2. So, by Theorem 10.1,

${OP \perp AB}$

Now AB is a chord of C1, and ${OP \perp AB}$. The perpendicular from the centre of a circle to a chord bisects the chord. So OP bisects AB, that is, ${AP = BP}$.
