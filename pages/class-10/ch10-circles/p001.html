Circles

10.1Introduction

In Class 9 you studied that a circle is the set of all the points in a plane that are at a fixed distance, the radius, from a fixed point, the centre. You also met words that go with a circle, such as chord, segment, sector and arc. In this chapter we look at what can happen when a circle and a line lie in the same plane.

Take a circle and a line PQ. Fig. 10.1 shows the three possibilities.

In Fig. 10.1 (i), the line PQ and the circle have no common point. We call PQ a non-intersecting line with respect to the circle. In Fig. 10.1 (ii), the line and the circle have two common points, A and B. We call the line PQ a secant of the circle. In Fig. 10.1 (iii), the line and the circle have only one common point, A. We call the line PQ a tangent to the circle.

You may have seen a pulley fixed over a well, used to draw water. Look at Fig. 10.2. The rope on each side of the pulley, thought of as a ray, is like a tangent to the circle that stands for the pulley.
