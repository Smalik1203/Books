Example 10

Find the nature of the roots of $2x^2 - 2\sqrt{2}x + 1 = 0$. Find the roots if they are real.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 2$, $b = -2\sqrt{2}$, $c = 1$
Step 2 $b^2 - 4ac = 8 - 8 = 0$ so two equal real roots
Step 3 each root is $-\dfrac{b}{2a} = \dfrac{2\sqrt{2}}{4} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$
Answer two equal real roots, $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ and $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$

Check: $2x^2 - 2\sqrt{2}x + 1 = (\sqrt{2}x - 1)^2$, which is 0 only when $x = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

Example 11

The equation $kx^2 + 4x + 1 = 0$ is a quadratic equation with real roots when

  1. $k \leq 4$
  2. $k \lt 4$
  3. $k \geq 4$
  4. $k \leq 4$, $k \neq 0$

Solution.

Step 1 $b^2 - 4ac = 16 - 4k$
Step 2 $16 - 4k \geq 0$, so $k \leq 4$ real roots
Step 3 $k \neq 0$ the $x^2$ term must stay
Answer (d)

Option (a) forgets $k \neq 0$, (b) leaves out the equal roots at $k = 4$, and (c) turns the inequality round.
