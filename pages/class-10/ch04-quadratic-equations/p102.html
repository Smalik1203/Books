The product of two consecutive odd integers is 143. Find the integers.

Let the integers be $x$ and $x + 2$. Then $x(x + 2) = 143$, that is, $x^2 + 2x - 143 = 0$, or $(x + 13)(x - 11) = 0$. So $x = 11$ or $x = -13$. Both roots answer this question: 11 and 13 work, and so do $-13$ and $-11$, whose product is also 143. If the question had said positive integers, only 11 and 13 would be allowed. Whether a negative root is kept depends on the question, not on the equation. Check both pairs: $11 \times 13 = 143$ and $(-13) \times (-11) = 143$.

Show that $x^2 + px - 1 = 0$ has two distinct real roots, whatever the real number $p$ is.

The discriminant is $p^2 - 4 \times 1 \times (-1) = p^2 + 4$. A square is never negative, so $p^2 \geq 0$ and $p^2 + 4 \geq 4$, which is greater than 0. So the discriminant is positive for every $p$, and the equation always has two distinct real roots. One fact about squares answered the question for infinitely many equations at once.

Problem 1

The roots of $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$ are

  1. $3$ and $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  2. $-3$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$
  3. $\dfrac{3}{2}$ and $1$
  4. $6$ and $1$
Solution

$2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 2x^2 - 6x - x + 3 = 2x(x - 3) - 1(x - 3) = (2x - 1)(x - 3)$

$2x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = \dfrac{1}{2}$, and $x - 3 = 0$ gives $x = 3$

The answer is (a). Option (b) gets the signs wrong: the roots of $(2x + 1)(x + 3) = 0$. Option (c) comes from a wrong split of the middle term, and (d) are the roots of $x^2 - 7x + 6 = 0$, which forgets the coefficient 2.
