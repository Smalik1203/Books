Show that $x^2 + px - 1 = 0$ has two distinct real roots, whatever the real number $p$ is.

The discriminant is $p^2 - 4 \times 1 \times (-1) = p^2 + 4$. A square is never negative, so $p^2 \geq 0$ and $p^2 + 4 \geq 4$, which is greater than 0. So the discriminant is positive for every $p$, and the equation always has two distinct real roots. One fact about squares answered the question for infinitely many equations at once.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · Recognising and forming quadratic equations

Example 1

Check whether $(x + 1)^3 = x^3 + 2x - 5$ is a quadratic equation.

Solution.

Step 1 $(x + 1)^3 = x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1$ expand the cube
Step 2 $x^3 + 3x^2 + 3x + 1 = x^3 + 2x - 5$
Step 3 $3x^2 + x + 6 = 0$ the cubes cancel
Answer Yes. It is a quadratic equation, with $a = 3$, $b = 1$ and $c = 6$
Example 2

The length of a rectangular garden is 3 m more than its breadth, and its area is 180 m². Write the quadratic equation, in standard form, that the breadth satisfies.

Solution.

Step 1 breadth $x$ m, so length $(x + 3)$ m
Step 2 $x(x + 3) = 180$ length times breadth
Answer $x^2 + 3x - 180 = 0$

Its roots are 12 and $-15$, so the garden is 12 m wide and 15 m long.
