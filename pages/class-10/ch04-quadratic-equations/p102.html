Show that $x^2 + px - 1 = 0$ has two distinct real roots, whatever the real number $p$ is.

The discriminant is $p^2 - 4 \times 1 \times (-1) = p^2 + 4$. A square is never negative, so $p^2 \geq 0$ and $p^2 + 4 \geq 4$, which is greater than 0. So the discriminant is positive for every $p$, and the equation always has two distinct real roots. One fact about squares answered the question for infinitely many equations at once.

Solved Examples
Example 1 · Single correct

What are the roots of $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$?

Choose one correct option.

  1. $3$ and $\frac{1}{2}$
  2. $-3$ and $-\frac{1}{2}$
  3. $3$ and $2$
  4. $6$ and $1$

Solution.

Step 1Split the middle term: $2x^2 - 6x - x + 3 = 2x(x - 3) - (x - 3) = (2x - 1)(x - 3)$.
Step 2The roots are $\frac{1}{2}$ and 3.
Answer(a) $3$ and $\frac{1}{2}$
Example 2 · Single correct

What is the discriminant of $3x^2 - 4x + 2 = 0$?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 8
  2. $-8$
  3. 40
  4. $-40$

Solution.

Step 1$D = b^2 - 4ac$ with $a = 3$, $b = -4$, $c = 2$.
Step 2$D = 16 - 24 = -8$.
Answer(b) $-8$
