A6 $x^2 - 49 = (x - 7)(x + 7)$, so the roots are 7 and $-7$: (b). Option (a) forgets that $(-7)^2 = 49$ as well.
A8 $x(2x + 3) = 0$ gives $x = 0$ or $x = -\frac{3}{2}$: (b). Option (a) divides by $x$ and loses the root 0, and (c) gets the sign wrong.
B1 $(2k)^2 - 4 \times 16 = 0$ gives $k^2 = 16$, so $k = 4$ or $-4$: (b). Option (a) keeps one value only, and (d) takes $b$ as $k$ instead of $2k$.
B3 Length plus breadth is 15, so $x(15 - x) = 56$, $x^2 - 15x + 56 = 0$ and $x = 7$ or 8. The length is the longer side, 8 cm: (b). Option (a) is the breadth, and neither 14 nor 4 is a root of the equation.
B5 Equal roots need $b^2 = 4ac$, so $c = \frac{b^2}{4a}$: (a). Option (b) is the equal root itself.
C1 $x^2 + x - 42 = (x + 7)(x - 6) = 0$, so $x = 6$ or $-7$, and both work: (a). Options (b) and (d) keep one root, and (c) gets both signs wrong.
C2 $x(x + 30) = 400$ gives $(x + 40)(x - 10) = 0$, so the son is 10: (a). Option (b) is the father’s age, and also the negative root with its sign dropped.
C3 $x^2 - 2x + 5 = 0$ has discriminant $4 - 20 = -16$: (c). Option (d) solves ${(x - 1)^2 = 4}$ instead, and (a) and (b) treat the $+4$ as if it were not there.
C4 No real roots needs $p^2 - 64 \lt 0$, that is, $p$ between $-8$ and 8: (c). For (d) the roots are equal, and for (a) and (b) they are distinct.
C5 $x^2 + (x + 5)^2 = 625$ gives $x^2 + 5x - 300 = (x + 20)(x - 15) = 0$, so the sides are 15 and 20: (a). Option (b) is the other side.

Three things from this section are worth keeping. The discriminant can show that something is impossible, or always possible, without solving anything. A root of the equation must still be checked against the question: a length or a speed cannot be negative, but an integer can. And the relations from Chapter 2 give the other root once one root is known.
