Everything depends on the number $b^2 - 4ac$ under the square root. If $b^2 - 4ac \gt 0$, we get two different real roots.

$$\dfrac{-b + \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \dfrac{-b - \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

If $b^2 - 4ac = 0$, then $x = \frac{-b \pm 0}{2a}$, so both roots are $-\frac{b}{2a}$. In this case we say that the equation has two equal real roots.

If $b^2 - 4ac$ is negative, there is no real number whose square is $b^2 - 4ac$. So in this case the equation has no real roots.

Since $b^2 - 4ac$ decides whether the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has real roots or not, it is called the discriminant of the equation. It tells us what kind of roots the equation has before we try to find them.

The discriminant and the nature of the roots

A quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has

(i) two distinct real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac \gt 0$;

(ii) two equal real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac = 0$;

(iii) no real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac \lt 0$.

Let us look at some examples. In each one we work out the discriminant first, and only then decide whether to look for the roots.

Example 7

Find the discriminant of the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$, and use it to find the nature of the roots.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 2$, $b = -4$ and $c = 3$ the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$
Step 2 $b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4 \times 2 \times 3 = 16 - 24 = -8$
Step 3 the discriminant is less than 0 so no real roots
Answer the discriminant is $-8$, and the equation has no real roots
