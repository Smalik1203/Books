Now $AB = 13$ m, and since $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle APB = 90^\circ$. (Why?) So, by the Pythagoras theorem,

$AP^2 + PB^2 = AB^2$

$(x + 7)^2 + x^2 = 13^2$, that is, $x^2 + 14x + 49 + x^2 = 169$

$2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0$, that is, $x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$

So the pole can be put up only if this equation has real roots. Its discriminant is $b^2 - 4ac = 7^2 - 4 \times 1 \times (-60) = 289$, which is greater than 0. So the equation has two real roots, and it is possible to put up the pole on the boundary of the park. Solving $x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$ by the quadratic formula,

$$x = \dfrac{-7 \pm \sqrt{289}}{2} = \dfrac{-7 \pm 17}{2}, \quad \text{so} \quad x = 5 \ \text{or} \ x = -12$$

Since $x$ is the distance from the pole to gate $B$, it must be positive, so we ignore $x = -12$. So $x = 5$: the pole must be put up on the boundary of the park 5 m from gate $B$ and 12 m from gate $A$.

Example 9

Find the discriminant of the equation $3x^2 - 2x + \frac{1}{3} = 0$, and use it to find the nature of the roots. Find the roots if they are real.

Here $a = 3$, $b = -2$ and $c = \frac{1}{3}$. So the discriminant is $b^2 - 4ac = (-2)^2 - 4 \times 3 \times \frac{1}{3} = 4 - 4 = 0$, and the equation has two equal real roots. The roots are $-\frac{b}{2a}$ and $-\frac{b}{2a}$, that is, $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{2}{6}$, that is, $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$.
