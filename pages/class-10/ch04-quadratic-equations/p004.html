4.3Solution of a Quadratic Equation by Factorisation

Take the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0$. If we put 1 in place of $x$ on the LHS, we get $2 \times 1^2 - 3 \times 1 + 1 = 0$, which is the RHS. We say that 1 is a root of the equation $2x^2 - 3x + 1 = 0$. It also means that the quadratic polynomial $2x^2 - 3x + 1$ has 1 as a zero.

Root of a quadratic equation

A real number $\alpha$ is called a root of the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, $a \neq 0$, if $a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$. We also say that $x = \alpha$ is a solution of the equation, or that $\alpha$ satisfies it.

The zeroes of the polynomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ and the roots of the equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ are the same.

In Chapter 2 you saw that a quadratic polynomial has at most two zeroes. So a quadratic equation has at most two roots.

In Class IX you learnt to factorise a quadratic polynomial by splitting its middle term. We now use this to find the roots of a quadratic equation. Let us see how.

Example 3

Find the roots of the equation $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ by factorisation.

Solution.

Step 1 split the middle term $-5x$ as $-2x - 3x$, because $(-2x) \times (-3x) = 6x^2$, which is $2x^2 \times 3$
Step 2 $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 2x^2 - 2x - 3x + 3$
Step 3 $= 2x(x - 1) - 3(x - 1) = (2x - 3)(x - 1)$ take out common factors
Step 4 $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ becomes $(2x - 3)(x - 1) = 0$, so $2x - 3 = 0$ or $x - 1 = 0$ a zero product
Step 5 $2x - 3 = 0$ gives $x = \frac{3}{2}$, and $x - 1 = 0$ gives $x = 1$
Answer the roots of $2x^2 - 5x + 3 = 0$ are 1 and $\frac{3}{2}$
