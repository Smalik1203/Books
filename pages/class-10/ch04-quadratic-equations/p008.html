Example 8

A circular park has a diameter of 13 metres, and $A$ and $B$ are two gates at the two ends of a diameter, on the boundary. A pole is to be put up at a point on the boundary so that the difference of its distances from the two gates is 7 metres. Is this possible? If so, how far from each gate should the pole be?

Solution.

Step 1 in Fig. 4.2, $BP = x$ and $AP = (x + 7)$ m the difference is 7 m
Step 2 $AB = 13$ m, and since $AB$ is a diameter, $\angle APB = 90^\circ$. (Why?)
Step 3 $AP^2 + PB^2 = AB^2$, so $(x + 7)^2 + x^2 = 13^2$ the Pythagoras theorem
Step 4 $x^2 + 14x + 49 + x^2 = 169$, so $2x^2 + 14x - 120 = 0$
Step 5 $x^2 + 7x - 60 = 0$ dividing by 2
Step 6 $b^2 - 4ac = 7^2 - 4 \times 1 \times (-60) = 289$, greater than 0, so real roots
Step 7 $x = \dfrac{-7 \pm \sqrt{289}}{2} = \dfrac{-7 \pm 17}{2}, \quad \text{so} \quad x = 5 \ \text{or} \ x = -12$ the quadratic formula
Answer Yes. A distance is positive, so we ignore $x = -12$. With $x = 5$, the pole goes on the boundary 5 m from gate $B$ and 12 m from gate $A$
Example 9

Find the discriminant of the equation $3x^2 - 2x + \frac{1}{3} = 0$, and use it to find the nature of the roots. Find the roots if they are real.

Solution.

Step 1 $a = 3$, $b = -2$ and $c = \frac{1}{3}$
Step 2 $b^2 - 4ac = (-2)^2 - 4 \times 3 \times \frac{1}{3} = 4 - 4 = 0$ so two equal real roots
Step 3 the roots are $-\frac{b}{2a}$ and $-\frac{b}{2a}$, that is, $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{2}{6}$ both are $-\frac{b}{2a}$
Answer the discriminant is 0; the equation has two equal real roots, $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$
