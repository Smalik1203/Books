Since $b^2 - 4ac$ decides whether the quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has real roots or not, it is called the discriminant of the equation. It tells us what kind of roots the equation has before we try to find them.

The discriminant and the nature of the roots

A quadratic equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has

(i) two distinct real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac \gt 0$;

(ii) two equal real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac = 0$;

(iii) no real roots, if $b^2 - 4ac \lt 0$.

Let us look at some examples. In each one we work out the discriminant first, and only then decide whether to look for the roots.

Example 7

Find the discriminant of the quadratic equation $2x^2 - 4x + 3 = 0$, and use it to find the nature of the roots.

The equation has the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ with $a = 2$, $b = -4$ and $c = 3$. So the discriminant is $b^2 - 4ac = (-4)^2 - 4 \times 2 \times 3 = 16 - 24 = -8$. This is less than 0, so the equation has no real roots.

Example 8

A circular park has a diameter of 13 metres, and $A$ and $B$ are two gates at the two ends of a diameter, on the boundary. A pole is to be put up at a point on the boundary so that the difference of its distances from the two gates is 7 metres. Is this possible? If so, how far from each gate should the pole be?

Let us first draw a diagram, Fig. 4.2. Let $P$ be the place for the pole, and let its distance from gate $B$ be $x$ m, so $BP = x$. The difference of its distances from the two gates is 7 m, so $AP = (x + 7)$ m.
