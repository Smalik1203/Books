Summary

People across India eat widely different food, and the difference is not random. It follows the crops that grow in each region, which follow soil and climate, and is then shaped by culture, religion and taste.

Culinary practices have changed sharply within living memory — from chulha to gas stove, from sil-batta to mixer-grinder — driven by new technology, better transport and faster communication. The changes brought real gains and some real losses.

Carbohydrates, fats, proteins, vitamins and minerals are nutrient groups studied here. Water is also an essential nutrient. Food supplies dietary fibre as well.

Carbohydrates and fats supply energy and are called energy-giving foods. Fat carries more energy per gram than carbohydrate and is the body's store.

Proteins build and repair the body and are called body-building foods. Growing children and athletes need proportionally more.

Lack of a nutrient can cause a deficiency disorder. Symptoms can also have other causes, so medical assessment matters. Examples include Vitamin C deficiency causing scurvy and iodine deficiency contributing to goitre.

Dietary fibre resists digestion by our own enzymes; gut microbes can break down some of it. Fibre helps bowel function. Water transports dissolved substances and helps regulate body temperature.

Starch, fat and protein can be detected with simple tests: iodine turns starch blue-black, fat leaves a translucent oily patch on paper, and copper sulfate with caustic soda turns protein violet. Most foods contain more than one nutrient.

A balanced diet supplies all the nutrients a particular person needs, in the right amounts, with enough roughage and water. What is balanced depends on age, sex, activity, health and way of life.

Junk foods carry plenty of energy from sugar and fat and little else. Millets are called nutri-cereals because they carry fibre and minerals along with their carbohydrate, and they grow on less water.

Food miles measures distance, not total environmental impact. Transport method, farming and storage also matter. Wasted food wastes resources used to produce it.
