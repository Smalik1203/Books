Would millets alone be enough?

No — and the reason is worth understanding. Millets are strong on carbohydrate, fibre and several minerals, but weak on some nutrients the body needs, and no cereal supplies everything. Eaten with dal, vegetables, milk or curd, millets make an excellent meal. Eaten alone, they leave gaps.

3.9 Food Miles: From the Field to the Plate

Aarav's lunch had rice in it. Ira's had wheat. Neither grew in the school. Follow a chapati backwards from the plate and you find a chain of work. A farmer prepares soil, sows wheat, waters and tends it for months, and harvests it. The grain is threshed and winnowed to separate it from the chaff. It is stored, often for months. It is transported to a mill and ground into flour. The flour is packed, transported again, and sold in a shop. Someone buys it, carries it home, kneads it, and cooks the chapati.

That is many months, many hands, and a great many kilometres, for one item on one plate.

Follow the Evidence

Food miles is the distance food travels from producer to consumer. A shorter journey may reduce transport, but total cost, freshness and emissions also depend on farming, storage, transport method and waste. Distance alone cannot establish the best choice.
