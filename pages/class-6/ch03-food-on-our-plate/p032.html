3.8 Millets: The Grains That Came Back

Jowar, bajra, ragi, sanwa, kodo, kutki — these are millets, and they are among the oldest cultivated grains in India. For centuries they were ordinary food across large parts of the country. Then, within a few decades, they largely disappeared from urban plates, pushed aside by polished rice and refined wheat.

Compare the grains by named nutrients and growing conditions. A single label such as healthy cannot replace those comparisons.

Millets are small-grained cereals, so they supply carbohydrate like any cereal. What sets them apart is what comes with the carbohydrate: substantially more dietary fibre than polished rice, along with iron, calcium and B vitamins. Because of this they are often called nutri-cereals — cereals that carry protective nutrients alongside energy.

Many millets tolerate relatively dry conditions and can need less irrigation than flooded rice. Water use still depends on crop, yield, climate and farming practice. Compare like growing conditions before making a numerical claim.

Millets can add variety and nutrients and can suit dry regions. This does not make every millet superior to every rice dish on every measure.
