Proteins

Carbohydrates and fats supply energy, and fats also form parts of cells. Proteins have an essential role in building and repairing tissues. These job labels overlap; they are not exclusive compartments.

Consider what a body has to do besides move. A six-year-old grows into a sixteen-year-old. A cut on your hand closes over in a week. Hair grows, nails grow, muscle thickens with training. All of that is construction work, and construction needs material.

The material is protein.

How It Works

Proteins are used to build new tissue and repair damaged tissue. This is why the diet of a growing child must contain enough protein, and why an athlete training for strength needs more than a person who is not. A body short of protein cannot grow properly, however much energy it is given — you cannot build a wall out of firewood.

Pulses, soya beans, nuts and seeds supply protein. Milk, curd, paneer, eggs, fish and meat are other sources. Compare amounts as well as presence: fresh mushrooms contain protein but are mostly water and supply less per usual serving than pulses.
