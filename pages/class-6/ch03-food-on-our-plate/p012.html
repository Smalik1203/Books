Now interpret Case 1. Lind's sailors were not short of food — ships carried salted meat and dry biscuit in quantity. They were short of something present in lemons and oranges and absent from salted meat.

That something is now called Vitamin C. Scurvy is caused by a lack of Vitamin C in the diet, and it is prevented and cured by foods that contain it — citrus fruits like lemon and orange, and also amla, guava and green chilli.

Follow the Evidence

The comparison supplied evidence favouring citrus fruit over the other treatments tested. It did not identify Vitamin C, and two sailors per treatment is a small sample. More observations and later research were needed to establish the cause.

Think It Through

Lind's sailors ate plenty of food and still fell ill. What does that tell you about the difference between eating enough and eating well?

Case 2 — The swelling in the neck

In the 1960s, Indian scientists studying the Himalayan region and the northern plains found that a large number of people there had a visible swelling at the front of the neck. It was common in some districts and rare in others only a few hundred kilometres away.

Following the scientists' findings, the Government of India promoted the addition of iodine to common salt — iodised salt. Where people switched to it, the swellings became far less common.

The condition is called goitre.

How It Works

Iodine deficiency can enlarge the thyroid gland, causing goitre. Diets relying on crops from iodine-poor soil can contribute. But a neck swelling can have other causes: symptoms alone do not diagnose a nutrient deficiency.
