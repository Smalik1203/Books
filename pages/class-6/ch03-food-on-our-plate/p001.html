The bell for lunch break rings at Meridian Public School, and within a minute the corridor smells of six different homes.

Aarav opens his tiffin — lemon rice, still warm. Ira opens hers — two rotis and a bowl of dal. From the next bench comes the smell of fish curry, from the bench behind, of dhokla.

Aarav looks around the room: the lunches differ. Which ingredients do they share, and what does the body obtain from them?

So what is actually happening inside all those tiffin boxes? Why does food make us able to run, and think, and grow?

Look at your own lunch today. Name three things in it. Where did each one come from?

Choose one dish and trace it back to its ingredients. A roti may begin with wheat flour; dal with pulses. What else went into preparing it? Ask the person who made it, and separate what you observed from what you found out by asking.

Keep these questions in your notebook. As you explore the chapter, connect the foods you know with nutrients, cooking practices and the journey from farm to plate. One lunch starts the enquiry; a week of observations gives you more to compare.
