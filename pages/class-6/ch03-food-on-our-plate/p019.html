3.5 How Do We Test for Nutrients?

So far you have taken this chapter's word for it. Time to check for yourself. Some nutrients need a well-equipped laboratory to detect. Three do not: starch, fat and protein can each be tested with equipment a school already has. Starch, incidentally, is a carbohydrate — the form in which plants store it.

Before each test, record what you expect and why. This is a prediction. Keep it beside the observation even if they disagree. Both matching and unexpected results matter; neither alone proves a complete explanation.
