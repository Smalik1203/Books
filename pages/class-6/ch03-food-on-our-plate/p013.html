Iodised salt is a way to supply a small, controlled amount of iodine through a widely used ingredient. This does not mean eating extra salt; it means using iodised salt within the usual limited amount.

You have probably read the words "iodised salt" on a packet without thinking about them. They mean common salt to which a small measured quantity of iodine salts has been added.

Vitamin C and iodine are examples of a larger group: the vitamins and minerals.

In the Little Rann of Kutch, Agariya salt workers pump underground brine into evaporation pans. Water evaporates and salt crystallises. This example connects a food ingredient to separation by evaporation and to the people who produce it.

Vitamin C and iodine are two of many. Each vitamin and each mineral has its own job, its own food sources, and its own set of symptoms when the body runs short. Together they are what keep the body's defences and daily processes working.
