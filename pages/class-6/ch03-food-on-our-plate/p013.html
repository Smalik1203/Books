Mushrooms belong to the fungi, not the plants. They obtain nutrients from organic matter rather than making sugars by photosynthesis. Fresh edible mushrooms contain water, fibre and nutrients, but are not interchangeable with pulses as a protein source. Never taste a wild mushroom to identify it.

3.3 The Components We Need in Tiny Amounts

Carbohydrates, fats and proteins are the components we eat by the spoonful. There is another set that we need in amounts so small they are measured in milligrams — and yet a person who goes without them falls seriously ill.

The strongest way to see what these components do is to look at what happens when they are missing. Read the two cases below and work out the answer yourself before reading on.

Case 1 — The sailors' disease

On long sea voyages in earlier centuries, sailors fell ill in a particular way. Their gums swelled and bled, their teeth loosened, old wounds reopened, and many died — often months from any battle or storm.

In May 1747, James Lind compared six treatments among twelve sailors with scurvy, two sailors per treatment. The pair receiving oranges and lemons improved most rapidly.

The condition is called scurvy.
