Dietary fibre, or roughage, comes mainly from plant foods such as whole grains, pulses, fruit and vegetables. Our own digestive enzymes do not break it down, although gut microbes can break down some fibre. Fibre helps bowel function; constipation can have several causes.

Water dissolves and transports substances, helps remove waste in urine and helps regulate temperature through sweating and evaporation. Needs vary with activity and weather. Water is a nutrient, not a source of food energy.

Coluthur Gopalan (1918-2019)

To study diets in India, researchers needed reliable measurements of the foods people ate, along with records of amounts and preparation. A foreign food table could not represent every local ingredient or recipe.

Coluthur Gopalan set out to find out. Over his career he and his colleagues analysed the nutritional content of more than five hundred Indian foods — not foods from foreign tables, but the grains, pulses, vegetables and greens that Indian households actually cooked. It is difficult, unglamorous laboratory work, and it produced something India had never had: reliable numbers for its own diet.

He then led large surveys of what Indians were eating and how healthy they were. The surveys found widespread shortages of protein and energy, particularly among children — not in a few unlucky families, but across whole populations.

The national school nutrition programme began in 1995 and developed into the cooked school-meal programme now called PM POSHAN. Nutrition research helped shape school feeding policy. A programme date alone does not prove its effects: those require measurements of children and meals.

Gopalan died in 2019, aged 101.
