Cooking tools, then and now

Pool the class's interview notes and a shared picture appears. The way food is prepared — what we can call culinary practice — has changed sharply within living memory.

A chulha burns fuel such as wood or dung cakes; a gas stove burns gas. A sil-batta grinds food by hand; a mixer-grinder uses an electric motor. These tools coexist today. Your interviews can show which changes occurred in particular homes and when.

Why did all this change?

Partly because the technology arrived. Gas connections, electricity and affordable appliances reached ordinary households, and once a tool that saves an hour a day is available, people use it.

Partly because transport improved. Roads, railways and cold storage mean that a fruit grown in Himachal can be sold in Chennai. Foods that were once purely local became available everywhere, and cuisines began to borrow from each other.

Television, phones and the internet can spread recipes beyond a region. To test a claim about how quickly one dish spread, we would need dated evidence from several places.
