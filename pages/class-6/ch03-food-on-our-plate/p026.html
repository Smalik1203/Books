How It Works

Iodine forms a blue-black complex with starch. Compare with the starch control: a matching change supports starch being present. A negative result cannot establish absence if the control failed.

Activity 3.5

You will need: the same food samples; plain paper.

Use cooking oil as a positive control and water as a negative control. Keep sample amounts and waiting times alike. Record observations before interpreting them.

1.

Take a small piece of each food from the iodine investigation.

2.

Place each on a separate piece of paper. Label the papers.

3.

Fold the paper over the food and press firmly. Do not tear it.

4.

If a food is watery, set the paper aside until it dries — a wet patch is not an oily patch, and confusing the two will ruin your result.

5.

Hold each paper up to the light.
