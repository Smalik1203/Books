3.1 What Do We Eat in a Week?

Aarav's question is a good one, and the honest answer is that we cannot say much about food in general until we look at food in particular. So begin with the food you know best: your own. Most of us eat without paying attention. We eat what is served, at the time it is served, and we rarely notice the pattern. But there is pattern. Over a week, your meals repeat, vary, and cluster in ways that tell you something about your household, your region, and your health.

Scientists call this kind of careful watching observation. Observation is not the same as looking. Looking is what you do all day. Observing is looking with a question in mind, and writing down what you find.

Activity 3.1

You will need: your notebook, seven days.

1.

Create a food diary in your notebook, with one entry for each day.

2.

Every evening for one week, write down everything you ate that day — meals, snacks, drinks, everything. Do not leave out the biscuit you ate at 4 p.m.

3.

Write it the same evening. If you wait till Sunday and try to remember Monday, you may forget or misremember items; mark any uncertain entries.

4.

At the end of the week, read the whole table at once.

In your notebook, use two columns: Day and What I ate and drank. Record one entry for each day, Monday to Sunday.

Now read your table as a scientist would. Did the same items appear on most days? Which days were different from the rest, and can you say why — a festival, a guest, a day you were unwell?

Next, compare. Ask three classmates to show you their tables. Find one food that appears in all four diaries, and one food that appears in only one. Comparing is how we tell what is common from what is particular.
