Supercells in a grid

Supercells also work in a table with more than one row. In a grid, the cells next to a cell are the ones just to its left and right, and just above and below it. The rule stays the same: a cell is a supercell if its number is bigger than the numbers in all the cells next to it.

In Fig. 3.6, 8632 is a supercell. It has four neighbours: 4795 above it, 1944 below it, 4580 to its left and 8280 to its right. 8632 is bigger than all four. A cell at the edge of the grid has fewer neighbours. The corner cell 5785 has only two, 4580 and 1944, and it is bigger than both. A cell on an edge, but not in a corner, has three neighbours. The cell 7500 has 2430, 7350 and 4795 next to it, and it is bigger than all three, so it is a supercell too.

The grid in Fig. 3.7 is only partly filled. Every number in it is a 5-digit number made from the digits 1, 0, 6, 3 and 9, each used once.

Copy the grid, and fill its empty cells with 5-digit numbers made from the digits 1, 0, 6, 3 and 9, so that only the coloured cells are supercells. Do not use any number twice. Write each number with a comma after the thousands digit, as in 96,301.
