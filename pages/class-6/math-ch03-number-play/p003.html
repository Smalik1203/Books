Exercise Set 3.2
  1. Find the supercells in the table in Fig. 3.4. Colour or mark them.
  1. Copy the table in Fig. 3.5. Fill it with 4-digit numbers only, so that the supercells are exactly the coloured cells.
  1. Draw a table of 9 cells in a row. Fill it with numbers between 100 and 1000 so that it has as many supercells as possible. Do not use any number twice.
  1. How many of the 9 numbers in your table for Question 3 are supercells?
  1. Find the largest number of supercells a table can have with 2 cells, with 3 cells, with 4 cells, and so on. Do you notice a pattern? How would you fill a table to get the most supercells? Share your method.
  1. Can you fill a table, without using any number twice, so that it has no supercells at all? Why or why not?
  1. Will the cell with the largest number in a table always be a supercell? Can the cell with the smallest number ever be a supercell? Why or why not?
  1. Fill a table so that the cell with the second largest number is not a supercell.
  1. Fill a table so that the cell with the second largest number is not a supercell, but the cell with the second smallest number is. Is this possible?
  1. Make up a new puzzle like these, and try it on your classmates.

Supercells in a grid

Supercells also work in a table with more than one row. In a grid, the cells next to a cell are the ones just to its left and right, and just above and below it. The rule stays the same: a cell is a supercell if its number is bigger than the numbers in all the cells next to it.
