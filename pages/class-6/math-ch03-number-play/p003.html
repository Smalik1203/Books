3.2Supercells

Look at the two tables of numbers in Fig. 3.3. Some of the cells are coloured. Why do you think these cells were chosen? Discuss with your classmates.

A cell is coloured when its number is bigger than the numbers in the cells next to it. In the second table, 626 is coloured because it is bigger than 577 and also bigger than 345. The number 200 is not coloured, because 577 is bigger. The last cell holds 198, and it has only one cell next to it. That cell holds 109, which is smaller, so 198 is coloured.

Supercells

A cell is a supercell if its number is bigger than the number in every cell next to it.

A cell in the middle of a row has two neighbours, and a cell at either end has only one. Example 1 checks each cell of a row in turn.

Example 1

Find the supercells in the row 18, 35, 27, 41, 12, 39.

Solution.

Step 1 35 is bigger than 18 and 27. a supercell
Step 2 41 is bigger than 27 and 12. a supercell
Step 3 39 is at the end. Its one neighbour, 12, is smaller. a supercell
Step 4 18, 27 and 12 each have a bigger neighbour. not supercells
Answer The supercells are 35, 41 and 39.
