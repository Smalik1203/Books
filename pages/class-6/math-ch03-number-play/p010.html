Here is what is known about the reverse-and-add question. Every 2-digit number reaches a palindrome in the end. For 3-digit numbers, nobody knows. Starting from 196, people have reversed and added a huge number of times without ever reaching a palindrome. But nobody has shown that it can never happen. Some 2-digit numbers take a long time: 89 needs 24 steps.

3.6The Magic Number of Kaprekar

D. R. Kaprekar was a mathematics teacher at a government school in Devlali, in Maharashtra. He loved playing with numbers, and he found many patterns in them that nobody had noticed before. In 1949 he found a surprising fact about 4-digit numbers.

Here are his steps. Pick any 4-digit number whose digits are not all the same, and follow Fig. 3.12. Doing the steps once, from A to C, is called one round. If C has fewer than 4 digits, write zeros in front of it: 558 is read as 0558. A is the largest number you can make from the four digits, and B is the smallest.
