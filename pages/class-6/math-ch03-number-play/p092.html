Assertion and reason

Choose (a) if A and R are both true and R is the correct explanation of A; (b) if A and R are both true but R is not the correct explanation of A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): In the row 5, 9, 2, the cell holding 9 is a supercell.

    Reason (R): A cell is a supercell if its number is bigger than the number in every cell next to it.

  1. Assertion (A): Every 4-digit number reaches 6174 by the Kaprekar steps.

    Reason (R): Since $7641 - 1467 = 6174$, one Kaprekar round on 6174 gives 6174 again.

  1. Assertion (A): 4554 is a palindrome.

    Reason (R): The digit sum of 4554 is 18.

  1. Assertion (A): The Collatz sequence that starts at 16 reaches 1 in 4 steps.

    Reason (R): Under the Collatz rule, an odd number always gives an odd number next.

  1. Assertion (A): The largest 3-digit number whose digit sum is 5 is 500.

    Reason (R): To make a number as large as possible, put the largest digits on the left.

Case-based questions

  1. From a train, Rekha sees a row of seven hills. From left to right, their heights in metres are 1240, 1510, 980, 1730, 1655, 2010 and 1120. The hills are numbered 1 to 7 in this order. A hill is a peak if it is higher than each hill next to it.

    Based on the above information, answer the following questions.

    1. Which hills are peaks?
    2. How much higher is the highest hill than the lowest?
    3. The third hill is measured again and found to be 1600 m high. Which hills are peaks now? Justify your answer.
