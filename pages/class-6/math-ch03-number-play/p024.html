Summary
  1. Numbers do many jobs. They give us information, make patterns, help us estimate, set and solve puzzles, and help us play and win games.
  2. A cell in a table is a supercell if its number is bigger than the number in every cell next to it. In the row 3, 8, 5, the 8 is a supercell.
  3. Along a number line, the numbers get bigger from left to right. When the marks are equally spaced, the numbers go up by the same amount from one mark to the next.
  4. The digit sum of a number is the total of its digits. Different numbers can have the same digit sum: 68, 176 and 545 all have the digit sum 14.
  5. A palindrome reads the same from left to right and from right to left, like 575 or 1111. Times and dates can be palindromes too, like 12:21 and 11/02/2011.
  6. The Kaprekar steps take every 4-digit number whose digits are not all the same to 6174, the Kaprekar constant. Following a fixed set of steps like this is called computational thinking.
  7. To add or subtract large numbers in your head, start from an easy number close to the answer. To find how many digits an answer can have, work out the smallest and the largest possible answers.
  8. The Collatz conjecture says that the Collatz rule takes every counting number to 1 in the end. Nobody knows yet whether it is true. Many problems about numbers are easy to state but very hard to solve.
  9. An estimate is a rough number that is close enough for what you need. Paromita estimated about 500 students in her school without counting them.
  10. A winning strategy is a way of playing a game that always wins, whatever the other player does.
