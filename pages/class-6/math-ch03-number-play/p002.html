Have you worked out what the numbers mean? Here is a clue: look at how tall each child is, and how tall the children beside them are.

The rule

Each child says how many of the children standing next to them are taller than they are. A child says 0 if neither neighbour is taller, 1 if one neighbour is taller, and 2 if both neighbours are taller.

Check the rule on Fig. 3.1. The first child stands at the end of the line, next to a shorter child, and says 0. The second child is shorter than both neighbours and says 2. Now check the other children in both lines.

Think and Reflect
  1. Can the tallest child in a line ever say 1 or 2? Why or why not?
  2. Can the shortest child ever say 0? What number can the shortest child say?
  3. Look at Fig. 3.2. Which children say 2? Must a child who says 2 be one of the shortest children in the whole line? Why or why not?
Exercise Set 3.1
  1. Can the children change places so that the two children at the ends of the line both say 2?
  1. Can the children stand in a line so that every child says 0?
  1. Can two children standing next to each other say the same number?
  1. Five children, all of different heights, stand in a line. Can they stand so that four of them say 1 and the last one says 0? Why or why not?
  1. For the same five children, is the sequence 1, 1, 1, 1, 1 possible?
  1. Is the sequence 0, 1, 2, 1, 0 possible? Why or why not?
