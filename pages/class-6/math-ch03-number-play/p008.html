Here is what is known about the reverse-and-add question. Every 2-digit number reaches a palindrome in the end. For 3-digit numbers, nobody knows. Starting from 196, people have reversed and added a huge number of times without ever reaching a palindrome. But nobody has shown that it can never happen.

3.6The Magic Number of Kaprekar

D. R. Kaprekar was a mathematics teacher at a government school in Devlali, in Maharashtra. He loved playing with numbers, and he found many beautiful patterns in numbers that nobody had noticed before. In 1949 he discovered something magical about 4-digit numbers.

Here are his steps. Pick any 4-digit number whose digits are not all the same, and follow Fig. 3.10. Doing the steps once, from A to C, is called one round. If C has fewer than 4 digits, write zeros in front of it: 558 is read as 0558.

Example 2

Start with 6382. Follow the Kaprekar steps.

Solution.

Step 1 Round 1: $A = 8632$, $B = 2368$, $C = 8632 - 2368 = 6264$
Step 2 Round 2: $A = 6642$, $B = 2466$, $C = 6642 - 2466 = 4176$ digits of the last C
Step 3 Round 3: $A = 7641$, $B = 1467$, $C = 7641 - 1467 = 6174$ digits of the last C
Answer 6382 reaches 6174 in 3 rounds.

The digits of 6174 give $A = 7641$ and $B = 1467$ again, so every round after this gives 6174 once more.

Now take some 4-digit numbers of your own, and follow the steps. What happens? Check with your friends what they got.
