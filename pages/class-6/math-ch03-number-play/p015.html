3.11Simple Estimation

Sometimes we do not know the exact number of things, and sometimes we do not need it. A rough idea of the number, called an estimate, is enough. Your headmaster knows exactly how many students are in your school, but you may only have an idea. Are there about 150 students? About 400? About a thousand?

Here is how one student made an estimate.

Example 4

Paromita’s section of Class 6 has 32 children. The other two sections of Class 6 have 29 and 35 children. Her school has Classes 6 to 10, and each class has 3 sections. About how many students are in her school?

Solution.

Step 1 Class 6 has $32 + 29 + 35 = 96$ children, which is about 100.
Step 2 Classes 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 make 5 classes.
Step 3 If each class has about 100 children, the school has about $5 \times 100 = 500$. classes about the same
Answer Paromita estimated about 500 students.

She did not count every student. She assumed that the other classes are about the same size as hers.

Now make some estimates of your own. You do not need exact numbers for these questions. It is fun, and you may be surprised by some of the numbers around you. Share how you made each estimate.
