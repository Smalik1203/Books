Type 7 · Clock and calendar numbers

Example 12

A clock shows 2:52, a time that reads the same from both ends. What is the next time that does, and how many minutes later is it?

Solution.

Step 1 before 3:00 the last digit must be 2, and after 2:52 that is 2:62, which is not a time minutes stop at 59
Step 2 in the next hour, the first such time is 3:03
Step 3 2:52 to 3:00 is 8 minutes, so 2:52 to 3:03 is $8 + 3 = 11$ minutes
Answer 3:03, which is 11 minutes later
Example 13

Today is 17/09/2026. Written as day/month/year, which is the next date that reads the same from both ends?

Solution. Without the slashes a date has 8 digits. It reads the same from both ends when its day and month, read together, are the year written backwards, as in Fig. 3.11.

Step 1 2026, 2027, 2028 and 2029 would need days 62, 72, 82 and 92 no such days
Step 2 2030 written backwards is 0302: day 03, month 02 a real date
Step 3 03022030 read from the right is 03022030 check
Answer 03/02/2030

Type 8 · Adding and subtracting large numbers

Example 14

Use 10,000, 2,500 and 600, as many times as you need, adding or subtracting, to make 26,900.

Solution.

Step 1 $10{,}000 + 10{,}000 = 20{,}000$ start close to it
Step 2 $20{,}000 + 2{,}500 + 2{,}500 + 2{,}500 = 27{,}500$
Step 3 $27{,}500 - 26{,}900 = 600$ 600 too big
Answer $26{,}900 = 10{,}000 + 10{,}000 + 2{,}500 + 2{,}500 + 2{,}500 - 600$
