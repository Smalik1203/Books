Type 2 · Supercells

Example 4

A grid has the rows 5, 9, 2 and 8, 1, 7 and 3, 6, 4. Cells on a slant are not neighbours. Which cells are supercells?

Solution.

Step 1 9 is bigger than 5, 2 and 1, and 8 is bigger than 5, 1 and 3 both are supercells
Step 2 7 is bigger than 2, 1 and 4, and 6 is bigger than 1, 3 and 4 both are supercells
Step 3 5, 2, 3, 4 and 1 each have a bigger neighbour none of these
Answer 9, 8, 7 and 6
Example 5

A row of four cells holds 42, an empty cell, 57 and 31. The empty cell is to hold a 2-digit number that is not already in the row. Which numbers make 57 a supercell, but not 42?

Solution.

Step 1 57 is already bigger than 31, so the empty cell must hold less than 57 57 must be bigger
Step 2 42 has only one neighbour, so the empty cell must hold more than 42 42 must be smaller
Answer 43, 44, 45, and so on up to 56: fourteen numbers

Type 3 · Numbers on a number line

Example 6

A number line has ten marks, spaced equally. The 3rd mark is labelled 7,080 and the 7th mark is labelled 7,120. What numbers go at the 1st mark and at the 10th mark?

Solution.

Step 1 from the 3rd mark to the 7th there are $7 - 3 = 4$ equal gaps count gaps, not marks
Step 2 the gaps cover $7{,}120 - 7{,}080 = 40$, so each gap is $40 \div 4 = 10$
Step 3 the 1st mark is two gaps before the 3rd: $7{,}080 - 10 - 10 = 7{,}060$
Step 4 the 10th mark is three gaps after the 7th: $7{,}120 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 7{,}150$
Answer 7,060 at the 1st mark and 7,150 at the 10th
