Problem 3

A 12-hour clock shows 9:59. The number of minutes until it next shows a palindromic time is

  1. 2
  2. 11
  3. 72
  4. 142
Solution

Read each time without its colon, and check whether it reads the same from both ends.

10:00 reads 1000, and 10:01 reads 1001, which is a palindrome.

From 9:59 to 10:01 is 2 minutes.

The answer is 2, option (a). Option (b) is 10:10, but 1010 read from the right is 0101, so it is not a palindrome. Option (c) is 11:11 and option (d) is 12:21, which are the palindromic times after 10:01.

Problem 4

Starting from 6, the number of steps the Collatz rule takes to reach 1 is

  1. 6
  2. 8
  3. 9
  4. never
Solution

Apply the rule one step at a time, and count the steps, not the numbers.

$6 \to 3 \to 10 \to 5 \to 16 \to 8 \to 4 \to 2 \to 1$

9 numbers in the sequence, so 8 steps between them

The answer is 8, option (b). Option (c) counts the numbers in the sequence instead of the steps between them. Option (a) misses the two odd steps, from 3 to 10 and from 5 to 16.

Problem 5

Anil adds a 4-digit number and a 3-digit number. His answer could have

  1. 3 digits
  2. 4 or 5 digits
  3. only 4 digits
  4. 6 digits
Solution

Find the smallest and the largest answers that are possible.

smallest: $1000 + 100 = 1100$, which has 4 digits

largest: $9999 + 999 = 10{,}998$, which has 5 digits

So the answer can have 4 digits or 5 digits, option (b). Option (c) forgets that a large sum can carry into a fifth digit. Options (a) and (d) are ruled out by the smallest and largest sums.

In Problem 5 we did not try any ordinary sums. We looked only at the smallest and the largest sums, because every other sum lies between them.
