The Kaprekar constant

Start with any 4-digit number whose digits are not all the same, and repeat the steps. You always reach 6174. This number is called the Kaprekar constant.

What about 3-digit numbers? Follow the same steps with a few 3-digit numbers, such as 321. Which number starts to repeat? Keep three digits all the way: 99 is read as 099.

3.7Clock and Calendar Numbers

Some times on a 12-hour clock make pretty patterns, such as 4:44, 10:10 and 12:21. In 4:44 every digit is the same. In 10:10 the hours and the minutes are the same. And 12:21 reads the same from both ends.

Manish was born on 20/12/2012. Look at the digits of his date of birth: 2, 0, 1, 2, and then 2, 0, 1, 2 again. His sister Meghana was born on 11/02/2011. Her date of birth reads the same from left to right and from right to left, just as the time 12:21 does. Fig. 3.13 shows how the digits pair up.

Jeevan was looking at this year’s calendar. He wondered why people buy a new calendar every year. Could an old calendar be used again? Last year’s calendar was different from this year’s, and next year’s will be different again. But will a year’s calendar ever come back, with every date falling on the same day of the week?
