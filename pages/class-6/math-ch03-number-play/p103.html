Solved Examples

Type 1 · Taller neighbours

Example 1

Six children stand in a line. Their heights, from left to right, are 120 cm, 135 cm, 128 cm, 140 cm, 132 cm and 125 cm. What number does each child say?

Solution.

Step 1 120 stands next to 135 only, and 125 next to 132 only, so both say 1 one neighbour each
Step 2 135 is taller than 120 and 128, and 140 is taller than 128 and 132, so both say 0
Step 3 128 is shorter than 135 and 140, so it says 2
Step 4 132 is shorter than 140 and taller than 125, so it says 1
Answer 1, 0, 2, 0, 1, 1
Example 2

Eight children of different heights stand in a line. Without knowing their heights, find the total of the numbers they say.

Solution.

Step 1 in any two neighbours, the shorter one counts the taller one, and the taller one counts nothing one pair at a time
Step 2 so each pair of neighbours adds exactly 1 to the total
Step 3 8 children in a line make $8 - 1 = 7$ pairs of neighbours
Answer 7

Check the rule on the example above. Six children make 5 pairs, and $1 + 0 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 1 = 5$.

Example 3

Five children stand in a line and say 0, 2, 0, 2, 0. Which of them could be the tallest? Which could be the shortest?

Solution.

Step 1 the tallest child has no taller neighbour, so says 0 nobody is taller
Step 2 the shortest child says 2, or 1 at an end, and nobody says 1 every neighbour is taller
Answer the 1st, 3rd or 5th child could be the tallest, and the 2nd or 4th the shortest
