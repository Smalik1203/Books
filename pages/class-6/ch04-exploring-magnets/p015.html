Notice that all three solve the same problem in the same way. A magnet is allowed to turn freely, and then it is left alone to find its own direction. The cork provides floating support; a pin provides a pivot. Liquid slows turning. The dial labels directions. These parts do different jobs.

Compare the two designs by asking the same questions: what is magnetised, what supports it, and what prevents it from turning freely? These questions explain why a floating needle fails if its cork touches the side of the bowl.

4.4 Pushing and Pulling

So far, magnets have pulled things towards them. Now let us see what a magnet does to another magnet.

Investigate

You will need: two bar magnets with their poles marked, five or six round pencils, a flat table.

1.

Call the magnets A and B.

2.

Lay the round pencils side by side on the table. Rest magnet A across them, so it can roll easily.

3.

Hold magnet B in your hand. Slowly bring one end of it near one end of magnet A. Do not let them touch.

4.

Watch magnet A carefully. Which way does it move?

5.

Now turn magnet B around. Bring its other end near the same end of magnet A.

6.

Watch again.
