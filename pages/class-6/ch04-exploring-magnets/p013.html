To read it, turn the whole compass box gently — not the needle — until the marked north-seeking end points to N on the dial. Matching only the needle's line could put the dial backwards. The dial now gives magnetic directions; these can differ from geographic directions.

This small device changed the world. With a compass, a ship could hold its course through cloud, fog and night. It supplied a direction reference; sailors still needed information about their position and planned route.

The compass needle turns; the letters printed on the dial do not. Turning the case brings those letters into line with the settled needle. If you carry the compass to another place or turn yourself around, let the needle settle and check the dial again.

A compass shows direction relative to the local magnetic field. Accurate map navigation also allows for the difference between magnetic north and geographic north.

Keep the compass level while reading it. A tilted needle may catch against its support or case. If it seems stuck, put the compass on a flat surface and wait before trusting the direction it shows.
