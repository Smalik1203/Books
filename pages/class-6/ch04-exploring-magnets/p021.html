Test both ends. If the first end attracts, turn the test bar around and try its other end against the same pole of your known magnet. Repulsion in either trial identifies a magnet. This is why a single observation of attraction is not enough.

A Magnet Near a Compass

A compass needle is a magnet too. So it should follow the same rules.

Activity 4.6

You will need: a magnetic compass, a bar magnet with poles marked.

1.

Put the compass on a flat table, away from anything made of iron or steel.

2.

Wait for the needle to settle along the north-south line.

3.

Mark a position diagonally beside the north-seeking end, clear of the needle. Slowly approach that position with the North pole, keeping the magnet horizontal.

4.

Watch the needle. Does it turn? Which way?

5.

Move the magnet away. Let the needle settle again.

6.

Reverse the bar magnet and approach the same marked position with its South pole. Keep the distance and height the same.

7.

Watch again.

Think It Through

Sketch the needle direction in each trial. Did reversing the pole change its direction? Did it return when the magnet was removed? Does this match what you found in the investigation with two bar magnets?
