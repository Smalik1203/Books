In these school tests, magnetic materials are materials, such as iron, that respond strongly enough to a magnet for us to notice the attraction. They need not already be permanent magnets.
Iron is a magnetic material. Steel is made mostly of iron. Many kinds of steel are magnetic, though some stainless steels are not. That is why a steel paper clip sticks. Two other metals, nickel and cobalt, are also magnetic. So are some mixtures of these metals with others. 		We call materials with no noticeable response in these tests non-magnetic materials. This does not mean that every magnetic effect on them is exactly zero. Wood, rubber, plastic, glass, paper and cloth are all non-magnetic.

Here is something worth noticing. Not every metal is magnetic. Try a magnet on an aluminium vessel or a copper wire. Neither will stick. So "metal" and "magnetic" do not mean the same thing.
