Examples from the same school test
Magnetic materialsNon-magnetic materials
  • Iron is a magnetic material.
  • Steel is made mostly of iron.
  • Many kinds of steel are magnetic, though some stainless steels are not.
  • That is why a steel paper clip sticks.
  • Two other metals, nickel and cobalt, are also magnetic.
  • So are some mixtures of these metals with others.
  • Wood, rubber, plastic, glass, paper and cloth are all non-magnetic.

Here is something worth noticing. Not every metal is magnetic. Try a magnet on an aluminium vessel or a copper wire. Neither will stick. So "metal" and "magnetic" do not mean the same thing.

Think It Through

A plastic-coated paper clip moves towards a magnet, but a plastic ruler does not. Does this show that some plastic is magnetic? Give another explanation and suggest a test that could distinguish between the two explanations.

Coins and keys can give different results because they are made from different metals or mixtures. Test your own examples rather than guessing from their colour. A shiny surface does not tell you whether the material underneath is magnetic.

Compare two objects using the same magnet at the same distance. If you change both the object and the magnet, you cannot tell which change caused a different result.
