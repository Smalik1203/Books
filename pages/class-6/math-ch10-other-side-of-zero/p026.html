Brahmagupta was the first to treat zero as a number just like the positive and negative numbers, and the first to give rules for working with all of them. This changed the way the world does mathematics.

It still took many centuries for the rest of the world to accept zero and negative numbers. They reached the Arab world by the 9th century, and Europe by the 13th century. Even in the 18th century, many mathematicians in Europe did not accept negative numbers. The French mathematician Lazare Carnot called them absurd. Today they are used everywhere in mathematics and science, just as Brahmagupta described in 628 CE. His rules also opened the way to algebra, which you will learn about in later classes.
