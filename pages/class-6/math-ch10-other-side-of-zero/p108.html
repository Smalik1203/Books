B3 Statement (iii) can fail: $(-2) - (-5) = (-2) + (+5) = +3$, which is positive. Statements (i) and (ii) are always true, because two movements down stay down, and a positive number minus a negative number is a positive number plus a positive number. So the answer is (b).
B5 The top row must add to the border sum: $4 + {?} + (-6) = -5$. Since $4 + (-6) = -2$, the missing number is $(-5) - (-2) = -3$, option (a). Option (c) works out $(-5) - 4 - 6$, reading the corner $-6$ as $+6$. Option (d) works out $(-5) + 4 - 6$, adding the corner 4 instead of taking it away.
C1 For every negative integer there is a smaller one: $-100$ is greater than $-101$, and $-1000$ is greater than $-1001$. The negative numbers go down without end, so there is no smallest, and (c) is false. The other three statements are all true.
C3 Each number is 4 more than the one before it. Counting on from $-17$ gives $-13, -9, -5, -1, 3, 7, 11$, so the 8th number is 11, option (d). Option (a) is the 7th number, one step short.
C4 Pair $-30$ with $30$, $-29$ with $29$, and so on. Every pair adds to 0. Only 31 and 32 have no partner, so the sum is $31 + 32 = 63$, option (a). Option (b) forgets the two numbers without a partner.
C5 The smallest sum is $(-2) + (-2) = -4$ and the largest is $3 + 3 = 6$. Every integer from $-4$ to $6$ can be rolled, which is 11 sums, option (c). Option (a) counts the 36 ways the two dice can fall, and many of those give the same sum. Option (b) forgets to count 0.

Most of these questions needed very little working. What they needed was a picture of the integers: a lift moving up and down, a walk along the number line, or tokens cancelling in zero pairs. When a sign seems to go the wrong way, go back to one of these pictures and let it decide.
