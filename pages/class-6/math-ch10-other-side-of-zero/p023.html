What this chapter showed
  1. There are numbers less than zero. They are written with a $-$ sign in front, such as $-2$, and are called negative numbers. On the number line they lie to the left of 0.
  2. The numbers $\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ are the integers. The numbers $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ are the positive integers, and $-1, -2, -3, \ldots$ are the negative integers. Zero is neither positive nor negative.
  3. Every integer has an inverse, and a number added to its inverse gives 0. The inverse of 7 is $-7$, and the inverse of $-543$ is 543.
  4. Addition can be read as Starting Position $+$ Movement $=$ Target Position, or as Movement 1 $+$ Movement 2 $=$ Total Movement.
  5. Subtraction can be read as Target Position $-$ Starting Position $=$ Movement needed. Subtracting an integer is the same as adding its inverse.
  6. A positive token and a negative token make a zero pair, worth 0. Tokens can show any addition or subtraction of integers.
  7. Integers can be compared: $\ldots \lt -2 \lt -1 \lt 0 \lt 1 \lt 2 \lt \ldots$ A smaller number is to the left of a larger one on the number line.
  8. Integers describe credits and debits in a bank account, heights above and below sea level, and temperatures above and below the freezing point of water.
  9. Brahmagupta gave the rules for adding and subtracting positive numbers, negative numbers and zero in 628 CE.

When a sign puzzles you, picture the lift. $+$ goes up and $-$ goes down, and a subtraction asks which button takes you from the start to the target.
