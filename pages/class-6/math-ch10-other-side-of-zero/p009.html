Subtracting a negative number

$(+2000) - (-200)$ and $(+2000) + (+200)$ give the same answer. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the matching positive number.

Back to the number line

The infinite lift looks very like a number line. Turn it a quarter turn, so that up becomes right, and it is a number line. This also answers the question from the start of the chapter. To make the number ray into a whole line, put the negative numbers $-1, -2, -3, \ldots$ to the left of 0.

We usually drop the $+$ sign on positive numbers, and write them simply as $1, 2, 3, \ldots$

Instead of riding a lift, you can imagine walking along the number line. Walking to the right is the positive, or forward, direction. Walking to the left is the negative, or backward, direction. Smaller numbers are to the left of bigger numbers. So $2 \lt 5$, $-3 \lt 2$ and $-5 \lt -3$.

Look at the jumps in Fig. 10.5. To go from 5 to 9, you walk 4 steps forward. That is why $5 + 4 = 9$, and the matching subtraction is $9 - 5 = 4$.

To go from 9 to 3, you walk 6 steps backward, which is a movement of $-6$. So $9 + (-6) = 3$, and the matching subtraction is $3 - 9 = -6$.
