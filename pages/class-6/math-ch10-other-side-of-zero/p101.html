Using What You Know

The chapter gave you three ways to think about integers: a lift moving between floors, a walk along the number line, and tokens that cancel in zero pairs. The questions below use only those ideas, but none of them tells you which one to use. Try each question before you read what follows it.

A lift starts on Floor $+3$. It goes down 7 floors, and then up 2 floors. Without drawing the building, find where it stops.

You can take the two movements together first. Going down 7 and then up 2 is a total movement of $(-7) + (+2) = -5$. Then Starting Floor $+$ Movement $=$ Target Floor gives $(+3) + (-5) = -2$. The lift stops on Floor $-2$. We did not need to know the floors it passed on the way, only the total movement.

At noon in Shimla, the temperature was 6 °C. By midnight it had fallen by 9 degrees. Was the temperature at midnight above or below 0 °C, and by how much?

A fall of 9 degrees is a movement of $-9$. Split it at zero: the first 6 degrees of the fall bring the temperature down to 0 °C, and 3 degrees of the fall are still left. So the temperature at midnight was $6 + (-9) = -3$ °C, which is 3 degrees below freezing. Splitting a movement at zero is the second way from Example 2 in the chapter, used on a thermometer instead of a number line.

Without working out either one, which is larger: $(-47) - (-19)$ or $(-47) + (+19)$?

Neither is larger. Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding the matching positive number, so the two expressions are equal. Both come to $-28$. The rule answered the question before any working was done.

Find the sum of all the integers from $-20$ to $21$.

Pair each number with its inverse: $-20$ with $20$, $-19$ with $19$, and so on down to $-1$ with $1$. Each pair adds to 0, just like a zero pair of tokens, and 0 adds nothing. The only number without a partner is 21. So the sum is 21. Adding 42 numbers took no adding at all.
