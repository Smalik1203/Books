Summary
  1. There are numbers less than 0. They are written with a $-$ sign in front, as in $-2$, and are called negative numbers. On the number line they lie to the left of 0.
  2. The numbers $\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$ are called integers. The numbers $1, 2, 3, \ldots$ are the positive integers, and $\ldots, -3, -2, -1$ are the negative integers. Zero is neither positive nor negative.
  3. Every integer has an inverse, also called its additive inverse. A number added to its inverse gives 0. The inverse of 7 is $-7$, and the inverse of $-543$ is 543.
  4. Addition can mean Starting Position $+$ Movement $=$ Target Position. It can also mean Movement 1 $+$ Movement 2 $=$ Total Movement: $(+2) + (-3) = -1$.
  5. Subtraction can mean Target Position $-$ Starting Position $=$ Movement needed: $(+5) - (+2) = +3$.
  6. To subtract an integer, add its inverse instead: $5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8$.
  7. Brahmagupta’s rules add any two integers. Two positive numbers give a positive sum. For two negative numbers, add them without their signs and put a minus sign in front. For one of each, take the smaller from the larger, both without signs, and give the answer the sign of the larger: $(-5) + 3 = -2$.
  8. Integers can be compared: $\ldots \lt -3 \lt -2 \lt -1 \lt 0 \lt 1 \lt 2 \lt 3 \lt \ldots$ A smaller number lies to the left of a larger one on the number line.
  9. With tokens, a positive token and a negative token make a zero pair, which is worth 0.
  10. Integers describe many things. Credits are positive and debits are negative. Heights above sea level and temperatures above 0 °C are positive, and those below are negative.
