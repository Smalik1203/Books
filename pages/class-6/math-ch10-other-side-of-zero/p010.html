To go from 3 to $-2$, you walk 5 steps backward, a movement of $-5$. So $3 + (-5) = -2$, and $-2 - 3 = -5$. Each time, Starting Number $+$ Movement $=$ Target Number, and Target Number $-$ Starting Number $=$ Movement needed.

Try one more walk, from $-4$ to $2$, in Fig. 10.6. You walk 4 steps forward to reach 0, and 2 more steps to reach 2. That is 6 steps forward in all, so $-4 + 6 = 2$, and the matching subtraction is $2 - (-4) = 6$. This time the movement needed is positive, because the target is to the right of the start. When the target is to the left of the start, as in the last two walks, the movement needed is negative.

So on the number line, the sign of a movement tells you which way to walk.
