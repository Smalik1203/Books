The Other Side of Zero

The first numbers you learned were the counting numbers $1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots$ Then you met the number 0. It stands for nothing, and it comes before 1. The number 0 has a long history in India. Today the whole world writes numbers with the ten digits 0 to 9, and with them you can write any number, however large.

After that you met numbers that sit between the whole numbers, such as $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{3}{2}$ and $\frac{13}{6}$. These are fractions.

Are there still more numbers? The number 0 comes before 1 and is less than 1. Could there be numbers that come before 0, and are less than 0?

Look at the number line you have used so far, in Fig. 10.1. It starts at 0 and goes on for ever to the right. In the language of geometry, it is a ray and not a line. Are there numbers to the left of 0 that would make it a whole line? That is the question of this chapter.
