Adding and subtracting larger numbers

Fig. 10.4 shows a mine, a place where people dig into rock for minerals. A truck waits at ground level. Minerals are found above the ground and below it, and a fast lift runs up and down a shaft to carry people and rock. It can stop at any of the levels on the way.

Some of the levels are marked. Ground level is 0. Levels above the ground are marked with positive numbers and levels below it with negative numbers. Each number tells you how many metres above or below the ground that level is.

The lift in the mine works just like the lift in the Building of Fun.

Starting Level $+$ Movement $=$ Target Level:

$(+40) + (+60) = +100$   and   $(-90) + (-55) = -145$

Target Level $-$ Starting Level $=$ Movement needed:

$(+40) - (-50) = +90$   and   $(-90) - (+40) = -130$

The first addition is a trip up inside the hill, and the second is a trip down into the mine.

Integers

The Building of Fun has floors from $-5$ to $+6$, and the mine has levels from $-200$ to $+180$. But you can imagine a taller building or a deeper mine. The positive numbers $+1, +2, +3, \ldots$ go up without end, and the negative numbers $-1, -2, -3, \ldots$ go down without end.

The positive numbers, the negative numbers and zero together are called integers: $\ldots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$
