1. Points $P$ and $Q$ are 8 cm apart. Construct the two points that are 5 cm from both $P$ and $Q$, and write your steps. Explain why each of them is 5 cm from both. Measure how far apart the two points are, and how far each is from the middle of $PQ$.
  1. Construct a rectangle $PQRS$ with $PQ = 6$ cm and a diagonal $PR = 10$ cm. Write the steps of construction, and explain how the compass finds $R$. Measure $QR$.
  1. Construct a square $ABCD$ with sides of 5 cm. With the needle on $A$, draw the curve from $B$ to $D$. With the needle on $C$, draw the curve from $B$ to $D$ again. Write your steps. Draw the diagonal $AC$ and measure it. Hence find how far apart the two curves are along $AC$.
  1. Three squares are placed as falling squares, each meeting the next only at a corner. The first has sides of 6 cm, and each square after it has sides 2 cm shorter than the one before. Construct the figure and write your steps. Find its width and height, and the side of the smallest square that the whole figure fits inside.
  1. Draw a circle with centre $O$ and a radius of 4 cm. Mark a point $P$ on it. Keep the compass open at 4 cm and draw a circle with centre $P$. The two circles cross at $A$ and $B$. Write your steps. Find $OA$, $PA$, $OB$ and $PB$, and explain why each has that length.
Assertion and reason

Choose (a) if A and R are both true and R is the correct explanation of A; (b) if A and R are both true but R is not the correct explanation of A; (c) if A is true but R is false; (d) if A is false but R is true.

  1. Assertion (A): A point that is 3 cm from $B$ and 3 cm from $C$ is equidistant from these two points.

    Reason (R): A point is equidistant from two points when it is the same distance from each of them.

  1. Assertion (A): A rectangle with sides of 6 cm and 4 cm can have a diagonal of 3 cm.

    Reason (R): A diagonal of a rectangle is longer than each of its sides.

  1. Assertion (A): A square with sides of 4 cm, turned on the page, still has sides of 4 cm.

    Reason (R): Turning a figure changes the sizes of its angles.

  1. Assertion (A): In rectangle $PQRS$, $PQ = 7$ cm, so $RS = 7$ cm.

    Reason (R): All the angles of a rectangle are $90^\circ$.

  1. Assertion (A): A compass opened to 5 cm draws a circle of radius 5 cm.

    Reason (R): Every point that the pencil draws is 5 cm from the needle.
