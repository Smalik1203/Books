Problem 4

Two points $P$ and $Q$ are 8 cm apart. A circle of radius 5 cm is drawn about $P$, and another about $Q$. How many points lie on both circles?

  1. 0
  2. 1
  3. 2
  4. 4
Solution

Compare the distance between the centres with the two radii added together.

$5 + 5 = 10$ cm, which is more than 8 cm, so the circles overlap

overlapping circles cross twice: once on each side of $PQ$

The answer is 2, option (c). Option (a) would be right if the centres were more than 10 cm apart, and option (b) if they were exactly 10 cm apart. Two different circles cross at most twice, so option (d) cannot happen.

This is the same idea that found the top of the roof in the house. There, the two circles were about $B$ and $C$, and $A$ was the crossing point above the walls. The other crossing point, below $BC$, was not needed.

Problem 5

Rectangle $ABCD$ has $AB = 7$ cm and $BC = 4$ cm. $X$ is on $AD$ and $Y$ is on $BC$, with $AX = BY = 1.5$ cm. Which of these is true?

  1. $XY = 7$ cm, and $ABYX$ is a rectangle
  2. $XY = 5.5$ cm, and $ABYX$ is a rectangle
  3. $XY = 7$ cm, and $ABYX$ is a square
  4. $XY = 8.5$ cm, and $ABYX$ is a rectangle
Solution

This is one of the cases in Table 8.2: $X$ and $Y$ are the same distance from $A$ and from $B$.

$AX = BY = 1.5$ cm

$ABYX$ is a rectangle with sides 7 cm and 1.5 cm

$XY$ is the side opposite $AB$, so $XY = AB = 7$ cm

The answer is option (a). Option (b) takes 1.5 cm away from 7 cm, and option (d) adds it on. Option (c) has the right length, but a square needs all four sides equal, and $ABYX$ has sides of 7 cm and 1.5 cm.
