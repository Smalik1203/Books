Example 2

Draw a circle with centre $O$ and radius 4 cm, and mark a point $A$ on it. Keep the compass open at 4 cm, put the needle on $A$, and draw an arc that cuts the circle at $B$. How long are $OA$, $OB$ and $AB$?

Solution.

Step 1 $OA = 4$ cm $A$ is on the circle
Step 2 $OB = 4$ cm $B$ is on the circle
Step 3 $AB = 4$ cm the arc about $A$
Answer all three are 4 cm

Nothing was measured after the compass was set. The three lines are equal because each one is a radius of a circle drawn with the same opening.

Type 2 · What makes a rectangle

Example 3

A rectangle has the corners $W$, $X$, $Y$ and $Z$, in that order round it. Which of these are names for it: $XYZW$, $WYXZ$, $ZYXW$, $YZXW$?

Solution.

Step 1 in a name, each corner is next to the one before it travel round
Step 2 $XYZW$: $X$, $Y$, $Z$, $W$ in order a name
Step 3 $WYXZ$: $W$ and $Y$ are opposite corners not a name
Step 4 $ZYXW$: the same order, the other way round a name
Step 5 $YZXW$: $Z$ and $X$ are opposite corners not a name
Answer $XYZW$ and $ZYXW$
Example 4

A 4-sided figure $ABCD$ has $AB = CD = 5$ cm and $BC = DA = 3$ cm. Its angle at $A$ is $70^\circ$. Is it a rectangle?

Solution.

Step 1 R1: its opposite sides are equal 5 and 5, 3 and 3
Step 2 R2: $\angle A = 70^\circ$, not $90^\circ$ R2 fails
Answer No. A rectangle needs both R1 and R2

Equal opposite sides alone do not make a rectangle. The figure can lean over, like a pushed door frame, and keep all four lengths.
