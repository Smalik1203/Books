Example 2

Draw a circle with centre $O$ and radius 4 cm, and mark a point $A$ on it. Keep the compass open at 4 cm, put the needle on $A$, and draw an arc that cuts the circle at $B$. How long are $OA$, $OB$ and $AB$?

Solution.

Step 1 $OA = 4$ cm $A$ is on the circle
Step 2 $OB = 4$ cm $B$ is on the circle
Step 3 $AB = 4$ cm the arc about $A$
Answer all three are 4 cm

Nothing was measured after the compass was set. The three lines are equal because each one is a radius of a circle drawn with the same opening.

Example 3

A moon is drawn with two circles of radius 3 cm, as in Fig. 8.35. The first has centre $O$. The second has centre $P$, 2 cm from $O$. The moon is the part of the first circle that is outside the second. How wide is the moon along the line through $P$ and $O$?

Solution.

Step 1 on the far side from $P$, the first circle ends 3 cm from $O$ its radius
Step 2 the second ends 3 cm from $P$, so $3 - 2 = 1$ cm past $O$ $P$ is 2 cm away
Step 3 the moon lies between them: $3 - 1 = 2$ cm
Answer 2 cm, the same as the distance from $O$ to $P$

The tips of the moon are the two points where the circles cross, one on each side of the line.
