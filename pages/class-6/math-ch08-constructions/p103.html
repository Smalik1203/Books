Problem 2

A rectangle has the corners $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$, in that order round it. Which of these is not a name for the rectangle?

  1. $ABCD$
  2. $CDAB$
  3. $ACBD$
  4. $DCBA$
Solution

Travel round the rectangle, and check that each letter in the name is a corner next to the one before it.

$ABCD$: $A$ to $B$ to $C$ to $D$, in order, so it is a name

$CDAB$: the same order, starting at $C$, so it is a name

$ACBD$: $A$ and $C$ are not next to each other, so it is not a name

$DCBA$: the same order the other way round, so it is a name

The answer is option (c). The line from $A$ to $C$ is a diagonal, not a side. Options (a), (b) and (d) all follow the corners in order, starting at different corners or going the other way round.

Problem 3

A rectangle has one side of 6 cm. One of its diagonals splits the opposite angles into $45^\circ$ and $45^\circ$. The other side of the rectangle is

  1. 3 cm
  2. 6 cm
  3. 12 cm
  4. impossible to find
Solution

Construct the rectangle in the way Section 8.5 showed, and measure.

draw $AB = 6$ cm, and a perpendicular to $AB$ through $B$

draw a line from $A$ at $45^\circ$ to $AB$; it meets the perpendicular at $C$

measure $BC$: it is 6 cm

So the other side is 6 cm, option (b), and the rectangle is a square. Options (a) and (c) halve and double the side, and neither gives a $45^\circ$ split. Option (d) is wrong because the angle fixes where $C$ is, so $BC$ can be found.

This matches what you explored in Section 8.5. A diagonal splits the opposite angles into two equal parts when all four sides are equal, that is, when the rectangle is a square.

You can also see why without measuring. Fold the rectangle along the diagonal $AC$. The two $45^\circ$ angles at $A$ lie on top of each other, so $AB$ falls along $AD$. In the same way, $CB$ falls along $CD$. So $B$ lands where $AD$ and $CD$ meet, which is at $D$, and $AB = AD$.
