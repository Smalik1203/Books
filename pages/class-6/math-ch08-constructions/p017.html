A rectangle from a side and a diagonal

So far you have constructed rectangles from their sides, or from the angles at a diagonal. What can you do if you know one side and one diagonal? Construct a rectangle with one side of 5 cm and a diagonal of 7 cm.

Draw a rough diagram first, as in Fig. 8.31. Which line can you draw first?

Step 1. Draw the side $DC$, 5 cm long.

Step 2. Draw a perpendicular to $DC$ through $C$, and call it $l$. The corner $B$ is somewhere on $l$. What else do you know about $B$? It is 7 cm from $D$.

You could slide a ruler around until you find a point on $l$ that is 7 cm from $D$. But that takes trial and error. Here is a better way. Instead of hunting for one point that is 7 cm from $D$, draw all the points that are 7 cm from $D$ at once. You already know what shape they make.

Step 3, Method 1. Draw a circle of radius 7 cm with centre $D$, as in Fig. 8.32. Where can $B$ be? It is 7 cm from $D$, so it is on the circle. It is also on the line $l$. Look at the point where the circle crosses $l$. How far is that point from $D$? Check it in your drawing.

Step 3, Method 2. Did you need the whole circle? Only the part near $l$ matters. So a short arc of radius 7 cm about $D$, drawn across $l$, is enough to mark $B$.
