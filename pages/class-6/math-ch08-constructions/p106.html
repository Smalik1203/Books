Example 11

Can a diagonal of a rectangle split the opposite angles into $50^\circ$ and $50^\circ$?

Solution.

Step 1 the two parts together make the whole corner
Step 2 each corner of a rectangle is $90^\circ$ property R2
Step 3 $50^\circ + 50^\circ = 100^\circ$, not $90^\circ$
Answer No. The two parts must always add up to $90^\circ$
Example 12

Construct a rectangle $ABCD$ with $DC = 6$ cm and a diagonal $DB = 10$ cm. Measure the side $BC$.

Solution.

Step 1 draw $DC = 6$ cm, and the perpendicular $l$ to $DC$ through $C$ $B$ is on $l$
Step 2 arc of radius 10 cm about $D$ cuts $l$ at $B$ $B$ is 10 cm from $D$
Step 3 perpendiculars through $D$ and through $B$ meet at $A$
Answer $BC$ measures 8 cm
Example 13

Riya tries to construct a rectangle with a side of 7 cm and a diagonal of 5 cm. She draws the side $DC$ and the perpendicular $l$ through $C$, but her arc about $D$ never reaches $l$. Why?

Solution.

Step 1 every point of $l$ is at least as far from $D$ as $C$ is $C$ is the nearest
Step 2 $C$ is 7 cm from $D$
Step 3 the arc holds points only 5 cm from $D$, and $5 \lt 7$
Answer No such rectangle exists: a diagonal must be longer than the side

Try it with the arc: open the compass to 5 cm and it stops short of $l$ however it is turned.
