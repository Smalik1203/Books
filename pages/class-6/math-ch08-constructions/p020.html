Did you need two whole circles to find $A$? Only the parts near $A$ were used. Short arcs are quicker to draw, and they keep the drawing neat.

Step 3, Method 2. Draw a short arc of radius 5 cm about $B$, and another about $C$. They cross at $A$. Join $A$ to $B$, and $A$ to $C$. These two lines are the sides of the roof.

Step 4. Only the curve is left. $A$ is 5 cm from both $B$ and $C$. So open the compass to 5 cm, put the needle on $A$, and draw the curve from $B$ to $C$. The house is ready. Check it: measure every line round its border.

Finding a point from two distances

To find a point at a given distance from each of two points, draw an arc of that radius about each point. Where the two arcs cross, the point is at the right distance from both.

Look again at Step 3, Method 1. The two circles cross at a second point, below $BC$. That point is also 5 cm from $B$ and 5 cm from $C$. Measure its distance from each of them in your drawing to check. A roof drawn up to it would point down into the house, so the upper point is the one you want. So there are exactly two points that are 5 cm from both $B$ and $C$.

Two circles that cross
  • Two circles that cross always cross at exactly two points.
  • The two points lie on opposite sides of the line joining the centres.
