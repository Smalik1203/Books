Can you finish the figure? Try it. You need a point $A$ that is 5 cm from $B$ and also 5 cm from $C$. A point that is the same distance from two points is called equidistant from them. You could find $A$ with a ruler, by trial and error, but there is a better way. Does this problem remind you of how you found $B$ in the last construction?

Step 2. Draw a circle of radius 5 cm with centre $B$. Every point on it is 5 cm from $B$. Does this help you to find $A$? You need the point on this circle that is also 5 cm from $C$. You could hunt for it with a ruler. Can a compass find it instead?

Step 3, Method 1. Draw a circle of radius 5 cm with centre $C$. Look at the points where the two circles cross. How far is the upper one from $B$? How far is it from $C$? That point is $A$.

Did you need two whole circles to find $A$? Only the parts near $A$ were used.

Step 3, Method 2. Draw a short arc of radius 5 cm about $B$, and another about $C$. They cross at $A$. Join $A$ to $B$, and $A$ to $C$.

Step 4. Only the curve is left. $A$ is 5 cm from both $B$ and $C$. So open the compass to 5 cm, put the needle on $A$, and draw the curve from $B$ to $C$. The house is ready.
