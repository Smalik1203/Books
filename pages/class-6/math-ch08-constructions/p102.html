Squares with sides of 7 cm, 5 cm and 3 cm are placed like the falling squares, each one meeting the next only at a corner. How wide is the whole figure, and how tall is it?

Going from left to right, each square starts where the one before it ends. So the widths add up: $7 + 5 + 3 = 15$ cm. Going upwards, each square starts where the one below it ends, so the heights add up too: $7 + 5 + 3 = 15$ cm. The whole figure fits exactly inside a square with sides of 15 cm. Three squares of side 4 cm, placed the same way, fit inside a square with sides of 12 cm.

The tops of the two walls of a house are 6 cm apart. Can you build a pointed roof from two lines of 3 cm each? What about two lines of 4 cm each?

The top of the roof must be 3 cm from the top of each wall. So draw an arc of radius 3 cm about each wall top. The wall tops are 6 cm apart, and $3 + 3 = 6$, so the two arcs only touch, at a point on the line between the wall tops. The roof would lie flat, with no point. With lines of 4 cm, $4 + 4 = 8$ is more than 6. So the arcs cross above the line, and you get a pointed roof. The two roof lines together must be longer than the gap between the walls.

None of these questions needed a new tool. Each one needed you to see what it was really asking for: equal parts of a length, all the points at one distance from a point, or the place where two sets of such points meet. When a construction looks hard, ask which of these it is really about.

Type 1 · The circle and the compass

Example 1

A circle has centre $O$ and radius 3 cm. The points $A$, $B$ and $C$ are 2 cm, 3 cm and 5 cm from $O$. Which point is inside the circle, which is on it, and which is outside it?

Solution.

Step 1 every point on the circle is 3 cm from $O$ 3 cm is the radius
Step 2 $A$ is 2 cm from $O$, less than 3 cm nearer than the circle
Step 3 $B$ is 3 cm from $O$, the same as the radius
Step 4 $C$ is 5 cm from $O$, more than 3 cm farther than the circle
Answer $A$ is inside, $B$ is on the circle, and $C$ is outside
