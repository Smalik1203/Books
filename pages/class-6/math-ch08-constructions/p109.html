Type 6 · A point from two distances

Example 16

Draw $PQ = 4$ cm. Find a point $R$ above $PQ$ that is 6 cm from $P$ and 6 cm from $Q$, and join it to $P$ and $Q$. Could $R$ be 2 cm from both instead?

Solution.

Step 1 draw $PQ = 4$ cm, and open the compass to 6 cm
Step 2 arcs about $P$ and about $Q$ cross above $PQ$ at $R$ $6 + 6$ is more than 4
Step 3 join $R$ to $P$ and to $Q$ a tall pointed roof
Step 4 with 2 cm, $2 + 2 = 4$: the arcs only touch, at the middle of $PQ$ as in Stage 1
Answer $R$ with $PR = QR = 6$ cm; at 2 cm there is no point above $PQ$

This is the house roof again, drawn on a narrower gap. Arcs of 6 cm also cross below $PQ$, so there is a second such point there.

Example 17

The tops of the two walls of a house, $B$ and $C$, are 6 cm apart. Each roof line is 5 cm long and meets the other at $A$. $M$ is the point halfway between $B$ and $C$. How high is the tip of the roof above $M$?

Solution.

Step 1 draw $BC = 6$ cm and mark $M$, 3 cm from $B$
Step 2 arcs of radius 5 cm about $B$ and about $C$ cross above $BC$ at $A$ $5 + 5 \gt 6$
Step 3 join $A$ to $M$ and measure it
Answer $AM$ measures 4 cm
