8.2Squares and Rectangles

Now look at some figures whose boundaries are made of straight lines, like the ones in Fig. 8.9.

You know these shapes. They are squares and rectangles. But what makes a square a square, and a rectangle a rectangle?

Look at rectangle $ABCD$ in Fig. 8.10. The points $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ are its corners. The lines $AB$, $BC$, $CD$ and $DA$ are its sides. Its angles are $\angle A$, $\angle B$, $\angle C$ and $\angle D$.

The thick sides $AB$ and $DC$ face each other, so they are called opposite sides. The sides $AD$ and $BC$ are the other pair of opposite sides.

Rectangles and squares

A rectangle has two properties.
R1. Its opposite sides are equal in length.
R2. All its angles are $90^\circ$.

A square has corners and sides in the same way. It has two properties.
S1. All its sides are equal in length.
S2. All its angles are $90^\circ$.
