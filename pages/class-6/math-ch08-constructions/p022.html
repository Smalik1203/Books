Summary
  1. A ruler and a compass are enough to draw circles, parts of circles, squares, rectangles and many designs.
  2. All the points of a circle are the same distance from one fixed point, its centre. This distance is the radius. A compass opened to 4 cm draws a circle of radius 4 cm.
  3. A rectangle has its opposite sides equal, and all its angles $90^\circ$. A square has all its sides equal, and all its angles $90^\circ$. Turning a square or a rectangle does not change it.
  4. A rectangle is named by its corners in order round it, such as $ABCD$ or $BADC$, but never $ABDC$.
  5. To construct a rectangle from its sides, draw one side and a perpendicular at each end. Then mark the other side's length on both perpendiculars.
  6. A compass can carry a length from one place to another without a ruler.
  7. A rough diagram helps you plan a construction. Its lengths need not be correct, but equal sides carry the same mark.
  8. A line joining two opposite corners is a diagonal. A rectangle can be constructed from one side and a diagonal, or from the angles a diagonal makes at its corners.
  9. To find a point at a given distance from each of two points, draw an arc of that radius about each point. Where the arcs cross, the point is at the right distance from both. When the two radii are equal, the point is equidistant from the two points.
