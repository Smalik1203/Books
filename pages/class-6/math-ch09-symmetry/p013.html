Rotational symmetry
  • A figure has rotational symmetry if it has an angle of symmetry more than $0^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$.
  • The number of its angles of symmetry is its order of rotational symmetry.

So the windmill and the square have order 4, and the strip in Example 2 has order 1.

Figures with radial arms

Fig. 9.24(a) shows a figure with 4 radial arms. Each arm goes straight out from the centre. The dashed lines through the middle of the arms meet at the centre, and the angle between two arms next to each other is $90^\circ$. The figure looks the same after turns of $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $270^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so it has 4 angles of symmetry.

Think and Reflect
  1. Can you change the angles between the arms of Fig. 9.24(a) so that the figure still has 4 angles of symmetry? Try drawing it.

To check a drawing, draw the figure on two sheets of paper and cut out the arms from one sheet. Keep the other sheet still, and turn the cut-out on top of it.

In Fig. 9.24(b), the up and down arms have been made longer. Turn it by $90^\circ$, and a long arm lands where a short arm was. So $90^\circ$ is not an angle of symmetry any more. A half turn still works, because each long arm lands on the other long arm. This figure has only two angles of symmetry, $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.

We have seen figures with 4 angles of symmetry and with 2. Can a figure with radial arms have exactly 3? Look at the three arms in Fig. 9.24(c). Trace it and cut out a copy. Turn the copy over the figure. Only a full turn of $360^\circ$ brings it back onto itself. So this figure does not have rotational symmetry.
