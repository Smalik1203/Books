What can we change to give it 3 angles of symmetry? Suppose a figure with three arms does have rotational symmetry. Then turning it must carry arm to arm. In Fig. 9.25, the three angles between the arms are marked A, B and C. When one arm moves onto the next, angle A must fit on angle B, B on C, and C on A. So A, B and C must be equal.

A full turn is $360^\circ$, and it is shared equally by the three angles. So each angle must be $360^\circ \div 3$, which is $120^\circ$.

So a figure with 3 equal arms has rotational symmetry when the angle between arms next to each other is $120^\circ$. Its angles of symmetry are $120^\circ$, $120^\circ + 120^\circ = 240^\circ$ and $120^\circ + 120^\circ + 120^\circ = 360^\circ$. Check this with a paper cut-out.

The arms must also be alike. Suppose one of the three arms were longer than the other two. A turn of $120^\circ$ would then carry the long arm onto a short one, and the figure would not look the same, even with $120^\circ$ between the arms. So a figure with radial arms has rotational symmetry when its arms are equal and equally spaced.
