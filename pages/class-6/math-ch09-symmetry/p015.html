Does every figure have a smallest angle of symmetry? Most figures do. A circle is different.

Symmetries of a circle

Turn a circle about its centre. It fits on itself, and it does not matter by how much you turn it. So every angle is an angle of symmetry of a circle.

Now mark a point on the edge of a circle and join it to the centre. Carry the line on across the circle to make a diameter. Is this diameter a line of symmetry? Yes. Every diameter of a circle is a line of symmetry, so a circle has more lines of symmetry than you could ever count.

Wheels turn easily because they are round. Many other things around you have rotational symmetry. Fig. 9.29 shows three of them. Find some more.

For each object in Fig. 9.29, find the smallest turn that brings it back onto itself. Then look for its lines of symmetry. Does every object that has rotational symmetry also have a line of symmetry?

Things that spin, such as fans and wheels, are made with rotational symmetry for a reason. If the blades of a fan were not alike and equally spaced, the fan would shake as it turned.
