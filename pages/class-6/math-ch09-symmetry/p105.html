Problem 5

Which of these can be the smallest angle of symmetry of a figure?

  1. $50^\circ$
  2. $80^\circ$
  3. $40^\circ$
  4. $70^\circ$
Solution

A figure turned again and again by its smallest angle must come back exactly to the start after a full turn. So the smallest angle must divide 360 exactly.

$360 \div 50 = 7$, remainder 10

$360 \div 80 = 4$, remainder 40

$360 \div 40 = 9$, remainder 0

$360 \div 70 = 5$, remainder 10

The answer is $40^\circ$, option (c). A figure with smallest angle $40^\circ$ has 9 angles of symmetry. With $50^\circ$, $80^\circ$ or $70^\circ$, the turns would pass $360^\circ$ without ever landing on it, so none of them can be the smallest angle of symmetry.

Problem 6

A rangoli has 8 petals, all alike and spaced equally round its centre. Its smallest angle of symmetry is

  1. $8^\circ$
  2. $40^\circ$
  3. $45^\circ$
  4. $90^\circ$
Solution

Each turn that fits must carry every petal onto the next one. A full turn carries a petal all the way round, past all 8 petals.

a full turn is $360^\circ$, shared by 8 equal gaps

$360 \div 8 = 45$

angles of symmetry: $45^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $135^\circ$, and so on up to $360^\circ$

The answer is $45^\circ$, option (c). Option (a) uses the number of petals as the number of degrees. Option (b) shares $360^\circ$ among 9 gaps instead of 8. A turn of $90^\circ$ does fit, because it moves each petal two places on, but it is not the smallest angle, so option (d) is wrong.

Look back at the six problems. Problems 1 and 3 needed you to picture a shape or a folded sheet. Problems 2, 4, 5 and 6 were answered with one fact from the chapter: the angles of symmetry of a figure are the multiples of its smallest angle, and the smallest angle goes into 360 exactly. A question about turning is often a question about dividing 360.
