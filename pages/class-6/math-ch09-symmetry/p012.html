The angles of symmetry of a square

Label the corners of a square A, B, C and D, and mark its centre. Draw a dashed line from the centre to the right side, to show where the square started. Now turn the square clockwise about its centre, as in Fig. 9.22.

After a turn of $90^\circ$, the square fits exactly on the place where it started. Corner A is now where B was, B is where C was, C is where D was, and D is where A was. The square fits on its starting place again after $180^\circ$ and after $270^\circ$, and it is back where it began after $360^\circ$. So a square has 4 angles of symmetry: $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $270^\circ$ and $360^\circ$. Its centre of rotation is the point where its diagonals cross. You can check this by pushing a pin through that point of a paper square and turning the square about it.

Example 2

Find the angles of symmetry of the strip in Fig. 9.23. It is turned about the marked point.

Solution.

Step 1 turn the strip clockwise about its centre by $180^\circ$ a half turn
Step 2 the longer edge is now at the top, but it started at the bottom so it does not fit
Step 3 another half turn brings it back: it fits on itself only after $360^\circ$ a full turn
Answer $360^\circ$ is its only angle of symmetry, so the strip does not have rotational symmetry
