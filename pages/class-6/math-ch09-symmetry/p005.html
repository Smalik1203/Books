Folding and cutting. In Fig. 9.7, a sheet of paper has been folded once. A cut is made along the dashed line, starting and ending at the fold.

Draw a sketch of how the sheet will look when you open it. Does the hole have a line of symmetry? Where is it? Then fold and cut some shapes of your own.

You can fold a long strip of thin coloured paper several times and cut it to make a pattern that repeats. Fig. 9.8 shows one such design. Find the lines of symmetry of the whole design, and of each shape in it. Designs like this make good decorations for festivals, and paper festoons for Diwali or a wedding are often cut in just this way.

Punching holes. Fold a square sheet of paper in half and punch one hole through both layers. When you open the sheet, there are two holes, as in Fig. 9.9. The fold is a line of symmetry of the pattern of holes. Punch holes in different places, and in sheets folded in different ways, to make other symmetrical patterns.

Ink blots, cut shapes and punched holes are all made in the same way. Whatever happens on one side of the fold happens at the same place on the other side. That is why the fold always becomes a line of symmetry of what you make.
