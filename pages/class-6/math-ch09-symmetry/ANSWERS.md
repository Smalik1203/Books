### The folded and cut sheet, Fig. 9.7

The opened hole is an 8-sided shape, its top and bottom edges each dipping
in to a point on the fold. **Yes, it has a line of symmetry: the fold.** Its
cut is also the same above and below its middle, so the line across the
middle of the hole, at right angles to the fold, is a second line of
symmetry. *(checked: 2 lines)*

### The paper cut-out design, Fig. 9.8

its middle and the across line through its middle. Each small diamond has
**2**; each eight-pointed star has **8**. *(checked)*

## 9.2 Rotational Symmetry

### Think and Reflect (after the windmill)

*Answers will vary.* A square; a plus sign with four equal arms; a
four-petal flower; the letter-free design of Fig. 9.24(a).

### Think and Reflect (after Fig. 9.24)

**No.** With four equal arms, a turn that fits must carry each arm onto the
next, so each angle between arms must fit onto the next one. All four
angles must be equal, and $360^\circ \div 4 = 90^\circ$. Any other spacing
loses the quarter turn: arms at $60^\circ$ and $120^\circ$ in turn, for
example, give only $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.

### Think and Reflect (5, 6 and 7 arms)

1. Five equal arms, $360^\circ \div 5 = 72^\circ$ apart. The angles of
   symmetry are $72^\circ$, $144^\circ$, $216^\circ$, $288^\circ$ and
   $360^\circ$.
2. Six equal arms, $360^\circ \div 6 = 60^\circ$ apart. The angles of
   symmetry are $60^\circ$, $120^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $240^\circ$,
   $300^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.
3. The smallest angle is $360^\circ \div 7$. It is **not** a whole number
   of degrees: $7 \times 51 = 357$, so the angle is
   $51\frac{3}{7}$ degrees.

1. **Yes.** Let the smallest angle be $s$, and suppose some angle of
   symmetry $a$ is not a multiple of $s$. Turn by $a$, then turn back by
   $s$ as many times as you can: the figure still fits, and what is left is
   a turn smaller than $s$ but more than $0^\circ$. That would be a smaller
   angle of symmetry than $s$, which cannot be.
2. **True.** A full turn brings every figure back to where it started.
3. **True.** Turning again and again by the smallest angle must come back
   exactly to $360^\circ$, so the smallest angle goes into 360 exactly.

### Think and Reflect (the game on a grid)

**The second player can always win.** After each move of the first
player, the second player draws the line that the first player's line
lands on after a half turn about the centre of the grid. The centre of a 6
by 6 grid is a corner shared by four squares, so no line can land on itself
or meet its own copy, and the copy's squares are always free. So the second
player always has a move, and the first player runs out first.
