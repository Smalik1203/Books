Reflection

So far we have said that the two parts fit on each other when we fold along a line of symmetry. There is another way to say this. The part on one side of the line is reflected by the line to the other side, as in a mirror. At the same time, the part on the other side is reflected back to the first side.

To see this, label the corners of a square A, B, C and D, as in Fig. 9.6. Take the vertical line of symmetry. When we reflect the square in this line, B and C on the right move to the left side. They take the places where A and D were. In the same way, A takes the place of B, and D takes the place of C.

?
Think and Reflect
  1. Reflect the square in the diagonal from A to C. Where do A, B, C and D go?
  2. Now reflect the square in its horizontal line of symmetry. Where does each corner go?

Because a line of symmetry reflects a figure onto itself, a figure with one or more lines of symmetry is also said to have reflection symmetry.

Making shapes that have a line of symmetry

You have seen figures that are symmetrical and figures that are not. How can you make a symmetrical figure yourself? Here are three ways.

Ink blots. You may have done this in Class 5. Fold a sheet of paper in half and open it. Drop a few drops of ink or paint on one half. Press the two halves together, and open the paper again.

?
Think and Reflect
  1. What do you see? Is the ink pattern symmetrical?
  2. If it is, where is its line of symmetry?
  3. Is there any other line along which you can fold the paper to get two parts that match? Make a few more patterns and check.
