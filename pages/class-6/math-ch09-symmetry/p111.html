Short answer
  1. Draw a figure made of 5 equal squares, joined along whole sides, that has exactly one line of symmetry. Then draw one that has rotational symmetry of order 2 and no line of symmetry.
  1. A figure has exactly 3 angles of symmetry. Can $90^\circ$ be one of them? Explain.
  1. A figure looks exactly the same after a turn of $80^\circ$. Show that it also looks the same after a turn of $40^\circ$.
  1. On squared paper, a slanting line of symmetry runs through the corners of the squares, from bottom left to top right. B is a corner on the line, and A is 3 squares directly below B. Where is the reflection of A in the line?
  1. Draw a square. Draw two straight lines inside it so that the whole drawing has exactly 2 lines of symmetry.
Long answer
  1. A square sheet of side 12 cm is folded in half from left to right. A hole is punched 2 cm from the fold and 3 cm from the top edge.
    1. How many holes are there when the sheet is opened?
    2. How far apart are they?
    3. How far is each hole from the nearer side of the sheet?
    4. Name the line of symmetry of the opened sheet with its holes. Does it have rotational symmetry? Explain.
  1. A figure has 30 radial arms, equally spaced.
    1. What is the angle between two arms next to each other?
    2. The arms are long and short, taking turns. Find its smallest angle of symmetry and its order.
    3. Instead, every third arm is long and the others are short. Find its smallest angle of symmetry and its order.
    4. Which of the turns $72^\circ$, $120^\circ$ and $144^\circ$ fit the figures in both (b) and (c)?
  1. Draw a grid of 4 rows of 4 squares. Put a cross in exactly 4 of the squares so that the grid with its crosses has
    1. exactly 4 lines of symmetry
    2. rotational symmetry of order 4, and no line of symmetry
    3. exactly 1 line of symmetry, along a diagonal of the grid
