Figures with more than one line of symmetry

Does a square have only one line of symmetry? Take a square sheet of paper and try folding it so that the two halves fit exactly.

First fold the sheet in half from top to bottom. This is a vertical fold. Open it, and fold it in half from side to side. This is a horizontal fold. Open it again. Now fold the square in half along a line joining two opposite corners. This line is a diagonal. Open it, and fold along the other diagonal. Fig. 9.4 shows the four folds.

A diagonal of a square is a line of symmetry. What about a rectangle that is not a square?

?
Think and Reflect
  1. Picture a rectangle that is longer than it is wide, with one diagonal drawn. Is the diagonal a line of symmetry? Decide first, just by looking.
  2. Now cut out such a rectangle and fold it along a diagonal. Do the two parts fit exactly? What do you see?
