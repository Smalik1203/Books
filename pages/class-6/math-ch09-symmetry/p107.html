Example 9

A figure looks exactly the same after a turn of $90^\circ$, and also after a turn of $120^\circ$. Show that it looks the same after a turn of $30^\circ$.

Solution.

Step 1 turn it by $120^\circ$: it looks exactly as it did at the start $120^\circ$ fits
Step 2 so a quarter turn from here fits too, and so do three of them, $270^\circ$ as in Stage 1
Step 3 in all it has turned $120^\circ + 270^\circ = 390^\circ$, and it still looks the same
Step 4 $390^\circ = 360^\circ + 30^\circ$, and the full turn brings every figure back from the chapter
Answer the two turns together have the effect of a $30^\circ$ turn, so $30^\circ$ is an angle of symmetry

Turning it by $30^\circ$ again and again, as in Stage 1, gives every multiple of $30^\circ$ up to $360^\circ$. So this figure has at least 12 angles of symmetry.

Type 5 · Figures with radial arms

Example 10

A figure has 15 equal radial arms, equally spaced. Find the angle between two arms next to each other, and list its first three angles of symmetry. What is its order of rotational symmetry?

Solution.

Step 1 angle between arms $= 360^\circ \div 15 = 24^\circ$ equal gaps share the turn
Step 2 $24 \times 2 = 48$ and $24 \times 3 = 72$
Answer $24^\circ$; the first three angles are $24^\circ$, $48^\circ$ and $72^\circ$; the order is 15
Example 11

In a figure with equal radial arms, equally spaced, the angle between two arms next to each other is $10^\circ$. How many arms are there? Are $90^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ angles of symmetry?

Solution.

Step 1 number of arms $= 360 \div 10 = 36$
Step 2 $10 \times 9 = 90$ so $90^\circ$ is one
Step 3 $10 \times 4 = 40$ and $10 \times 5 = 50$ 45 is not a multiple
Answer 36 arms; $90^\circ$ is an angle of symmetry, $45^\circ$ is not
