B3 Both are true: $360 \div 24 = 15$, so 15 turns of $24^\circ$ make a full turn. R is the reason A can happen, so the answer is (a).
B6 Each diagonal fold doubles the layers, so there are 4 layers and 4 holes, option (c). Option (d) gives each fold more than one doubling, and option (a) counts only one fold.
C3 The angles below $60^\circ$ and $60^\circ$ itself are the first 5 multiples of the smallest angle, so it is $60 \div 5 = 12$ degrees, option (b). With $15^\circ$ there are only 3 angles below $60^\circ$, and with $10^\circ$ there are 5.
C5 For exactly 3 angles, the colouring must repeat every 4 parts but not every 2. Colour 1 part in each group of 4 parts, 3 parts in all. One coloured part gives only 1 angle of symmetry, and 6 parts can work but is not the least, so the answer is (b).

Most of these questions did not need a drawing. They needed one of two facts: a fold that fits shows a line of symmetry, and the angles of symmetry are the multiples of the smallest one, up to $360^\circ$. When a symmetry question looks hard, ask whether it is about folding or about turning.
