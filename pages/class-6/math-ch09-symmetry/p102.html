After one quarter turn the figure looks exactly as it did. So a second quarter turn gives the same picture again, and two quarter turns make $90^\circ + 90^\circ = 180^\circ$. In the same way, three quarter turns make $270^\circ$. So both of these turns must fit, and we did not need to see the figure.

Nothing says that a turn of $45^\circ$ works. A square looks the same after a quarter turn, but after a turn of $45^\circ$ it stands on a corner, which is a different picture. So a figure with $90^\circ$ as an angle of symmetry need not have $45^\circ$ as one.

A figure is made of equal radial arms, spaced equally. For which of these numbers of arms is the smallest angle of symmetry a whole number of degrees: 8, 9, 11, 12?

With equal arms spaced equally, the smallest angle of symmetry is $360^\circ$ shared out equally among the arms. This is a whole number only when the number of arms divides 360 exactly. $360 \div 8 = 45$, $360 \div 9 = 40$ and $360 \div 12 = 30$, so 8, 9 and 12 arms work. But $360 \div 11 = 32\frac{8}{11}$, so 11 arms do not. The question about angles became a question about the factors of 360.

The words TOOT, NUN, SOS and MOM are written in capital letters. Which of them look exactly the same after a half turn? Which look the same in a mirror held along an up-and-down line beside them?

A word given a half turn is read from the other end, and every letter is upside down. So each letter must look the same after a half turn, and the word must read the same backwards. S and O pass, and SOS reads the same backwards, so SOS works. T, U and M do not look the same upside down, so TOOT, NUN and MOM fail, even though N itself passes.

A mirror also makes the word read backwards, but it flips each letter from left to right. So each letter needs an up-and-down line of symmetry. T, O and M have one, so TOOT and MOM look the same in the mirror. N and S do not, so NUN and SOS fail. The two tests picked out different words.

Two equal squares touch at one corner only, and a diagonal of each lies along the same straight line. How many lines of symmetry does this figure have? Does it have rotational symmetry?

Fold along the straight line through the two diagonals: each square folds onto itself, so this is a line of symmetry. Fold along the line through the shared corner at right angles to it: each square lands exactly on the other. That is a second line. Any other fold sends a square where nothing is drawn, so the figure has 2 lines of symmetry.

Now give the figure a half turn about the shared corner. Each square lands on the other, so the figure has rotational symmetry of order 2. A quarter turn would lay the squares along the other line, where there are none. This is the same thing we saw with the letters H and X: two lines of symmetry at right angles bring a half turn with them.
