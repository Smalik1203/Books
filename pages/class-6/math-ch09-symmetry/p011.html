Example 1

Find the angles of symmetry of the strip in Fig. 9.23. It is turned about the marked point.

Turn the strip clockwise about its centre. After a turn of $180^\circ$, the strip is upside down. Its longer edge is now at the top, but at the start it was at the bottom. So the turned strip does not fit on the original one.

Another half turn brings the strip back to where it started. The strip fits on itself only after a full turn of $360^\circ$. Since $360^\circ$ is its only angle of symmetry, we say that the strip does not have rotational symmetry.

Rotational symmetry

A figure has rotational symmetry if it has an angle of symmetry that is more than $0^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$. The number of angles of symmetry of a figure is called its order of rotational symmetry.

So the windmill and the square both have order 4. The strip in Example 1 has only one angle of symmetry, so its order is 1.

Figures with radial arms

Fig. 9.24(a) shows a figure with 4 radial arms. Each arm goes straight out from the centre. The dashed lines through the middle of the arms meet at the centre, and the angle between two arms next to each other is $90^\circ$. The figure looks the same after turns of $90^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $270^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so it has 4 angles of symmetry.
