Beyond the Book

The chapter showed you how to test a figure by folding it and by turning it. The questions below use only those two tests. What is new is that you must decide which test a question needs. Try each question before you read what follows it.

A circle is drawn with one of its diameters. How many lines of symmetry does this figure have? What are its angles of symmetry?

A circle on its own folds along every diameter, but now a fold must also leave the drawn diameter where it was. A fold along the drawn diameter does. So does a fold along the diameter at right angles to it, because the drawn diameter folds onto itself. Any other fold moves the drawn diameter onto a diameter that is not drawn. So there are 2 lines of symmetry.

A turn must also leave the drawn diameter in place. A half turn about the centre does, because it swaps the two ends. Any other turn short of a full turn moves it to where nothing is drawn. So the angles of symmetry are $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$.

Fig. 9.34 shows seven capital letters drawn with straight lines. Which letters have a line of symmetry? Which have rotational symmetry?

Fold each letter in your mind. A and T fold onto themselves along an up-and-down line. E folds along a line across its middle. H and X fold both ways. N and Z do not fold onto themselves along any line.

Now give each letter a half turn. H, N, X and Z look the same after the half turn, so they have rotational symmetry of order 2. A, E and T do not. So N and Z have rotational symmetry with no line of symmetry. H and X pass both tests, but each test had to be tried on its own.
