Using What You Know
Lines and angles of symmetry, in questions that do not say which to look for.

The chapter showed you how to test a figure by folding it and by turning it. The questions below use only those two tests. What is new is that you must decide which test a question needs. Try each question before you read what follows it.

How many lines of symmetry does a regular octagon have? What are its angles of symmetry?

An octagon has 8 corners and 8 sides. Because it is regular, the corners come in 4 pairs of opposite corners, and a fold through each pair fits the shape on itself. That gives 4 lines. The sides come in 4 pairs of opposite sides, and a fold through the middles of each pair also works. That gives 4 more lines, so there are 8 in all.

A turn that carries each corner to the next one is $360^\circ \div 8 = 45^\circ$. So the angles of symmetry are the multiples of $45^\circ$ up to $360^\circ$: $45^\circ$, $90^\circ$, $135^\circ$, $180^\circ$, $225^\circ$, $270^\circ$, $315^\circ$ and $360^\circ$. The octagon has 8 lines of symmetry and 8 angles of symmetry, just as a square has 4 and 4.

Fig. 9.34 shows seven capital letters drawn with straight lines. Which letters have a line of symmetry? Which have rotational symmetry?

Fold each letter in your mind. A and T fold onto themselves along an up-and-down line. E folds along a line across its middle. H and X fold both ways. N and Z do not fold onto themselves along any line.

Now give each letter a half turn. H, N, X and Z look the same after the half turn, so they have rotational symmetry of order 2. A, E and T do not. So N and Z have rotational symmetry with no line of symmetry. Notice that H and X, the two letters with two lines of symmetry at right angles, also have a half turn. Reflecting a figure in one of these lines and then in the other has the same effect as a half turn.
