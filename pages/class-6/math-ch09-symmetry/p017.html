A game on a grid

Here is a game for two players. Draw a grid of 6 rows of 6 squares. The players take turns. In each turn, a player covers two squares that share a side by drawing a line joining their centres. The line can go across or up and down. A square can be covered only once, so two lines may not meet or cross. The first player who cannot draw a line loses the game.

What this chapter showed
  1. A figure made of parts that repeat in a fixed pattern has symmetry. We say it is symmetrical.
  2. A line of symmetry cuts a figure into two parts that fit exactly on each other when folded along the line. A figure with a line of symmetry has reflection symmetry.
  3. A figure may have more than one line of symmetry. A square has 4, and every diameter of a circle is one.
  4. An angle of symmetry is an angle through which a figure can be turned about a fixed point, the centre of rotation, so that it looks exactly the same. $360^\circ$ is an angle of symmetry of every figure.
  5. A figure with an angle of symmetry between $0^\circ$ and $360^\circ$ has rotational symmetry. The number of its angles of symmetry is its order of rotational symmetry.
  6. Some figures have lines of symmetry but no rotational symmetry. Some have rotational symmetry but no line of symmetry. Some have both.

To test a line of symmetry, fold along it. To test an angle of symmetry, trace the figure, turn the tracing about the centre, and see whether it fits.
