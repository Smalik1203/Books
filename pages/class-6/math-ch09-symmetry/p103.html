Fold along the straight line through the two diagonals: each square folds onto itself, so this is a line of symmetry. Fold along the line through the shared corner at right angles to it: each square lands exactly on the other. That is a second line. Any other fold sends a square where nothing is drawn, so the figure has 2 lines of symmetry.

Now give the figure a half turn about the shared corner. Each square lands on the other, so the figure has rotational symmetry of order 2. A quarter turn would lay the squares along the other line, where there are none. As with the letters, the half turn was found by turning the figure, not by folding it.

Single correct
Example 1

A point is 4 cm to the left of an up-and-down line of symmetry. How far is it from its reflection?

  1. 2 cm
  2. 4 cm
  3. 6 cm
  4. 8 cm

Solution.

Step 1The reflection is 4 cm to the right of the line, at the same height.
Step 2The point and its reflection are $4 + 4 = 8$ cm apart.
CheckThe line is halfway between them: $8 \div 2 = 4$ cm on each side.
Answer(d) 8 cm
Example 2

A figure has 20 equal radial arms, equally spaced. Every fifth arm is coloured red, and the others are left white. What is the smallest angle of symmetry of the coloured figure?

  1. $18^\circ$
  2. $72^\circ$
  3. $90^\circ$
  4. $360^\circ$

Solution.

Step 1The arms are $360^\circ \div 20 = 18^\circ$ apart.
Step 2The red arms are $5 \times 18^\circ = 90^\circ$ apart, and there are 4 of them.
Step 3A turn must carry each red arm onto a red arm. A turn of $18^\circ$ does not; a turn of $90^\circ$ does.
Answer(c) $90^\circ$
