Type 1 · Counting lines of symmetry

Example 1

How many lines of symmetry does each figure in Fig. 9.35 have?

Solution.

Step 1 a square alone has 4 lines: 2 diagonals, and 2 through the middles of opposite sides start here
Step 2 (a) a fold along either diagonal keeps the drawn diagonal in place both diagonals fit
Step 3 (a) an up-and-down or across fold moves it onto the other diagonal these two fail
Step 4 (b) only the up-and-down fold leaves the drawn line where it was the other three fail
Answer (a) 2 lines of symmetry (b) 1 line of symmetry

A line drawn inside a figure can take away some of its lines of symmetry.

Example 2

Rani cuts the same small triangle off each corner of a square (Fig. 9.36). She says the new shape has 8 lines of symmetry, because it has 8 sides. Is she right?

Solution.

Step 1 fold up and down, or across: each fold fits 2 lines
Step 2 fold along either diagonal of the old square: each fits 2 more lines
Step 3 fold through two opposite corners: a long side lands on a short side no fit
Answer No. The shape has 4 lines of symmetry, not 8
