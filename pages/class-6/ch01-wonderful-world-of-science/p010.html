So when you are stuck, ask someone. Compare what you saw with what your friend saw. If you disagree about what happened, that disagreement is worth more than either of your answers.

One last thing, and it is important. You will not find the answers to all your questions this year. Some of what you wonder about in Grade 6 you will only understand in Grade 9, or later, or possibly never — because nobody has worked it out yet.

That is not a warning. It is the best part.

Describe an event separately from a friend, then compare your accounts. Did you notice the same details? Which statements describe what you saw, and which suggest a reason? Differences give you something to check together. Finding an answer may need longer observation, a better comparison, or a more precise question.

Think It Through

Write down one "Why?" question about something you have noticed but never had explained. How might you begin to find the answer? Who could you ask, and what could you test?

Science begins with noticing. It continues with asking. And it never quite ends.
