And you do not have to do any of it alone. Science has a reputation for lonely geniuses, and it is mostly wrong. Discoveries today come from teams, often spread across many countries, arguing with one another and checking each other's work. Being told you are wrong by someone who has looked carefully at your evidence is not an insult. It is one of the most useful things that can happen to you.

So when you are stuck, ask someone. Compare what you saw with what your friend saw. If you disagree about what happened, that disagreement is worth more than either of your answers.

One last thing, and it is important. You will not find the answers to all your questions this year. Some of what you wonder about in Grade 6 you will only understand in Grade 9, or later, or possibly never — because nobody has worked it out yet.

That is not a warning. It is the best part.
