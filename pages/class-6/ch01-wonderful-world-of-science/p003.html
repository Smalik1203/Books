Think It Through

Why might this happen? A flower has no eyes and no clock. What could it be responding to? You do not need the right answer yet — only a reasonable guess. A possible explanation is a hypothesis. A prediction states what you expect to observe if it is right. For example, if light triggers opening, what should happen when the plant is shaded?

One Idea in Many Places

Some of what you learn in this book will be useful the same week you learn it. You will understand why food spoils faster in May than in December, why a frayed electrical wire is dangerous, how boiling kills many disease-causing microbes in water, while leaving dissolved chemical contaminants behind.

But the real reward comes later, and it is stranger than that.

Leave a wet shirt on a line and by evening it is dry. Pour water into an earthen matka and the water inside stays cool even on a hot afternoon. Watch the sky before the monsoon and see clouds thickening where there was clear blue an hour ago.

Three ordinary things. They seem to have nothing to do with each other.

Two involve evaporation directly; cloud formation needs another change as well.
