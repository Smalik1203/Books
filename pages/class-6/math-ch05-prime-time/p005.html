5.2Prime Numbers

Guna and Anshu grow figs, or anjeer, and pack them in boxes. Guna puts 12 figs in a box, and Anshu puts 7. In a box the figs must lie in a rectangle of rows and columns.

Try it before you read on. In how many ways can Guna arrange 12 figs in a rectangle? In how many ways can Anshu arrange 7 figs?

Guna finds three ways: 1 row of 12, 2 rows of 6, and 3 rows of 4. The number of rows times the number of columns is always 12: $1 \times 12$, $2 \times 6$ and $3 \times 4$. So the numbers of rows and columns are all factors of 12.

Anshu finds only one way, 1 row of 7, because 7 has only two factors, 1 and 7. The number 12 has more than two factors, so it can be arranged in more than one way.

Prime and composite numbers
  • A number with exactly two factors, 1 and the number itself, is a prime number, or a prime. A number with more than two factors is a composite number.
  • The number 1 has only one factor, so it is neither prime nor composite.

The first few primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17 and 19. The first few composite numbers are 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 18 and 20.
