Only the last three digits matter for 8. So we need $3\square4$ to be a multiple of 8. $304 = 8 \times 38$ works. Adding 40 to a multiple of 8 gives another multiple of 8, because $40 = 8 \times 5$. So 344 and 384 work too. Checking the others, 314, 324, 334, 354, 364, 374 and 394 each leave a remainder when divided by 8. The digit can be 0, 4 or 8. The 7 at the front never mattered.

Without listing all their factors, find out whether 84 and 275 are co-prime.

Both numbers are composite, and each has many factors, so listing them all would take a while. Their prime factorisations are quicker: $84 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $275 = 5 \times 5 \times 11$. The prime factors of 84 are 2, 3 and 7. Those of 275 are 5 and 11. No prime appears in both, so 84 and 275 are co-prime.

None of these questions needed a new fact. Each one needed you to see which idea from the chapter it was about: a common multiple, a factor, a prime factorisation or a divisibility test. Once you saw that, the working was short.

Example 1

Find the smallest number that is a multiple of 4, of 6 and of 10.

Solution.

Step 1 list the multiples of 10, the largest: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, … fewest to check
Step 2 10, 30 and 50 are not multiples of 4
Step 3 20 and 40 are multiples of 4, but not of 6
Step 4 $60 \div 4 = 15$, $\ 60 \div 6 = 10$, $\ 60 \div 10 = 6$ all three work
Answer 60
Example 2

How many numbers from 1 to 100 are multiples of both 4 and 6?

Solution.

Step 1 multiples of 6: 6, 12, … and 12 is a multiple of 4
Step 2 the first common multiple is 12 not $4 \times 6$
Step 3 12, 24, 36, 48, 60, 72, 84, 96
Step 4 $96 = 12 \times 8$, and $12 \times 9 = 108$ is past 100
Answer 8
