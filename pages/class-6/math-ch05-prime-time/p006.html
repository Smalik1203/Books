The Sieve of Eratosthenes

Can we list all the primes from 1 to 100? Here is a quick way to do it. Write the numbers from 1 to 100 in ten rows, and follow these steps.

Step 1. Cross out 1, because it is neither prime nor composite.

Step 2. Circle 2. Then cross out every multiple of 2 after it: 4, 6, 8, and so on.

Step 3. Circle 3, the next number not crossed out. Cross out every multiple of 3 after it.

Step 4. Circle 5, the next number not crossed out. Cross out every multiple of 5 after it.

Step 5. Carry on in the same way until every number is either circled or crossed out.

The circled numbers are the primes, and the crossed-out numbers other than 1 are composite. This is the Sieve of Eratosthenes, named after a Greek mathematician.

Primes through the ages

Every whole number greater than 1 can be made by multiplying primes. People have studied primes for over 2000 years, and many questions about them still have no answer. Is there a largest prime? Euclid found the answer, and you will too, in a later class.

The largest prime that anyone has found would fill about 6500 pages if it were written out. It was found with the help of a computer.
