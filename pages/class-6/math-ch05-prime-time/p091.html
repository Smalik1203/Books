Long answer
  1. For a school function, 84 chairs and 126 stools are set out in rows. Every row has the same number of seats, and a row holds only chairs or only stools. Write the prime factorisations of 84 and 126, and use them to find all the possible numbers of seats in a row. Also find the number of rows of chairs and of stools when each row has 14 seats.
  1. Three bells at a railway station ring every 6 minutes, every 8 minutes and every 12 minutes. All three ring together at 9:00 am. Find the time when they next ring together. How many more times do all three ring together, up to and including 11:00 am?
  1. Anshu says, “A number that is divisible by 4 and also by 6 must be divisible by 24.” Test what he says with 12, 36 and 48. Use the prime factorisations of 4 and 6 to explain what goes wrong. Hence find the smallest number that divides every number divisible by both 4 and 6.
  1. Take the numbers from 1 to 200. Find how many of them are multiples of 6, how many are multiples of 8, and how many are multiples of both. Hence find how many are multiples of 6 or of 8, or of both.
  1. The prime factorisation of a number is $2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 7$. Find the number. Use the prime factorisation to find whether the number is divisible by 14 and whether it is divisible by 8, giving a reason for each. Write the number as 14 times another number.
  1. Find all the prime numbers between 100 and 130. Show that 119 and 121 are composite by writing each as a product of two numbers greater than 1. How many primes lie between 100 and 130?
  1. Figs are packed in a box in a rectangle of rows and columns. Turning a rectangle on its side does not make a new one, and a square counts as a rectangle. Find the number of different rectangles that can be made with 36 figs, with 37 figs and with 40 figs. Which of these three numbers is a prime? Explain how the number of rectangles shows it.
  1. Write 420 as a product of primes. Leena wants to write 420 as a product of two co-prime numbers, both greater than 1. Explain why the two 2s must go into the same number. Hence find all the pairs of co-prime numbers whose product is 420.
  1. Find the smallest 4-digit number that is divisible by all of 2, 4, 5, 8 and 10. Also find the largest such 4-digit number. Use the divisibility tests to show your working.
  1. Without multiplying, find the prime factorisation of $45 \times 56$. Hence show that $45 \times 56$ is divisible by 12 and by 35, but not by 27.
