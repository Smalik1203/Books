Using prime factorisation to check divisibility

How can we check whether one number is divisible by another, without a long division? Prime factorisation helps again.

Example 5

Is 168 divisible by 12?

Solution.

Step 1 $168 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$
Step 2 $168 = (2 \times 2 \times 3) \times (2 \times 7) = 12 \times 14$ group 168’s primes
Answer Yes, 168 is divisible by 12

Now try 75 and 21: $75 = 3 \times 5 \times 5$ and $21 = 3 \times 7$. If 75 were a multiple of 21, every prime factor of 21 would be a prime factor of 75. But 7 is not, so 75 is not divisible by 21. Dividing shows the same: $75 = 21 \times 3 + 12$.

Is 42 divisible by 12? $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$. Every prime of 12 is in 42, but 2 appears twice in 12 and only once in 42. So 42 is not divisible by 12. Dividing shows the same: $42 = 12 \times 3 + 6$.

Checking divisibility

If one number is divisible by another, then the prime factorisation of the second number is included in the prime factorisation of the first. Every prime of the second number appears in the first, at least as many times.
