5.5Divisibility Tests

So far, we have found the factors of numbers for many reasons: to find out whether a number is prime, and whether two numbers are co-prime. Finding the factors of a small number is easy. How do we find the factors of a large number?

Take 8560. Does it have any factors from 2 to 10? For some of these numbers, you can find out without doing a long division. Can you find which ones?

Divisibility by 10

Is 8560 divisible by 10? This is another way of asking whether 10 is a factor of 8560. To find out, look for a pattern in the multiples of 10.

The first few multiples of 10 are 10, 20, 30, 40, … Carry on the list and look at it closely. What pattern do you see? Is 125 a multiple of 10? Will 125 ever appear in the list? Why, or why not? Which of 70, 705 and 7000 are multiples of 10? Write down a multiple of 10 that is larger than 1000, and one smaller than 1000. Now, is 8560 divisible by 10?

Divisibility by 5

Divisibility by 5 is also easy to check. How can we do it? Explore by listing the multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, … What do you see in these numbers? Is there a pattern in the last digit?

Which numbers between 50 and 80 are divisible by 5? What is the largest number less than 399 that is divisible by 5? Is 8560 divisible by 5? What about 8565, and 8567? Look back at the multiples of 10. Is every multiple of 10 also a multiple of 5? Is every multiple of 5 also a multiple of 10?
