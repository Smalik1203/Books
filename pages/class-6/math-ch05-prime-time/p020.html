A prime puzzle

Fig. 5.12 shows a puzzle on the left and its solution on the right. Before you read the rules, look at the two grids. Can you work out what the rules must be?

Here are the rules. Fill every empty square with a prime. The product of each row is the number to its right, and the product of each column is the number below it. In the solution, the top row gives $5 \times 5 \times 3 = 75$, and the first column $5 \times 2 \times 17 = 170$.

Think and Reflect
  1. Solve the four puzzles A, B, C and D in Fig. 5.13.
  2. Which row or column did you start with in each puzzle? Why did you choose it?

Here is one way to begin puzzle A. Below the middle column is 125, and $125 = 5 \times 5 \times 5$, so all three squares in that column are 5. Next, look for a row with few prime factors: $20 = 2 \times 2 \times 5$ has only three. A prime may appear more than once in a row. Each puzzle has only one solution. When you finish, multiply along every row and column to check.

Numbers and puzzles

A number can be special for many reasons. In a prime puzzle, the prime factorisation of a product gives the primes in its row or column.
