5.1Common Multiples and Common Factors

The idli-vada game

Children sit in a circle to play a number game. The first child says “1”, the next child says “2”, and the count goes on around the circle.

There are three rules. On a multiple of 3, the player says idli instead of the number. On a multiple of 5, the player says vada. On a multiple of both 3 and 5, the player says idli-vada. A player who makes a mistake is out, and the game goes on until one player is left.

So the players say idli for 3, 6, 9, 12, 18, … and vada for 5, 10, 20, 25, … The first time they say idli-vada is at 15, because 15 is a multiple of 3 and also a multiple of 5. The next time is at 30. Which numbers come after that?

Common multiples

A number that is a multiple of two numbers is a common multiple of them. The common multiples of 3 and 5 are 15, 30, 45, 60, …

Fig. 5.1 sorts the multiples of 3 and the multiples of 5, up to 30, into two circles that overlap.

Numbers such as 1, 2, 4 and 7 are multiples of neither 3 nor 5, so they are left out. The numbers in the left circle are said as idli, and those in the right circle as vada. The numbers in the overlap are in both circles: these are the idli-vada numbers.

Look at the two numbers in the overlap, 15 and 30. Both of them are also multiples of 15. Will every common multiple of 3 and 5 be a multiple of 15?
