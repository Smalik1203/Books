Exercise Set 5.3
  1. 2 is a prime, and it is also an even number. Is there any other even prime?
  1. Look at the primes up to 100 in Fig. 5.6. Take two primes that come one after the other. What is the smallest difference between two such primes? What is the largest difference?
  1. Does every row of Fig. 5.6 have the same number of primes? Which rows have the fewest primes? Which rows have the most?
  1. Which of these numbers are prime: 23, 51, 37, 26?
  1. Write three pairs of primes less than 20 whose sum is a multiple of 5.
  1. 13 and 31 are both primes, and both are made of the digits 1 and 3. Find all such pairs of primes up to 100.
  1. Find seven composite numbers that come one after the other, between 1 and 100.
  1. Two primes with a difference of 2 are called twin primes. For example, 3 and 5 are twin primes, and so are 17 and 19. Find all the other twin primes up to 100.
  1. Is each statement true or false? Explain your answer.
    1. No prime number has 4 as its units digit.
    2. A product of primes can also be a prime.
    3. Prime numbers do not have any factors.
    4. All even numbers are composite numbers.
    5. 2 is a prime, and so is the next number, 3. For every other prime, the next number is composite.
  1. Which of these numbers is the product of exactly three different primes: 45, 60, 91, 105, 330?
  1. How many 3-digit primes can you make using each of 2, 4 and 5 once?
  1. 3 is a prime, and $2 \times 3 + 1 = 7$ is also a prime. For which other primes does doubling and adding 1 give a prime? Find at least five.
