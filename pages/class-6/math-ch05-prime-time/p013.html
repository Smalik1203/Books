Example 2

Find the prime factorisation of 180, starting from $180 = 18 \times 10$.

Solution.

Step 1 $18 = 2 \times 3 \times 3$ and $10 = 2 \times 5$ split each factor
Step 2 $180 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5$
Step 3 $2 \times 2 = 4$, then $4 \times 9 = 36$, then $36 \times 5 = 180$ multiply back
Answer $180 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$

If you had started from $180 = 12 \times 15$ instead, you would reach the same five primes.

Using prime factorisation to check for co-primes

Prime factorisation is very useful in the study of numbers. Here are two ways to use it. The first is the method we were looking for, to check whether two numbers are co-prime. Take 56 and 63 again, and find the prime factorisation of each.

$56 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 7 \quad \text{and} \quad 63 = 3 \times 3 \times 7$

7 is a prime factor of 56 and also of 63. So 56 and 63 are not co-prime.

What about 80 and 63? Their prime factorisations are $80 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5$ and $63 = 3 \times 3 \times 7$. No prime appears in both. Can we now say that they are co-prime? Suppose they had a common factor that was composite. Would the prime factors of that common factor appear in the prime factorisations of 80 and 63? They would, in both. But no prime appears in both, so there is no such common factor.
