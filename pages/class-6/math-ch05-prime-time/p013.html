Prime factorisation of a product

First write the number as a product of two factors, such as $72 = 12 \times 6$. Then find the prime factorisation of each factor, $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$ and $6 = 2 \times 3$, and put them together.

$72 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 2 \times 3 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$

Multiply the primes to check that you get 72 back. How many times does each prime appear in 72? Compare this with the factorisations of 12 and 6 put together.

Example 2

Find the prime factorisation of 180, starting from $180 = 18 \times 10$.

Solution.

Step 1 $18 = 2 \times 3 \times 3$ and $10 = 2 \times 5$ split each factor
Step 2 $180 = 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 5$
Step 3 $2 \times 2 = 4$, then $4 \times 9 = 36$, then $36 \times 5 = 180$ multiply back
Answer $180 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$

If you had started from $180 = 12 \times 15$ instead, you would reach the same five primes.
