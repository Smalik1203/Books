Divisibility by 4

Divisibility by 4 can be checked quickly too. Look at the multiples of 4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, … For 10, 5 and 2, the last digit was enough. Does the last digit work for 4? Try it with a few numbers before you read on.

It does not. 12 and 22 have the same last digit, but 12 is a multiple of 4 and 22 is not. So do 14 and 24, 16 and 26, and 18 and 28. So the last digit alone cannot tell us whether a number is a multiple of 4.

Would more digits help? List the multiples of 4 up to 200, and circle the ones between 100 and 200. Compare each circled number with the number 100 less than it: 104 with 4, 112 with 12, and so on. What do you notice? Is 100 a multiple of 4?

Find the multiples of 4 between 330 and 340, between 1730 and 1740, and between 2030 and 2040. What do you see? Is 8536 divisible by 4? Is 8538?

Divisibility by 8

Checking for divisibility by 8 can be made simple too. Can the last two digits be used again? Look at 112 and 212, which end in the same two digits. Is each of them a multiple of 8? Now find the numbers that are divisible by 8 between 120 and 140, between 1120 and 1140, and between 3120 and 3140. What do you see? Then change the last two digits of 8560 so that the new number is also a multiple of 8.
