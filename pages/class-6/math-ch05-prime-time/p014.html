So if two numbers have no common prime factor, they are co-prime.

Example 3

Are 40 and 231 co-prime?

Solution.

Step 1 $40 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 5$ 40 has 2 and 5
Step 2 $231 = 3 \times 7 \times 11$ 231 has 3, 7, 11
Step 3 No prime divides both numbers
Answer 40 and 231 are co-prime

The numbers do not have to be small. The same check works just as quickly for larger numbers.

Example 4

Are 242 and 195 co-prime?

Solution.

Step 1 $242 = 2 \times 11 \times 11$ 242 has 2 and 11
Step 2 $195 = 3 \times 5 \times 13$ 195 has 3, 5, 13
Step 3 There is no common prime factor
Answer 242 and 195 are co-prime

Using prime factorisation to check divisibility

48 is divisible by 12, because dividing 48 by 12 leaves a remainder of 0. How can we check whether one number is divisible by another, without doing a long division? Prime factorisation helps again.

Example 5

Is 168 divisible by 12?

Solution.

Step 1 $168 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7$
Step 2 $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$
Step 3 $168 = (2 \times 2 \times 3) \times (2 \times 7) = 12 \times 14$ group 168’s primes
Answer Yes, 168 is divisible by 12

Now try 75 and 21. Their prime factorisations are $75 = 3 \times 5 \times 5$ and $21 = 3 \times 7$. If 75 were a multiple of 21, every prime factor of 21 would also be a prime factor of 75. But 7 is a prime factor of 21, and it is not a prime factor of 75. So 75 is not divisible by 21. Dividing shows the same thing: $75 = 21 \times 3 + 12$.
