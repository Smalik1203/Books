Example 4

Are 242 and 195 co-prime?

$242 = 2 \times 11 \times 11$

$195 = 3 \times 5 \times 13$

The prime factors of 242 are 2 and 11. The prime factors of 195 are 3, 5 and 13. There is no common prime factor, so 242 and 195 are co-prime.

Using prime factorisation to check divisibility

48 is divisible by 12, because dividing 48 by 12 leaves a remainder of 0. How can we check whether one number is divisible by another, without doing a long division? Prime factorisation helps again.

Example 5

Is 168 divisible by 12?

$168 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 7$

$12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$

We can multiply in any order, so we can group the primes of 168 like this:

$168 = (2 \times 2 \times 3) \times (2 \times 7) = 12 \times 14$

So 168 is divisible by 12.

Now try 75 and 21. Their prime factorisations are $75 = 3 \times 5 \times 5$ and $21 = 3 \times 7$. If 75 were a multiple of 21, every prime factor of 21 would also be a prime factor of 75. But 7 is a prime factor of 21, and it is not a prime factor of 75. So 75 is not divisible by 21. Dividing shows the same thing: $75 = 21 \times 3 + 12$.

Is 42 divisible by 12? The prime factorisations are $42 = 2 \times 3 \times 7$ and $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$. Every prime factor of 12 is also a prime factor of 42. But 2 appears twice in the factorisation of 12, and only once in the factorisation of 42. So the prime factorisation of 12 is not included in that of 42, and 42 is not divisible by 12. Dividing shows the same thing: $42 = 12 \times 3 + 6$, which leaves a remainder of 6.

Checking divisibility

If one number is divisible by another, then the prime factorisation of the second number is included in the prime factorisation of the first. Every prime of the second number appears in the first, at least as many times.
