Think and Reflect
  1. In each box, say how each number is special compared with the other three.
  2. Share your reasons with your classmates. Who gave the same reasons as you? Did anyone give a reason that you had not thought of?

Some reasons use the ideas of this chapter, such as being a prime, being a multiple of a number, or having a certain factor. Other reasons use different ideas, such as the number of digits, or being a square number. For each box, try to find at least one reason that uses an idea from this chapter.

A number can also be special in more than one way. Look at 43 again. It is the only prime, it is the only number that is not a square number, and it is the only number greater than 40. Which of these reasons did you think of first?

A prime puzzle

Fig. 5.12 shows a puzzle on the left and its solution on the right. Before you read the rules, look at the two grids. Can you work out what the rules must be?

Here are the rules. Fill every empty square of the grid with a prime number. The product of each row must be the number to the right of the row. The product of each column must be the number below the column. In the solution, for example, the top row gives $5 \times 5 \times 3 = 75$, and the first column gives $5 \times 2 \times 17 = 170$.
