Summary
  1. A number that is a multiple of two numbers is a common multiple of them. The common multiples of 3 and 5 are 15, 30, 45, …
  2. A number that is a factor of two numbers is a common factor of them. The common factors of 14 and 36 are 1 and 2. The number 1 is a common factor of any two numbers.
  3. A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself. A composite number has more than two factors. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite.
  4. The Sieve of Eratosthenes finds the primes in a list: circle the next number that is not crossed out, and cross out its multiples. The primes up to 100 begin 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, …
  5. Two numbers are co-prime if their only common factor is 1. For example, 4 and 9 are co-prime, but 15 and 39 are not, because 3 is a factor of both.
  6. Every number greater than 1 can be written as a product of primes. This is its prime factorisation, and it is always the same, whatever order the primes are written in: $56 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 7$.
  7. Two numbers are co-prime if no prime appears in both of their prime factorisations. So 40 and 231 are co-prime.
  8. One number is divisible by another if the prime factorisation of the second is included in that of the first. So 168 is divisible by 12.
  9. Divisibility tests check for a factor without a long division. For 10, 5 and 2, look at the last digit. For 4, look at the number made by the last two digits, and for 8, the last three digits.
