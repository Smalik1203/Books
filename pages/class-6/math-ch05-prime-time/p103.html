Problem 2

Which pair of numbers is co-prime?

  1. 21 and 35
  2. 39 and 91
  3. 25 and 36
  4. 51 and 85
Solution

Find the prime factorisation of every number, and look for a prime that appears in both numbers of a pair.

$21 = 3 \times 7$, $\ 35 = 5 \times 7$: both have 7

$39 = 3 \times 13$, $\ 91 = 7 \times 13$: both have 13

$25 = 5 \times 5$, $\ 36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$: no common prime

$51 = 3 \times 17$, $\ 85 = 5 \times 17$: both have 17

The answer is option (c). 91 and 51 look like primes, but they are not, and each of them shares a prime with the other number in its pair. 25 and 36 are both composite, yet they are co-prime.

Problem 3

The prime factorisation of 360 is

  1. $2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 10$
  2. $2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 4 \times 5$
  3. $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 5$
  4. $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$
Solution

Split 360 into two factors, and keep splitting until only primes are left.

$360 = 36 \times 10$

$36 = 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3$ and $10 = 2 \times 5$

$360 = 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 3 \times 3 \times 5$

The answer is option (d). Options (a) and (b) both multiply to 360, but they are not prime factorisations: option (a) still contains 10, and option (b) contains 4. Option (c) has lost one 3, and multiplies to 120.

Whichever way you split 360, you reach the same primes. Start from $360 = 8 \times 45$ instead: $8 = 2 \times 2 \times 2$ and $45 = 3 \times 3 \times 5$. Or start from $360 = 12 \times 30$: $12 = 2 \times 2 \times 3$ and $30 = 2 \times 3 \times 5$. Both give three 2s, two 3s and one 5, exactly as option (d) says. Multiplying back is always a good last check, because a wrong factorisation, like option (c), gives a different number.
