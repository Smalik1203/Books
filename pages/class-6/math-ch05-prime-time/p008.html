5.3Co-prime Numbers

Which pairs are safe?

Let us go back to Jump Jackpot. Grumpy puts treasures on two numbers, and Jumpy must reach both with the same jump size. This time there is a new rule: a jump size of 1 is not allowed.

Where should Grumpy put the treasures, so that Jumpy can never reach both of them?

Would 12 and 26 work? No. With a jump size of 2, Jumpy lands on both 12 and 26. What about 4 and 9? The factors of 4 are 1, 2 and 4, and the factors of 9 are 1, 3 and 9. The only jump size that lands on both is 1, and it is not allowed. So Grumpy knows that 4 and 9 are a safe pair.

What do the safe pairs have in common? Their only common factor is 1. There is a name for two numbers like this.

Co-prime numbers

Two numbers are co-prime if the only common factor they have is 1.

Example 1

Are 15 and 39 co-prime? Are 4 and 9 co-prime?

List the factors of each pair.

Factors of 15: 1, 3, 5, 15

Factors of 39: 1, 3, 13, 39

3 is a common factor of 15 and 39, so they are not co-prime.

Factors of 4: 1, 2, 4

Factors of 9: 1, 3, 9

The only common factor of 4 and 9 is 1, so 4 and 9 are co-prime.
