2.2Line Segment

Fold a sheet of paper and open it again. The crease is straight, and it stops at the two edges of the paper. It gives you the idea of a line segment.

Mark two points $A$ and $B$ on a sheet of paper. There are many ways to travel from $A$ to $B$. Fig. 2.2 shows a few of them. Which route is the shortest?

The shortest route is the straight one. This straight path from $A$ to $B$, together with the points $A$ and $B$, is called the line segment $AB$. We write it as $\overline{AB}$ or $\overline{BA}$. The points $A$ and $B$ are called the end points of the line segment.

Line segment

A line segment is the shortest path between two points. The line segment $\overline{AB}$ has two end points, $A$ and $B$.

2.3Line

Now imagine that the line segment $\overline{AB}$ is made longer. It is stretched past $A$ in one direction and past $B$ in the other, and it never stops. This gives you the idea of a line.
