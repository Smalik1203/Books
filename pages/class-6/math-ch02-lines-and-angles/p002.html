The shortest route is the straight one. This straight path from $A$ to $B$, together with the points $A$ and $B$, is called the line segment $AB$. We write it as $\overline{AB}$ or $\overline{BA}$. The points $A$ and $B$ are called the end points of the line segment.

2.3Line

Now imagine that the line segment $\overline{AB}$ is made longer. It is stretched past $A$ in one direction and past $B$ in the other, and it never stops. This gives you the idea of a line.

Can you draw the whole of a line? No, you cannot, because it goes on without end. So we draw only a part of it, with an arrowhead at each end. The arrowheads say that the line carries on.

The line through $A$ and $B$ is written as $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$. Sometimes a line is named by one small letter, such as $l$ or $m$.

Two points and a line

Through any two points there is exactly one line.

2.4Ray

A ray is a part of a line that starts at one point and goes on without end in one direction. The point where it starts is called its starting point. A beam of light from a lighthouse, the light from a torch and the rays of the sun are all models of a ray.

Fig. 2.4 shows a ray. Its starting point is $A$, and $P$ is another point on it. We name the ray $\overrightarrow{AP}$. The starting point is always written first.

So a line segment has two end points, a ray has one, and a line has none.
