B4 An obtuse angle is less than $180^\circ$, so half of it is less than $90^\circ$: (i) is true. A reflex angle is between $180^\circ$ and $360^\circ$, so half of it is between $90^\circ$ and $180^\circ$: (iii) is true. Double $30^\circ$ is $60^\circ$, so (ii) is false. The answer is (b).
B5 Three equal parts make the straight angle: $\angle COB$ is one part and $\angle AOC$ is two. So $\angle COB = 180^\circ \div 3 = 60^\circ$, option (b). Option (c) is $\angle AOC$, and option (a) splits the straight angle into two equal parts.
C1 Each angle is a pair of rays. The first ray pairs with the other 4, the next with 3 more, and so on: $4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10$, option (c). Option (a) counts only the angles between rays next to each other.
C4 $\angle PQS = 64^\circ \div 2 = 32^\circ$, and $\angle PQT = 32^\circ \div 2 = 16^\circ$. The angle from $\overrightarrow{QT}$ round to $\overrightarrow{QR}$ is what is left: $64^\circ - 16^\circ = 48^\circ$, option (c). Option (a) is $\angle PQT$, and option (b) is $\angle PQS$.
C5 Double the angle is less than $90^\circ$, so the angle is at most $44^\circ$. Three times the angle is more than $90^\circ$, so the angle is at least $31^\circ$. From $31^\circ$ to $44^\circ$ there are 14 whole numbers, option (b). Option (a) leaves out one end, and option (c) wrongly includes $30^\circ$, whose triple is a right angle.

Most of these questions did not need a protractor. They needed you to see a full turn, a straight angle or a right angle hidden in the question, and to use its measure. When a question about angles looks hard, ask which of these three it is really about.
