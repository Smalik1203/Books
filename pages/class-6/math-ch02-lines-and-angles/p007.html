Exercise Set 2.2
  1. Find angles in things around you, such as a bicycle, a gate, a ladder or a bridge. In Fig. 2.12, one angle of the bicycle frame is $\angle BDC$. Name its vertex and its arms. Then find two more angles in the frame and name them.
  1. Draw an angle whose arms are $\overrightarrow{ST}$ and $\overrightarrow{SR}$. Label it.
  1. In Fig. 2.13, explain why $\angle APB$ cannot be named $\angle P$.
  1. Name the two angles marked in Fig. 2.14.
  1. Mark three points on your paper that are not on one line. Label them $A$, $B$ and $C$. Draw every line that passes through two of these points. How many lines do you get? Name them. How many angles can you name using only the letters $A$, $B$ and $C$? Write them down, and mark each one with a curve.
  1. Now mark four points so that no three of them are on one line. Label them $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$. Draw every line through two of the points. How many lines do you get? Name them. How many angles can you name using only these four letters? Write them all down, and mark each one with a curve.
