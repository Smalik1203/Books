What this chapter showed
  1. A point shows an exact place. It is named by a capital letter.
  2. A line segment is the shortest path between two points. The segment joining $S$ and $T$ is written $\overline{ST}$.
  3. A line is a line segment carried on without end in both directions. It is written $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$, or named by a small letter such as $m$.
  4. A ray starts at a point and goes on without end in one direction. The ray from $D$ through $P$ is written $\overrightarrow{DP}$.
  5. An angle is made by two rays with the same starting point. The rays $\overrightarrow{OP}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ make $\angle POM$, also named $\angle MOP$. Here $O$ is the vertex, and the rays are the arms.
  6. The size of an angle is the amount of turn about the vertex that moves one arm onto the other.
  7. Angles are measured in degrees. A full turn is $360^\circ$. We measure angles with a protractor.
  8. An angle can be acute (more than $0^\circ$ and less than $90^\circ$), right ($90^\circ$), obtuse (more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$), straight ($180^\circ$) or reflex (more than $180^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$).

When you measure an angle, first look at it and decide whether it is acute, obtuse or reflex. Then check your reading against that. A reading of $150^\circ$ for an angle that is clearly acute means you used the wrong scale.
