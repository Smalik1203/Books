2.9Measuring Angles

You can now compare two angles. But can you say how big an angle is with a number, without comparing it with another angle?

You have already used a circle to compare angles. Perhaps a circle can also give angles a measure. Mathematicians took the angle at the centre of a circle, which is one full turn, and divided it into 360 equal parts.

Each of these small equal parts is an angle of 1 degree, written $1^\circ$. We use this unit to measure any angle. The measure of an angle is the number of $1^\circ$ units that fit inside it. The angle in Fig. 2.30 holds 30 of these units, so its measure is $30^\circ$.

Full turn, straight angle and right angle

What is the measure of a full turn? A full turn holds all 360 units, so it measures $360^\circ$.

A straight angle is half of a full turn. Half of $360^\circ$ is $180^\circ$, so a straight angle measures $180^\circ$. Two right angles make a straight angle, so a right angle measures half of $180^\circ$, which is $90^\circ$.
