2.5Angle

An angle is made by two rays that have the same starting point. In Fig. 2.9 the rays $\overrightarrow{BD}$ and $\overrightarrow{BE}$ both start at $B$.

The common starting point $B$ is called the vertex of the angle. The two rays $\overrightarrow{BD}$ and $\overrightarrow{BE}$ are called its arms.

How do we name this angle? We can use the vertex alone and call it angle $B$. To be clearer, we use one point on each arm as well, with the vertex in the middle. So this is angle $DBE$, or angle $EBD$. The symbol $\angle$ stands for the word angle, so we write $\angle DBE$ or $\angle EBD$. The vertex is always the middle letter.

To show which angle we mean, we draw a small curve near the vertex, between the two arms. The curve does not change the angle in any way. It only points to the opening between the arms that we are talking about. You can draw it close to the vertex or a little further out, and it marks the same angle.
