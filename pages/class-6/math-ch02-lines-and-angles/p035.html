Summary
  1. A point shows an exact place. It has no length, width or height. We name it with a capital letter, such as point $P$.
  2. A line segment is the shortest path between two points. The line segment joining $S$ and $T$ is written $\overline{ST}$, and $S$ and $T$ are its end points.
  3. A line is a line segment made longer in both directions without end. The line through $S$ and $T$ is written $\overleftrightarrow{ST}$, or named by one small letter such as $m$. Through any two points there is exactly one line.
  4. A ray starts at a point and goes on without end in one direction. The ray that starts at $D$ and passes through $P$ is written $\overrightarrow{DP}$.
  5. An angle is made by two rays with the same starting point. In $\angle POM$, the point $O$ is the vertex, and the rays $\overrightarrow{OP}$ and $\overrightarrow{OM}$ are the arms. The vertex is always the middle letter.
  6. The size of an angle is the amount of turn about the vertex that moves one arm onto the other. The lengths of the arms do not change it. We can compare two angles by placing one on the other, which is called superimposition.
  7. Angles are measured in degrees with a protractor. A full turn is $360^\circ$, a straight angle is $180^\circ$ and a right angle is $90^\circ$. Two lines that meet at a right angle are perpendicular.
  8. Folding an angle in half bisects it. The crease is the angle bisector, and it makes two equal angles: half of $70^\circ$ is $35^\circ$.
  9. An angle is acute if it is more than $0^\circ$ and less than $90^\circ$, right if it is $90^\circ$, obtuse if it is more than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$, straight if it is $180^\circ$, and reflex if it is more than $180^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$.
