Example 2

Draw $\angle PQR = 125^\circ$.

Solution.

Step 1 Draw the base $\overrightarrow{QR}$ pointing to the right
Step 2 Put the centre of the protractor on $Q$, with $\overrightarrow{QR}$ along the 0 line
Step 3 Count up the inner scale to 120, and then 5 more marks; mark $P$ there its 0 is on the inner scale
Step 4 Join $Q$ to $P$
Answer $\angle PQR = 125^\circ$

Check: $125^\circ$ is more than a right angle, so $\angle PQR$ must open wider than the corner of a page. If it looks acute, you counted on the outer scale and drew $180^\circ - 125^\circ = 55^\circ$ instead of $125^\circ$.

The base does not have to point to the right. When it points to the left, the 0 on the base belongs to the other scale.

Example 3

Draw $\angle XYZ = 40^\circ$, with the base $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ pointing to the left.

Solution.

Step 1 Draw the base $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ pointing to the left
Step 2 Put the centre of the protractor on $Y$, with $\overrightarrow{YZ}$ along the 0 line
Step 3 Count up the outer scale to 40; mark $X$ there its 0 is on the outer scale
Step 4 Join $Y$ to $X$
Answer $\angle XYZ = 40^\circ$

Check: $40^\circ$ is a little less than half of a right angle. If your angle opens wider than a right angle, you counted on the inner scale and drew $180^\circ - 40^\circ = 140^\circ$ instead.

So whichever way the base points, start from the 0 that lies on the base, and count up that scale from there.
