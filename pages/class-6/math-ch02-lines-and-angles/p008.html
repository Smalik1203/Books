2.6Comparing Angles

Watch a crocodile or a bird open its mouth. The jaws are the arms of an angle, and the vertex is where they meet. The wider the mouth opens, the bigger the angle.

Is it always easy to tell which of two angles is bigger? Copy and label the angles in Fig. 2.15. How will you compare them? Draw a few angles of your own and compare those too.

Comparing by superimposing

You can compare two angles by placing one on top of the other. This is called superimposition. Trace one angle and lay the tracing over the other. Put the vertex exactly on the other vertex, and lay one arm along the other arm. Now you can easily see which angle is bigger.

In Fig. 2.16, the arm $\overrightarrow{QP}$ lies outside $\angle ABC$. So $\angle PQR$ is bigger than $\angle ABC$. If $\overrightarrow{QP}$ lay inside $\angle ABC$, $\angle PQR$ would be the smaller angle. If it lay along $\overrightarrow{BA}$, the two angles would be equal.

Comparing angles

Place one angle on the other, vertex on vertex and one arm along one arm. The angle whose other arm lies outside is the bigger angle.
