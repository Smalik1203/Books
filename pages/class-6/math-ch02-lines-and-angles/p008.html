When the two angles are superimposed, the vertices match. The arm $\overrightarrow{OA}$ lies along $\overrightarrow{OX}$, and $\overrightarrow{OB}$ lies along $\overrightarrow{OY}$. So the angles are equal.

Think of each angle as a turn. The turn that moves $\overrightarrow{OB}$ to $\overrightarrow{OA}$ is the same as the turn that moves $\overrightarrow{OY}$ to $\overrightarrow{OX}$. The lengths of the arms play no part.

Superimposing also shows how much bigger one angle is. In Fig. 2.16, once $\angle ABC$ is placed on $\angle PQR$, the arm $\overrightarrow{BA}$ lies inside $\angle PQR$. The part left over, between $\overrightarrow{QA}$ and $\overrightarrow{QP}$, is how much bigger $\angle PQR$ is.

When you superimpose two angles, check three things. The two vertices must sit on the same point. One arm of each angle must lie along the same line. The other two arms must be on the same side of that line. If you miss any one of these, you are not really comparing the two angles.

You can compare angles that cannot be moved, too. To compare the corner of your desk with the corner of your book, trace the corner of the book on thin paper. Then place the tracing on the corner of the desk.

Equal angles

Two angles are equal if, when one is placed on the other, the vertices match and both arms lie along each other.
