The size of an angle

Vidya opens the cover of her book. Fig. 2.10 shows the cover in six positions. In each case, where are the arms of the angle, and where is its vertex?

Which angle is bigger, the one in Case 1 or the one in Case 2? We talk about the length of a line segment. In the same way, we talk about the size of an angle. The size depends on how far the cover has turned.

In Case 2, Vidya turns the cover further than in Case 1, so the angle in Case 2 is bigger. In Case 3 she turns it further still. The angles keep growing up to Case 6, because the turn keeps growing.

The size of an angle

The size of an angle is the amount of turn about the vertex that moves one arm onto the other arm.

Angles made by turning are all around you. When you open a compass or a divider, its arms turn about the joint. The joint is the vertex, and the two legs are the arms. When you open a pair of scissors, the blades make an angle. Look also at a pair of spectacles, a wallet or a box with a lid. Where are the arms? Where is the vertex? In each one, the angle is made by turning one arm away from the other. The further you turn it, the bigger the angle becomes.

Try it with your own arm. Keep your elbow still and raise your hand slowly. Your upper arm and your lower arm make an angle at the elbow, and the angle grows as your hand rises.
