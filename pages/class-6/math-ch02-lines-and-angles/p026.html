Two angle games

Play these games with your classmates, in two teams. They will help you get a feel for the size of an angle.

Game 1: guess the angle.

  • Team 1 secretly chooses a measure, such as $49^\circ$, and draws that angle with a protractor. Team 2 must not see it being drawn.
  • Team 2 looks at the angle, talks it over quickly and guesses its measure, without a protractor.
  • Team 1 measures the angle with a protractor for everyone to see.
  • Team 2 scores the difference between its guess and the true measure. A guess of $39^\circ$ for a $49^\circ$ angle scores 10 points, because $49 - 39 = 10$.
  • Each team has five turns. The team with the lower total score wins.

Game 2: draw the angle.

  • Team 1 calls out a measure, such as $34^\circ$.
  • A player from Team 2 draws that angle on the board without a protractor. The rest of Team 2 may call out make it bigger or make it smaller.
  • A player from Team 1 measures the angle with a protractor for everyone to see.
  • Team 2 scores the difference between the angle drawn and the angle called. An angle of $25^\circ$ drawn for $34^\circ$ scores 9 points, because $34 - 25 = 9$.
  • Each team has five turns. Again, the lower total score wins.

Play the games again on another day. You will find that your guesses get closer each time.

Guessing well

To guess an angle well, compare it with angles you already know. A right angle is the corner of a page. Half of it is $45^\circ$. A third of it is $30^\circ$, and two thirds of it is $60^\circ$.

Suppose an angle opens a little wider than the $45^\circ$ angle but less than the $60^\circ$ one. Then a guess of about $50^\circ$ makes sense. An angle a little wider than a right angle might be about $100^\circ$.
