Lines and Angles

Look at the edge of a ruler, the corner of your desk or the hands of a clock. Each one gives an idea of a line or an angle. In this chapter you will meet points, line segments, lines, rays and angles. They are the building blocks of geometry, and you will use them to study every shape that comes later.

2.1Point

Press the sharp tip of a pencil on paper. You get a tiny dot. The sharper the tip, the smaller the dot. This dot gives you the idea of a point.

A point shows an exact place. It has no length, no width and no height. The tip of a compass, the point of a sharpened pencil and the end of a needle are all good models of a point.

To tell points apart, we name each one with a capital letter. In Fig. 2.1 the points are read as point $Z$, point $T$ and point $P$. Remember that a real point is thinner than any dot you can draw. The dot only shows where the point is.

2.2Line Segment

Fold a sheet of paper and open it again. The crease is straight, and it stops at the two edges of the paper. It gives you the idea of a line segment.
