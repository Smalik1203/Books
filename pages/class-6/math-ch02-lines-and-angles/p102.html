One example is enough to show that a rule like this is wrong. Take $20^\circ$. It is acute. Double it and you get $40^\circ$, which is still acute. So Meena is not right. Double $50^\circ$ and you get $100^\circ$, which is obtuse, and double $45^\circ$ and you get $90^\circ$, a right angle. The answer depends on the angle you start with.

The hand of a clock points halfway between 12 and 3. It moves on, the way a clock’s hands move, until it points to 12. Through how many degrees has it turned?

Count the turn in parts you know. Halfway between 12 and 3 is halfway through a quarter turn, so from there to 3 is half of a right angle, $45^\circ$. From 3 to 6, 6 to 9 and 9 to 12 are three quarter turns: $3 \times 90^\circ = 270^\circ$. Altogether the hand turns $45^\circ + 270^\circ = 315^\circ$, a reflex angle. Turning the other way, from halfway between 12 and 3 back to 12, takes only $45^\circ$. The two turns together make a full turn: $315^\circ + 45^\circ = 360^\circ$.

None of these questions needed a new rule. Each one needed you to see a turn you already knew inside it: a full turn, a straight angle or a right angle.

Example 1 · Single correct

Five distinct points lie on one straight line. How many different line segments have both endpoints among these points?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 5
  2. 10
  3. 20
  4. 15

Solution.

Step 1Choose pairs: the first point gives 4 segments, then 3, 2 and 1 new segments.
Step 24 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10. Reversing the endpoints does not make a new segment.
Answer(b) 10
Example 2 · Single correct

Rays OA, OB and OC occur in that order inside a right angle AOC. If angle AOB is 37°, what is angle BOC?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 37°
  2. 127°
  3. 53°
  4. 143°

Solution.

Step 1The adjacent parts add to the right angle: 37° + angle BOC = 90°.
Step 2Angle BOC = 90° − 37° = 53°.
Answer(c) 53°
