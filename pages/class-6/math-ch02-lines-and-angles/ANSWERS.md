### Think and Reflect (after the key idea *Equal angles*)

1. Line segments are compared by placing one along the other with one end
   on the other's end; shapes are compared by placing one on top of the
   other, as when checking whether two cut-outs are the same.
2. If the vertices do not match, the arms of the tracing do not start at
   $O$. An arm can then cross the arm of $\angle AOB$ or lie beside it, and
   Raju may decide that equal angles are unequal, or the wrong one is
   bigger.

### Exercise Set 2.3

1. Answers will vary. A crease and a side of the paper make two angles on
   each side of the crease, and the two angles at one point together make a
   straight angle. A fold at right angles to a side makes two equal angles;
   any other fold makes one angle bigger and one smaller. The more slanting
   the crease, the smaller the smallest angle.

2. In Fig. 2.18, $\overrightarrow{OA}$ is at 90°, $\overrightarrow{OX}$ at
   60° and $\overrightarrow{OY}$ at 26° from the bottom ray:
   - (i) **$\angle AOB$** is bigger: $\angle AOB = 90°$ and $\angle XOY = 34°$;
   - (ii) **$\angle AOB$** is bigger: $\overrightarrow{OX}$ lies inside
     $\angle AOB$, so $\angle XOB$ (60°) is only part of it;
   - (iii) **they are equal**: $B$ and $C$ lie on the same ray, so
     $\angle XOB$ and $\angle XOC$ are the same angle, 60°.

3. **They are equal.** Both angles contain $\angle AOY$. $\angle XOY$ is
   $\angle AOY$ with $\angle XOA$ added, and $\angle AOB$ is $\angle AOY$
   with $\angle YOB$ added; $\angle XOA$ and $\angle YOB$ are both drawn at
   36°. Measured, $\angle XOY = \angle AOB = 88°$.

## 2.9 Measuring Angles

### Think and Reflect (Fig. 2.32)

One part in each circle: 1 part $360°$; 2 parts $360 \div 2 = 180$°;
3 parts $360 \div 3 = 120$°; 4 parts $360 \div 4 = 90$°; 5 parts
$360 \div 5 = 72$°; 6 parts $360 \div 6 = 60$°; 8 parts $360 \div 8 = 45$°;
9 parts $360 \div 9 = 40$°; 10 parts $360 \div 10 = 36$°; 12 parts
$360 \div 12 = 30$°. The marked curve in each circle is drawn at exactly that
angle.
