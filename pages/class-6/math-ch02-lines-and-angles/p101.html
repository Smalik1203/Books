Using What You Know

The chapter gave you points, lines and rays, and a way to measure the turn between two rays. Every question below uses only those ideas. What is different is that no question tells you which idea to use. Try each one before you read what follows it.

The hand of a clock points to 12. It moves on, the way a clock’s hands move, until it points halfway between 6 and 9. Through how many degrees has it turned?

Break the turn into parts you know. From 12 to 3 is a quarter turn, $90^\circ$. From 3 to 6 is another $90^\circ$. Halfway between 6 and 9 is halfway through the next quarter turn, so from 6 to there is half of a right angle, $45^\circ$. Altogether the hand turns $90^\circ + 90^\circ + 45^\circ = 225^\circ$. This is more than a straight angle, so it is a reflex angle. Notice that you did not need a protractor. Quarter turns and half turns were enough.

A ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ divides a straight angle $\angle AOB$ into two angles. One of them is $50^\circ$ bigger than the other. Find both angles.

The two angles together make the straight angle, so they add up to $180^\circ$. Take away the extra $50^\circ$ first: $180^\circ - 50^\circ = 130^\circ$. What is left is two equal angles, so each is $65^\circ$. The smaller angle is $65^\circ$, and the bigger one is $65^\circ + 50^\circ = 115^\circ$. Check: $65^\circ + 115^\circ = 180^\circ$. The straight angle gave you a total, and the total did the work.

Five rays $\overrightarrow{OA}$, $\overrightarrow{OB}$, $\overrightarrow{OC}$, $\overrightarrow{OD}$ and $\overrightarrow{OE}$ start at $O$, one after another, all above a straight line through $O$. How many angles can you name using these rays?

Each angle uses two of the rays as its arms. So count the pairs. $\overrightarrow{OA}$ pairs with $\overrightarrow{OB}$, $\overrightarrow{OC}$, $\overrightarrow{OD}$ and $\overrightarrow{OE}$: that is 4 angles. $\overrightarrow{OB}$ pairs with $\overrightarrow{OC}$, $\overrightarrow{OD}$ and $\overrightarrow{OE}$: 3 more. $\overrightarrow{OC}$ pairs with $\overrightarrow{OD}$ and $\overrightarrow{OE}$: 2 more. $\overrightarrow{OD}$ pairs with $\overrightarrow{OE}$: 1 more. That makes $4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10$ angles. With 6 rays you would get $5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 15$. These are the triangular numbers from Chapter 1.
