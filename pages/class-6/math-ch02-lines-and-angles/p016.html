Example 1

Use Fig. 2.33 to find the measure of $\angle KAL$.

The vertex $A$ is on the centre of the protractor. So count the $1^\circ$ units between the arms $\overrightarrow{AL}$ and $\overrightarrow{AK}$. From $\overrightarrow{AL}$ to $\overrightarrow{AK}$ there are three long marks, one every 10 units.

$\angle KAL = 10^\circ + 10^\circ + 10^\circ = 30^\circ$

You could also count the medium marks. There are six of them from $\overrightarrow{AL}$ to $\overrightarrow{AK}$, one every 5 units, and $6 \times 5 = 30$. Counting in 5s and 10s like this is much quicker than counting every unit.

Counting marks works, but it is slow for a big angle, and it is easy to lose count. Numbers on the scale save you the counting. The protractor in your geometry box has them, as you will see next. With numbers, you read an angle instead of counting it.
