2.8Special Types of Angles

Think again about Vidya and her book. When she holds the book in her hand to write, she folds the cover all the way round to the back. The cover makes a full turn.

When she opens the book flat on her table, the cover makes a half turn. Now the two arms of the angle lie along one straight line. An angle like this is called a straight angle.

Take a straight angle $\angle AOB$. Draw any ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ from $O$. It divides the straight angle into two angles, $\angle AOC$ and $\angle COB$.

Can you draw $\overrightarrow{OC}$ so that the two angles are equal? Try it with paper. A fold in paper always makes a straight crease, which will help.

Take a rectangular sheet of paper. Mark a straight angle $\angle AOB$ along one of its sides. Now fold the paper through $O$ so that $\overrightarrow{OB}$ lies exactly on $\overrightarrow{OA}$. Open it out and look at the crease.

The two angles at $O$ are equal, because the fold laid one of them exactly on top of the other. Each of these equal angles is called a right angle. So a straight angle is made of two right angles.

A straight angle is half of a full turn. A right angle is half of that, so it is a quarter of a full turn. A right angle looks like the corner of the letter L. An angle is a right angle only if it is exactly half of a straight angle.
