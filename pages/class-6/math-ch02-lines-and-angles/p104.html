Example 4

Ria traces $\angle X$ on thin paper. She lays the tracing on $\angle Y$, and then on $\angle Z$, each time with vertex on vertex, one arm along one arm, and the other arms on the same side. On $\angle Y$, the free arm of the tracing lies outside $\angle Y$. On $\angle Z$, it lies exactly along the other arm of $\angle Z$. Put the three angles in order of size.

Solution.

Step 1 on $\angle Y$ the tracing reaches past the arm, so $\angle X$ is bigger than $\angle Y$ it covers $\angle Y$
Step 2 on $\angle Z$ both pairs of arms lie along each other, so $\angle X = \angle Z$ equal angles
Step 3 $\angle Z$ is the same size as $\angle X$, so it is also bigger than $\angle Y$
Answer $\angle Y$ is the smallest; $\angle X$ and $\angle Z$ are equal, and bigger

Ria never measured anything, and the lengths of the arms played no part.

Type 4 · Turns, right angles and straight angles

Example 5

The hand of a clock points to 6. It moves on, the way a clock’s hands move, through three right angles. Where does it point now? What part of a full turn has it made?

Solution.

Step 1 6 to 9, 9 to 12, 12 to 3 three quarter turns
Step 2 $3 \times 90^\circ = 270^\circ$
Step 3 four right angles make a full turn, so three make three quarters of it
Answer It points to 3. It has made three quarters of a full turn, $270^\circ$.
Example 6

A ray $\overrightarrow{OC}$ divides the straight angle $\angle AOB$ so that $\angle AOC$ is 5 times $\angle COB$. Find both angles.

Solution.

Step 1 $\angle COB$ is 1 part and $\angle AOC$ is 5 parts: 6 equal parts in all together a straight angle
Step 2 one part $= 180^\circ \div 6 = 30^\circ$
Step 3 $\angle AOC = 5 \times 30^\circ = 150^\circ$
Answer $\angle COB = 30^\circ$ and $\angle AOC = 150^\circ$

Check: $150^\circ + 30^\circ = 180^\circ$, the straight angle.
