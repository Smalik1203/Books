1 · Using What You Know

Try each question in your notebook before reading its explanation.

Use the chapter’s ideas where evidence is incomplete or a first explanation seems convincing. Decide what each observation can actually establish.

1. Two dry seeds look alike. One is viable and one was killed by strong heating. Both swell on moist tissue. Has this proved that both are alive? What later observation would help?

No. Water can enter material that is no longer living. Swelling shows water uptake, not necessarily the growth of an embryo. If a young root emerges and continues to grow, it provides much stronger evidence of germination. The viable seed may do this; the dead seed cannot resume life processes.

A seed that has not germinated after a short wait is still an uncertain case. It may need more time or different conditions. To interpret its failure, compare it with several known viable seeds of the same kind growing in suitable conditions. Notice how a comparison makes an absence of change more meaningful.
