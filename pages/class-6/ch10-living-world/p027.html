Observe with care

Caring for living things includes caring for the places that support their stages. Keep rubbish out of ponds, avoid damaging plants during observations and prevent unwanted water collection around buildings. Curiosity and care can belong to the same investigation.

Explain a shared habitat

A learner sees mosquito larvae and tadpoles in the same water and concludes that one will grow into the other. The shared habitat does not support this claim:

  • The two belong to different kinds of animals.
  • Each follows its own developmental sequence, and each adult produces offspring of its own kind.

In your notebook, draw two separate routes from eggs to adults. Put the mosquito’s pupa on one route and the frog’s leg-bearing tadpole on the other. Add a reproduction arrow to each. This diagram should show what the cycles share without joining stages that do not belong together.

Read a photograph carefully

A photograph preserves one view at one moment. It can show a root, a leaf or an animal’s body form, but cannot by itself show how quickly that organism grew or what happened next.

Choose two photographs from this chapter. For each, write one feature you can actually see and one question the picture cannot answer. For example, an adult frog photograph shows legs, but does not reveal the date on which its tail disappeared. A dated series of observations would provide better evidence for that change.

Words to use accurately

Observation: something noticed or measured. Prediction: what you expect before a check. Experiment: a planned investigation of a question. Conclusion: what the evidence supports, with limits stated.

Stimulus: a change that prompts a response. Germination: the beginning of seed growth into a seedling. Habitat: the place and conditions in which an organism lives. Metamorphosis: major changes of body form during development.
