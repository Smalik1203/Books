Germination is the start of growth of the embryo into a seedling. The seed takes up water and swells, the coat opens, and a young root usually emerges first. Swelling alone is not reliable evidence: even a dead seed can soak up water.

What starts the change?

A viable seed needs three conditions for germination:

  • Water allows growth processes to resume and softens the coat.
  • Oxygen supports respiration, releasing energy from stored food.
  • A suitable temperature allows these processes to work. Too cold or too hot can delay or prevent germination.

Light requirements vary. Many seeds, including beans and gram, can germinate without light. Some kinds germinate better in light, while others need darkness. Follow guidance for the seed kind being tested.

Germination and later growth

A bean can begin growing in darkness using its reserves. For continued healthy growth, its green seedling needs light to make food, as well as water, air and suitable nutrients.
