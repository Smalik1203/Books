A plant that catches insects

Sundews, also called Drosera, have leaves with sticky, hair-like structures. A small insect caught on a leaf can trigger movement that helps hold it. Digesting insects supplies extra nutrients, especially in nutrient-poor habitats. The plant still makes sugars using light; catching insects does not replace photosynthesis.

Growth is a change in the living body

Your shoes may become too small as your feet grow. A seedling adds roots, stem and leaves. These are examples of growth. Development includes changes in form and ability: a plant begins to flower, or a tadpole develops legs. Growth and development often occur together, but they do not mean exactly the same thing.

Nutrition

Nutrition is the way organisms obtain and use food and other needed materials. Food provides materials for growth and repair, and supplies energy released during respiration.

Green plants use light energy to make food from water and carbon dioxide. They also take mineral nutrients from their surroundings.

Animals obtain food by eating plants, other animals or their products.

Roots take up water and minerals; they do not normally take ready-made food from soil.
