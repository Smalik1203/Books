Plants respire too

Plants have no lungs, but their living cells need energy. Gases can pass through tiny pores called stomata on leaves and through other plant surfaces. Respiration occurs in living plant parts during both day and night. Photosynthesis requires light; respiration does not stop when photosynthesis starts. A teacher may show stomata on a prepared microscope slide.

Keeping the body’s conditions suitable

Life processes produce wastes. Excretion is the removal of metabolic wastes and excess substances from the body. Animals remove carbon dioxide in breathed-out air; urine carries dissolved wastes, and sweat contains water and salts. Passing undigested food is different from removing wastes made by cells.

Plants also manage wastes. Gases may leave through surfaces, and some unwanted substances are stored in parts that are later shed. Droplets at some leaf tips contain water and dissolved minerals. These droplets can be pushed out by the plant; they are not the same as dew that condenses from the air. A droplet’s appearance alone cannot tell you its source.

Find a stoma. Look for a narrow dark pore between two guard cells. A pore provides a passage for gases; the still photograph does not measure gas movement.
