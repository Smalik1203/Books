Investigate

A pond from a safe distance

Question: which visible features distinguish the stages you see? Prediction: sketch the features you expect in a tadpole and a froglet before viewing.

Procedure: with an adult, observe from a firm, safe edge, or use a teacher-provided pond video. Sketch only stages you can identify. Note body shape, legs, tail and the place where each animal is seen. Do not label an unknown water animal “tadpole” simply because it wriggles.

Record: use one entry for each sighting. Leave identity uncertain if the features are unclear.

Think It Through

Read a sample record

The observed interval from tadpole to froglet is 54 days; from froglet to tailless young frog it is 20 days. A young frog is not necessarily a reproducing adult. These are intervals between recorded sightings, not exact stage durations, because a change may have occurred between visits.

To estimate durations more closely, observe more frequently. Do not conclude that all frogs follow this timetable. Species, water temperature and food supply can change the timing.
