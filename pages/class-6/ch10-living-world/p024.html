Which arrow introduces a new generation? Which stages still have a tail?

Check your reasoning. The dashed adult-to-eggs arrow marks reproduction. The tadpole, hind-leg stage and froglet have tails, with the tail much reduced in the froglet.

A froglet looks like a small frog but may retain a shrinking tail. Lungs develop and the animal becomes able to spend more time out of water. The tail is gradually absorbed by the body. Stronger legs support swimming and jumping. The adult in this familiar pattern lives in and around water and breathes using lungs and moist skin.

The adult produces the next generation of eggs. A froglet is not yet a reproducing adult. Frogs have varied life histories, so this pond example should not be treated as a description of every frog species.

Compare the photographs by body features, not by image size: each picture has a different scale.

Changing body, changing way of life

The tadpole’s tail and gills support an aquatic life. Developing legs, lungs and a changing body allow the young frog to use a different range of places. Structure and habitat are connected.

Put observations in order

Look for a change that links one stage to the next: an embryo developing in an egg, a tail after hatching, hind legs, front legs, then a shrinking tail. A larger animal is not automatically a later stage if you are comparing different individuals or different species.
