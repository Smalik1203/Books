3. A learner draws the arrow “adult frog → eggs” and labels it “the frog changes shape”. What should the label say, and why?

The label should say “reproduction”. An adult does not change into a cluster of eggs. Eggs begin a new generation, while the parent remains a separate individual. Arrows from egg to tadpole and from tadpole to froglet describe development of an individual; the return arrow has a different role.

This distinction also applies to a plant diagram. A mature bean plant forms seeds inside pods. The next seedling grows from one of those seeds; it is not the old plant starting its life again. A cycle represents continuity across generations, not an individual living forever.

4. A class compares seeds beside a sunny window with seeds in a cupboard. The window group is warmer and its tissue dries faster. Can a difference in germination be assigned to light?

No. Light, temperature and moisture all differ. Any of these could contribute to the outcome. Keep both groups at similar measured temperatures, maintain equal moisture, and arrange light and dark treatments without changing the other conditions. A ventilated cover in the same room can help, provided it does not warm or dry the treatment differently.

Use the same seed kind and several seeds in each group. Check daily. You cannot make a comparison perfect merely by naming it an experiment; the actual conditions must support the question being asked.

5. An adult animal is no longer increasing much in size. It breathes, eats and responds normally. Is “no visible growth” enough to call it non-living?

No. Growth rates change with age, and not every life process is visible at every moment. The animal is maintaining its body and carrying out other life processes. Likewise, an individual that never produces offspring is still living. The living world is recognised by a pattern of processes over a life history.

A useful test of an explanation is to try it on more than one example. “Everything alive must be visibly growing now” fails for this adult and for a dormant seed. Revise the rule so that it explains the evidence without excluding ordinary living organisms.
