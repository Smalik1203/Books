Answer: C. An egg hatches into a larva; the larva develops into a pupa; an adult emerges from the pupa. A reverses larva and pupa. B begins with a developing larva producing an egg, which is not the sequence. D places the pupa after the adult.

A photograph of several stages together is useful for identifying shapes. Evidence of one stage becoming another is needed to confirm their order.

A single picture can identify a form, but it cannot usually establish how that form developed. A dated series following the same organism connects earlier and later observations. A drawing of the final adult alone cannot tell us which immature stage came just before it.

Problem 5

A school wants to reduce mosquito breeding in unused water containers. Which action best follows from the life cycle?

1. Keep the water until the adults emerge.

2. Ask adults to empty and scrub suitable containers regularly.

3. Collect larvae and raise them in class.

4. Pour kerosene into nearby ponds.

Explanation

Answer: B. Removing breeding water and cleaning suitable containers interrupts development and removes eggs that may remain on surfaces. A allows development to continue. C creates an avoidable breeding activity. D introduces a harmful household chemical into a habitat; it is not a student action.

The question concerns unused artificial containers. It does not justify draining natural ponds. A good application preserves the conditions in the question.

Read the reasoning as well as the letter

The five answers depended on five distinctions: soaking versus germination; starting growth versus continuing growth; starting position versus new growth; coexisting stages versus an observed sequence; and a biological explanation versus a suitable action. Each distinction comes from the lesson rather than from a new topic.
