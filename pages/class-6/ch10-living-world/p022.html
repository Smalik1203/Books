From the experiment to the grain jar

Keeping stored grains and pulses dry discourages germination and also reduces spoilage. Dry them properly before putting them into a clean, dry storage container. Protect them from moisture and pests. A grain that is not sprouting is not necessarily dead; it may be waiting for conditions that allow growth.

If no seeds sprout in any group, the investigation has not shown that water is unnecessary. Check seed viability and conditions, then repeat. A suitable comparison group that germinates gives meaning to groups that fail.

10.8 Which way will a seedling grow?

A seed can lie in many positions in the ground. Yet its root generally grows downwards and its shoot upwards. If we gently turn a young seedling, the new growing parts can bend. The root does not need to “know” where the bottom of a pot is: gravity is a directional stimulus. Observe the direction of the new tip as the young plant grows.
