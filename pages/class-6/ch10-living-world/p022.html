Use the life cycle to prevent breeding

Some mosquitoes transmit infections, including malaria, dengue and chikungunya; different kinds transmit different infections. Knowing where young mosquitoes develop helps us act before new adults emerge. Ask an adult to inspect flowerpot saucers, stored buckets, cooler trays and other artificial containers that collect water.

Once a week, adults should empty and scrub suitable water-holding items, turn unused containers upside down and cover water that must be stored. Record each location, the action taken and a date for checking it again. Natural ponds support many organisms and should not be emptied as a school activity.

An oil film can interfere with air access at the water surface, which explains an example sometimes discussed in textbooks. It is not a classroom treatment: do not pour kerosene or other oils into water. Report persistent breeding sites to responsible adults or local mosquito-control services.

Another insect with four stages

A silk moth also develops through egg, larva, pupa and adult. Its larva, the silkworm, produces a silk thread and wraps itself in a cocoon before becoming a pupa. The cocoon is a protective covering; the pupa is the developing insect inside. The fibres are used to make silk. India’s village-industry organisations, including KVIC, are connected with silk-related livelihoods.

A butterfly follows the same broad four-stage pattern, but its caterpillar, pupa and adult look different from a mosquito’s. Shared stage names do not mean identical bodies or habitats.
