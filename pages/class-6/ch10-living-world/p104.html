The strongest conclusion is often the one whose scope matches the experiment. A broader statement is not automatically a better explanation.

Problem 3

A seedling with an upward-pointing root is kept moist in even light. Several days later, what is most likely?

1. The root grows straight up.

2. New root growth bends down.

3. The whole root rotates at once.

4. The root grows toward the shoot.

Explanation

Answer: B. A young root generally responds to gravity by growing downward. Turning the seedling changes its starting position, not gravity’s direction. A overlooks the growth response, C mistakes new growth for rotation of the whole root, and D assigns the root the shoot’s usual direction.

The existing root may retain a bend. We predict the direction of new growth, not that all older tissue will suddenly rotate.

A gradual change needs observations across time. The same is true when we investigate the order of animal stages.
