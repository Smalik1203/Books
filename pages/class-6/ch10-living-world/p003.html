10.2 Looking for signs of life

A wind-up toy moves across the floor. A puddle becomes larger in the rain. Neither observation, by itself, establishes that something is living. Movement can be caused by a motor, wind or a push. A puddle grows because more water joins it. Living growth involves processes within an organism: it uses materials and energy to build and maintain its body.

An organism is a living thing. Organisms take in or make food, respire, remove wastes and respond to their surroundings. Over their lives they grow and develop. Living kinds continue through reproduction. These processes form a connected pattern; we should not make a decision from one feature alone.

Activity 10.1

Improve a first guess

1. Choose six things you can observe: for example, a potted herb, an ant, a spoon, a dry seed, a fallen twig and a battery toy.

2. Record: make four notebook columns: thing; first decision; evidence; revised decision. Leave room for observations over several days.

3. Describe evidence, such as “new leaves appeared”, rather than repeating the decision as “because it is alive”.

4. Conclusion: for an uncertain case, write what you would need to observe next. Do not invent evidence to complete a row.
