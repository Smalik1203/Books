Investigate

Improve a first guess

1. Choose six things you can observe: for example, a potted herb, an ant, a spoon, a dry seed, a fallen twig and a battery toy.

2. Record: make four notebook columns: thing; first decision; evidence; revised decision. Leave room for observations over several days.

3. Describe evidence, such as “new leaves appeared”, rather than repeating the decision as “because it is alive”.

4. Conclusion: for an uncertain case, write what you would need to observe next. Do not invent evidence to complete a row.

Living now, once living, never living

A growing tree is living. A fallen, dried piece of its wood is no longer a living organism, though it came from one. A stone was never living. Paper and cotton cloth also come from living sources; their origin does not make the finished objects alive. A cut stem may still contain living tissue and can sometimes grow roots, so examine the particular object rather than its name alone.

Think It Through

A toy that follows light

A light-sensitive toy turns towards a lamp. That is a response, but its body does not grow through life processes or carry out respiration. A designed response does not supply all the other evidence needed to identify an organism.
