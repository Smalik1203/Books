Reading what happened

We would usually expect little or no germination in dry group A. Seeds in moist groups C and D should often germinate, showing that these seeds can begin growth without light. Submerged group B may germinate poorly because oxygen becomes harder to obtain. These are predictions, not results to copy into an observation column.

Think It Through

Invented example data

Imagine A has 0 sprouts, B has 1, C has 4 and D has 5 after seven days. C is the important comparison for the light question: four seeds sprouted in darkness. The difference between four and five is too small, in these tiny groups, to establish that light improved germination.

Write: “In this trial, gram seeds germinated in both light and darkness.” Do not write: “Every seed germinates in every dark place.” Temperature, moisture, oxygen and seed condition still matter.

Why moist soil works

Loose soil contains air spaces as well as water. Roots can grow through these spaces. Flooding fills many spaces with water, making oxygen less available. Soil supports a developing plant and supplies minerals, but soil itself is not essential for the first emergence of a root: seeds can germinate on moist tissue. The moist tissue must still allow air to reach the seeds.
