Recognising life through evidence of change

A snail rests on a stone. A seedling stands in damp soil. Both may look still, yet both are alive. Water moves when the ground slopes, but movement alone does not make it living.

Start with a question

A seed and a pebble sit still. What changes could you look for over time to test for life?

10.1 A pebble, a seed and a beetle

Meera places a smooth pebble beside a dry green gram seed. A beetle crosses the windowsill and disappears behind a pot. The beetle makes the easiest decision: it is alive. But the seed and the pebble both sit still. Does that put them in the same group?

A week later, the pebble looks much the same. The seed, kept on damp cloth, has opened. A pale root reaches out and a shoot begins to rise. Something important was happening inside a thing that had looked inactive. This chapter is about noticing such evidence, including changes too slow to see in one glance.
