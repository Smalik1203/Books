How can we find the correct order?

A photograph showing larvae and pupae together tells us that both occur in the container. It does not, by itself, prove which becomes which. To establish a sequence, we need observations of change through time.

Activity 10.9

Reconstruct a sequence safely

Question: which evidence connects stages? Prediction: write the stage order you expect.

Procedure: use a teacher-provided video or dated picture series that follows individual mosquitoes through development. Record the starting shape, the later shape and the date for each tracked individual. Conclusion: if an elongated larva becomes a pupa, and a pupa later releases an adult, those transitions establish the order.

For a mixed group, repeatedly counting larvae and pupae is less certain: new eggs may hatch and some individuals may die. A continuous recording of a particular individual, or controlled observations supplied by a trained observer, gives stronger evidence.
