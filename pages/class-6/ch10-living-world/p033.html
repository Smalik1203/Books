The cycle in words and pictures

Make a six-frame strip: eggs, embryo in egg, tadpole, tadpole with hind legs, froglet and adult. Draw a final arrow from the adult to new eggs and label it “reproduction”. Write one original sentence under each frame. You can turn the sentences into a short poem, but check that every stage remains scientifically accurate.

Notice that the table groups embryos with eggs and leg-bearing tadpoles with tadpoles. Six drawings and four broad stages need not disagree. They can describe the same continuous development at different levels of detail.

10.12 Patterns across the living world

Plants and animals both grow, develop, use resources and reproduce. A life cycle connects one generation to the next. The stages differ, and some young organisms resemble adults much more closely than others do.
