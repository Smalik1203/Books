One animal, changing form

The larva and pupa are not two different animals. They are successive forms in one mosquito’s development. In many common mosquitoes, both obtain air at the water surface. A larva is usually elongated; the pupa has a larger rounded front and a curled abdomen. A pupa is not a dead larva, even though its feeding and appearance change.

Four stages

Egg → larva → pupa → adult → eggs of the next generation. Major changes in body form during development are called metamorphosis.

Temperature, food, species and other conditions affect development and adult lifespan. There is no single number of days that describes every mosquito. Use dates from a particular observation record when answering a timing question.
