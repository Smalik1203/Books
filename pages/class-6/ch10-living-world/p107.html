Set B · Put two ideas together

  1. A wind-up toy moves; a seed stays still. Which statement is best?

    1. Only the toy is living.
    2. Neither is living.
    3. Motion alone cannot decide.
    4. Both must be living.

  1. Moist seeds germinate on tissue. What follows?

    1. Plants never need minerals.
    2. Roots absorb food from soil.
    3. Seeds do not need oxygen.
    4. Soil is not needed to sprout.

  1. Why can a bean seed start growing in darkness?

    1. Stored food supports growth.
    2. Water supplies all its food.
    3. Soil food is already absorbed.
    4. Germination needs no energy.

  1. In Figure 10.7, which change links tadpole and froglet?

    1. Legs replace the tail at once.
    2. Legs develop; the tail shrinks.
    3. The tail grows into hind legs.
    4. The tail drops off before legs form.

  1. Which comparison tests water supply most fairly?

    1. Different seeds and rooms
    2. Dry-cold versus moist-warm
    3. Same seeds and heat; vary water
    4. Dry-light versus moist-dark

  1. A dry wooden ruler came from a tree. Which explanation is best?

    1. Its tree origin makes it alive.
    2. Bending makes it alive.
    3. Stillness means death.
    4. Origin does not show life now.

For each answer, name the two ideas you used. Check that neither part of your explanation contradicts the other.

When checking the key, follow the reason as well as the answer letter. Use the explanation to revise any connection between ideas that you missed.

For an incorrect option, explain which observation or chapter idea would rule it out.
