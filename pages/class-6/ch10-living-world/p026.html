Reading a life diary

A diary records events as they occur, not as a timetable says they should occur. Light, temperature, water, variety and seed condition affect the pace. If no flowers appear by the final observation, write that honestly and continue if possible. A missing event is a result, not an empty space to fill with an imagined date.

Root emergesDate; sketch of rootGermination has begun
First leaves openNumber; appearanceSeedling development
Flower opensDate; number of flowersA reproductive stage
Pod developsPosition; size changesFruit formation
Seeds matureSeed count; appearancePossible next generation

A photograph beside each dated sketch can help you compare small changes without disturbing the plant.

Not every new plant begins with a seed

Seeds are one route to a new plant, but they are not the only one. A potato tuber can sprout from a bud, and a suitable stem cutting, such as money plant, can form roots. These are examples of reproduction from plant parts. A florist’s cut flower and a rooted cutting therefore should not be judged only by whether they are detached from a plant.
