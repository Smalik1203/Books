Does temperature matter?

Question: does temperature affect germination? Prediction: state which group you expect to sprout first and why.

Procedure: use two groups of ten seeds of one kind on equally moist tissue. With an adult, place one group in a cool location and the other in a warmer, suitable room location. Keep light exposure and air access alike. Record the temperature in each place and count emerging roots daily for a week.

Record: date, temperature, moisture and roots visible out of ten. Conclusion: compare counts and time to the first root in each group.

Repeat with fresh seeds if possible. Compare both how many germinate and how soon they do. Check moisture daily: a warmer group may dry faster. Two temperatures can show a difference, but cannot reveal the best temperature across the whole possible range.

Care while exploring.

Record root emergence separately from leaf opening: they are different events in development.

From the experiment to the grain jar

Keeping stored grains and pulses dry discourages germination and also reduces spoilage. Dry them properly before putting them into a clean, dry storage container. Protect them from moisture and pests. A grain that is not sprouting is not necessarily dead; it may be waiting for conditions that allow growth.

If no seeds sprout in any group, the investigation has not shown that water is unnecessary. Check seed viability and conditions, then repeat. A suitable comparison group that germinates gives meaning to groups that fail.
