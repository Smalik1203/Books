2 · Behind Each Answer

Choose one answer before reading the reasoning. Each problem has four options.

Problem 1

Which observation gives the strongest evidence that a dry seed has germinated?

  1. It darkens when wet.
  2. It gains weight on soaking.
  3. A root emerges and grows.
  4. Its coat peels off.

Explanation

Answer: C. Germination involves renewed growth of the embryo. An emerging root that grows is evidence of that process. The other observations can result from water entering and softening a seed, even if the embryo is no longer alive.

A good answer identifies what is being tested. The question concerns germination, not simply whether water entered the seed.

Now distinguish the start of growth from the needs of an established seedling.

Problem 2

Two groups of the same viable seeds are kept equally moist and warm. One is in light and one in darkness. Both germinate. What is supported?

  1. These seeds can sprout in darkness.
  2. Darkness makes germination faster.
  3. Light is needed for the first root.
  4. Later growth needs no light.

Explanation

Answer: A. The result concerns the tested seed kind and the beginning of growth. It does not describe every plant or later development. B claims a speed difference that was not reported. C contradicts the dark group’s germination. D confuses germination with the light needs of continued growth.

The strongest conclusion is often the one whose scope matches the experiment. A broader statement is not automatically a better explanation.
