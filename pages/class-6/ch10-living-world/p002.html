Our question

How can observations help us distinguish living things from non-living things, and follow a living thing from one stage of its life to another?

What you will learn

Use several characteristics together to recognise life.

Explain nutrition, growth, respiration, excretion, response and reproduction.

Investigate what seeds need and how roots and shoots grow.

Follow the life cycles of a flowering plant, a mosquito and a frog.

Make predictions, record observations and explain conclusions.

You will need a notebook for sketches and dated records. Write what you expect before an investigation, then record what actually happens. A result that surprises you is useful evidence; it is not a reason to change your record.

10.2 Looking for signs of life

A wind-up toy moves across the floor. A puddle becomes larger in the rain. Neither observation, by itself, establishes that something is living. Movement can be caused by a motor, wind or a push. A puddle grows because more water joins it. Living growth involves processes within an organism: it uses materials and energy to build and maintain its body.

An organism is a living thing. Organisms take in or make food, respire, remove wastes and respond to their surroundings. Over their lives they grow and develop. Living kinds continue through reproduction. These processes form a connected pattern; we should not make a decision from one feature alone.
