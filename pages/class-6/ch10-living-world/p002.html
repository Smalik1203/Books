10.1 A pebble, a seed and a beetle

Meera places a smooth pebble beside a dry green gram seed. A beetle crosses the windowsill and disappears behind a pot. The beetle makes the easiest decision: it is alive. But the seed and the pebble both sit still. Does that put them in the same group?

A week later, the pebble looks much the same. The seed, kept on damp cloth, has opened. A pale root reaches out and a shoot begins to rise. Something important was happening inside a thing that had looked inactive. This chapter is about noticing such evidence, including changes too slow to see in one glance.

Our question

How can observations help us distinguish living things from non-living things, and follow a living thing from one stage of its life to another?

What you will learn

Use several characteristics together to recognise life.

Explain nutrition, growth, respiration, excretion, response and reproduction.

Investigate what seeds need and how roots and shoots grow.

Follow the life cycles of a flowering plant, a mosquito and a frog.

Make predictions, record observations and explain conclusions.

You will need a notebook for sketches and dated records. Write what you expect before an investigation, then record what actually happens. A result that surprises you is useful evidence; it is not a reason to change your record.
