Type 6 · Graphs that mislead

Example 11

Fig. 4.27 shows the points of three teams. (a) How many times Team A’s points does Team C have? (b) How many times as tall as Team A’s bar does Team C’s bar look? (c) What is wrong with the graph?

Solution.

Step 1 (a) Team A 20 points, Team C 100 points; $100 \div 20 = 5$ read the numbers
Step 2 (b) Team A’s bar is 2 steps tall and Team C’s is 4 steps; $4 \div 2 = 2$ count the steps
Step 3 (c) the steps stand for 10, 10, 30 and 50 points unequal steps
Answer (a) 5 times (b) 2 times (c) equal steps on a scale must stand for equal amounts, and here they do not.
Example 12

On a bar graph with 1 unit of length for 10 runs, Arjun’s bar is 4 units tall and 2 units wide, and Bilal’s bar is 5 units tall and 1 unit wide. Which statement is true?

  1. Bilal scored 10 more runs.
  2. Arjun scored more; his bar is bigger.
  3. Both scored 40 runs.
  4. Arjun scored twice as many.

Solution.

Step 1 Only the height of a bar gives its number width means nothing
Step 2 Arjun: $4 \times 10 = 40$ runs; Bilal: $5 \times 10 = 50$ runs
Step 3 $50 - 40 = 10$
Step 4 Arjun’s bar covers $4 \times 2 = 8$ squares, Bilal’s only 5 bigger bar, fewer runs
Answer (a)
