Why the other options are wrong

B1 Whole symbols show 10, 20, 30, and so on, and a half symbol adds 5. So a row can show only multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, … Since 27 is not a multiple of 5, R is the reason for A, which gives (a).
B3 A is true: a score of 0 runs has a bar of height 0, so nothing is drawn. R is also true, but equal widths have nothing to do with a score of 0. So the answer is (b), not (a).
B6 Class 5 has $2 \times 4 + 2 = 10$ girls, and Class 6 has $4 \times 4 = 16$ girls. The difference is 6, option (b). Option (a) is the difference in symbols, not in girls. Option (c) is the number of girls in Class 6 alone, and option (d) is the number in Class 5.
C3 Every number is a multiple of 6, so one symbol for 6 dogs gives rows of 3, 6, 2, 8, 3 and 4 whole symbols, option (d). A key of 5 or 10 does not fit 18 exactly, even with half symbols. Neither does a key of 8: 18 dogs would need 2 symbols and a quarter of a symbol.
C4 Each number of wickets must be multiplied by its number of matches: $0 \times 2 + 1 \times 4 + 2 \times 6 + 3 \times 8 = 0 + 4 + 12 + 24 = 40$, option (b). Option (a) adds the matches, and option (c) adds 0, 1, 2 and 3, as Mayank did.
C5 Whole and half symbols show only multiples of 5. Of the four numbers, only 45 is a multiple of 5: it is 4 whole symbols and a half, option (b).
C6 The growth in each four years is $1700 - 1400 = 300$, $2200 - 1700 = 500$, $3000 - 2200 = 800$ and $3700 - 3000 = 700$. The largest is 800, from 2014 to 2018, option (c). Option (d) has the largest number of tigers, but not the largest growth.
C7 Twice the height of Elbrus is $5642 \times 2 = 11284$ m, option (c). Everest is 8848 m, so it is much less than twice as tall as Elbrus, whatever a picture of the two may suggest.

Most of these questions came back to one habit. Before you use a picture of data, find out what one symbol or one unit stands for, and where the scale starts. A picture can be drawn well or badly, but the numbers behind it stay the same.
