Data Handling and Presentation

Ask your classmates to name their favourite colour, and you will get a list of colours. Weigh every student in your class, and you will get a list of weights. Each of these lists is data.

What data is

Data is a collection of facts, numbers, measurements or observations that tells us something about the things they describe.

You see data every day: cricket scores, weather reports, prices in a shop, marks in a test. In this chapter you will learn how to collect data and how to arrange it. You will show data as pictures and as graphs, and read what they tell you. You will also learn to spot a picture that gives the wrong idea.

Naresh plays cricket sometimes, but he likes hockey best. “I am not sure,” he says. “How can we find out which game is the most popular in our class?”

There are many ways to collect data. You can ask people a question, and write down each answer. You can count things, such as the buses that pass your school gate in an hour. You can measure things, such as the height of each plant in the school garden. You can also watch something and write down what you see, such as the weather each day.
