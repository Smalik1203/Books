Drawing a bar graph
  • Choose a scale, starting from 0, so that the bar for the largest value fits.
  • Divide each value by the scale to find the height of its bar.

Here is what the bar graphs in this chapter have shown.

  • A bar graph shows data with bars of the same width, set at equal gaps. The length or height of each bar gives the frequency or amount for its category, such as the runs in a match or the money spent on food.
  • The equal gaps show that each bar stands on its own, for a separate category.
  • You can read and compare bars much faster than the numbers in a frequency table.
  • Give the graph a title, and name what each line shows.
  • The bars can stand up, or they can lie on their side.
  • Choose the scale by looking at the smallest and the largest values, so that the graph fits your paper and is easy to read. The marks on the scale must start from 0.
