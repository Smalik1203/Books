This picture may look more attractive, but it has a problem. In a column graph every column has the same width, so that you compare only the heights. Here the taller triangles are also wider. Are taller mountains always wider? The picture suggests something that the data does not say, and it may not be true. A more attractive picture can mislead without meaning to.

When you make a picture of data look attractive, take care that it does not mislead anyone about the facts. Take the same care when you read a picture of data that someone else has made, so that you are not misled either.

Before you trust a picture of data, look at its scale, and check that each bar or shape is as tall as its value.

Showing data fairly
  • Show heights with bars that stand up, and lengths along the ground with bars that lie on their side.
  • In a fair graph, every bar or shape is exactly as tall as its value on the scale.
