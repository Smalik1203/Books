The key of a pictograph

A pictograph shows data with pictures or symbols. Its key, also called its scale, says how many things one symbol stands for.

In Fig. 4.5, one symbol stands for one student. In many pictographs, one symbol stands for several people or things.

Nand Kishor teaches at a middle school in Berasia. He asked the children how often they sleep for at least 9 hours at night. He drew Fig. 4.6 from their answers. In this pictograph, one symbol stands for 10 children.

Example 1

Use Fig. 4.6 to answer these questions. (1) How many children always sleep for at least 9 hours? (2) How many sometimes do? (3) How many always sleep for less than 9 hours?

Solution.

Step 1 (1) Always: 5 symbols, so $5 \times 10 = 50$ children one symbol, 10 children
Step 2 (2) Sometimes: 2 whole symbols and half a symbol. The whole symbols show $2 \times 10 = 20$ children, and half of 10 is 5 half a symbol
Step 3 $20 + 5 = 25$ children
Step 4 (3) A child who never sleeps for 9 hours always sleeps for less than 9 hours
Step 5 Never: 4 symbols, so $4 \times 10 = 40$ children
Answer (1) 50 children (2) 25 children (3) 40 children
