Example 3

A frequency table shows the colours chosen by 32 students: red 8, blue 12, green 5 and yellow 7. Each student chose one colour. Which statements are correct?

  1. Blue was chosen most often.
  2. Red and yellow together have frequency 15.
  3. Green was chosen by one-fourth of the class.
  4. The frequencies add up to 32.

Solution.

Step 1The largest frequency is 12, for blue. Red and yellow: $8 + 7 = 15$
Step 2All four: $8 + 12 + 5 + 7 = 32$. One-fourth of 32 is 8, and green has 5
Answer(a), (b), (d)
Example 4

A bar graph starts at 0, with equally spaced marks 0, 5, 10, 15, and so on, and bars of equal width. Which statements are correct?

  1. A bar that reaches the fourth mark above 0 stands for 20.
  2. A bar twice as tall stands for twice the number.
  3. A wider bar would stand for a larger number.
  4. Two bars that differ by two marks differ by 10.

Solution.

Step 1Each gap between marks stands for 5: $4 \times 5 = 20$ and $2 \times 5 = 10$
Step 2From 0, twice the height is twice the number. Width shows nothing
Answer(a), (b), (d)
