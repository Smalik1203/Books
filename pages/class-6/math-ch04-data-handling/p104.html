Example 4 · Single correct

A bar graph starts at zero. Each 1 cm of height represents 12 visitors. The bars for Monday and Tuesday are 3 cm and 5 cm high. How many more visitors came on Tuesday?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 2
  2. 12
  3. 60
  4. 24

Solution.

Step 1The difference in height is 5 − 3 = 2 cm.
Step 2At 12 visitors per centimetre, the difference is 2 × 12 = 24 visitors.
Answer(d) 24
Example 5 · Single correct

A class has 40 students. A frequency table lists 13 walkers, 11 bus users and 9 cyclists; the rest travel by car. Each student is counted once. What is the missing frequency?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 6
  2. 7
  3. 8
  4. 9

Solution.

Step 1The listed frequencies sum to 13 + 11 + 9 = 33.
Step 2The missing count is 40 − 33 = 7.
Answer(b) 7
Example 6 · Single correct

Two bars represent 48 and 54 sales. A misleading graph starts its vertical axis at 42, making the visible heights 6 and 12 units. Which statement about the actual sales is correct?

Choose one correct option.

  1. The second count is twice the first.
  2. The counts differ by 12.
  3. The first count is half the second.
  4. The second count exceeds the first by 6.

Solution.

Step 1Use the actual values, not just the heights above the cut-off: 54 − 48 = 6.
Step 2The visible height doubles, but 54 is not twice 48.
Answer(d) The second count exceeds the first by 6
