Problem 3

A bar graph must show 450, 300, 750 and 600 tickets. Every bar must end exactly on a unit mark, and no bar may be taller than 16 units. Which scale works?

  1. 1 unit for 10 tickets
  2. 1 unit for 25 tickets
  3. 1 unit for 50 tickets
  4. 1 unit for 100 tickets
Solution

The tallest bar is the one for 750 tickets, so try each scale on it first.

1 unit for 10: $750 \div 10 = 75$ units, too tall

1 unit for 25: $750 \div 25 = 30$ units, too tall

1 unit for 50: $450 \div 50 = 9$, $300 \div 50 = 6$, $750 \div 50 = 15$, $600 \div 50 = 12$

1 unit for 100: 450 tickets would need 4 and a half units

The answer is option (c): every bar ends on a mark, and the tallest is 15 units. Options (a) and (b) make the bar for 750 taller than 16 units. Option (d) fits on the paper, but the bars for 450 and 750 end half-way between two marks.

Problem 4

In a bar graph of the students in two schools, the numbers on the line going up start at 900 instead of 0. The bar for School P reaches 1000, and the bar for School Q reaches 1100. Which statement is true?

  1. School Q has twice as many students as School P.
  2. School Q has 100 more students than School P.
  3. School P has 100 more students than School Q.
  4. School Q has 1100 more students than School P.
Solution

Read the numbers where the bars end, not the heights of the bars.

School P: 1000 students

School Q: 1100 students

$1100 - 1000 = 100$

The answer is option (b). Above 900, the bar for P is 100 tall and the bar for Q is 200 tall, so Q’s bar looks twice as tall. Option (a) believes the picture instead of the numbers. Option (c) swaps the two schools. Option (d) takes the number of students in Q as the difference.
