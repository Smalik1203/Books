Example 1 · Single correct

A survey records 12 students choosing cricket, 9 football and 7 badminton. Each student chooses exactly one sport. How many additional students must choose badminton for its count to equal cricket?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 3
  2. 4
  3. 5
  4. 7

Solution.

Step 1The difference between the counts is 12 − 7 = 5.
Step 2Adding 5 to badminton gives 12; the cricket count stays unchanged.
Answer(c) 5
Example 2 · Single correct

A pictograph uses one full symbol for 8 books and a half-symbol for 4 books. A row shows 4 full symbols and one half-symbol. How many books does it represent?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 36
  2. 40
  3. 32
  4. 34

Solution.

Step 1Four full symbols represent 4 × 8 = 32 books.
Step 2The half-symbol adds 4: 32 + 4 = 36.
Answer(a) 36
Example 3 · Single correct

A pictograph must show 18, 30 and 42 items using only whole symbols. Which key uses the fewest symbols in total?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 1 symbol = 2 items
  2. 1 symbol = 3 items
  3. 1 symbol = 6 items
  4. 1 symbol = 12 items

Solution.

Step 1A whole-symbol key must divide all three counts. Among the choices, 2, 3 and 6 do, but 12 does not.
Step 2The largest valid key, 6, uses 3 + 5 + 7 = 15 symbols, fewer than the smaller keys.
Answer(c) 1 symbol = 6 items
