Behind Each Answer
Five questions, with the reasoning as well as the working. Try each one before reading its solution.
Problem 1

In a pictograph, one symbol stands for 6 dogs. The row for Village F shows 4 whole symbols and half a symbol. The number of dogs in Village F is

  1. 24
  2. 27
  3. 30
  4. 45
Solution

Each whole symbol stands for 6 dogs, and half a symbol stands for half of 6.

4 whole symbols: $4 \times 6 = 24$ dogs

half a symbol: half of 6 is 3 dogs

$24 + 3 = 27$

The answer is 27, option (b). Option (a) counts only the whole symbols. Option (c) counts the half symbol as a whole one, which gives $5 \times 6$. Option (d) reads “4 and a half symbols” as the number 45, which mixes up the number of symbols with the number of dogs.

Problem 2

Leela rolled a die many times. The number 1 came up 4 times, 2 came up 6 times, 3 came up 5 times, 4 came up 3 times, 5 came up 7 times and 6 came up 5 times. How many times did she roll the die?

  1. 21
  2. 30
  3. 108
  4. 7
Solution

Every roll gives exactly one number, so every roll is counted once, in the frequency of that number. Adding the frequencies counts every roll.

$4 + 6 + 5 + 3 + 7 + 5 = 30$

The answer is 30, option (b). Option (a) adds the numbers on the die, $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 = 21$, and leaves out the frequencies. Option (c) is the total of all the numbers she rolled, $4 + 12 + 15 + 12 + 35 + 30 = 108$, which answers a different question. Option (d) is the largest frequency, which belongs to the number 5 alone.

A frequency table can answer two different questions. Adding the frequencies tells you how many observations there were. Multiplying each value by its frequency, and then adding, tells you the total of all the values. Problem 2 asked the first question. Mayank’s question about Bumrah’s wickets was the second.
