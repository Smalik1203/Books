Are taller mountains always wider? The picture suggests something that the data does not say, and it may not be true. A more attractive picture can mislead without meaning to.

A picture of data can look good and still give the wrong idea of the facts. When you make one, check that it does not mislead the people who will read it. When you read one, check it too, so that you are not misled yourself.

What this chapter showed
  1. Data is a collection of facts, numbers, measurements or observations.
  2. Tally marks and a frequency table organise data, so it is easy to read.
  3. The frequency of an item is the number of times it appears in the data.
  4. A pictograph shows data with symbols. Its key says what one symbol stands for.
  5. A bar graph shows data with bars of equal width. Its scale starts from 0.
  6. Choose a scale that shows the data correctly and fits the page.
  7. A decorated graph can look clearer, but it can also mislead.

Before you read any graph, look at its key or its scale, and check that the scale starts from 0. The same data can tell a very different story on a different scale.
