Here is what the pictographs in Section 4.2 have shown.

  • A pictograph shows data with pictures, so you can answer questions about the data at a glance.
  • It shows the frequency of each category, such as cricket or hockey, and lets you compare the frequencies.
  • The categories can go down the page or across it. For each category, you draw as many symbols as its frequency needs.
  • The key says what one symbol stands for. A symbol can stand for one thing or for many things.
  • A pictograph is harder to draw when there is a lot of data, or when the frequencies are not multiples of the key.

4.3Bar Graphs

You may have seen graphs made of bars on television or in a newspaper. Like a pictograph, a bar graph helps you read data quickly. You can see the largest value at once, and compare the values of different categories.

When there is a lot of data, a pictograph takes a long time to draw, and it can be hard to draw well. A bar graph is often easier.
