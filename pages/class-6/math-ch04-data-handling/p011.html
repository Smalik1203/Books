Bar graphs
  • A bar graph shows data with bars of the same width, with equal gaps between them.
  • The length of each bar, read on the scale, gives the number it stands for.

When you draw a bar graph, make every bar the same width, and leave equal gaps between the bars. The bars can stand up, or they can lie on their side. The length of each bar shows its number. As with pictographs, you can use a scale when the numbers are large.

The traffic police in Delhi counted the vehicles passing a busy road crossing on one day. Fig. 4.15 shows how many passed in each hour from 6 a.m. to 12 noon. Here the bars lie on their side, and one unit of length stands for 100 vehicles.

The longest bar is for 7 to 8 a.m., when 1200 vehicles passed. That was the busiest hour. The next longest bar is for 8 to 9 a.m., with 1000 vehicles. The shortest bar is for 6 to 7 a.m., when only about 150 vehicles passed. The next shortest is for 11 a.m. to 12 noon, with about 600 vehicles.

From 8 a.m. to 10 a.m., the graph shows about $1000 + 800 = 1800$ vehicles.
