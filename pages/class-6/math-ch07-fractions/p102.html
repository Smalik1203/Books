Priya eats $\frac{2}{5}$ of a cake, and Sunil eats $\frac{1}{4}$ of the same cake. Is more than half of the cake left?

The two pieces are counted in different units, so choose one unit for both. Twentieths work for fifths and for quarters. Priya eats $\frac{8}{20}$ and Sunil eats $\frac{5}{20}$, so together they eat $\frac{13}{20}$. What is left is $\frac{20}{20} - \frac{13}{20} = \frac{7}{20}$. Half the cake is $\frac{10}{20}$, so less than half is left. In twentieths, the three amounts were easy to compare.

None of these questions needed a new rule. Each one asked you to choose a unit to count in: the missing piece, twenty-firsts, quarters and eighths, twelfths or twentieths. Once the unit was right, the working was short.

Example 1

A tank is $\frac{3}{8}$ full. When 15 litres of water are added, it is $\frac{3}{4}$ full. How many litres does the full tank hold?

  1. 20 litres
  2. 30 litres
  3. 40 litres
  4. 60 litres

Solution.

Step 1The 15 litres filled $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{6}{8} - \frac{3}{8} = \frac{3}{8}$ of the tank.
Step 2So $\frac{1}{8}$ of the tank holds $15 \div 3 = 5$ litres.
Step 3The full tank is 8 eighths, so it holds $8 \times 5 = 40$ litres.
Check3 eighths of 40 litres is 15 litres, and 6 eighths is 30 litres. Adding 15 litres to 15 litres gives 30 litres.
Answer(c) 40 litres
Example 2

Which fraction lies between $\frac{5}{8}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$, and is equal to neither of them?

  1. $\dfrac{11}{16}$
  2. $\dfrac{1}{2}$
  3. $\dfrac{13}{16}$
  4. $\dfrac{6}{8}$

Solution.

Step 1In sixteenths, $\frac{5}{8} = \frac{10}{16}$ and $\frac{3}{4} = \frac{12}{16}$.
Step 2$\frac{11}{16}$ lies between them. $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{8}{16}$ is too small, $\frac{13}{16}$ is too big, and $\frac{6}{8} = \frac{12}{16}$ is equal to $\frac{3}{4}$.
Answer(a) $\frac{11}{16}$
