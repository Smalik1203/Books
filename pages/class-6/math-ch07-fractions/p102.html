Priya eats $\frac{2}{5}$ of a cake. Sunil eats one third of what is left. What fraction of the whole cake does Sunil eat? How much is left after that?

After Priya, $\frac{3}{5}$ of the cake is left. That is 3 fifths. One third of 3 fifths is 1 fifth, so Sunil eats $\frac{1}{5}$ of the whole cake. What remains is $\frac{3}{5} - \frac{1}{5} = \frac{2}{5}$, which is exactly as much as Priya ate. Counting in fifths turned a question about thirds into simple counting.

None of these questions needed a new rule. Each one asked you to choose a unit to count in: the missing piece, twenty-firsts, quarters, twelfths or fifths. Once the unit was right, the working was short.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · Fractional units and the number line

Example 1

Write $\frac{1}{7}$, $\frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{1}{10}$ and $\frac{1}{5}$ in order, from the smallest to the largest.

Solution.

Step 1 All four have numerator 1, so all four are fractional units
Step 2 More equal parts make a smaller unit more people sharing
Step 3 The denominators from the largest to the smallest are 10, 7, 5, 3
Answer $\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{7} < \frac{1}{5} < \frac{1}{3}$
Example 2

On a number line, each unit is cut into 4 equal parts. A bar starts at 0 and ends at the seventh mark after 0. How long is the bar? Is it longer than 1 unit?

Solution.

Step 1 Each part is $\frac{1}{4}$ unit long 4 equal parts
Step 2 The bar covers 7 parts: $7 \times \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{4}$
Step 3 $\frac{7}{4} = \frac{4}{4} + \frac{3}{4} = 1 + \frac{3}{4}$ 4 quarters make 1
Answer The bar is $\frac{7}{4}$ units long, so it is longer than 1 unit
