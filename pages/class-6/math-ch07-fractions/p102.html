Priya eats $\frac{2}{5}$ of a cake, and Sunil eats $\frac{1}{4}$ of the same cake. Is more than half of the cake left?

The two pieces are counted in different units, so choose one unit for both. Twentieths work for fifths and for quarters. Priya eats $\frac{8}{20}$ and Sunil eats $\frac{5}{20}$, so together they eat $\frac{13}{20}$. What is left is $\frac{20}{20} - \frac{13}{20} = \frac{7}{20}$. Half the cake is $\frac{10}{20}$, so less than half is left. In twentieths, the three amounts were easy to compare.

None of these questions needed a new rule. Each one asked you to choose a unit to count in: the missing piece, twenty-firsts, quarters and eighths, twelfths or twentieths. Once the unit was right, the working was short.

Solved Examples

Type 1 · Fractional units, parts of a whole and the number line

Example 1

Write $\frac{1}{7}$, $\frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{1}{10}$ and $\frac{1}{5}$ in order, from the smallest to the largest.

Solution.

Step 1 All four have numerator 1, so all four are fractional units
Step 2 More equal parts make a smaller unit more people sharing
Step 3 The denominators from the largest to the smallest are 10, 7, 5, 3
Answer $\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{7} < \frac{1}{5} < \frac{1}{3}$
Example 2

A chocolate bar is marked into 30 small squares, in 5 rows of 6 squares. What part of the whole bar is a piece made of one row? Of three rows? Of 15 squares?

Solution.

Step 1 5 rows make the whole bar, so one row is $\frac{1}{5}$ of the bar how many make the whole
Step 2 Three rows are 3 pieces of $\frac{1}{5}$, which is $\frac{3}{5}$ count the units
Step 3 $15 + 15 = 30$, so two pieces of 15 squares make the bar
Answer $\frac{1}{5}$, $\frac{3}{5}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ of the bar

Counting squares agrees: three rows are $3 \times 6 = 18$ squares, and each fifth of the bar is $30 \div 5 = 6$ squares.
