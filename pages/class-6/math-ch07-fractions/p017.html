Subtracting fractions with the same denominator

Brahmagupta’s method works for subtraction too. To find $\frac{6}{7} - \frac{4}{7}$, draw a strip cut into 7 equal parts and shade 6 of them. Each shaded part is $\frac{1}{7}$. Now take away 4 of the shaded parts.

Both fractions count sevenths, so you can take the parts away directly. Two shaded parts are left, so $\frac{6}{7} - \frac{4}{7} = \frac{2}{7}$.

Subtracting fractions with different denominators

Example 4

Find $\frac{3}{4} - \frac{2}{3}$. First make the denominators the same.

$\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{3 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \dfrac{9}{12}, \quad \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{2 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \dfrac{8}{12}$

$\dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{9}{12} - \dfrac{8}{12} = \dfrac{1}{12}$
