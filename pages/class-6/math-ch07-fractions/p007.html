7.5Mixed Fractions

Fractions greater than one

Look back at the bars you measured. Some were shorter than 1 unit, such as $\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{4}{5}$. Others were longer than 1 unit, such as the bars in Fig. 7.10. Sort the fractions into two groups.

$\frac{1}{2},\ \frac{1}{3},\ \frac{2}{3},\ \frac{2}{5},\ \frac{4}{5}$$\frac{3}{2},\ \frac{6}{5},\ \frac{7}{5},\ \frac{8}{5},\ \frac{9}{5}$

In the first group, every numerator is smaller than its denominator. In the second group, every numerator is bigger than its denominator. A fraction like that is more than one whole.

How many whole units are inside $\frac{3}{2}$ and $\frac{5}{2}$? Two halves make a whole, so group the halves in twos.

$\dfrac{3}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 1 + \dfrac{1}{2}$

$\dfrac{5}{2} = \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 2 + \dfrac{1}{2}$

Thirds work the same way. You know that $\frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} + \frac{1}{3} = 1$. Add one more $\frac{1}{3}$ and you go past 1. So $\frac{4}{3}$ is greater than 1.
