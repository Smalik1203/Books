Comparing shares

Which gives each child more chikki: 1 chikki for 2 children, or 5 chikkis for 8 children? Shabnam knows that $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{8}$, and $\frac{4}{8} < \frac{5}{8}$. So the second group gets more.

Now compare 1 chikki shared by 2 children with 4 chikkis shared by 7 children. That is $\frac{1}{2}$ against $\frac{4}{7}$. Mukta multiplies the numerator and the denominator of $\frac{1}{2}$ by 4.

$\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1 \times 4}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{4}{8}$

Now $\frac{4}{7}$ and $\frac{4}{8}$ share the same 4 chikkis. With 7 children each share is bigger than with 8 children, so $\frac{4}{7} > \frac{4}{8}$. And $\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $\frac{4}{7} > \frac{1}{2}$.

Sharing among more people

When the same number of things is shared among more people, each share is smaller.

Making the fractional units the same

Two fractions are easy to compare when they have the same denominator. $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{3}{6}$ both count sixths, so $\frac{2}{6} < \frac{3}{6}$. When the denominators are different, first write the two fractions with the same denominator.

Take $\frac{5}{6}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$. Write equivalent fractions for each one, and stop at the product of the denominators, $6 \times 4 = 24$.
