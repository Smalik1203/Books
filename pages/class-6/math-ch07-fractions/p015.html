Adding fractions with the same denominator

To find $\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5}$, draw two strips, each cut into 5 equal parts. Shade 2 parts of one and 1 part of the other, as in the top row of Fig. 7.15. Every part is $\frac{1}{5}$, and 3 parts are shaded in all. So $\frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{5} = \frac{3}{5}$.

Example 1

Find $\frac{4}{7} + \frac{6}{7}$.

Both fractions count sevenths. Together there are $4 + 6 = 10$ sevenths, as the bottom row of Fig. 7.15 shows.

$\dfrac{4}{7} + \dfrac{6}{7} = \dfrac{10}{7} = 1 + \dfrac{3}{7} = 1\dfrac{3}{7}$

Adding fractions with different denominators

To add $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$, make the units the same. A common denominator is $4 \times 3 = 12$.

$\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \dfrac{3}{12}, \quad \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \dfrac{4}{12}$

$\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{12} + \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$
