Equivalent fractions as equal shares

One roti is shared equally by 4 children, so each child gets $\frac{1}{4}$ roti. You can write what happened in three ways.

Division: $1 \div 4 = \dfrac{1}{4}$

Addition: $1 = \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{4}$

Multiplication: $1 = 4 \times \dfrac{1}{4}$

  1. Anil is in a group where 2 cakes are shared equally by 5 children. How much cake does Anil get?
  1. Anil’s group grows to 10 children. How many cakes give each child the same share as before?

Here is another pair of groups. In Group 1, 3 cakes are shared equally by 5 children. Each cake is cut into 5 equal parts, and each child gets one part of every cake. So each child gets $\frac{3}{5}$ cake.

Group 2 is two such groups put together: 6 cakes and 10 children. Each child gets one tenth of every cake, which is $\frac{6}{10}$ cake. That is the same share, so $\frac{3}{5} = \frac{6}{10}$.
