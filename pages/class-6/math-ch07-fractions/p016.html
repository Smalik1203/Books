This method works for any fractions.

It was first written down as a general rule by the Indian mathematician Brahmagupta, in the year 628 CE.

Brahmagupta’s method for adding fractions

Step 1. Give the fractions a common denominator, such as the product of the denominators.

Step 2. Add the numerators, and keep the denominator.

Step 3. Write the answer in lowest terms if you need to.

Example 2

Find $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{5}$. The smallest common multiple of 3 and 5 is 15.

$\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{10}{15} + \dfrac{3}{15} = \dfrac{13}{15}$

Example 3

Find $\frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{3}$. The smallest common multiple of 6 and 3 is 6, so $\frac{1}{6}$ stays as it is.

$\dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{3}{6}$

$= \dfrac{3 \div 3}{6 \div 3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$

3 is the largest common factor of 3 and 6, so dividing both by 3 gives the answer in lowest terms.
