Adding fractions with different denominators

To add $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{3}$, make the units the same. A common denominator is $4 \times 3 = 12$.

$\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1 \times 3}{4 \times 3} = \dfrac{3}{12}, \quad \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1 \times 4}{3 \times 4} = \dfrac{4}{12}$

$\dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{3}{12} + \dfrac{4}{12} = \dfrac{7}{12}$

This method works for any fractions.

It was first written down as a general rule by the Indian mathematician Brahmagupta, in the year 628 CE.

Brahmagupta’s method for adding fractions

Step 1. Give the fractions a common denominator, such as the product of the denominators.

Step 2. Add the numerators, and keep the denominator.

Step 3. Write the answer in lowest terms if you need to.

Example 5

Find $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{5}$.

Solution.

Step 1 The smallest common multiple of 3 and 5 is 15 a common denominator
Step 2 $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{5} = \dfrac{10}{15} + \dfrac{3}{15}$ write in fifteenths
Step 3 $\dfrac{10}{15} + \dfrac{3}{15} = \dfrac{13}{15}$ add the numerators
Answer $\dfrac{13}{15}$
