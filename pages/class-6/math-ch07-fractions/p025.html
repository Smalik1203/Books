Summary
  1. A fraction tells you how big each share is when something is shared equally. When one whole is cut into equal parts, each part is a fractional unit, such as $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{1}{3}$ or $\frac{1}{4}$.
  2. The more equal parts a whole is cut into, the smaller each part is. So $\frac{1}{9} < \frac{1}{5}$, and no fractional unit is bigger than $\frac{1}{2}$.
  3. A fraction counts fractional units. In $\frac{3}{4}$, the denominator 4 names the unit, a quarter, and the numerator 3 counts how many quarters there are.
  4. Fractions can be marked on the number line. Cut each unit into equal parts, and count the parts from 0.
  5. A fraction whose numerator is bigger than its denominator is more than 1. It can be written as a mixed number, with a whole part and a fractional part: $\frac{11}{4} = 2\frac{3}{4}$.
  6. Fractions that stand for the same share or the same length are equivalent fractions, such as $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}$. Multiplying or dividing the numerator and the denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction.
  7. A fraction is in lowest terms when its numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1. For example, $\frac{16}{20}$ in lowest terms is $\frac{4}{5}$.
  8. To compare two fractions, write them with the same denominator and compare the numerators: $\frac{4}{5} = \frac{36}{45}$ is greater than $\frac{7}{9} = \frac{35}{45}$.
  9. To add or subtract fractions, write them with a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators: $\frac{1}{4} + \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{12} + \frac{4}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$. Brahmagupta set out this method in 628 CE.
  10. The way we write fractions today, one number above another, began in India.
