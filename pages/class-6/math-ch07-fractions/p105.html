Example 9

Is $\frac{7}{15}$ more or less than $\frac{1}{2}$? By how much?

Solution.

Step 1 $\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{15}{30}$ and $\dfrac{7}{15} = \dfrac{14}{30}$ write in thirtieths
Step 2 $\frac{14}{30} < \frac{15}{30}$ compare the numerators
Step 3 $\dfrac{15}{30} - \dfrac{14}{30} = \dfrac{1}{30}$
Answer $\frac{7}{15}$ is less than $\frac{1}{2}$, by $\frac{1}{30}$

There is a quicker check. $7 + 7 = 14$, which is less than 15, so 7 is less than half of 15.

Example 10

Write $\frac{3}{5}$, $\frac{5}{8}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$ in ascending order, that is, from the smallest to the largest.

Solution.

Step 1 $5 \times 8 \times 3 = 120$ is a common multiple of 5, 8 and 3 product of the denominators
Step 2 $\dfrac{3}{5} = \dfrac{72}{120}$, $\ \dfrac{5}{8} = \dfrac{75}{120}$, $\ \dfrac{2}{3} = \dfrac{80}{120}$
Step 3 $72 < 75 < 80$ compare the numerators
Answer $\frac{3}{5} < \frac{5}{8} < \frac{2}{3}$

Each fraction is a little more than $\frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{3}{5}$ by $\frac{1}{10}$, $\frac{5}{8}$ by $\frac{1}{8}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$ by $\frac{1}{6}$. Since $\frac{1}{10} < \frac{1}{8} < \frac{1}{6}$, this gives the same order.

Example 11

Find $\frac{2}{3} + \frac{3}{4} - \frac{5}{6}$.

Solution.

Step 1 12 is a common multiple of 3, 4 and 6
Step 2 $\dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{3}{4} - \dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{8}{12} + \dfrac{9}{12} - \dfrac{10}{12}$ write in twelfths
Step 3 $8 + 9 - 10 = 7$ work on the numerators
Answer $\dfrac{7}{12}$
