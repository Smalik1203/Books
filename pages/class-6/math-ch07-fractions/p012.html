Making the fractional units the same

To compare two fractions, both must use the same fractional unit. That means they must have the same denominator. For example, $\frac{2}{6}$ and $\frac{3}{6}$ both count sixths, so they are easy to compare.

Take $\frac{5}{6}$ and $\frac{3}{4}$. Write equivalent fractions for each one, and stop at the product of the denominators, $6 \times 4 = 24$.

$\dfrac{5}{6} = \dfrac{10}{12} = \dfrac{15}{18} = \dfrac{20}{24}$

$\dfrac{3}{4} = \dfrac{6}{8} = \dfrac{9}{12} = \dfrac{12}{16} = \dfrac{15}{20} = \dfrac{18}{24}$

Both lists reach 24 as a denominator: $\frac{20}{24} > \frac{18}{24}$, so $\frac{5}{6} > \frac{3}{4}$. Look at the lists again. They already share the denominator 12, and $\frac{10}{12} > \frac{9}{12}$ gives the same answer sooner.

Writing a fraction in lowest terms

Lowest terms

A fraction is in lowest terms, or in its simplest form, when its numerator and denominator have no common factor except 1.

To write a fraction in lowest terms, find the equivalent fraction with the smallest numerator and denominator.

Example 1

Is $\frac{16}{20}$ in lowest terms? If not, write it in lowest terms.

No. Both 16 and 20 are divisible by 4. Divide both of them by 4.

$\dfrac{16}{20} = \dfrac{16 \div 4}{20 \div 4} = \dfrac{4}{5}$

4 and 5 have no common factor except 1. So $\frac{4}{5}$ is the simplest form of $\frac{16}{20}$.

You can also do it in steps. Both 36 and 60 are even, so divide both by 2. Keep going until no common factor is left.
