Share 1 roti between 2 children, 2 rotis among 4 children, or 3 rotis among 6 children. In each case, every child gets half a roti, so $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}$. These are equivalent fractions, because the shares are equal.

Comparing shares

Which gives each child more chikki: 1 chikki for 2 children, or 5 chikkis for 8 children? Mukta compares $\frac{1}{2}$ and $\frac{5}{8}$. Shabnam knows that $\frac{1}{2} = \frac{4}{8}$, and $\frac{4}{8} < \frac{5}{8}$. So the second group gets more.

Now compare 1 chikki shared by 2 children with 4 chikkis shared by 7 children. That is $\frac{1}{2}$ against $\frac{4}{7}$. Mukta multiplies the numerator and the denominator of $\frac{1}{2}$ by 4.

$\dfrac{1}{2} = \dfrac{1 \times 4}{2 \times 4} = \dfrac{4}{8}$

Now $\frac{4}{7}$ and $\frac{4}{8}$ share the same 4 chikkis. With 7 children each share is bigger than with 8 children, so $\frac{4}{7} > \frac{4}{8}$. And $\frac{4}{8} = \frac{1}{2}$, so $\frac{4}{7} > \frac{1}{2}$.
