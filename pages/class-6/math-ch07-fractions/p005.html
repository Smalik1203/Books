You can count halves of a roti in the same way. Each line below adds one more half.

$\dfrac{1}{2} = 1 \text{ times half}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 2 \text{ times half}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 3 \text{ times half}$

$\dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{2} = 4 \text{ times half}$

Two halves make one whole roti. So 2 times half is 1 roti, and 4 times half is 2 rotis, as Fig. 7.7 shows.
