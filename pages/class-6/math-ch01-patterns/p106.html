Example 12

Two triangular numbers that are next to each other add up to 81. Which are they?

Solution. Two triangular numbers next to each other always add up to a square number.

Step 1 $81 = 9 \times 9$ a square of 9 rows
Step 2 a square of 9 rows splits into triangles of 9 rows and 8 rows
Step 3 $1 + 2 + \cdots + 9 = 45$ and $1 + 2 + \cdots + 8 = 36$
Step 4 $36 + 45 = 81$ check
Answer 36 and 45
Example 13

Find $1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + \cdots + 256 + 512$, which adds the powers of 2 up to 512.

Solution. Add 1 to the sum first. Then each power of 2 doubles the total so far.

Step 1 $1 + 1 = 2$, $\ 2 + 2 = 4$, $\ 4 + 4 = 8$, and so on
Step 2 the total reaches 512 just before 512 is added
Step 3 $512 + 512 = 1024$ the sum plus 1
Step 4 $1024 - 1 = 1023$ take the 1 away
Answer 1023

Example 14

In a kho-kho tournament, every team plays every other team once. 15 matches are played in all. How many teams are there?

Solution. Draw each team as a point, and each match as a line joining two points. The picture is a complete graph.

Step 1 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 points have 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 lines triangular numbers
Step 2 15 lines go with 6 points
Answer 6 teams

The same count answers any question in which every pair meets once: handshakes at a party, matches in a league, or lines joining points. Each new point is joined to every point already there, so the counts grow by 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, …, which is how the triangular numbers grow.
