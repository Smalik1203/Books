  1. Why do you think 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, … are called triangular numbers? Why are 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, … called square numbers, and 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, … called cube numbers?
  1. The number 36 is a triangular number and also a square number. Draw 36 dots as a triangle, and then draw 36 dots as a square. Can you find another number that can be drawn in two different ways?
  1. Here is a new sequence: 1, 7, 19, 37, … Draw each number as dots so that all the pictures have the same shape. What shape do you get? What is the next number in the sequence?
  1. Can you draw the powers of 2 as pictures? What about the powers of 3?

Try Question 5 before you read on. Here is one way to draw the powers of 2.

Start with a single point. Join it to a copy of itself, and you have a line with 2 ends. Join that line to a copy of itself, and you have a square with 4 corners. Do it again, and the square becomes a cube with 8 corners. Do it once more, and you get a shape with 16 corners.

Each step doubles the number of corners, because each step adds a copy of every corner there already was. That is why the numbers of corners — 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 — are the powers of 2.

Drawing the powers of 3 needs a different idea. That part of Question 5 is still yours to try.
