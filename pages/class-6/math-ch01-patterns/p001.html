Looking for Patterns

1.1What Is Mathematics?

Ask mathematicians what their work is, and many will give the same answer. They look for patterns, and then they try to explain them.

Patterns are all around you. The sun rises in the east every morning. The moon grows round and then thin again about once a month. The tiles on a floor repeat across the room. Once you start looking, you will find patterns in cooking, games, music and the weather.

Noticing a pattern is only the first step. The second step is to ask why the pattern happens, and this step matters just as much. When you know why something happens, you can often use that reason somewhere new, far away from where you first noticed it.

Here is one example. For thousands of years, people watched the planets move across the night sky along regular paths. Much later, scientists explained why the planets move that way. That explanation is what let India send Chandrayaan-3 to the Moon, where it landed in August 2023.

What mathematics does

Mathematics looks for patterns, and for the reasons why those patterns happen.

Here is an older example, from Bhārat. Astronomers watched the moon and saw that it returns to the same star after about 27 days. They used this pattern to divide the sky into 27 parts, called nakshatras, and to build a calendar from them. A pattern you can explain is a pattern you can plan with.

In this book you will do both. You will find patterns, and you will look for the reason behind each one.
