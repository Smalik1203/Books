Adding up and then down

Here is a second pattern. Add the counting numbers up to some number, and then back down again:

$1 = 1$

$1 + 2 + 1 = 4$

$1 + 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 9$

$1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 16$

$1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 25$

The square numbers have appeared again, this time from the counting numbers. Does the pattern keep going? Example 1 checks the next line.

Example 1

Is $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1$ a square number?

You can add numbers in any order. So put together numbers that make 10.

$1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10$

$6 + 4 = 10$

$5 + 5 = 10$

$3 + 2 + 1 = 6$

The total is $10 + 10 + 10 + 6 = 36$, and $36 = 6 \times 6$. So the pattern works for 6 as well.

Checking more lines makes you more sure that the pattern is true. But checking can never show that it is true for every number, because there are too many numbers to check. For that you need a reason. Question 1 below asks you to find a picture of dots that gives the reason.
