Why the other options are wrong

4 Four sides of 10 dots make 40, but each corner dot is on two sides and was counted twice. Take away the 4 extra corner dots to get 36. Option (a) forgets the corners, option (b) counts every dot in the square, and option (c) adds the corners instead of taking them away.
7 39 is the 20th odd number, so the sum makes a square of 20 rows, which is 400. Option (c) is $21 \times 21$, which counts one odd number too many.
15 A is false. 4 is a square number, but the triangular numbers go 1, 3, 6, 10, so 4 is not one of them. R is true, but one number that is both kinds does not make every square number triangular. So the answer is (d).
16 A is true: 7 odd numbers make a square of 7 rows, and $7 \times 7 = 49$. R is false: the triangular numbers go 36, 45, 55, and 49 is not one of them. So the answer is (c).

Most of these questions were not about adding at all. They were about seeing which pattern a question belonged to. Once you knew that the numbers were odd numbers, or a square, or a triangle, the pattern gave you the answer with very little work.
