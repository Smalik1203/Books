What this chapter showed
  1. Mathematics looks for patterns, and for the reasons why those patterns happen.
  2. A number sequence is a list of numbers made by following a rule. Some important ones are the counting numbers, the odd and even numbers, the triangular, square and cube numbers, the Virahānka numbers, and the powers of 2 and 3.
  3. Many number sequences can be drawn as pictures, and a picture can show why a pattern happens.
  4. Two sequences can be connected. Adding the odd numbers from 1 gives the square numbers, and so does adding the counting numbers up and then down.
  5. A shape sequence is a list of shapes made by following a rule. Regular polygons, complete graphs, stacked squares and triangles, and the Koch snowflake are all shape sequences.
  6. Counting the sides, lines or small pieces in each shape of a shape sequence often gives a number sequence.

The sequences in this chapter come back again and again in this book, and in later ones. When a new pattern looks strange, ask which of these old friends it is hiding.
