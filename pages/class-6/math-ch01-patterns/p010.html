1.5Patterns in Shapes

Patterns in shapes are just as important as patterns in numbers. A line has length only, so we call it one-dimensional, or 1D. A flat shape such as a square is two-dimensional, or 2D. A solid such as a cube is three-dimensional, or 3D. Mathematicians also study shapes with more than three dimensions.

The part of mathematics that studies patterns in shapes is called geometry. A list of shapes made by following a rule is called a shape sequence. Figs. 1.8, 1.9, 1.10 and 1.11 show five of them. As you look at each one, ask the question you asked of the number sequences: what is the rule that makes the next shape? Some rules add a new piece to the shape; others change every part of it.
