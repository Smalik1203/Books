The square numbers have appeared again, this time from the counting numbers. Does the pattern keep going? Example 1 checks the next line.

Example 1

Is $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1$ a square number?

Solution. You can add numbers in any order. So put together numbers that make 10.

Step 1 $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10$
Step 2 $6 + 4 = 10$
Step 3 $5 + 5 = 10$
Step 4 $3 + 2 + 1 = 6$ the numbers left
Answer Yes. The total is $10 + 10 + 10 + 6 = 36$, and $36 = 6 \times 6$

So the pattern works for 6 as well.

Example 2

Use the pattern to find $1 + 2 + 3 + \cdots + 19 + 20 + 19 + \cdots + 3 + 2 + 1$ without adding the numbers.

Solution. The sum goes up to 20 and back down to 1, with 20 counted once.

Step 1 Up to 4 and back gives $4 \times 4$; up to 5 and back gives $5 \times 5$ the pattern
Step 2 So up to 20 and back gives $20 \times 20$
Answer $400$

Adding the 39 numbers one by one would take a long time. The pattern gives the answer in one line.

Checking more lines makes you more sure that the pattern is true. But checking can never show that it is true for every number, because there are too many numbers to check. For that you need a reason. In Question 1 below, you find it with a picture of dots.
