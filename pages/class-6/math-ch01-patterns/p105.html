Example 9

Find $11 + 13 + 15 + \cdots + 29$.

Solution. The sum does not start at 1. Start it at 1, and then take away what was added.

Step 1 29 is the 15th odd number, so $1 + 3 + \cdots + 29 = 15 \times 15 = 225$
Step 2 9 is the 5th odd number, so $1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 5 \times 5 = 25$ the part to remove
Step 3 $225 - 25 = 200$
Answer 200

In the picture of L-shapes, this is a square of 15 rows with a square of 5 rows cut from its corner.

Type 4 · Adding up and then down

Example 10

Kiran adds the counting numbers up to some number and then back down to 1. His total is 169. What is the biggest number in his sum, and how many numbers does he add?

Solution.

Step 1 $169 = 13 \times 13$ a square of 13 rows
Step 2 the biggest number is 13
Step 3 up: 1 to 13 is 13 numbers; down: 12 to 1 is 12 numbers
Step 4 $13 + 12 = 25$
Answer the biggest number is 13, and he adds 25 numbers
Example 11

Find $1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6 + 7 + 8 + 8 + 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1$, where 8 is written twice.

Solution. Take away one of the two 8s. What is left goes up to 8 and down again.

Step 1 $1 + 2 + \cdots + 8 + \cdots + 2 + 1 = 8 \times 8 = 64$ up and then down
Step 2 put the second 8 back: $64 + 8 = 72$
Answer 72

The sum is two copies of $1 + 2 + \cdots + 8$, so it is also two triangles of 8 rows. They make a rectangle of 8 rows of 9, and $8 \times 9 = 72$.
