Example 6

A cube is built from 64 small cubes, as in Fig. 1.3. How many layers does it have, and how many small cubes are in each layer? The outside of the cube is painted. How many small cubes have no paint on them?

Solution.

Step 1 $4 \times 4 \times 4 = 64$, so there are 4 layers 64 is a cube number
Step 2 each layer is a square of $4 \times 4 = 16$ small cubes
Step 3 remove the painted cubes on every side: $4 - 2 = 2$ left along each edge
Step 4 $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ a cube of 2 layers
Answer 4 layers of 16 small cubes; 8 small cubes have no paint

Type 3 · Adding odd numbers

The L-shapes of Fig. 1.5 also tell you where each odd number comes in the list. The 5th L-shape has 5 dots along each arm, but the corner dot is in both, so it has $5 + 5 - 1 = 9$ dots, and 9 is the 5th odd number. In the same way, the 25th odd number is $25 + 25 - 1 = 49$. To go back, add 1 and halve: $49 + 1 = 50$ counts the corner twice, and half of 50 is 25.

Example 7

Find $1 + 3 + 5 + \cdots + 49$ without adding the numbers one by one.

Solution. First find how many odd numbers are being added.

Step 1 $(49 + 1) \div 2 = 25$ add 1, then halve
Step 2 49 is the 25th odd number
Step 3 25 odd numbers make a square of 25 rows of 25
Step 4 $25 \times 25 = 625$
Answer 625
Example 8

Salma adds the odd numbers in order, starting from 1, and stops when her total is 121. What is the last number she adds?

Solution.

Step 1 $121 = 11 \times 11$ a square of 11 rows
Step 2 so she adds the first 11 odd numbers
Step 3 the 11th odd number is $2 \times 11 - 1 = 21$
Answer 21
