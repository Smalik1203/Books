There is a second way to see it. Each of these numbers is double a triangular number: $2 = 2 \times 1$, $6 = 2 \times 3$, $12 = 2 \times 6$, and so on. Two copies of a triangle of dots fit together to make a rectangle.

Squares in a row are made with matchsticks. 1 square needs 4 matchsticks, 2 squares need 7 and 3 squares need 10. How many matchsticks are needed for 10 squares in a row?

The first square needs 4 matchsticks. Each square after it shares one side with the square before, so it needs only 3 more. For 10 squares that is the first 4, and then 3 more for each of the other 9 squares: $4 + 9 \times 3 = 31$.

There is another way to count. Look first at the matchsticks that lie flat: 10 along the top and 10 along the bottom. Then count the ones that stand up. There is one at each end and one between each pair of squares, which makes 11. So the total is $10 + 10 + 11 = 31$. Two different ways gave the same answer, which is a good sign that it is right. The second way also works for any number of squares: the flat matchsticks are twice the number of squares, and the standing ones are 1 more than the number of squares.

None of these questions needed a new rule. Each one needed you to see which pattern it belonged to, and then that pattern did the work. When a question looks new, ask which of the chapter's sequences is hiding inside it: the odd numbers, the square numbers, the triangular numbers or the powers of 2.
