Look at the lengths of the gaps. On the two short sides, each gap joins two dots next to each other. We call these straight units. On the long side, each gap goes across a square from one corner to the opposite corner. We call these diagonal units. Measure one of each with your scale. Are they the same length?

Since the two kinds of unit are not the same, we count them separately. The triangle has 6 straight units and 3 diagonal units. We write its perimeter in short as $6s + 3d$ units.

Perimeter of a regular polygon

A closed figure with all its sides equal and all its angles equal is called a regular polygon. A square is one. You met the sequence of regular polygons in Chapter 1. An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three equal angles. A regular pentagon has five equal sides and five equal angles.

A rectangle that is not a square is not a regular polygon. Its four angles are equal, but its four sides are not all equal.

The perimeter of any triangle is the sum of its three sides. In the equilateral triangle $ABC$ of Fig. 6.6, all three sides are equal.

Perimeter $= AB + BC + CA = AB + AB + AB = 3 \times AB$

Perimeter of an equilateral triangle

Perimeter of an equilateral triangle $= 3 \times \text{length of a side}$

For example, an equilateral triangle with sides of 7 cm has a perimeter of $3 \times 7 = 21$ cm. You need to measure only one side.
