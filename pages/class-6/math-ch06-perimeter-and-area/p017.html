Exercise Set 6.12
  1. In each puzzle in Fig. 6.21, find the missing area or length.
  1. A rectangle 9 cm long is cut straight across into two rectangles of area 30 sq cm and 15 sq cm. How wide is the rectangle? How long is each part?
  1. A rectangle is cut into four rectangles by one line across and one line down. The top two have areas 6 sq cm and 9 sq cm. The bottom-left one has area 10 sq cm. Find the area of the bottom-right one.
  1. A square of side 3 cm is joined to a rectangle 5 cm long and 4 cm wide, so that one side of the square lies along a 4 cm side of the rectangle. Find the area and the perimeter of the new figure.
  1. A square of area 49 sq cm is cut straight across into two rectangles. One of them is 7 cm by 3 cm. Find the length, width and area of the other one.
  1. A rectangle with an area of 45 sq cm has one side of 9 cm. One straight cut splits it into a square and a smaller rectangle. Find the area of the square.
  1. Two rectangles side by side share a side of 6 cm. Their areas are 30 sq cm and 42 sq cm. How long is the whole figure?
  1. Make an area maze puzzle of your own with one missing value. Give it to a friend to solve.

Perimeter is a length, so it is measured in cm or m. Area is a number of squares, so it is measured in sq cm or sq m. Check the unit before you write an answer.
