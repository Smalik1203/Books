Draw a few triangles of your own on squared paper to check what you found.

Two triangles that look different

Rectangle $ABCD$ in Fig. 6.16 is 5 units long and 4 units wide, so its area is $5 \times 4 = 20$ square units. Triangle $BAD$ is one of the two halves made by the diagonal $BD$. So its area is half of 20, which is 10 square units.

Triangle $ABE$ looks very different. Its top corner $E$ is not a corner of the rectangle. What is its area?

The line $EF$ splits triangle $ABE$ into two triangles, $AEF$ and $BEF$. Each of these is half of a rectangle. Triangle $AEF$ is half of rectangle $AFED$, and triangle $BEF$ is half of rectangle $BFEC$.

Area of $ABE$ $=$ area of $AEF$ $+$ area of $BEF$

$=$ half of $AFED$ $+$ half of $BFEC$

$=$ half of $(AFED + BFEC)$

$=$ half of $ABCD$ $= 10$ square units

So triangles $BAD$ and $ABE$ have the same area, even though they look so different. Both have the side $AB$ as base, and both reach the opposite side of the rectangle.

A triangle in a rectangle

A triangle drawn with one side of a rectangle as its base, and its top corner on the opposite side, has half the area of the rectangle.
