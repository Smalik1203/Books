Area with tangram pieces

A tangram is a square cut into seven pieces, as in Fig. 6.12. Trace the pieces onto thick paper and cut them out. You can compare the areas of two pieces by placing one on top of the other, or by covering one piece with other pieces.

Exercise Set 6.5
  1. Which pieces have the same area? Find out by placing the pieces on one another.
  1. How many times as big as Shape $C$ is Shape $D$? How are Shapes $C$, $D$ and $E$ related?
  1. Which has more area, Shape $D$ or Shape $F$? Give reasons.
  1. Which has more area, Shape $F$ or Shape $G$? Give reasons.
  1. How does the area of Shape $A$ compare with the area of Shape $G$? Is it twice as big? Four times as big?

    Place one piece over another, or try to cover one piece exactly with two smaller ones.

  1. Find the area of the big square made of all seven pieces, counting in Shape $C$s.
  1. Arrange all seven pieces to make a rectangle. What is its area, counting in Shape $C$s? Give reasons.
  1. Do the square and the rectangle made from the seven pieces have the same perimeter? Explain your answer.
