Perimeter of a rectangle

Perimeter of a rectangle $= 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$

Perimeter of a square

Debojeet wants to stick coloured tape all around a square photo frame. Each side of the frame is 1 m. How much tape does he need? The tape goes all the way around, so he needs the perimeter of the square.

Length of tape $= 1\text{ m} + 1\text{ m} + 1\text{ m} + 1\text{ m} = 4\text{ m}$

All four sides of a square are equal. So instead of adding the same length four times, you can multiply it by 4: $4 \times 1\text{ m} = 4\text{ m}$.

Perimeter of a square

Perimeter of a square $= 4 \times \text{length of a side}$

Perimeter of a triangle

A triangle has sides of 4 cm, 5 cm and 7 cm, as in Fig. 6.2. Its perimeter is $4\text{ cm} + 5\text{ cm} + 7\text{ cm} = 16\text{ cm}$. For any triangle, add the lengths of its three sides.

Example 1

Akshi wants to sew lace all around a rectangular tablecloth. The tablecloth is 3 m long and 2 m wide. How much lace does she need?

The lace goes all around the edge, so its length is the perimeter of the tablecloth.

Perimeter $= 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$

$= 2 \times (3\text{ m} + 2\text{ m}) = 2 \times 5\text{ m} = 10\text{ m}$

Akshi needs 10 m of lace.

Sometimes you go around a shape more than once. Then multiply the perimeter by the number of rounds.
