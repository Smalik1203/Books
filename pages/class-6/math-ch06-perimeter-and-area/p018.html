Exercise Set 6.13
  1. One rectangle is 5 m by 10 m and another is 2 m by 7 m. Give the length and width of a rectangle whose area is the sum of their areas.
  1. A rectangular garden is 50 m long. Its area is 1000 sq m. How wide is the garden?
  1. A room is 6 m long and 5 m wide. A square mat of side 4 m is laid on its floor. What area of the floor is not covered by the mat?
  1. A garden is 15 m long and 12 m wide. In each of its four corners a flower bed 2 m long and 1 m wide is dug. How much area is left for a lawn?
  1. Shape $A$ has an area of 18 square units and Shape $B$ has an area of 20 square units. But Shape $A$ has a longer perimeter than Shape $B$. Draw two shapes like this on squared paper.
  1. On a page of your notebook, draw a rectangular border that is 1 cm from the top and bottom edges and 1.5 cm from the left and right edges. What is the perimeter of the border?
  1. Draw a rectangle 12 units long and 8 units wide. Inside it, draw another rectangle that does not touch the first one and has exactly half its area.
  1. A square sheet of paper is folded in half and cut along the fold into two rectangles. Whatever the size of the square, one of these statements is always true. Which one?
    1. The area of each rectangle is more than the area of the square.
    2. The perimeter of the square is more than the two perimeters of the rectangles added together.
    3. The two perimeters of the rectangles added together are always $1\frac{1}{2}$ times the perimeter of the square.
    4. The area of the square is always three times the two areas of the rectangles added together.
What this chapter showed
  1. The perimeter of a closed figure is the distance around its boundary. The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the lengths of its sides.
  2. The perimeter of a rectangle is $2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$. The perimeter of a square is $4 \times \text{side}$, and of an equilateral triangle $3 \times \text{side}$.
  3. The area of a closed figure is the amount of surface it covers. Area is measured in square units.
  4. The area of a rectangle is length $\times$ width. The area of a square is side $\times$ side.
  5. Two figures can have the same area but different perimeters, or the same perimeter but different areas.
  6. The area of a shape can be found, or estimated, by counting unit squares, or by splitting it into rectangles and triangles.
  7. A triangle with one side of a rectangle as its base and its top corner on the opposite side has half the area of the rectangle.
