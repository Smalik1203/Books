Why the other options are wrong

A9 The 6 full squares and the 4 more-than-half squares count 1 each. The 2 half squares count $\frac{1}{2}$ each, which makes 1. The 3 less-than-half squares are left out. That is $6 + 4 + 1 = 11$, option (d). Option (a) counts every square, and option (b) counts the half squares as whole ones.
B1 Both areas are 36 sq cm, so A is true. But the perimeters are 24 cm and 26 cm, so R is false. The answer is (c).
B3 The inside of the path is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m, because the path takes 1 m from each end. So the path is $96 - 60 = 36$ sq m, option (b). Option (a) adds up four strips along the walls, $12 + 12 + 8 + 8 = 40$, and counts the four corner squares twice.
B4 Statement (i) is false: 9 by 4 and 6 by 6 both have area 36, with perimeters 26 and 24. Statement (ii) is true: $5 \times 5 = 25$ and $6 \times 4 = 24$. Statement (iii) is true, since $4 \times$ the side doubles when the side doubles. The answer is (b).
B5 A side of 2 gives area 4, and a side of 4 gives area 16, which is four times as much. So A is false. R is true, so the answer is (d).
C1 Length and width add up to 15. The pairs are 1 and 14, 2 and 13, and so on to 7 and 8. The areas grow as the two sides get closer, and 7 by 8 gives 56, option (c). Option (d) is $15 \times 15$, which takes half the perimeter as a side.
C2 In 6 m there are 12 tiles of 50 cm, and in 4 m there are 8. So $12 \times 8 = 96$ tiles, option (d). Option (a) puts one tile in each square metre, but a square metre holds 4 of these tiles.
C6 Triangle $ABE$ has half the area of the rectangle wherever $E$ is: 20 sq cm. The two other triangles fill the rest, $40 - 20 = 20$ sq cm, option (a). So option (d) is wrong: the position of $E$ does not matter.
C8 Both perimeters have $4s$ in them, so compare $4d$ with $4s$. A diagonal unit crosses a square from corner to corner, so it is longer than a straight unit. So $4s + 4d$ is longer, option (b). Option (d) is wrong because we know which unit is longer without measuring.

Many of these questions turned on one thing: a perimeter is a length around a shape, and an area is the number of squares inside it. When a shape changes, ask which of the two stays the same. The answer usually shows you the way.
