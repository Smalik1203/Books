Area on squared paper

Look at two leaves of different shapes. Can you tell just by looking which one covers more surface? It is hard to be sure. So how do you find the area of a shape that is not made of rectangles, such as a leaf? Trace the shape onto squared paper, where each square is 1 unit by 1 unit, or 1 square unit. Then count the squares inside the shape. Most shapes will cut through some squares at their edge. These part squares are counted by four rules, and the rules give an estimate of the area, not an exact value.

Counting squares
  • Count a full square, or a part more than half, as 1 square unit.
  • Count exactly half as $\frac{1}{2}$ square unit. Leave out less than half.

The smaller the squares, the closer this estimate comes to the true area. A shape drawn on squares of 1 cm gives a better estimate than the same shape on squares of 2 cm.
