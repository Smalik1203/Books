Area on squared paper

How do you find the area of a shape that is not made of rectangles, such as a leaf? Trace the shape onto squared paper, where each square is 1 unit by 1 unit, or 1 square unit. Then count the squares inside the shape. Most shapes will cut through some squares at their edge. These part squares are counted by four rules, and the rules give an estimate of the area, not an exact value.

Counting squares

1. Count each full square as 1 square unit.

2. Leave out a part of a square that is less than half.

3. Count a part that is more than half a square as 1 square unit.

4. Count a part that is exactly half a square as $\frac{1}{2}$ square unit.

Why do we measure area in squares?

Draw a circle of diameter 3 units on squared paper. Count the squares to find its area. Now think: why not use small circles as units instead of squares?

Circles cannot be packed together without gaps. Fig. 6.13 shows the same rectangle packed with circles in two ways. One way fits 42 circles and the other fits 44, and both leave gaps. So circles do not give a clear count of area.
