Problem 4

On squared paper, $AB$ is 6 units long along a grid line. Point $C$ is 4 units above a point $F$ on $AB$, and $AF$ is 4 units. The area of triangle $ABC$ is

  1. 24 square units
  2. 12 square units
  3. 8 square units
  4. 4 square units
Solution

The line $CF$ splits the triangle into two triangles. Each is half of a rectangle 4 units tall.

Triangle $AFC$ $=$ half of $4 \times 4 = 8$

Triangle $FBC$ $=$ half of $2 \times 4 = 4$

Triangle $ABC$ $= 8 + 4 = 12$ square units

The answer is (b). It is also half of the 6 by 4 rectangle on $AB$. Option (a) is the whole rectangle, with the half left out. Options (c) and (d) are the two small triangles, each counted alone.

Point $C$ could be anywhere along the line 4 units above $AB$, between $A$ and $B$. The two small triangles would change size, but together they would always make 12 square units.

Problem 5

Ten unit squares are joined in one row. The square at one end is taken away and placed on top of the second square from the other end. The perimeter of the figure

  1. goes up by 2 units
  2. goes down by 2 units
  3. stays the same
  4. goes up by 4 units
Solution

Follow the change in two steps.

Row of 10: perimeter $= 10 + 10 + 1 + 1 = 22$ units

Take away the end square: $9 + 9 + 1 + 1 = 20$ units

Add a square on top: it shares 1 side, so $20 - 1 + 3 = 22$ units

The answer is (c). Taking the end square away removed 3 sides and uncovered 1, so the perimeter went down by 2. Placing it on top added 3 sides and covered 1, so it went up by 2. Option (a) sees only the second step, option (b) only the first, and option (d) forgets the side that is covered.
