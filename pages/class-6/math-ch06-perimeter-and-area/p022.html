Summary
  1. The perimeter of a closed figure is the distance around its boundary, going around it once. The perimeter of a polygon is the sum of the lengths of all its sides.
  2. Perimeter of a rectangle $= 2 \times (\text{length} + \text{breadth})$. A rectangle 12 cm long and 8 cm wide has a perimeter of $2 \times 20 = 40$ cm.
  3. Perimeter of a square $= 4 \times \text{side}$, and perimeter of an equilateral triangle $= 3 \times \text{side}$. A regular polygon has all its sides equal and all its angles equal.
  4. To find the distance run in full rounds of a track, multiply the perimeter of the track by the number of rounds.
  5. On dot paper, a gap between two dots next to each other is a straight unit, and a gap across a square from corner to corner is a diagonal unit. The two are not the same length, so we count them separately.
  6. The area of a closed figure is the amount of surface it covers. We measure it in unit squares, such as sq cm and sq m.
  7. Area of a rectangle $= \text{length} \times \text{width}$, and area of a square $= \text{side} \times \text{side}$. To find the area of a shape made of rectangles, split it into rectangles, or take a part away from a whole.
  8. On squared paper, count a full square, or a part more than half, as 1; count exactly half as $\frac{1}{2}$; and leave out less than half. This gives an estimate of the area.
  9. A triangle with one side of a rectangle as its base, and its top corner on the opposite side, has half the area of the rectangle.
  10. Figures with the same area can have different perimeters. Figures with the same perimeter can have different areas.
