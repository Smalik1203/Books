So far you have grouped plants using only the stem. Leaves give us another way to sort the same plants. We can compare these groups with the stem groups.

Lines on a Leaf

Hold a leaf up against the light. You will see thin lines running through it. These lines are veins. The pattern the veins make is called venation.

Veins contain tissues that transport water and dissolved substances. They carry water into the leaf and sugars made in the leaf to other parts of the plant. They also help support the leaf.

Activity 2.4

You will need: fallen leaves of at least four different plants, thin paper, a pencil with soft lead.

1.

Put a leaf on the desk with its back side facing up. The veins stand out more on that side.

2.

Place a sheet of thin paper over the leaf.

3.

Hold the paper still. Rub the side of the pencil lead flat across it, using light strokes.

4.

Check the print against the leaf. If the veins are unclear, adjust the paper or pressure and try again.

5.

Do this for every leaf. Write the plant's name next to each print.

Put all your prints side by side.

Group the prints by their vein patterns. How many groups does your sample support? Explain your rule. If all the patterns look alike, compare with another group’s leaves.
