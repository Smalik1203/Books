Two Kinds of Root

Compare several seedlings of each kind. Did they germinate at the same time? Root systems change as seedlings develop, so record the age as well as the shape.

Compare root systems
Taproot systemFibrous root system
A chana seedling usually develops one main root with smaller side roots. A main root of this kind is a taproot. Mustard and radish also develop taproots. The enlarged edible part of a radish includes a swollen taproot that stores food. As the wheat seedling develops, several thin roots appear. Later, more roots grow from the base of the stem, forming a spreading bunch without one dominant main root. These are called fibrous roots. Grass, maize and lemongrass have them.

Both root systems anchor plants and take up water and dissolved minerals. Their branching patterns differ; their jobs overlap.

A taproot can grow deep and reach water below the surface. A deep root system can help a plant through a dry spell, although root depth also depends on the species and the soil.

Many fibrous root systems spread through the upper soil. A dense network helps hold loose soil together and take up water near the surface. Some fibrous roots also reach deep into the ground.
