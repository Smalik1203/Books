2.2.2 Grouping Animals

Animals vary as much as plants do. So the same question comes up. Which feature should we use to group them?

Colour or size may be useful for some questions. To compare movement, we need to record how an animal moves and which body parts it uses.

Use your animal records as a starting point. Add observations from direct watching or a reliable video, and identify which source you used.

Investigate
1.

Choose four animals you can safely watch or observe in a reliable video. Include one that moves in more than one way.

2.

For each one, write how it moves and which body part it uses.

3.

Use Table 2.5 as a guide for your notebook table.

4.

Group your animals using the body parts you observed in use. Allow a record to appear in more than one group if your rule requires it.

Think It Through

A pigeon walks and flies. How would you record it in a grouping by body parts used for movement? Would forcing every animal into only one group lose information? Explain with your records.
