Know a scientist

Salim Ali (1896–1987) spent many years travelling across India, watching birds. He recorded where they lived, when they arrived, and where they went next. Watching carefully like this, year after year, is how scattered sightings turn into real knowledge. His fieldwork and advocacy helped strengthen bird conservation in India. His books also made it possible for ordinary people to name the birds around them. He is remembered as the Birdman of India.

Protecting habitats

Habitat loss has threatened animals such as the Bengal tiger and the Great Indian bustard. Project Tiger began in 1973 to protect tigers and their habitats. The cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952; a reintroduction programme began in 2022. Protecting suitable habitat is essential to conservation.

Governments and communities both protect habitats. Sacred groves are patches of vegetation protected through local cultural or religious traditions. Rules about cutting, collecting and hunting vary. Some groves preserve old trees and habitat missing from nearby cleared land. Some also have legal protection. Find out how a grove near you is protected.

Checking Whether Protection Helps

Suppose a class records more kinds of birds in a protected grove than in a nearby clearing. Does that record alone show that protection caused the difference?

Check how the records were made. Were both places watched for the same length of time, at similar times of day? Could differences in area, water or vegetation also matter?

Plan repeated visits using the same method. Decide what to record so another class could check your comparison. A useful conclusion must account for both the evidence and its limits.
