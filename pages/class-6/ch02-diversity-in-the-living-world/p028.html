Learning Further

Walk around your school with your teacher or the gardener. Label the plants with their local names. Make a list in your notebook. For each plant, record whether its stems are woody and how it grows: upright, climbing or trailing. Use herb, shrub or tree where the evidence supports it; more than one description can apply.

Over one term, build a class biodiversity register. Revisit the same places for the same length of time. Record dates, weather and how you searched. Compare records, but treat a missing sighting as "not recorded", not proof of disappearance. Plan another check before concluding that a kind has gone.

Interview an older person about local plants and animals. Ask where and when they saw them. Compare one recollection with a dated photograph, written record or another account. Identify what remains uncertain.

Find out if there is a sacred grove, a protected forest or a bird sanctuary near your district. Who protects it? What is it being protected from?

Read about one Indian scientist working on biodiversity today. Write a short report. You could start with Divya Mudappa, Vidya Athreya, Uma Ramakrishnan, Nandini Velho, Ghazala Shahabuddin, Usha Lachungpa or Divya Karnad.

Grow chana and wheat again. For each kind, use equal numbers of seeds from the same batch in two trays: one in light and one in darkness. Keep water, tray material and temperature as similar as possible. For seven days, record the number germinating, shoot length and leaf colour at the same time each day. Predict first; compare each seed kind’s light and dark trays separately. Briefly inspect the dark trays and return them to darkness.
