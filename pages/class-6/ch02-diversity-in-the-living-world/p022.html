Among flowering plants, those whose seeds have two cotyledons are called dicotyledons. We say dicots for short. Plants whose seeds have one cotyledon are called monocotyledons, or monocots.

Now put all three findings together.

Comparing flowering plants — typical features
— Dicots Monocots
Cotyledons in the seed Two One
Leaf venation Reticulate Parallel
Roots Taproot Fibrous
Examples Chana, mustard, hibiscus, mango Wheat, maize, grass, banana

If seed anatomy identifies a plant as a monocot, you can predict that it is likely to have parallel venation and fibrous roots. Check those features before claiming the prediction is correct.
