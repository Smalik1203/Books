What Is Inside a Seed

A soaked chana can separate into two fleshy seed leaves called cotyledons. They store food for the young plant. Between them is the rest of the embryo, including a tiny root and shoot. Compare your specimen with the labelled illustration. Trace each label to the part it names, then locate that part in your specimen if it is visible.

In maize, one thin cotyledon lies beside the embryo’s tiny shoot and root. The larger pale region is stored food called the endosperm; it is not a second cotyledon. Failure to split the grain is not itself proof of the cotyledon count.

Cotyledon number, leaf venation and root type are associated features. The comparison does not show that cotyledon number causes the other two.

Among flowering plants, those whose seeds have two cotyledons are called dicotyledons. We say dicots for short.

Plants whose seeds have one cotyledon are called monocotyledons, or monocots.
