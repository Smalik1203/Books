The Two Go Together

Pool the class records. Count how many support a connection and how many do not. Compare the result with these common patterns in flowering plants.

Many familiar flowering plants with reticulate venation develop taproots; those with parallel venation commonly develop fibrous root systems.

Sadabahar has net-like veins and a taproot. Chana has net-like veins and a taproot. Lemongrass has parallel veins and fibrous roots. Wheat has parallel veins and fibrous roots.

These are typical associations, not rules without exceptions. Age, how a plant was propagated, and unusual features can affect what you observe. Keep exceptions in the record.

Could another feature help us recognise these groups? Look inside a seed.

Investigate
1.

Soak a few chana seeds and a few maize seeds in water for two days, until they are soft and swollen.

2.

Peel the thin outer coat off a soaked chana. Now try to split the seed with your thumbnail.

3.

Try gently to separate a soaked maize grain. Do not use a knife or force it apart. Record what you can see; compare with the labelled section afterwards.
