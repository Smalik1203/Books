Go outside your classroom. Stand still for two minutes.

Look for grass in a crack, insects near a wall, or birds overhead. Choose two plants. Compare their leaves. What differences can you describe?

A planted tree and a plant growing without anyone sowing it both belong in your record. You do not need to know how something arrived before you can observe it.

This chapter is about that variety. How much of it is there? How do we make sense of it? And why are the living things in one place so different from the living things in another?

2.1 The Variety Around Us

A quick glance may tell you that a tree is there. A closer look can reveal the shape of its leaves, where its branches begin, or insects on its bark. Listening may reveal a bird you cannot see.

Looking carefully is a skill. Like any skill, it gets better with practice.
