Adaptations

Inherited features that help organisms survive and reproduce in their usual environment are called adaptations. They develop in populations over generations; an individual does not acquire a needed feature simply by trying.

Built for the Place

Look at a plant from a hot desert. Now look at a deodar tree, high in the Himalayas.
  • Consider a cactus adapted to a hot, dry habitat.
  • Its fleshy stem stores water and its waxy surface reduces water loss.
  • In many cacti, leaves are reduced to spines and the green stem carries out photosynthesis.
  • On snowy mountain slopes, snow can load branches heavily.
  • The conical form and sloping branches of a deodar can help shed snow.
  • Temperature, soil and water also affect where a tree can grow.

These features were not chosen by individual plants. They are inherited features found in populations living in particular conditions. Other plants may survive there using different features.
