Removing a tree can remove shade, nest sites and a source of fallen leaves. The effects depend on which organisms use it and what other resources are available nearby.

Choose one plant or animal from your own record. Draw it in the centre of a notebook page. Add other living things it may depend on, or that may depend on it. Use arrows labelled "food", "shelter" or another relationship. Mark each link as observed, read about, or still a guess. Compare your diagram with a partner. Choose one uncertain link and suggest how you could check it. If one organism disappeared from your diagram, which links would be affected? Could another organism supply the same resource? Explain what you know and what you would need to find out.

2.2 Making Sense of the Variety

Your records may contain many kinds of living things. Grouping them can help you compare a feature without searching through every entry.

In a stationery shop, keeping pens together helps a customer find a pen. Arranging goods by price answers a different question: what can I buy with this amount of money?
