2.2 Making Sense of the Variety

Your records may contain many kinds of living things. Grouping them can help you compare a feature without searching through every entry.

In a stationery shop, keeping pens together helps a customer find a pen. Arranging goods by price answers a different question: what can I buy with this amount of money?

Scientists do the same with living things. Putting things into groups by the features they share is called grouping.

Investigate

You will need: old magazines or newspapers, scissors, glue, small cards.

1.

Cut out pictures of plants and animals. Paste one on each card. Try to make at least thirty cards in the class.

2.

Work in groups of five or six. Put all your cards together.

3.

Sort the pile into groups. You decide what the groups will be.

4.

Write down, in one line, the rule you used.

5.

Now mix all the cards again. Sort them a second time, using a completely different rule.

Compare both your sortings with another group's.

Did any two groups in the class use the same rule?

Safety: Use scissors carefully, with your teacher’s guidance.
