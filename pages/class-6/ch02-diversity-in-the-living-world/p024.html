Keywords

Adaptation — An inherited feature that helps an organism survive and reproduce in its usual environment.

Amphibian — An animal belonging to the group that includes frogs and toads; many use water and land during their lives.

Aquatic — Living in water, or describing a habitat in water.

Biodiversity — The variety of living things found in a region.

Climber — A plant that grows upwards using external support.

Cotyledon — A seed leaf that is part of the embryo inside a seed.

Creeper — A plant with stems that trail along the ground.

Dicot — A flowering plant whose seed has two cotyledons.

Endosperm — Food-storing tissue in a seed that nourishes the developing embryo.

Fibrous root system — A spreading bunch of roots without one dominant main root.

Grouping — Arranging things by features they share, using a stated rule.

Habitat — The place where an organism lives, including its conditions and resources.

Herb — A plant with soft, non-woody stems; it need not be small.

Monocot — A flowering plant whose seed has one cotyledon.

Parallel venation — A leaf-vein pattern in which many veins run alongside one another.

Pseudostem — A trunk-like structure formed by overlapping leaf bases, as in banana.

Reticulate venation — A leaf-vein pattern in which veins branch and join to form a network.

Sacred grove — A patch of vegetation protected through local cultural or religious traditions.

Shrub — A woody plant that usually branches near the ground, often with several main stems.

Taproot — The main root of a root system, with smaller roots branching from it.

Terrestrial — Living on land, or describing a habitat on land.

Tree — A woody plant that usually develops a main trunk supporting a crown of branches.

Venation — The pattern of veins in a leaf.
