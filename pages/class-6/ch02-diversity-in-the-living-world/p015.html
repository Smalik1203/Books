Many fibrous root systems spread through the upper soil. A dense network helps hold loose soil together and take up water near the surface. Some fibrous roots also reach deep into the ground.

A Pattern Appears

You now know two ways to sort plants. One uses leaves. One uses roots.

Can a record of leaf venation help you predict root type? First collect observations, then test any pattern against another plant.

Investigate
1.

With your teacher or gardener, select five kinds of flowering plants with developed leaves and visible roots. Use spare nursery plants, not plants pulled from the wild.

2.

Make a notebook table using the headings in Table 2.4. Examine four kinds first and record their venation and root type. Mark unclear features as uncertain.

3.

Look for a connection in these four records. Use it to predict the root type of the fifth kind from its leaf. Record the prediction before examining its roots.

4.

Check the roots of the fifth kind. Does this new observation support your proposed pattern? Compare results across the class.

5.

Handle the roots gently and replant the plants with your teacher’s help.
