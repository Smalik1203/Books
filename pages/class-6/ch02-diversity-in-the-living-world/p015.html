Compare vein patterns
Reticulate venationParallel venation
In reticulate venation, veins branch to form a net. A thick middle vein is visible in the hibiscus leaf; finer veins branch and join around it. Mango, neem and peepal also have net-like venation. In other leaves, many veins run alongside one another. This pattern is called parallel venation. In a grass blade they run along the length of the leaf. A banana leaf has a strong middle vein, with many parallel side veins running out towards its edges. Parallel venation does not mean that a leaf has no middle vein.

These two patterns help you compare many of the leaves around you. Look closely: real leaves sometimes show exceptions.

Predict the venation of wheat or maize from the examples. Then inspect a leaf or a clear photograph. Was your prediction supported? Leaf shape alone is not proof of its vein pattern.
