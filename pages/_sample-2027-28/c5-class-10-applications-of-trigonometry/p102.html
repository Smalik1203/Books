Let the tower be $h$ m high and let you start $d$ m from its foot, so ${\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{d} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}}$. At the new point you are $\frac{d}{3}$ m from the foot, so the new angle $\theta$ has ${\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d/3} = 3 \times \frac{h}{d} = \frac{3}{\sqrt{3}} = \sqrt{3}}$, and from Table 8.19, ${\theta = 60^\circ}$. Neither $h$ nor $d$ was needed, only their ratio. Dividing the distance by 3 multiplied the tangent by 3, but the angle only doubled, from $30^\circ$ to $60^\circ$: it did not become $90^\circ$. An angle and its tangent do not grow in the same proportion.

Example 1 · Single correct

A kite's string is 60 m long and makes an angle of $30^\circ$ with the ground. Taking the string as straight, how high is the kite?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 30 m
  2. $30\sqrt{3}$ m
  3. 60 m
  4. $20\sqrt{3}$ m

Solution.

Step 1The string is the hypotenuse and the height is opposite the $30^\circ$ angle: $h = 60 \sin 30^\circ$.
Step 2$h = 60 \times \frac{1}{2} = 30$ m.
Answer(a) 30 m
Example 2 · Single correct

From the top of a building 30 m tall, the angle of depression of a car on the ground is $30^\circ$. How far is the car from the foot of the building?

Choose one correct option.

  1. 30 m
  2. 15 m
  3. 60 m
  4. $30\sqrt{3}$ m

Solution.

Step 1The angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the car: $\tan 30^\circ = \frac{30}{d}$.
Step 2$d = 30\sqrt{3}$ m.
Answer(d) $30\sqrt{3}$ m
