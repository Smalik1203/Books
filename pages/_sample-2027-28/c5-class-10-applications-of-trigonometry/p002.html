Now look again at Fig. 8.2. The girl on the balcony is looking down at a flower pot on a step of the temple. This time the line of sight is below the horizontal level of her eye. The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal is called the angle of depression of the flower pot, seen from her eye.

So when the point you are looking at is below the horizontal level of your eye, you lower your head to look at it. The angle between the line of sight and the horizontal is then the angle of depression of the point (Fig. 9.3).

Three words for heights and distances

The line of sight is the line from the eye of an observer to the point being looked at.

The angle of elevation of a point above the horizontal level of the eye is the angle between the line of sight and the horizontal.

The angle of depression of a point below the horizontal level of the eye is the angle between the line of sight and the horizontal.

Now go back to Fig. 9.1. Suppose you want to find the height CD of the minar without measuring it. What do you need to know? You need three things:

  1. the distance DE of the student from the foot of the minar
  2. the angle of elevation, $\angle BAC$, of the top of the minar
  3. the height AE of the student.

If you know these three, how can you find the height of the minar? From the figure, ${CD = CB + BD}$, and BD is equal to AE, the height of the student. So you need only BC.
