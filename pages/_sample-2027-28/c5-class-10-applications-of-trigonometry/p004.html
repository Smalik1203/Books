Example 2

An electrician has to repair a fault on an electric pole 5 m high. She needs to reach a point 1.3 m below the top of the pole (Fig. 9.5). She will use a ladder inclined at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. How long should the ladder be to reach that point? How far from the foot of the pole should she place the foot of the ladder? (You may take ${\sqrt{3} = 1.73}$.)

Solution.

Step 1 in Fig. 9.5, AD is the pole and B is the point she has to reach, so ${BD = AD - AB = 5 - 1.3 = 3.7}$ m
Step 2 the ladder BC is the hypotenuse of the right triangle BDC, and BD is opposite the angle at C, so $\dfrac{BD}{BC} = \sin 60^\circ$ opposite over hypotenuse
Step 3 $\dfrac{3.7}{BC} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$,   so   $BC = \dfrac{3.7 \times 2}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{7.4}{1.73} = 4.28$   (approx.)
Step 4 $\dfrac{DC}{BD} = \cot 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ DC is adjacent to the angle at C
Step 5 $DC = \dfrac{3.7}{\sqrt{3}} = \dfrac{3.7}{1.73} = 2.14$   (approx.)
Answer The ladder should be about 4.28 m long, and its foot about 2.14 m from the pole
