Example 3 · Single correct

A 10 m ladder leans against a wall at $60^\circ$ to the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?

Choose one correct option.

  1. $5\sqrt{3}$ m
  2. 5 m
  3. $10\sqrt{3}$ m
  4. 10 m

Solution.

Step 1The height is opposite the $60^\circ$ angle: $h = 10 \sin 60^\circ$.
Step 2$h = 10 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3}$ m.
Answer(a) $5\sqrt{3}$ m
Example 4 · Multiple correct

The top of a tower 30 m tall is seen at $30^\circ$ from $A$ and at $60^\circ$ from $B$, on the same side and in line with its foot. Which statements are correct? Select all correct options.

  1. $A$ is $30\sqrt{3}$ m from the foot.
  2. $B$ is $10\sqrt{3}$ m from the foot.
  3. $AB = 20\sqrt{3}$ m
  4. $AB = 30$ m

Solution.

Step 1From $A$: $30 \div \tan 30^\circ = 30\sqrt{3}$; from $B$: $30 \div \tan 60^\circ = 10\sqrt{3}$.
Step 2$AB = 30\sqrt{3} - 10\sqrt{3} = 20\sqrt{3}$ m.
Answer(a), (b), (c)
Example 5 · Multiple correct

Which statements are correct? Select all correct options.

  1. The angle of depression of $B$ from $A$ equals the angle of elevation of $A$ from $B$.
  2. Walking towards a tower, the angle of elevation of its top increases.
  3. When a pole's shadow equals its height, the sun's elevation is $60^\circ$.
  4. An angle of depression is measured from the vertical.

Solution.

Step 1Alternate angles are equal; and as the distance shrinks, $\tan\theta = \frac{h}{d}$ grows.
Step 2$\tan\theta = 1$ gives $45^\circ$, not $60^\circ$; and both angles are from the horizontal.
Answer(a), (b)
