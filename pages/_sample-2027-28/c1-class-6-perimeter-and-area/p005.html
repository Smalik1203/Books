Straight units and diagonal units

The dots in Fig. 6.5 are 1 unit apart. Akshi counts the gaps along the sides and says the perimeter is 9 units. Toshi says it must be more. What do you think?

On the two short sides, each gap joins two dots next to each other. We call these straight units. On the long side, each gap crosses a square from corner to corner. We call these diagonal units. Measure one of each with your scale. Are they the same length?

Since the two kinds of unit are not the same, we count them separately. The triangle has 6 straight units and 3 diagonal units. We write its perimeter in short as $6s + 3d$ units.

Perimeter of a regular polygon

A closed figure with all its sides equal and all its angles equal is called a regular polygon. A square is one. So is an equilateral triangle, with three equal sides and three equal angles, and a regular pentagon, with five of each. You met them in Chapter 1.

A rectangle that is not a square is not regular: its angles are equal, its sides are not.

The perimeter of any triangle is the sum of its three sides. In the equilateral triangle $ABC$ of Fig. 6.6, all three sides are equal.

Perimeter $= AB + BC + CA = AB + AB + AB = 3 \times AB$

Perimeter of an equilateral triangle

Perimeter of an equilateral triangle $= 3 \times \text{length of a side}$
