Suppose there is one, and call it $(x, y)$. Being $3$ units from the origin means $x^2 + y^2 = 9$. A square is never negative, so $x^2$ is at most $9$, and $x$ lies somewhere from $-3$ to $3$. Then $10 - x$ is at least $7$, and the distance from $(x, y)$ to $(10, 0)$ is at least $7$ as well, since adding $y^2$ under the root can only make it larger. A distance that is at least $7$ is never $5$, so there is no such point. Notice that we did not solve any equation. One fact, that a square cannot be negative, kept $x$ between $-3$ and $3$, and that was enough. Notice also that a question can have no answer at all, and that saying so, with the reason, is the answer.

None of these questions used anything outside the chapter. What each needed was the question: what can I use from what I already know? The examples that follow ask it again.

For which values of $k$ does the point $(k - 3, 5)$ lie in Quadrant II?

Choose one correct option.

  1. $k \lt 3$
  2. $k \gt 3$
  3. $k = 3$
  4. $k \lt 5$

Solution.

Step 1Quadrant II needs $x \lt 0$ and $y \gt 0$; 5 is positive, so $k - 3 \lt 0$, that is $k \lt 3$.
Answer(a) $k \lt 3$
