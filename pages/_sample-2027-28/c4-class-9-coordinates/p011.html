Fig. 1.7 adds the missing lines. Draw a line straight down from $A$ and a line across from $D$. They meet at $C (3, 1)$. Now $ACD$ is a right-angled triangle whose two shorter sides are parallel to the axes, and we already know how to measure those.

The distance moved in the x-direction is the length $CD$, and the distance moved in the y-direction is the length $AC$. Both are differences of coordinates:

$CD =$ x-coordinate of $D -$ x-coordinate of $A = 7 - 3 = 4$

$AC =$ y-coordinate of $A -$ y-coordinate of $D = 4 - 1 = 3$

Now use the Baudhāyana–Pythagoras Theorem:

$$AD = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5 \text{ units}$$

Find the other two sides in the same way. For $DM$ the shifts are 2 and 5; for $MA$ they are 6 and 2.

$DM = \sqrt{2^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{29}$ units

$MA = \sqrt{6^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{40}$ units

Fig. 1.8 shows the same construction with letters in place of numbers. Call the two points $A (x_1, y_1)$ and $D (x_2, y_2)$. Draw a vertical line from $A$ and a horizontal line from $D$. They meet at $F (x_1, y_2)$, which has the same x-coordinate as $A$ and the same y-coordinate as $D$. So the two legs are easy to measure: $FD$ runs horizontally, so its length is $x_2 - x_1$, and $AF$ runs vertically, so its length is $y_2 - y_1$.
