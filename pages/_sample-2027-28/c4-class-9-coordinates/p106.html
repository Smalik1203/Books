Example 9 · Matching

Match List I with List II. Each entry has exactly one match.

List IList II
(P) $(-3, 5)$(1) on the $x$-axis
(Q) $(4, -2)$(2) Quadrant IV
(R) $(0, -6)$(3) Quadrant II
(S) $(-7, 0)$(4) on the $y$-axis

Choose the correct combination.

  1. P–2, Q–3, R–4, S–1
  2. P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
  3. P–3, Q–4, R–2, S–1
  4. P–1, Q–2, R–4, S–3

Solution.

Step 1$x \lt 0 \lt y$ is Quadrant II and $y \lt 0 \lt x$ Quadrant IV; $x = 0$ is the $y$-axis and $y = 0$ the $x$-axis.
Answer(b) P–3, Q–2, R–4, S–1
Example 10 · Paragraph-based

Two mobile towers stand at $A(-4, 0)$ and $B(4, 0)$ on a map where one unit is one kilometre. A phone at $P(x, y)$ is equally far from both towers.

  1. Which of these could be $P$?
    1. $(3, 0)$
    2. $(-2, 4)$
    3. $(0, 5)$
    4. $(1, 1)$
  2. Numerical answer. $P$ is above the x-axis and 5 km from each tower. How far is $P$ from the origin, in km?

Solution.

Step 1$PA^2 = PB^2$ gives $(x + 4)^2 = (x - 4)^2$, so $16x = 0$ and $x = 0$: $P$ lies on the y-axis.
Step 2For $(0, y)$: $16 + y^2 = 25$, so $y = 3$, and $P(0, 3)$ is 3 km from the origin.
Answer(i) (c) $(0, 5)$; (ii) 3
