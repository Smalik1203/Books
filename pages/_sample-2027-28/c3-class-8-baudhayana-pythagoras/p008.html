2.6.2 Using it twice: the diagonal of a box

A hall is $4$ m by $3$ m on the floor and $12$ m to the ceiling. How far is it from one bottom corner to the opposite top corner? This is the longest straight line that fits inside the room.

First find the floor diagonal. It is one short side of a second right triangle. The other short side is the height, and the hypotenuse is the line we want.

across the floor $d^2 = 4^2 + 3^2 = 25$
then up $D^2 = d^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$, so $D = 13$ m

We never needed the floor diagonal itself, only its square. So the whole working can be written in one line, $\ D^2 = l^2 + b^2 + h^2$. Two Baudhāyana triples appeared on the way: $(3,4,5)$ across the floor and $(5,12,13)$ up to the corner.

2.6.3 A lotus from the Līlāvatī

Bhāskarāchārya gave this in his Līlāvatī in the twelfth century. The depth is not given.

Example 3

A lotus stands with the tip of its stem $1$ unit above the water. A breeze pushes it over until the tip touches the water $3$ units from where it stood. How deep is the lake?

Let $x$ be the depth. The stem is $x + 1$ long, and bending it over does not change its length, so the slanting stem is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with short sides $3$ and $x$.

Solution.

Step 1 $3^2 + x^2 = (x+1)^2$ the theorem
Step 2 $9 + x^2 = x^2 + 2x + 1$ expand
Step 3 $9 = 2x + 1$ take $x^2$ off both sides
Step 4 $x = 4$ solve
Answer The lake is $4$ units deep.

The $x^2$ cancels from both sides, leaving a linear equation of the kind solved in Class 7.
