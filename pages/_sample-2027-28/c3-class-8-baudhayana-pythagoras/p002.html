2.2The Square on the Diagonal

Draw a square and one of its diagonals, and build a new square standing on that diagonal. The new square is tilted, and Baudhāyana says its area is exactly twice the first. Why?

Extend the bottom and right-hand sides of the original square. Those two lines are the diagonals of the new square, and they cross at a corner of the old one.

Every one of those four triangles has the same two short sides, each a side of the original square, and a right angle between them. So they are all the same size and shape. Each is half the original square, because the diagonal of the original square cuts it into two of them.

the first square two triangles
the tilted square four of the same triangles

Twice as many pieces of the same size is twice the area, and Baudhāyana's rule is proved.

And each new square can stand on the diagonal of the last, doubling the area each time.
