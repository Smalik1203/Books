Using What You Know

This chapter gave you three facts, and the questions below need nothing else. The square on the diagonal of a square has twice its area. In any right-angled triangle $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$: this finds a third length from two, and used the other way it tests whether a corner is a right angle. And whole-number triples like $(3,4,5)$ are rare, so learn to spot them.

Most of the questions below do not mention a right angle. First, find the right-angled triangle that the question has not drawn. Once you draw it, the arithmetic is short. Try each question before reading the answer that follows it.

A square has an area of $200$ cm2. The midpoints of its four sides are joined to make a smaller square. How long is that smaller square's side?

Joining the midpoints of a square's sides halves the area, as Section 2.3 showed with the four folded corners. So there is no need to find the first square's side at all.

inner area half of $200 = 100$ cm2
inner side $\sqrt{100} = 10$ cm

You could instead find the outer side as $\sqrt{200}$ and work with it. That gives the same answer, but through a number that is not exact. The easier way is to halve the area first.

This works in general. In this chapter, areas are often whole numbers and lengths often are not. So for a question about lengths, work with areas for as long as you can, and take the square root once, at the end.

A triangle has sides $12$, $16$ and $20$. Find its area.

To find an area you need a base and a perpendicular height. This triangle gives neither, unless it is right-angled. Test it.

test $12^2 + 16^2 = 144 + 256 = 400 = 20^2$
so area $= \tfrac12 \times 12 \times 16 = 96$
