2.3Halving a Square

Baudhāyana's construction also works in reverse, and Fig. 2.3 has already shown how: to make a square of half the area, join the midpoints of the four sides.

Why is the inside square half? The four corners left over are triangles, each a quarter of the inside square. Fold them inwards and they cover it exactly.

Halving the side does not work. It makes the area a quarter, just as doubling the side made it four times as large. Four squares of half the side fit inside the original.

  1. Which is larger: the square built on the diagonal of a square of side $5$, or a square of side $7$? By how much?
  1. A square has side $10$ cm. Find the areas of the square on its diagonal and of the square on that square's diagonal. Which of the three squares have a whole-number side?

2.4How Long Is the Diagonal?

Baudhāyana's construction never says how long the new side is. This is not a weakness. On a building site, a rule you can carry out with a cord and two pegs is more useful than a decimal. But the length is interesting, and we can find it with what we already know.

Cut a square of side $1$ along a diagonal. Each half is a right triangle with two sides of $1$. Its longest side, opposite the right angle, is called the hypotenuse. Call it $c$.
