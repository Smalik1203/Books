Look at Table 7.9 again. The shorter way keeps every equation that has a new RHS, and leaves out only the lines where the same operation is written on both sides. So the two ways do the same steps, and the shorter way just writes less. Use whichever you find clearer.

What happens with an equation like $\frac{u}{15} = 6$? Here $u$ is divided by 15. Multiplying both sides by 15 undoes the division and leaves only $u$ on the LHS, so $u = 6 \times 15$, which gives $u = 90$. The divisor 15 has moved to the other side, where it multiplies. We can check this: $90 \div 15 = 6$.

Moving a term or a factor

A term added or subtracted on one side can be removed from that side if its additive inverse is written as a term on the other side. For example, $2y + 7 = 21$ becomes $2y = 21 - 7$.

If one side is a product and we remove one of its factors, we divide the other side by that factor. For example, $2y = 14$ becomes $y = 14 \div 2$.

If one side is a quotient and we remove the divisor, we multiply the other side by that divisor. For example, $\frac{u}{15} = 6$ becomes $u = 6 \times 15$.
