Using What You Know

A question is put first and explained after, in running text. A student tries it, then reads how a simple idea becomes a harder question.

A square and a rectangle have the same perimeter. The rectangle is 9 cm long and 5 cm wide. Which of the two has the bigger area?

First find the perimeter they share: $2 \times (9 + 5) = 28$ cm. The square has four equal sides, so each side is $28 \div 4 = 7$ cm, and its area is $7 \times 7 = 49$ sq cm. The rectangle’s area is $9 \times 5 = 45$ sq cm. So the square has the bigger area, even though the two perimeters are equal.

Example 5 · Multiple correct

Two separate, identical squares of side 5 cm are joined along one complete side without overlapping. Which statements are correct?

Select all correct options.

  1. The combined area is 50 cm².
  2. The combined perimeter is 40 cm.
  3. The combined perimeter is 30 cm.
  4. The outside boundary is a rectangle.

Solution.

Step 1The joined shape is a 10 cm by 5 cm rectangle, with area 50 cm² and perimeter 30 cm.
Step 2The shared 5 cm edge is inside; it must not be counted twice in the outside perimeter.
Answer(a), (c), (d)
