The Baudhāyana–Pythagoras theorem set out in the Śulbasūtra, centuries before Pythagoras now gives the remaining side:

d^{2} = 10^{2} - 8^{2} = 100 - 64 = 36, \qquad d = 6

The chord lies 6 cm from the centre — less than the radius, as it must be: a line 10 cm out would only touch the circle.

5.7How Far a Chord Lies from the Centre

The distance from a point to a line means the perpendicular distance — the shortest way across. So the distance from the centre to a chord is measured along the perpendicular from the centre, and Theorem 5 has already told us where that perpendicular lands: on the midpoint of the chord.

Take a paper circle and fold a piece of the boundary inwards; the crease is a chord. Fold again so that the two ends of that crease meet, and open both folds. The second crease crosses the first at its midpoint, square on. Theorems 4 and 5, in paper.

Now make a second chord of the same length somewhere else on the circle, and measure how far it lies from the centre. The two distances come out equal. Try another pair and they come out equal again.
