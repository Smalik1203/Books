The two extreme cases are worth following to the end. Slide a chord towards the centre until d = 0: the formula gives length 2r, and the chord has become a diameter. That is Section 5.2's result — no chord is longer than a diameter — arriving by an entirely different road. Slide it the other way until d = r and the length falls to zero: the chord has shrunk to a single point of the circle. Which is the answer to the question left hanging in Section 5.2. There is no shortest chord, because the moment a chord is short enough to be a candidate it has stopped being a chord at all.

Example 9

A circle has radius 25 cm. One of its chords is 48 cm long and another is 14 cm long. Which lies nearer the centre, and by how much?

Theorem 8 answers the first half before any arithmetic starts: the longer chord is the nearer, so the 48 cm chord is the closer of the two. Only the how much needs calculating, and for that we halve each chord and use the right-angled triangle again.

the 48 cm chord \sqrt{25^{2}-24^{2}} = \sqrt{49} 7 cm
the 14 cm chord \sqrt{25^{2}-7^{2}} = \sqrt{576} 24 cm
