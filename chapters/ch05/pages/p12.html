5.5Chords and the Angles They Subtend

Tie a thread between two points of a wheel and pull it tight. What you have is a chord. Join its two ends to the centre, and the thread subtends an angle there — the angle named in Section 5.2.

Now turn the wheel. The thread goes round with it and comes to rest across a different pair of points; but it is the same thread, so its length has not changed. Has the angle at the centre changed with it?

Section 5.3 all but answers this. Turning the circle about its centre carries the circle onto itself and carries the thread along without stretching it, so nothing in the picture is different except which part of the rim the ends are tied to. The angle cannot have moved.

That is a good reason to believe what follows. It is not yet a proof of it, because the argument assumed the second chord was the first one turned — and a chord of the same length lying somewhere else on the circle has not been shown to be that. Congruent triangles settle the matter without the assumption.

Equal chords of a circle subtend equal angles at the centre.
