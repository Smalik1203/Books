The three angles of \triangle ABC add to 180^\circ, so

\angle BAC = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ

The corollary did all the work: without it, one angle and a diameter would not determine the triangle at all.

5.9Points on One Circle

Section 5.4 counted the circles through two points — infinitely many — and through three, where there is exactly one so long as they are not in a row. Four points are a different matter altogether. Any three of them already fix a circle, so the fourth has no freedom left: either it happens to land on that circle or it does not. Points that all lie on one circle are called concyclic.
