5.6Chords, Midpoints and Perpendiculars

Section 5.5 took the triangle made by a chord and the two radii to its ends, and got two theorems out of it. That triangle has more to give. It is isosceles — which is what Exercise Set 5.3 asked you to show — and an isosceles triangle is symmetric about the line through its apex and the midpoint of its base.

Here the apex is the centre. So draw a circle with centre C, take any chord AB, and let M be the midpoint of AB. What can be said about the segment CM?

Theorem 4

The segment joining the centre of a circle to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to that chord.

Why is this true? Compare the two triangles that CM cuts the big one into.

CA = CB, both being radii.

AM = BM, because M is the midpoint.
