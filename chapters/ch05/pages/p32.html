End-of-Chapter Exercises
  1. A chord of a circle lies 5 cm from the centre, and the radius is 13 cm. How long is the chord?
  2. A circle has diameter 26 cm, and a chord of length 24 cm is drawn in it. How far is that chord from the centre?
  3. A chord of length 16 cm lies 6 cm from the centre. Find the radius.
  4. Prove that the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of the circle.
  5. AB is a diameter of a circle and C is another point on it. What is \angle ACB? Give the reasoning, not just the number.
  6. A cyclic quadrilateral has \angle A = 75^\circ and \angle B = 110^\circ. Find \angle C and \angle D.
  7. A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 5, 5, 12, 12 units, in that order round the shape. Find its area.
  8. Two parallel chords, of lengths 10 cm and 24 cm, lie on the same side of the centre and 7 cm apart. Find the radius of the circle.
  9. Draw a circle in which a chord of length 6 cm stands 3 cm from the centre. What must the radius be?
  10. Justify the claim that no chord of a circle is longer than a diameter. Give two arguments, one of them from Section 5.7.
  11. Take all the chords of a circle that have one fixed length. What shape do their midpoints trace out?
  12. Two chords AB and AC of a circle have the same length. Explain why the centre must lie on the bisector of \angle BAC.
