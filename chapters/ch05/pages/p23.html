The angle an arc subtends at the centre is the angle swept out as you travel along it: start with the radius at A, move round to B the way the arc goes, and measure how far the radius has turned. For the minor arc that is the ordinary angle \angle AOB. For the major arc you sweep the other way and get the reflex angle instead. The two together make a complete turn.

Now a question that is not about the centre at all. Take the arc AXB, and a point D lying on the circle but not on that arc. Join D to A and to B. How big is \angle ADB?

You would expect the answer to depend on where D sits. It does not. Slide D anywhere along the rest of the circle and the angle under which it sees AB does not move at all. Check it before reading on — draw a circle, fix a chord, and measure the angle at three or four points of the far arc.

Theorem 9

The angle an arc subtends at the centre of a circle is twice the angle it subtends at any point of the circle outside the arc.

Let the arc be AFB with centre C, and let D be a point of the circle outside it. Join D to C and carry the line on until it meets the circle again, at E. Two isosceles triangles now do all the work, because CA, CB and CD are all radii.
