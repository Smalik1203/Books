\text{chord} = 2\sqrt{r^{2}-d^{2}} = 2\sqrt{17^{2}-8^{2}}

= 2\sqrt{289-64} = 2\sqrt{225} = 2 \times 15 = 30

The chord is 30 cm long. Compare it with the diameter, 34 cm: it is shorter, as every chord must be, and it would have reached 34 cm only if d had been 0.

Example 8

Two parallel chords of a circle of radius 10 cm are 16 cm and 12 cm long. How far apart are they?

Take each chord's distance from the centre first. The combining comes second, and it is where the care is needed, because the question as asked has two answers.

the 16 cm chord d_1 = \sqrt{10^{2}-8^{2}} = \sqrt{36} 6 cm
the 12 cm chord d_2 = \sqrt{10^{2}-6^{2}} = \sqrt{64} 8 cm

Nothing in the question says which side of the centre each chord is on, and the two arrangements do not agree:

opposite sides 6 + 8 14 cm
the same side 8 - 6 2 cm

Both are right. A question like this is not finished until you say which case you are in — or give both.

It settles the comparison of unequal chords too, which no amount of congruence was going to give us.

Theorem 8

Of two chords of a circle that are not equal, the longer lies nearer the centre.

Suppose AB > DE, at distances d_1 and d_2 from the centre. Then 2\sqrt{r^2 - d_1^2} > 2\sqrt{r^2 - d_2^2}, so r^2 - d_1^2 > r^2 - d_2^2, so d_1^2 < d_2^2 — and since distances are never negative, d_1 < d_2. The longer chord is the nearer one.
