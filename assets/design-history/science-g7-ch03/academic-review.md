## Scientific and safety revisions

- Restrict experiments to approved small 1.5 V cells, suitable holders and rated components. A generic instruction to use any battery would include dangerous high-current packs. Add no-short-circuit, dry-hand, damaged-cell, disconnection and disposal guidance.
- Correct the suggestion that more cells necessarily extend runtime. Series voltages add when oriented alike; reversing a cell opposes the other source. Cell orientation demonstrations require a suitable simple apparatus, not reversal in electronic devices.
- Keep the cap/disc observation specific to familiar cylindrical cells. The term battery can refer to a single cell in ordinary use.
- Separate current from transferred energy. Conventional current is described through the external circuit. A lamp does not consume current.
- Add a series resistor to every buildable LED circuit. The specific task uses a red LED suitable for 3 V, two 1.5 V cells, and 330 Ω, with component ratings checked by the teacher. Reverse demonstrations depend on the actual reverse-voltage rating; otherwise they remain diagram discussion. A generic resistor identification image is not a colour-code instruction.
- Qualify long/short LED lead identification because trimmed leads and other packages differ. Anode/cathode and the flat-edge convention are introduced without claiming universality.
- Preserve all six lamp connection topologies, redrawn as clear live connection maps. The filament path is shown with an unlit lamp symbol; the symbol key later formalises this convention. No results are coloured into setup figures.
- Use a paper clip and teacher-installed split-pin fasteners for the source homemade switch, with ends covered. Limit the “switch anywhere” statement to the single series loop being investigated.
- Keep no-glow observations separate from an absolute claim of zero conduction. Add a working tester control, contact and coating checks, graphite, and sensitivity limits.
- Correct the implication that DC only powers small devices. AC/DC describes direction behaviour, not safety or device size.
- Preserve the Bhakra Nangal learning context. Omit incidental travel-fare and train-route detail, which is not needed for the electrical concepts and may change.
- Generalise the two/four-cell picture to the connection principle rather than recommending a four-cell supply. Prefer holders over taped exposed contacts in the learner instructions.
