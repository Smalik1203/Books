activities and twelve assessment prompts are mapped in source-coverage.json.

## Corrections

The blue-litmus endpoint is basic, not proof of exact neutrality. An excess of
either reactant can remain. The salt-and-water word equation is scoped to an
acid reacting with a hydroxide base, so the carbonate question does not contradict
it. The tiny demonstration need not show a measurable temperature rise.

Tap water and plant extracts vary. Unchanged litmus or turmeric is bounded by
the test. Rose extract is compared with an unchanged control; shade is not an
exact measurement. Onion results with dilute baking soda may be inconclusive.

Learners do not prepare quicklime, taste/rub chemicals, smell unknown liquids,
mix cleaners or wear blindfolds in investigations. Adult handling of hot water,
cutting and prepared lime water is explicit. The source's ant-bite chemistry is
retained as a limitation of a beaker model, not a treatment instruction.

Soil treatment needs a diagnosis. Organic matter is not a universal alkaline-soil
remedy, and neutralising wastewater does not remove every pollutant. Cultural
context, Odia greeting, traditional turmeric use and Indian-language education
remain, without medical overclaims or superfluous historic absolutes.
